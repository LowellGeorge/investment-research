# MediaTek — The Seven Questions

**Ticker:** 2454 TT | **FY 2025 revenue:** NT$596bn (~US$19.1bn) | **GEM weight:** 5.0% (#4 in portfolio)
**Source base:** Quartr-grounded (Q4 2025 / FY 2025 earnings call, 4 February 2026; Q3 2025 call, 31 October 2025)
**Written:** April 2026

---

## Cold open

MediaTek has quietly become **the second-largest custom AI silicon design house in the world after Broadcom** — and nobody in the GEM world is framing it that way yet.

Rick Tsai told the Street on the Q4 2025 call that MediaTek expects **data center ASIC revenue of more than US$1 billion in 2026**, **multi-billion US dollars in 2027**, with a third programme already secured for 2028. He raised his addressable market estimate from US$50bn to **US$70bn by 2028**, with a 10–15% market share target. The first product — the Nvidia DGX Spark CPU (GB10) — is already shipping. This is no longer a smartphone chipmaker. It is a smartphone chipmaker that has bolted on an HPC/AI ASIC franchise the market hasn't fully priced.

Seven questions.

---

## 1. What is it?

MediaTek is the world's leading fabless designer of high-volume system-on-chip silicon, with three revenue groups:

- **Mobile (~59% of Q4 revenue, >US$10bn for FY 2025)** — the cash engine. Dimensity 9500 (flagship Android SoC) and Dimensity 8500 (premium tier) are gaining share against Qualcomm. Flagship SoCs alone contributed US$3bn in 2025
- **Smart Edge (~37% of revenue, US$3bn+ connectivity sub-segment)** — WiFi 7, WiFi 8, 5G modems, 10G PON, tablets, Chromebooks, smart TVs. Grew 21% in USD in 2025. WiFi 7 tripled, 5G modem doubled
- **Power IC (~5% of revenue)** — small, mostly automotive and industrial

The new fourth leg is **data center ASIC and HPC**. <2% of revenue today but the single most important strategic line. Tsai listed the technology investment areas explicitly: 400G SerDes, co-packaged optics, 3.5D packaging, custom HBM, integrated voltage regulators. That is a list of *Broadcom AVGO inputs*. MediaTek is positioning to be a co-second-source for hyperscaler custom silicon alongside Marvell.

The thing to internalise: MediaTek is structurally different from any other GEM holding. It's a fabless semi whose customers are Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, Honor, Oppo, *and now* Nvidia and at least one hyperscaler (unnamed but widely believed to be Google). R&D spend at **24.9% of revenue** is more like a US tech leader than an Asian commodity chipmaker.

## 2. Can we trust them?

Yes — the management quality is meaningfully under-appreciated outside Taiwan.

**Rick Tsai** has been CEO since 2018. He was previously Chairman and CEO of TSMC's wholly-owned subsidiary Vanguard International Semiconductor, and before that ran TSMC's North America operations. He is one of the most experienced foundry-savvy operators in the industry. **David Ku** is CFO.

**The 2022-23 tell.** When the post-pandemic smartphone downcycle hit in 2022-23, MediaTek's revenue dropped ~25%. Tsai did *not* cut R&D — he held R&D spend roughly flat in absolute terms, which meant R&D/sales spiked toward 27%. That investment through the trough is what funded the 2nm tape-out, the data center ASIC programme, and the Dimensity 9500 flagship wins coming to market now. A less disciplined operator would have cut to protect margins and been a smaller company today.

**Capital discipline from Q4 2025:**

- **2025 R&D spend NT$148.3bn — record, 24.9% of revenue** — entirely funded from operating cash flow
- Net cash balance sheet, conservatively NT$230bn+ (2024 was NT$235bn; similar territory after FY25 dividends + buybacks)
- Tsai quote on pricing (Q4 2025): *"we will adjust our pricing to reflect the rising supply chain cost and allocate our supply across products based on the overall profitability"* — protecting margins through pricing, not absorbing wafer cost
- 2026 guidance deliberately conservative (46% gross +/- 1.5pts, OpEx ratio 31% +/- 2) — they under-promise while investing heavily
- Dividend payout has been consistently ~80% of earnings for years — MediaTek is one of the most shareholder-friendly Taiwan large-caps

**The watch item.** Tsai is ~75. Succession over the next 24-36 months is the live question.

## 3. Why does value compound?

Three engines, each with a different time horizon.

**The smartphone franchise is structurally winning, not maturing.** Conventional wisdom is that smartphone chips are a low-growth, two-horse race that goes to Qualcomm. Actual data: MediaTek's mobile revenue grew 8% in USD in 2025 to >US$10bn, with Q4 setting a quarterly record. They are taking share at the *flagship* end (Dimensity 9500 selected by major Chinese OEMs for 2026 flagships). Qualcomm is still bigger, but MediaTek is the only player closing the gap. The market has under-modelled this base business for years.

**Data center ASIC is the AI re-rate.** Hyperscaler ASIC programmes typically run US$500m–$2bn per design over 4–5 year lifecycles, at 30–40% gross margins (lower than mobile, but enormous absolute dollars). MediaTek's stated trajectory — >$1bn 2026, multi-$bn 2027, follow-on starting 2028 — implies they have **at least three secured customer programmes** (one ramping, one pre-ramp, one in design). If even half materialises, the segment becomes 15–20% of revenue by 2028 and re-rates the multiple.

**Connectivity and smart edge as a quiet compounder.** US$3bn+ connectivity revenue, growing strongly. WiFi 7 is in a tripling phase. WiFi 8 launched at CES 2026 with end-of-year design wins expected. 5G modem doubled in 2025. Leading positions in Chromebooks, smart TVs, tablets, broadband CPE. None exciting individually; together a US$5bn+ revenue base growing 15–20%.

**The kicker nobody is pricing:** the Nvidia GB10 / DGX Spark partnership. MediaTek co-designed the CPU portion of Nvidia's flagship developer AI workstation. This is the entry point — the real question is whether Nvidia uses MediaTek for *more* of its silicon stack going forward. Tsai talked very deliberately on the Q4 call about "high-performance heterogeneous compute systems on advanced process nodes" — language designed for an Nvidia-listening audience.

## 4. What proves us right and what to track?

Six specific KPIs:

- **Data center ASIC revenue trajectory** — the single most important number. >US$1bn in 2026 is the first proof point; track quarterly disclosures
- **Customer concentration in data center ASIC** — if MediaTek discloses that they're working with 2+ named hyperscalers (Google, AWS, Meta, Microsoft), the moat narrative strengthens dramatically
- **Gross margin trajectory** — 2025 GM compressed 210bps to 47.5%; if 2026 holds at 46%+ as guided, mix is being managed well. Below 45% is a yellow flag
- **Smartphone flagship share** — Dimensity 9500 wins at major Chinese OEMs (Oppo, Vivo, Xiaomi) is the leading indicator. Track design wins for the 2027 Dimensity 9700 cycle
- **Automotive ramp** — DENSO ADAS partnership is the quality signal. If a Tier 1 like Continental or Bosch follows, auto becomes a real third leg
- **Operating margin recovery in 2H 2026** — if margins expand from Q3 2026 as data center ASIC scales, AI thesis is working financially

## 5. What's it worth?

Worth thinking about what's already in the price.

- Market cap at NT$1,990/share × ~1.60bn shares ≈ **NT$3.18tn (~US$102bn)**
- FY 2025 net income: NT$106bn (~US$3.4bn)
- Trailing P/E: **~30x**
- Dividend yield: ~3.5–4%

The stock is **already pricing in the AI re-rate** — at 30x, not cheap on trailing earnings. Question is whether forward earnings power grows into the multiple. Rough 2027 building blocks:

- Mobile NT$330bn (modest growth)
- Smart edge NT$240bn (mid-teens growth)
- Data center ASIC NT$60–90bn (multi-$bn USD scenario)
- Total revenue ~NT$700–750bn vs 2025's NT$596bn — i.e. 18–25% growth
- If operating margin recovers to 18–19% as ASIC scales, operating profit → ~NT$135bn
- ~30%+ EPS growth 2027 vs 2025

If you believe the data center ASIC trajectory, 30x trailing for ~30% forward growth is not heroic. If ASIC misses, stock is overvalued.

**Key sensitivity:** the second hyperscaler customer. First programme could be a one-off. Second confirms MediaTek is an institutional vendor in this market.

## 6. Where is the market wrong?

**One — MediaTek is valued as a mobile chipmaker with AI optionality, when it's becoming the opposite.** Bear framing is "smartphone TAM mature, data center is a side bet, fairly priced at 30x." But revenue mix is shifting fast: data center ASIC goes from <0.5% to ~5-7% in 2026 alone, and ~15-20% by 2028. At that point the *valuation framework* changes — you stop comparing to Qualcomm and start comparing to a small Broadcom or a large Marvell, both of which trade in the high-30s to mid-40s P/E range. The re-rate, if it comes, is bigger than consensus is modelling.

**Two — the smartphone business is structurally getting better, not worse.** Market treats smartphone as a tired commoditising end-market. Three counter-signals: (i) MediaTek won Dimensity 9500 into multiple flagships against Qualcomm — first time at this scale; (ii) flagship SoC revenue alone hit US$3bn — meaningful absolute dollars; (iii) Tsai's commentary on memory cost-driven smartphone weakness in 2026 was aimed at *Apple's iPhone* as much as anything else, setting MediaTek up for share gains in Android if Apple stumbles.

**The honest counter:** if Qualcomm's next Snapdragon dramatically improves on power efficiency and modem integration (historical strengths), MediaTek could lose flagship momentum. And if data center ASIC consolidates around Broadcom alone (what Broadcom wants), MediaTek's 10–15% share target is at risk. Both scenarios are real.

## 7. What could kill it, and when do we sell?

**Real risks, ranked:**

- **Hyperscaler ASIC concentration.** If data center is one customer (likely Google) and that customer dual-sources to Broadcom or builds in-house, the ramp stalls. Biggest binary risk
- **Smartphone share reversal.** Qualcomm is bigger and well-capitalised. If their next 2nm flagship beats Dimensity on benchmarks AND price, MediaTek loses momentum just as ASIC ramp needs the cash flow
- **Margin compression continues.** 2025 saw 210bps GM compression; if wafer cost pressure persists and pricing doesn't offset, operating margin drifts down despite revenue growth
- **Apple Modem/SoC vertical integration.** Not near-term but real over 5 years
- **China-specific risk.** Biggest customers are Chinese smartphone OEMs; a US export control extension targeting MediaTek shipments to specific named Chinese customers would be material
- **Foundry capacity allocation.** MediaTek depends entirely on TSMC for leading-edge; as TSMC tightens through 2027, MediaTek competes with Apple/Nvidia/AMD/Qualcomm for slots

**Sell triggers (specific, not vague):**

- Data center ASIC revenue meaningfully misses the >US$1bn 2026 guide (e.g. <US$700m for full year)
- Single largest ASIC customer concentration stays >70% by end-2026 — implies no second meaningful programme
- Two consecutive quarters of operating margin below 13%
- Loss of a major Chinese flagship smartphone slot (Oppo, Vivo, Xiaomi) to Qualcomm in the 2027 cycle
- Rick Tsai retires without an obvious technical successor
- US export controls extended to MediaTek's HPC or premium mobile silicon shipments to Chinese customers

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. MediaTek is the cleanest pure-play on a particular thesis: that the AI silicon market doesn't end with Broadcom and that fabless designers with deep customer relationships and TSMC-grade execution can build genuine data center franchises. The stock is not cheap on trailing earnings but reasonable if you believe even half of what management is signalling about 2027–28. The Q1 2026 print on 30 April — six days from now — is the next checkpoint, particularly for any updated colour on the data center ASIC trajectory.
