# SOSOC Top 20 briefing notes

A Quartr-grounded run through the twenty names in the SOSOC Top 20, designed to be listened to as a podcast. Each name has a pitch, an evidence paragraph, a read on the balance sheet and cash, a word on portfolio fit, and the live debate from the most recent earnings calls.

## 1. Century Pacific Food

**Pitch.** Century Pacific Food is the Philippines' largest branded canned and packaged food group, built around marine, meat and dairy categories. Its house brands — Century Tuna, 555, Argentina corned beef, Angel evaporated milk, Birch Tree, Kaffe de Oro and the Loma Linda plant-based range — sit in the everyday baskets of Filipino households across every income tier. Alongside the branded business, the group runs a meaningful original-equipment manufacturing operation that supplies private-label tuna and coconut products to global customers, anchored by a long-running supply contract with Vita Coco. It belongs in an emerging-market growth portfolio because it pairs a genuinely dominant domestic staples franchise with a hard-currency export earner, in a country with one of Asia's most attractive consumer demographics.

**Evidence.** In the most recent reported full year the company delivered revenue of approximately seventy-five and a half billion Philippine pesos, up from roughly sixty-seven billion the year before, a topline gain in the low teens. Operating income reached around seven and a half billion pesos for an operating margin near ten percent, and reported net income came in at about six and a third billion pesos for a net margin of roughly eight and a half percent. Diluted earnings per share were just under one peso eighty. Through the first nine months of the most recent year, branded volumes grew at a double-digit pace and net income rose ten percent to about five and three quarter billion pesos, with the branded segment now contributing close to eighty percent of group revenue.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The balance sheet is conservative. Cash sat at roughly three and a quarter billion pesos against total interest-bearing debt of around three and a third billion pesos, leaving the group essentially in a net-neutral position. Operating cash flow of about eight billion pesos comfortably funded capital expenditure of roughly two and a third billion and dividends paid of about three and four tenths billion. Capex intensity is modest, cash conversion is healthy, and management has guided full-year capex to around four billion pesos as it expands coconut processing capacity.

**Portfolio fit.** Century Pacific is the high-quality Philippine consumer staples anchor of the basket. It provides defensive ballast against the more cyclical Asian technology and industrial names, exposure to a young and underpenetrated domestic market, and a credible coconut and tuna export angle that benefits from global premiumisation in plant-based and functional categories.

**Debate.** The live debate on recent calls is about margins and the OEM cycle. Gross margin has been compressing on higher input costs, and analysts keep pushing management on how much of that is structural versus transient. The export tuna and coconut business swung to a year-on-year decline before stabilising, raising questions about how much the group should lean further into branded at the expense of OEM. Acquisitions of Loma Linda and a third coconut facility have widened the platform but added integration risk, and the stock's premium multiple leaves little room for execution slips.

## 2. Atour Lifestyle Holdings

**Pitch.** Atour is a Chinese upper-midscale hotel operator that has quietly become one of Asia's most interesting consumer compounders. The core business franchises lifestyle-branded hotels across mainland China under the Atour, Atour Light, Atour Origin and SAVHE banners, with two thousand and fifteen properties open at year-end and a membership base of one hundred and twelve million. Sitting alongside the hotel network is a fast-growing retail arm, Atour Planet, which sells sleep products built around the hotel experience and is now the leading branded sleep label in China. The combination of an asset-light franchise engine and a captive consumer brand makes it a rare way to own Chinese discretionary consumption without owning the property.

**Evidence.** Full-year twenty twenty-five net revenue reached renminbi nine point seven nine billion, roughly USD one point three six billion, up thirty-five percent year on year from renminbi seven point two five billion. Managed hotel revenue grew twenty-eight percent and the retail business surged sixty-seven percent to renminbi three point six seven billion. Operating income rose to renminbi two point three one billion from one point six two billion, a margin of twenty-four percent. Net income climbed to renminbi one point six two billion, around USD two hundred and twenty-five million, a net margin near seventeen percent. Operating cash flow was renminbi one point nine nine billion, close to USD two hundred and seventy-five million.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The balance sheet is exceptionally clean. Cash and short-term investments stood at renminbi five point eight seven billion, against total borrowings of just renminbi two hundred and fifty-two million, leaving a net cash pile north of USD seven hundred and fifty million. Capex was only renminbi eighty-six million for the year, a fraction of operating cash flow, reflecting the franchised model. Management returned capital aggressively, paying renminbi seven hundred and seventy-two million in dividends and repurchasing renminbi three hundred and thirty million of shares, with a stated target payout ratio of one hundred percent of prior-year GAAP net income.

**Portfolio fit.** Inside the SOSOC Top 20 this is the high-growth China consumer sleeve. It is not a recovery trade and not a defensive anchor. It is a mid-cap compounder where unit growth, retail extension and a Starbucks China loyalty tie-up combine into a genuine brand flywheel, paid for entirely by internal cash flow. The valuation-disconnect angle comes from the China discount applied to a business growing thirty-five percent with twenty-four percent operating margins.

**Debate.** The bear case sharpened over the last two calls. Same-hotel RevPAR ran below prior-year levels in both the third and fourth quarters, with Q4 RevPAR at ninety-nine point six percent of twenty twenty-four. Analysts pressed management on whether the hotel side is approaching saturation as franchisees turn more cautious and closures rise. Management guided twenty twenty-six revenue growth to twenty to twenty-four percent, a sharp deceleration, and openly flagged that net margin will slip as G and A and R and D step up. The retail debate is whether sixty-seven percent growth is repeatable against intensifying imitators in the Chinese sleep category, where Atour Planet is now the target rather than the challenger.
