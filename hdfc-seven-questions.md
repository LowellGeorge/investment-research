# HDFC Bank — The Seven Questions

HDFC Bank is the twenty-ninth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at one-point-six percent, with a market cap of around two hundred and thirty billion US dollars. Here is HDFC Bank in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

HDFC Bank just closed its fiscal year ending March 2026. Standalone net profit rose eleven percent to seven hundred and forty-seven billion rupees. Consolidated profit after tax reached seven hundred and sixty billion. Gross advances grew twelve percent, deposits grew fourteen-and-a-half percent — both outpacing Indian banking system growth. Gross non-performing assets fell to one-point-one-five percent. Net non-performing assets are just zero-point-three-eight percent. The customer base nearly doubled to one hundred million post-merger. Capital adequacy stands at nearly twenty percent with CET1 at seventeen-point-three. Cost-to-income is thirty-nine-point-five percent on a core basis. A final dividend of thirteen rupees per share has been recommended. And the IPO of subsidiary HDB Financial Services during the year generated a net gain of over ninety billion rupees standalone.

This is the largest Indian private bank three years into the largest Indian corporate merger ever, delivering operational stability, asset quality, and capital discipline.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

HDFC Bank is the largest private sector bank in India by market capitalisation, by deposits, and by loans. Following the 2023 merger with parent company HDFC Limited — India's largest mortgage lender — the combined entity is the second-largest bank in India overall after State Bank of India and has no real private-sector peer in scale.

The scale is institutional. Gross advances of twenty-nine-point-six trillion rupees. Deposits of thirty-one trillion. Nine thousand seven hundred branches post-merger — roughly doubling the pre-merger footprint. One hundred million customers. Capital adequacy at nearly twenty percent. The balance sheet is enormous by Indian private-bank standards and diversified across retail banking, commercial and wholesale banking, and specialty segments.

The loan-book mix shows the franchise breadth. Retail loans grew six-and-a-half percent. SME loans grew seventeen percent — notably strong. Corporate and wholesale lending grew thirteen percent. The MSME business grew twenty percent year-on-year and is ranked number one in fifteen of twenty-eight Indian states. The card business captures twenty-one to twenty-two percent of Indian credit card system issuance and twenty-six to twenty-eight percent of market spend — dominant in a high-margin retail product. Mortgage-business liability penetration rose from thirty-six percent to fifty percent post-merger, with ninety-eight percent of new home-loan customers also opening liability accounts.

The subsidiary portfolio compounds alongside the bank. HDB Financial Services — the consumer-finance subsidiary — completed an IPO during the year and posted forty-one percent profit growth in the fourth quarter. HDFC Life recorded full-year profit after tax of nineteen billion rupees. HDFC ERGO General Insurance at eight-point-one billion. HDFC Asset Management at twenty-nine billion profit after tax — one of India's largest mutual fund companies by assets.

The thing to internalise: HDFC Bank is not just a bank. It is the largest Indian financial services platform by scale, with the most complete product suite across retail banking, mortgages, consumer finance, life insurance, general insurance, and asset management.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2023-to-2026 post-merger integration is the clearest capital-allocation and execution case study in Indian banking.

Sashidhar Jagdishan has been Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer since October 2020. The founding institutional legacy of Deepak Parekh at HDFC Limited, combined with the HDFC Bank heritage under Aditya Puri, represents one of the cleanest governance records in Indian financial services over three decades.

The past-stress case that matters is the 2023 merger itself. In July 2023, HDFC Bank completed the merger with parent HDFC Limited — the largest corporate merger in Indian history, creating a combined entity with total assets roughly doubling. The integration required merging information technology systems, risk frameworks, credit policies, branch networks, product suites, and workforce cultures across two organisations that had operated independently for decades. The bank also had to absorb HDFC's entire mortgage book, deal with the reclassification of certain assets under bank regulation, rebuild net interest margin from post-merger lows, and continue growing retail banking and corporate lending.

Three years in, the integration result is remarkable: gross non-performing assets at one-point-one-five percent — better than pre-merger standalone HDFC Bank. Capital adequacy at nearly twenty percent — deeply over-capitalised. Core cost-to-income at thirty-nine-point-five percent — among the lowest of any major Indian bank. Retail banking growing, SME and MSME scaling, subsidiaries compounding, and the HDB Financial Services IPO crystallising value. That is the evidence of an exceptionally well-executed corporate transformation.

The capital discipline signals from the fiscal 2026 print are consistent. Provisioning buffer of one hundred and twenty-five basis points beyond required provisions. A floating provision of ninety billion rupees made during the year — conservative reserving. Credit cost at forty-one basis points. Book value per share at three hundred and sixty-six rupees standalone. Dividend recommendation of thirteen rupees per share plus the special interim dividend of two-and-a-half rupees paid in August 2025. Bonus share issuance of one-for-one in August 2025 improving liquidity. Technology investment approaching one billion US dollars and launch of a unified AI platform — meaningful capital commitment to the digital future. Board approval for bond issuance up to six hundred billion rupees for capital and infrastructure financing. Stated target to be carbon neutral by fiscal year 2032.

The watch item is the ongoing legal review related to former Chairman Atanu Chakraborty's earlier resignation and the Dubai branch regulatory restriction from the Dubai Financial Services Authority. Both are under review; no material financial impact is currently anticipated. Worth tracking.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is Indian retail banking and deposit franchise. Deposits grew fourteen-and-a-half percent, well above system growth. Time deposits grew fifteen-and-a-half percent. The funding mix has been deliberately shifted from borrowings toward time deposits, with forty-seven percent of incremental time deposits in the year under three crore rupees — meaning granular retail deposits. This is a structural advantage: cheap, granular deposits fund a growing loan book profitably for decades.

The second engine is mortgage cross-sell and the post-merger flywheel. Mortgage liability penetration rose from thirty-six percent to fifty percent post-merger. Ninety-eight percent of new home-loan customers now open a liability account. That is the merger synergy materialising: HDFC's mortgage customers now bank with HDFC Bank, generating deposits, payments, credit cards, and other products from the existing relationship. Over time this compounds into higher revenue per customer and better capital efficiency.

The third engine is the subsidiary portfolio. HDB Financial Services post-IPO with forty-one percent quarterly profit growth is a meaningful consumer-finance contributor. HDFC Life, HDFC ERGO, and HDFC Asset Management together contribute diversified earnings across insurance and asset management — categories with multi-decade Indian financialisation tailwinds.

The kicker is the digital and AI platform. The one-billion-dollar technology investment and unified AI platform enable operational leverage — ninety-seven percent of payments and ninety-two percent of acquisition journeys are digital. As digital share expands, fixed costs amortise over more activity, customer-acquisition cost falls, and revenue per customer compounds. Indian banking is in the early stages of AI-enabled efficiency gains; HDFC Bank is best-positioned to capture them.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, deposit growth — fourteen-and-a-half percent; maintenance above system growth confirms the funding franchise. Second, net interest margin — three-point-three-eight percent in the fourth quarter; stability or recovery confirms the spread engine. Third, gross non-performing assets — one-point-one-five percent; maintenance below one-point-five confirms asset quality. Fourth, cost-to-income ratio — thirty-nine-point-five percent core; progression toward thirty-seven or lower confirms operating leverage. Fifth, subsidiary earnings — HDB, Life, ERGO, AMC; continued compound confirms the platform thesis. Sixth, mortgage-liability attach rate — fifty percent today from thirty-six percent; continued improvement confirms post-merger flywheel.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year fiscal 2026 standalone net profit was seven hundred and forty-seven billion rupees. Consolidated profit after tax was seven hundred and sixty billion. Basic earnings per share was forty-nine rupees consolidated. Return on assets was one-point-one-five percent. Return on equity in the fourth quarter was fourteen percent. Book value per share at three hundred and sixty-six rupees. Market cap is around two hundred and thirty billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, HDFC Bank trades at roughly three to three-and-a-half times book and around twenty times consolidated earnings. For a Indian bank with nearly twenty percent capital adequacy, one-point-one-five percent gross non-performing assets, fourteen-percent return on equity, and dominant market position, that is a premium multiple that reflects the quality. Comparable set: ICICI Bank at similar-to-lower multiples; Kotak Mahindra at lower multiples; global banking leaders at materially lower multiples for lower returns on equity and less favourable macro backdrops.

The key sensitivity is Indian interest rate cycle. Further Reserve Bank of India rate cuts compress net interest margin modestly. Second sensitivity is Indian credit cycle — if retail or MSME credit quality deteriorates, provisions rise. Third is political and regulatory — the ongoing Dubai branch review and former Chairman resignation are live items.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus prices HDFC Bank as fully-valued after a period of outperformance post-merger integration. The variant is that the merger-synergy flywheel — specifically mortgage-liability attachment rising from thirty-six to fifty percent, with ninety-eight percent of new home loans opening liability accounts — is a multi-year compounder that sell-side models do not fully capture. As the attached deposit base generates cross-sell and the funding cost advantage compounds, the return on equity trajectory could exceed current consensus.

Second: the subsidiary portfolio is priced as a modest consolidated premium rather than as a set of category-leading standalone businesses. HDFC Asset Management alone, with twenty-nine billion rupees of profit and top-three position in Indian asset management, commands a material standalone multiple. HDFC Life and HDFC ERGO similarly. HDB Financial Services post-IPO provides a transparent market valuation. On a sum-of-parts basis, the consolidated premium should be higher.

The honest counter: Indian banking is globally priced high. HDFC Bank's premium to global banks reflects Indian growth, but also leaves little room for disappointment on execution. Any material slip — on asset quality, on net interest margin, on cost-to-income — would compress the multiple quickly. And the sheer size of the bank means incremental market share gain is harder from here than from a smaller base.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Indian credit cycle turn. If unemployment rises or MSME stress elevates non-performing asset formation, provisions rise and earnings compress. Two: Reserve Bank of India rate cuts compressing net interest margin. Three: regulatory events. The Dubai branch restriction is live; the former Chairman resignation review is ongoing; any material adverse outcome compresses sentiment. Four: Jagdishan succession. He has been in role since 2020; explicit succession planning is a multi-year watch item. Five: Subsidiary underperformance. HDB, Life, ERGO, or AMC hiccups compress consolidated. Six: Indian equity market valuation compression. Any broader Indian bank de-rating wave hits HDFC alongside peers.

Specific sell triggers. Gross non-performing assets rising above one-point-seven-five percent for two consecutive quarters. Return on equity falling below twelve percent for two consecutive quarters without a clear one-off. Deposit growth decelerating below system growth for two consecutive quarters — funding franchise weakness. Net interest margin falling below three-point-one percent for two consecutive quarters. Material adverse outcome in the Dubai branch review or the former Chairman resignation review. Sashidhar Jagdishan stepping back without a named credible successor.
