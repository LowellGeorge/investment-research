# Totvs — The Seven Questions

Totvs is the tenth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at three-point-five percent. It is a small-to-mid-cap Brazilian software company with a market value in the low single-digit billions of US dollars. Here is Totvs in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Totvs just closed 2025 with recurring revenue at ninety-one percent of total revenue, free-cash-flow conversion of ninety-eight percent of adjusted net income, and the largest share repurchase programme in the company's history announced alongside the results. Full-year adjusted earnings per share grew twenty-five percent. Return on invested capital reached twenty percent. In February 2026 the company officially launched its proprietary artificial intelligence foundation, called LYNN, with a committed six-hundred-million Brazilian reais of investment over four years. And the long-awaited Linx acquisition — Brazil's dominant retail software business — received regulatory approval and is now being integrated.

This is what a Brazilian software compounder looks like when the model is working. The question is whether the rest of the world will still pay emerging-market prices for emerging-market risk on a recurring-revenue software business with these numbers.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Totvs is the dominant enterprise software company in Brazil. Three segments, one franchise.

The first segment is Management. This is the classic ERP core — the accounting, manufacturing, payroll, and operations software that runs the back office of Brazilian companies. Totvs has number-one share in the Brazilian management-software market — more than twice the share of the nearest competitor, with the local combination of SAP, Oracle and Microsoft behind. Management-segment revenue grew seventeen percent year-on-year in the fourth quarter, with recurring revenue up nineteen percent, and the segment's adjusted earnings-before-interest-taxes-depreciation-and-amortisation margin expanded to nearly twenty-nine percent.

The second segment is Techfin — financial services products embedded into the client base. This is Totvs using its incumbent position inside client workflows to cross-sell credit, payments, and working-capital products. Techfin's full-year adjusted margin improved from roughly eleven percent in 2024 to thirteen-and-a-half percent in 2025, with full-year adjusted profit up forty-three percent year-on-year.

The third segment is Business Performance, which includes RD Station — the leading sales and marketing software platform in Brazil — alongside human-resources and manufacturing-specific modules. RD Station recurring revenue grew sixteen percent and its full-year earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation grew forty-five percent.

On top of these three segments sits a unique distribution asset: the franchise network. Totvs sells through a nation-wide network of franchisees who own the local client relationships in their regions. This is how a single Brazilian software company reaches small and mid-sized manufacturers in the interior of the country. No multinational competitor has figured out how to replicate it.

The thing to internalise: Totvs is not a software company hoping for growth through acquisition. It is an infrastructure incumbent embedded in the operating systems of Brazilian business, at ninety-one percent recurring revenue, with a distribution network that multinationals cannot buy or build.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the Linx story is the single clearest evidence we have.

Laércio Cosentino founded the company in 1983 as Microsiga and still chairs it. The current Chief Executive is Dennis Herszkowicz, in the role since 2019 — a career Totvs insider who previously ran the flagship management segment.

The past-stress case that matters is the Linx saga from 2020 to 2025. In 2020 Totvs made a public offer to acquire Linx, the dominant retail-specific software company in Brazil. Stone — the Brazilian payments company — intervened with a higher offer. Totvs walked away rather than overpay. Stone paid a premium price, integrated Linx poorly, and spent the following four years trying and failing to extract the expected synergies. In 2024 and 2025 Totvs came back, negotiated the asset out of Stone at a materially better price than Stone had paid, and received regulatory clearance. This is the single cleanest capital-allocation tell in the company's history: they walked away from a bad price, waited, and bought at a better one. Most management teams do the opposite.

The fourth-quarter capital discipline signals reinforce the story. Recurring revenue at ninety-one percent of total revenue, up two hundred basis points year-on-year. Adjusted earnings margin at twenty-seven percent in the quarter, up a hundred-and-seventy basis points. Full-year free cash flow reached eight-hundred-and-eighty-nine million Brazilian reais, converting ninety-eight percent of adjusted net income. Return on invested capital reached twenty percent, up eighty basis points. The company ended the year in a net cash position of roughly two hundred million Brazilian reais despite funding the Linx acquisition and raising three billion in a debenture issuance in January 2026 rated top-tier by Fitch. Alongside all of this: the largest share buyback programme in the company's history, and the sale of the minority stake in Dimensa announced in February 2026 — a portfolio pruning, not empire-building.

Management's framing of the artificial intelligence investment is telling. They launched LYNN explicitly as an *artificial narrow intelligence* foundation — not an artificial general intelligence ambition, not a model-of-models story. It is narrow, vertical-specific, embedded in ERP workflows, with a bounded six-hundred-million-reais capital plan over four years. Operators setting clear budget boundaries for a hyped technology — not chasing it.

The watch item is Herszkowicz tenure. He is still the same person who executed the 2020 walk-away and the 2025 completion. Continuity of him, Laércio's board presence, and the senior finance team is the succession story that matters.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is the incumbent ERP flywheel. Totvs sits at number-one share in Brazilian management software. The churn characteristic of enterprise resource planning software is very low — once a company installs the system, rips and replaces are rare and expensive. Every new module bolted onto an existing client — human resources, manufacturing-specific, sales CRM via RD Station, financial services via Techfin — extends the revenue-per-customer curve at near-zero customer-acquisition cost. Recurring revenue per employee is up over eleven percent in 2025, reflecting both price and module attach.

The second engine is the franchise distribution moat. The franchise network is the thing no foreign competitor can replicate. When a Brazilian mid-market manufacturer in the interior needs their ERP updated, the franchisee shows up on-site. SAP, Oracle and Microsoft cannot economically field that. This is why Totvs keeps gaining share year after year in a market that, in theory, should have been fully penetrated a decade ago.

The third engine is the cloud and software-as-a-service migration. Cloud revenue inside the management segment grew twenty-four percent in the quarter and twenty-eight percent for the full year. This is a high-quality revenue upgrade: higher gross margin, longer-duration recurring revenue, and operating leverage as the infrastructure investment amortises. The company is in the mid-stage of the on-premise-to-cloud transition — a multi-year tailwind with visibility.

The kicker is the Techfin and TaaS monetisation. Techfin — embedded financial services — is still only thirteen-and-a-half percent margin and growing forty-three percent. The real optionality is the adjacent "Technology-as-a-Service" enabler revenues — bundling Totvs technology through partners — which already represented over seventeen percent of total revenue in 2025, up thirty-five percent year-on-year. Incumbent client base plus embedded fintech plus open technology layer is the same architecture Constellation Software runs. Totvs is the Brazilian version of that playbook, at a much earlier stage and at much cheaper prices.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, recurring revenue as a percentage of total — ninety-one percent today, up two hundred basis points year-on-year. Watch for continued mix shift toward ninety-three percent plus. Second, cloud revenue growth inside the management segment — currently twenty-four to twenty-eight percent; a sustained reading above twenty percent confirms the transition is still running. Third, Techfin margin — thirteen-and-a-half percent today. The scaling target is twenty percent-plus over three years. Fourth, Technology-as-a-Service enabler revenues as a percentage of total — seventeen percent today and up thirty-five percent; a continued mix lift toward twenty-five percent re-rates the business. Fifth, Linx integration milestones — the synergy realisation and the revenue stability of the retail-software franchise through the integration period. Sixth, free-cash-flow conversion — ninety-eight percent is near the structural ceiling; if it drops below eighty, investigate.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 adjusted net income was roughly nine hundred and ten million Brazilian reais, up twenty-six percent. Free cash flow was eight hundred and eighty-nine million. Adjusted earnings per share over the last twelve months was one-point-five-five reais, up twenty-five percent. Return on invested capital reached twenty percent. The company sits on a small net cash position after the Linx deal.

Market cap at time of writing is in the low single-digit billions of US dollars — small by global software standards, which is part of what keeps it off the radar of international institutions. On local valuation metrics the stock trades at roughly twenty to twenty-five times forward earnings and a mid-teens multiple of enterprise value to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation. Free-cash-flow yield is around five to six percent.

The simple framing: a ninety-one-percent-recurring-revenue Brazilian software compounder with a twenty-percent return on invested capital, a dominant share position, a unique franchise distribution moat, a fresh artificial intelligence investment cycle, and the Linx integration ahead, trading at low-twenties on forward earnings. For comparison, Constellation Software in Canada trades at thirty-plus times on comparable metrics but a lower growth rate.

The key sensitivity is Brazilian currency and interest rates. A weaker real translates to lower US-dollar reported earnings. Higher Brazilian rates compress the valuation multiple via discount-rate mechanics. Both have been the structural headwinds of the last three years and both could reverse just as mechanically.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: the market treats Totvs as a "Brazilian software stock" and prices it accordingly, when the right comparable set is global mid-market ERP compounders. Constellation Software, Sage in the United Kingdom, and mid-cap United States vertical software names all trade at materially higher multiples for worse underlying metrics — lower recurring-revenue share, lower return on invested capital, or weaker franchise moats. Totvs is genuinely a software compounder in the Constellation mould. Its price reflects local Brazilian risk rather than the global business characteristics.

Second: the Linx acquisition is being treated by consensus as "another integration risk event" when the evidence of the 2020-to-2025 saga is that Totvs management is exceptional at disciplined acquisition and integration. They walked away from Linx at a bad price in 2020; they bought it at a better price in 2025 after Stone had done the expensive lesson-learning on the asset. The pessimistic case on Linx assumes Totvs repeats Stone's mistakes. The base case assumes they do what their track record says they do: integrate carefully and extract the retail-vertical cross-sell into the existing franchise network.

The honest counter to both variants: Brazilian equity risk is real and is not going to disappear quickly. Rates, currency, political uncertainty, and tax reform implementation through 2033 all create structural headwinds on the multiple. Even a perfect operational outcome may not produce a Constellation-style re-rating if Brazilian rates stay high and the currency stays weak.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Brazilian tax reform implementation. The reform runs through 2033 and reshapes client compliance and product requirements. Totvs benefits from being the incumbent updater — but if the rules change too fast for the engineering teams, it could strain margins. Two: Linx integration execution. This is the largest acquisition in the company's history and the first big retail-vertical expansion. Stone-style execution problems would damage both earnings and the track record. Three: artificial intelligence disruption of the ERP moat. The six-hundred-million-reais LYNN investment is precisely because this risk is real. Vertical-specific large-language-model agents could, in theory, reduce the switching cost of enterprise resource planning — a long-dated but genuine risk. Four: Brazilian rates staying structurally high. Higher rates compress the valuation multiple regardless of operating performance. Five: franchise channel conflict. As Totvs goes more direct through cloud delivery, the incentive alignment with franchisees becomes harder to manage. Six: Techfin credit-cycle exposure. Embedded credit products carry credit risk. A Brazilian small-business credit downturn would hit Techfin first.

Specific sell triggers. Recurring revenue as a percentage of total falling below eighty-eight percent for two consecutive quarters — mix regression. Free-cash-flow conversion dropping below seventy-five percent for more than one quarter without a one-off explanation — cash-quality deterioration. Return on invested capital falling below fifteen percent for two consecutive quarters — compounding machine breaking. Linx integration producing material synergy misses or client losses in the first twenty-four months. Herszkowicz leaving without a named, credible internal successor. A material artificial-intelligence-driven enterprise-resource-planning displacement win by a foreign vendor inside a Totvs flagship account.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. Totvs is the compounder hiding in plain sight in Brazil: dominant share, unique franchise distribution, ninety-one percent recurring revenue, ninety-eight percent cash conversion, twenty-percent return on invested capital, and a fresh artificial intelligence and retail-software leg of growth. The market keeps pricing Brazilian risk more heavily than the operating characteristics warrant. The next checkpoint is the first-quarter 2026 results on the seventh of May. Watch the recurring-revenue mix, cloud growth inside Management, and the first public Linx integration milestones.
