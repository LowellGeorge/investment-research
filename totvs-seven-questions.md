# TOTVS — Seven Questions

An investment summary of TOTVS — Latin America's largest enterprise-software company, based in Brazil — prepared on the 2nd of July 2026, through a lens of quality and long-term ownership.

## Question One: Business Description — What Is It?

TOTVS is Latin America's largest enterprise-software company and the dominant ERP provider to Brazilian small and mid-sized businesses. Its core Management segment sells mission-critical business systems — ERP, human resources, and vertical solutions across a dozen industries — where deep localisation for Brazil's notoriously complex tax and labour rules is the moat. Around this core sit two adjacencies built over the last five years: Business Performance, which is RD Station, a digital-marketing software-as-a-service business; and Techfin, business-to-business credit and financial services distributed through the ERP base, in partnership with Itau. Revenue is over 91 percent recurring, with software-as-a-service and cloud the growth engine. In February 2026 TOTVS closed the 3.05 billion Brazilian reais acquisition of Linx, the leading Brazilian retail-software specialist, adding roughly 1 billion Brazilian reais of revenue and consolidating its position in retail verticals.

## Question Two: Management and Governance — Can We Trust Them?

This is one of Brazil's better governance stories: a true corporation with a dispersed shareholder base, professional management, and a founder who moved upstairs deliberately. Laercio Cosentino, who built the company from 1983, handed the chief executive role to Dennis Herszkowicz in 2019 and chairs the board alongside vice-chair Maria Leticia Costa. The Herszkowicz era has delivered: financial-year 2025 adjusted net income up 26 percent, return on invested capital of 20 percent — up 80 basis points — and free cash flow converting 98 percent of adjusted net income. Capital allocation has been disciplined and shareholder-aware — the largest buyback programme in the company's history, the divestment of the non-core Dimensa, and a triple-A-rated 3 billion Brazilian reais debenture to fund Linx rather than dilutive equity. The founder's public refusal to sell shares into the 2026 "SaaSpocalypse" scare is the right signal of owner alignment. Items to keep under review: an acquisitive culture that must now prove Linx integration, and Techfin's credit risk appetite.

## Question Three: Investment Thesis — Why Does Value Compound?

A dominant, defensible franchise: roughly half the Brazilian ERP market overall, and about 52 percent of the small-company segment, in a market where the top three vendors hold about 83 percent and where SAP and Oracle focus up-market on the largest corporates. Brazil's tax complexity — now compounded by tax reform — is a structural barrier that favours the local specialist.

Mission-critical stickiness: software-as-a-service retention of 98.2 percent and recurring revenue above 91 percent of the total; clients do not rip out the system that files their taxes.

Compounding with expanding returns: first-quarter 2026 revenue up 16 percent year on year, with software-as-a-service up 24 percent; adjusted EBITDA margin at a record 28.5 percent, up 200 basis points year on year; return on invested capital of 21 percent and rising; and free cash flow up 70 percent year on year.

Multiple engines on one distribution base: cross-selling Techfin credit — at 3.3 billion Brazilian reais of quarterly production — and RD Station, with annual recurring revenue up 22 percent, into the captive ERP base; and Linx adds the retail vertical with clear margin-convergence potential.

And AI as total-addressable-market expansion, not disruption: the proprietary LYNN AI platform and a new infrastructure-as-a-service offering in T-Cloud position TOTVS to monetise AI agents inside workflows it already owns — management is explicit that it sees itself insulated from the generic software-as-a-service disruption trade.

## Question Four: Key Milestones and KPIs — What Proves Us Right, and What to Track?

Linx integration — full consolidation from the second quarter of 2026; watch recurring-revenue growth and the promised convergence of Linx margins toward the Management segment's 30-percent-plus level.

The organic engine — software-as-a-service growth sustained above about 20 percent, retention holding around 98 percent, and Management-segment margin above 30 percent.

Techfin credit quality — provisions for expected credit losses, with agribusiness flagged, and the margin path as the Selic rate moves; management is prioritising portfolio quality over growth.

The balance sheet — deleveraging from 3.4 billion Brazilian reais of post-Linx net debt on more than 900 million Brazilian reais of annual free cash flow.

And a consensus check, from FactSet — the Street models sales of about 7.5 billion Brazilian reais in estimated financial year 2026, rising to about 10.2 billion by financial year 2028, a Linx-driven step-up, and earnings per share of 1.75, then 2.11, then 2.61 Brazilian reais; rating consensus is a buy, at 11 of 12.

On the financials, in Brazilian reais: net revenue was more than 5.7 billion in financial year 2025, 1.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and is estimated at about 7.5 billion in financial year 2026, about 8.76 billion in 2027, and about 10.24 billion in 2028. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 26.2 percent in 2025 and 28.5 percent in the first quarter. Adjusted net income was 910 million in 2025 and 252 million in the first quarter. Earnings per share ran 1.55 Brazilian reais on a last-twelve-months basis, and were up 21 percent year on year in the first quarter, with estimates of 1.75, then 2.11, then 2.61 across financial years 2026 to 2028. Free cash flow was 889 million in 2025 and 201 million in the first quarter. The forward estimates are FactSet consensus means as of the 2nd of July 2026, with the step-up reflecting Linx consolidation.

## Question Five: Five-Year Fair Value and Return — the Team's View

At 28.98 Brazilian reais the shares trade at about 16.6 times estimated financial-year-2026 earnings, about 13.7 times 2027, and about 11.1 times 2028 consensus earnings per share — an undemanding multiple for an about-20-percent return-on-invested-capital compounder, reflecting a Brazil-risk discount and post-Linx integration uncertainty. TOTVS is not memory-cyclical, but its earnings carry Brazilian macro cyclicality — the Selic rate, foreign exchange, and small-and-mid-business health — so anchor on a sensible mid-cycle growth path rather than the current acceleration.

Illustrative scenarios, as placeholders for the team's house numbers. The bull case: Linx synergies land and Techfin scales, earnings per share compound about 20 percent to more than 4 Brazilian reais by financial year 2030, with a re-rating toward 20 times as rates fall — a high-teens to about 25 percent internal rate of return in Brazilian reais. The base case: mid-teens earnings-per-share growth to about 3.0 to 3.5 Brazilian reais, with the multiple roughly unchanged — a low-to-mid-teens internal rate of return plus a modest dividend. The bear case: macro stress, Linx disappoints, and Techfin provisions bite; high-single-digit earnings-per-share growth on a de-rated low-teens multiple — roughly flat to mid-single-digit internal rate of return. Currency is an unhedged swing factor for a foreign owner in all three.

## Question Six: Variant Perception — Where the Market Is Wrong

Two mispricings, in our view. First, the market treats TOTVS as a Brazil macro proxy — the stock de-rates with the Selic rate and the real — when recurring revenue above 91 percent, 98 percent retention, and inflation-indexed contracts make the earnings stream far more resilient than the multiple implies. Second, the global "SaaSpocalypse" narrative lumps TOTVS in with generic seat-based software-as-a-service that AI may disintermediate. The variant view is that localised, mission-critical systems of record with a captive distribution base are among the best-placed AI beneficiaries: LYNN and agent-based offerings monetise AI inside the workflow, and Brazil's tax reform forces every client into upgrade cycles only the incumbent can deliver. Note the tension honestly: consensus financial-year-2026 sales revisions have been drifting down — zero up, six down over the recent window — partly mechanical after the Dimensa divestment, so the Street is not yet paying for this view.

## Question Seven: Risks, Premortem, and Sell Discipline

The premortem — if this goes wrong in five years, the likely causes are these. Linx indigestion: Stone never made retail software work; TOTVS pays 3.05 billion Brazilian reais and margins fail to converge, absorbing management attention and cash. A Techfin credit cycle: agribusiness provisions are already rising; a Brazilian credit downturn turns the financial-services engine into a loss centre. Macro and currency: a persistently high Selic rate pressures small-and-mid-business clients and contract pricing, and Brazilian-real depreciation erodes hard-currency returns. AI disruption from below: AI-native challengers compress ERP pricing for smaller clients faster than TOTVS can shift monetisation to agents and consumption. And an acquisition treadmill: post-Linx leverage, at 3.4 billion Brazilian reais of net debt, plus continued acquisition appetite, degrades returns on capital.

Sell discipline: trim or exit on, first, software-as-a-service retention falling materially below the about-98-percent level, or recurring growth decelerating below low-double-digits organically; second, Linx margins failing to converge toward segment levels within about two years of consolidation; third, Techfin losses forcing a strategic retreat; or fourth, capital allocation shifting from disciplined compounding to serial empire-building. Hold through ordinary Brazilian macro noise — that volatility is the price of the entry multiple, not a thesis break.
