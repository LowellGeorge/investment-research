# Tsingtao Brewery — The Seven Questions

Tsingtao Brewery is the thirty-sixth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at zero-point-two percent, with a market cap of around six-and-a-half billion US dollars. Here is Tsingtao in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Tsingtao Brewery just closed 2025 with revenue of thirty-two-and-a-half billion yuan, up one percent year-on-year, and net profit attributable to shareholders of four-and-a-half billion yuan, up five-and-a-half percent. Beer sales volume reached seven-point-six-five million kiloliters, up one-and-a-half percent. Earnings per share rose to three-point-three-six yuan from three-point-one-nine. Net profit margin expanded to fourteen-and-a-half percent. The proposed final cash dividend of two-point-three-five yuan per share takes total proposed 2025 dividends to three-point-two billion yuan. Greenhouse gas emission intensity declined thirteen-and-a-half percent and ninety-one percent of production now uses renewable energy. The company commenced localised international production for the first time in 2025.

This is the iconic Chinese brewer in a structurally consolidating Chinese beer industry, slowly compounding through premium-mix expansion.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Tsingtao Brewery is one of China's two largest beer companies, alongside China Resources Beer, with a heritage dating to 1903. The flagship Tsingtao brand is the most internationally recognised Chinese beer label. Domestic operations dominate the portfolio, with the Shandong region remaining the largest revenue contributor, followed by North China and South China.

The structural backdrop matters. Total Chinese beer industry output declined one-point-one percent in 2025, with market concentration among the top five brewers exceeding ninety percent. This is a consolidated, mature industry where volume growth is structurally muted but price-mix improvement is the lever. Consumer trends have shifted toward premium, health-oriented, and personalised products, supporting the higher-margin segments where Tsingtao has been investing.

The 2025 print reflects the disciplined operating model. Revenue grew one percent. Gross margin improved as cost of sales declined from nineteen-point-two billion to eighteen-point-nine billion yuan. Net profit margin expanded to fourteen-and-a-half percent from fourteen percent. Sustainability metrics improved meaningfully, with greenhouse gas emission intensity declining thirteen-and-a-half percent and ninety-one percent of production powered by renewable energy.

The thing to internalise: Tsingtao is not a growth story. It is a stable, brand-iconic Chinese consumer-staples compounder running at fifteen-percent net margins in a consolidated category, with capital return and modest premium-mix tailwinds.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the disciplined dividend record plus the green-manufacturing investment is the evidence.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are consistent. Total cash dividends distributed for fiscal 2024 were three billion yuan; proposed 2025 dividends are three-point-two billion. The dividend payout ratio is high — Tsingtao returns the majority of cash earnings to shareholders. Production investments continued with new and expansion projects in 2025, including new production bases and a biomanufacturing project. Restricted-share incentive plan unlocking and listing was managed cleanly. No material litigation or arbitration during the period.

The sustainability commitment is genuine and specific. Greenhouse gas emission intensity down thirteen-and-a-half percent. Ninety-one percent of production using renewable energy. The biomanufacturing project moves toward emerging revenue streams beyond beer. ESG positioning is meaningful for a Chinese consumer-staples company.

The watch item is competitive intensity. New entrants and channels are increasing market expenditures, and the consumer trend toward premium and personalised products is real. Tsingtao must invest in brand and innovation to maintain pricing power.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a kicker. First: premium-mix expansion. Chinese consumer trends toward premium, health-oriented, and personalised beer products favour brands with heritage and quality positioning — Tsingtao's flagship product is the most recognisable. Second: industry consolidation, with top-five concentration above ninety percent meaning rational pricing across the major operators rather than price wars. Third: cost-of-goods discipline through renewable energy and operational efficiency. The kicker is international expansion through localised production — a multi-decade lever as Chinese beer brands gain global presence beyond the diaspora market.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six metrics. Net profit margin — fourteen-and-a-half percent today, watch for stability or expansion. Sales volume growth — one-and-a-half percent today; watch for premium-product mix shift even if total volume is flat. Gross margin — improving in 2025; sustained improvement confirms premium discipline. Dividend payout — three-point-two billion yuan proposed for 2025; continuation confirms capital return. International revenue contribution — emerging in 2025; watch for scale. Renewable energy share of production — ninety-one percent today.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

Full-year 2025 net profit attributable to shareholders was four-and-a-half billion yuan, roughly six hundred and fifty million US dollars. Earnings per share was three-point-three-six yuan. Total equity attributable to shareholders rose to thirty-and-a-half billion yuan. Market cap is around six-and-a-half billion US dollars. Trading multiples sit at around ten times trailing earnings — value-priced for a Chinese consumer staples name.

The simple framing: a stable, brand-iconic Chinese brewer with high payout, in a consolidated industry, at value multiples. Comparable set: Heineken at higher multiples for global diversification; Carlsberg at similar multiples for slower growth; Anheuser-Busch InBev at variable multiples; China Resources Beer at similar multiples.

The key sensitivity is Chinese consumer beer demand and premiumisation pace. A faster shift away from beer to spirits or non-alcoholic alternatives compresses volume; slower premiumisation compresses margin expansion.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

First variant: consensus prices Tsingtao as a flat-volume Chinese consumer cyclical with no growth. The reality is fifteen-percent net margins, three billion yuan of annual dividends, premium-mix expansion, and emerging international optionality — a stable yield-plus-modest-growth profile that warrants better than ten-times earnings if executed. Second: the international localisation move is priced at zero. Tsingtao is the most globally-recognised Chinese beer brand; localised production in foreign markets opens a multi-decade growth leg.

The honest counter: Chinese beer category demand is genuinely declining at the volume level. Premiumisation is real but slower than executives forecast. Competitive intensity is rising. The valuation discount is partly justified by genuine category headwinds.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

Risks: Chinese beer category accelerating volume decline; raw material price spikes; weather and climate effects on consumption; competitive intensity from China Resources Beer, Anheuser-Busch InBev, and craft entrants; international expansion execution; consumer shift to spirits or non-alcoholic categories.

Sell triggers: net profit margin falling below twelve percent for two consecutive years; sales volume declining more than three percent year-on-year; dividend payout ratio cut materially; major loss of market share in flagship Shandong region; renewable-energy commitment reversing.

One company, seven questions. Tsingtao Brewery is the iconic Chinese brewer running at stable margins in a consolidated industry, with capital return, premium-mix expansion, and emerging international optionality.
