# NetEase — The Seven Questions

NetEase is the twenty-third-largest position in the GEM portfolio at two-point-one percent, with a market cap of around seventy-five to eighty billion US dollars. Here is NetEase in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

NetEase just closed 2025 with full-year net revenues of one hundred and thirteen billion yuan, up seven percent, including games and value-added services at ninety-two billion, up ten percent. That is the twenty-third consecutive year of online-games revenue growth — one of the longest compounding records in global technology. Gross margin expanded to sixty-four percent. Non-GAAP net income grew eleven percent to thirty-seven billion yuan, roughly five-point-three billion US dollars. Operating cash flow reached fifty-one billion yuan. Net cash on the balance sheet sits at one hundred and sixty-four billion yuan — about twenty-three billion US dollars — which is roughly a third of the market cap. Where Winds Meet — one of NetEase's flagship new titles — surpassed eighty million players globally. Marvel Rivals and Sword of Justice achieved top rankings internationally. Blizzard titles in China, restored after the 2023 dispute, delivered record-high annual revenue.

This is what a twenty-eight-year Chinese technology compounder looks like when the games model, the global expansion, the AI integration, and the capital return are all firing.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

NetEase is the second-largest Chinese gaming company after Tencent, and a diversified internet company with a clear gaming core plus meaningful educational and music segments. Three main businesses plus innovative layers.

The first and dominant business is Games and related Value-Added Services. Full-year 2025 revenue was ninety-two billion yuan, up ten percent. This includes NetEase's proprietary game franchises, the long-running Fantasy Westward Journey series — both the online and mobile versions delivered record-high annual revenues in 2025 — the newer global hits like Where Winds Meet, Marvel Rivals, and Sword of Justice, and the China operation of Blizzard's Warcraft, Overwatch, and other titles.

The second is Youdao — the educational technology subsidiary. Full-year 2025 revenue grew to five-point-nine billion yuan, up modestly. More importantly, Youdao achieved its first net cash inflow from operating activities in 2025, marking the inflection point from investment-stage business to self-funding subsidiary.

The third is NetEase Cloud Music — the music streaming and social business. Full-year 2025 revenue was seven-point-eight billion yuan, down slightly from the prior year, reflecting category maturation and competitive pressure.

Innovative businesses and other segments — which includes emerging AI-related products, gaming adjacencies, and experimental services — contributed six-point-eight billion yuan for the year.

The thing to internalise: NetEase is not a commodity gaming company. It is the most disciplined Chinese gaming operator by long-run franchise extension, with twenty-three consecutive years of online-games revenue growth, and now a globally relevant portfolio with Where Winds Meet at eighty million players, AI integration as a real commercial lever, and twenty-three billion US dollars of net cash. That combination in Chinese technology is structurally rare.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2021 gaming license freeze plus the 2023 Blizzard fallout are the clearest evidence.

William Ding — Ding Lei — founded NetEase in 1997 and remains Chief Executive Officer and controlling shareholder. The company's strategic consistency over twenty-eight years is built around him, and his continued execution is the single biggest quality signal.

The past-stress case that matters is twofold. First, the 2021 Chinese gaming license freeze. Chinese regulators paused issuing new online-game licenses for roughly nine months in 2021 and early 2022, affecting every Chinese gaming company. NetEase did not abandon pipeline investment — they continued building and when licenses resumed in 2022, NetEase had a disciplined release cadence that compounded share. Second, the 2023 Blizzard fallout. NetEase and Blizzard terminated their fourteen-year operating partnership in China in early 2023, with no new Warcraft or Overwatch content live in China for nearly two years. NetEase did not panic, kept its engineering teams intact, continued to invest in proprietary games, and patiently negotiated a new agreement with Blizzard that restored Chinese operations in 2024. By 2025 Blizzard titles delivered record annual revenue. That is founder-led patience through two major stress events in three years.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are striking. Non-GAAP net income up eleven percent to five-point-three billion US dollars. Gross margin expanded three-and-a-half percentage points from sixty-one to sixty-four percent. A fourth-quarter dividend of twenty-three cents per share was declared. Share repurchase has totalled two billion US dollars across twenty-two million American Depositary Shares since the current programme began, and the programme has been extended to January 2029 with total authorisation expanded to five billion US dollars over thirty-six months. Net cash position grew from one hundred and thirty-two to one hundred and sixty-four billion yuan during the year.

The watch item is William Ding's succession. He remains deeply involved and engaged, but at twenty-eight years of founder leadership, explicit succession planning becomes a multi-year question.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is franchise longevity. Fantasy Westward Journey — both online and mobile versions — is delivering record annual revenues in 2025, despite being originally released in 2001 and 2015 respectively. That kind of twenty-year-old franchise still compounding is unusual and speaks to NetEase's live-operations and content-refresh capability. Even as games age, they retain paying players if the operator is disciplined about content updates, balance, and community. NetEase is the best Chinese operator at this discipline.

The second engine is global expansion. Where Winds Meet at eighty million players, Marvel Rivals top-ranked internationally, Sword of Justice with strong global engagement — these are real international successes, not token launches. Global gaming revenue is structurally higher-margin than Chinese domestic because payment economics are better and there is no regulatory monetisation cap. As the international mix rises, group gross margin expands, as the three-and-a-half-percentage-point expansion in 2025 demonstrates.

The third engine is AI integration. NetEase has deployed proprietary AI tools — CodeMaker, DreamMaker, Danqing — across design, programming, art, and quality assurance. This is not a hype story; it is commercial. AI tools improve production efficiency, shorten design-to-launch cycles, and enable dynamic gameplay features that genuinely differentiate NetEase's titles from competitors. Crucially, AI integration raises the threshold for successful AAA game development, which advantages scaled operators over new entrants.

The kicker is the balance-sheet optionality. Twenty-three billion US dollars of net cash on a company earning five billion of non-GAAP net income is an unusual ratio in global gaming. It funds the five-billion-dollar buyback authorisation without touching operational cash flow. It creates optionality for global acquisitions — NetEase has looked at Western gaming assets in the past. And it is a structural defensive moat during any Chinese regulatory or macro shock.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, Games and VAS revenue growth — plus ten percent in 2025; sustained high-single-digit-to-low-teens growth confirms the franchise compounder. Second, gross margin — sixty-four percent today, up three-and-a-half percentage points; continued expansion confirms the international-mix story. Third, new title launch performance — Where Winds Meet at eighty million players; watch the next two-to-three titles for similar global traction. Fourth, AI integration contribution — qualitative signals on production efficiency and title differentiation; the evidence point is NetEase's revenue growth outpacing Chinese gaming industry growth. Fifth, share repurchase execution — two billion of five billion done; continued pace through 2026 signals capital-return commitment. Sixth, net cash trajectory — one hundred and sixty-four billion yuan; any material decline without M&A or exceptional distribution signals operating cash flow weakness.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 non-GAAP net income was thirty-seven billion yuan, about five-point-three billion US dollars. Full-year net income was thirty-four billion yuan. Operating cash flow was fifty-one billion yuan. Net cash is one hundred and sixty-four billion yuan or twenty-three billion US dollars. Market cap is around seventy-five to eighty billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, NetEase trades at roughly fifteen to eighteen times trailing non-GAAP earnings on the headline, but closer to ten to twelve times on an ex-cash basis. For a twenty-three-year compounding franchise with sixty-four percent gross margins, proven global traction, and a five-billion-dollar buyback programme, that is a compressed multiple. The Chinese-internet structural discount is doing most of the work.

The simple framing: one of the highest-quality gaming franchises globally, trading at roughly half the forward multiple of Activision at the time of its acquisition, a third of Nintendo's current multiple, and well below Electronic Arts and Take-Two on an ex-cash basis. If NetEase were headquartered in Tokyo or Los Angeles, the multiple would be materially higher.

The key sensitivity is Chinese gaming regulation. Another license freeze, monetisation caps, or minor-protection rules that get tighter would compress the domestic-revenue trajectory. Second sensitivity is international title cadence — if Where Winds Meet proves to be a one-off rather than the start of a sustained hit cadence, the global-expansion leg of the thesis weakens.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus prices NetEase as a Chinese gaming cyclical with regulatory overhang. The variant is that NetEase has twenty-three consecutive years of revenue growth, a proven global hit with Where Winds Meet, Blizzard restored and contributing record revenue, and AI integration that is commercial rather than hype. The right comp set is Nintendo, Activision pre-acquisition, Electronic Arts, and Take-Two — all of which trade at materially higher multiples for worse operating histories. The Chinese-listing structural discount is doing more than the fundamentals warrant.

Second: the AI integration and global expansion legs are priced at zero. If Where Winds Meet's eighty-million-player-base is a genuine sustained hit, and Marvel Rivals and Sword of Justice continue to scale, NetEase becomes the first Chinese gaming company to credibly challenge global incumbents on their home turf. That changes the multiple dramatically. Most sell-side models do not incorporate this yet.

The honest counter: Chinese gaming regulatory risk is structural, not cyclical. License issuance, content approval, minor-protection, and cross-border data rules can change quickly. The Blizzard 2023 fallout is a reminder that partnership economics can shift abruptly. And William Ding's founder-led control, while a quality signal, creates single-person risk that is under-discussed. Position sizing should reflect that the thesis has a structural ceiling even with continued execution.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Chinese gaming regulation. Another license freeze, a stricter monetisation rule, or minor-protection tightening would compress domestic revenue and reset expectations. Two: global title cadence failure. If Where Winds Meet proves isolated rather than the start of a sustained global hit portfolio, the international-expansion leg breaks. Three: AI disruption. AI tools lower development barriers — this advantages NetEase at scale but also enables faster competitive entry by new operators. Four: Blizzard partnership instability. The 2024 reconciliation is only two years old; any renewed dispute would compress revenue and confidence. Five: William Ding succession. Founder control is quality but single-person risk is real. Six: US-China delisting risk. NetEase is US-listed and exposed to audit and regulatory tension.

Specific sell triggers. Games and VAS revenue growth declining below five percent for two consecutive quarters without a clear one-off — franchise compounder breaking. Gross margin declining for two consecutive quarters — mix story reversing. Where Winds Meet monthly active users declining for two consecutive quarters — global hit thesis weakening. Blizzard titles revenue materially contracting in China after the 2024 restoration. Net cash position materially declining without M&A or distribution explanation. William Ding stepping back without a named credible successor.
