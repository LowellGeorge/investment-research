# Prosus (PRX NA) — Seven Questions Flashcards

## The Seven Questions Frame

These flashcards are organised around the seven questions you ask of any investment. The question is the cognitive move you're training; the facts hang underneath. When reviewing, hold the parent question in mind — that's the superstructure that compounds across companies.

1. **What is it?** — what specifically is unique about this business that makes it irreplaceable?
2. **Can we trust them?** — how did management behave under specific past stress, not slogans
3. **Why does value compound?** — the durable engines of return, mechanism by mechanism
4. **What proves us right?** — specific KPIs you'd track to know
5. **What's it worth?** — building blocks and sensitivity, no false precision
6. **Where is the market wrong?** — variant perception plus the honest counter
7. **What could kill it / when do we sell?** — ranked risks plus specific exit triggers

**Anchor question for Prosus:** Q6 — recognising that the "Tencent proxy" framing is two years out of date *is* the trade.

> Frame: "What specifically is it about *this* business that makes it compound? Strip the marketing and name the mechanism."

- Engine 2 — e-commerce inflecting to multi-billion EBITDA <> {{two years ago cost centre; H1 FY26 ran at US$530m aEBITDA with FY26 guide US$1.1bn+; Bloisi ambition "couple of billion dollars of profit" → operating business alone worth US$30-50bn at 15-25x}}
- Engine 3 — Large Commerce Models (LCM) <> {{proprietary AI trained on 500m daily customer interactions; 10x cheaper than commercial LLMs; at iFood: +15% homepage conversion, -51% ad cost, +75% notification CTR; rolling across Just Eat, OLX, eMAG}}

## Q7 — What could kill it / when do we sell?

> Frame: "What specifically kills the thesis on *this* business, and what observable event tells me the kill is happening?"

- Risk #1 — Tencent value collapse <> {{~70%+ of group NAV is the Tencent stake; anything that tanks Tencent tanks Prosus regardless of iFood performance}}
- Risk #2 — JET turnaround failure <> {{big-ticket bet on European food delivery; if Just Eat continues losing share to Uber Eats/Wolt/Deliveroo, writedown could be material}}
- Risk #3 — Indian competitive intensity <> {{Swiggy/Zomato war, PayU vs PhonePe/Razorpay, Meesho vs Flipkart/Amazon; any material failure impacts NAV}}

- Risk #5 — AI capex blowout <> {{LCM and Toqan investments real and rising; if productivity/conversion gains don't sustain, spend becomes drag}}
- Risk #6 — EM currencies <> {{reported earnings hit by BRL, INR, TRY, ZAR; operating economics often unaffected, reported numbers ugly}}
- Sell triggers (six specific):
    - {{Two consecutive halves of e-commerce aEBITDA missing the trajectory implied by management guidance}}
    - {{JET running at meaningfully negative aEBITDA 18 months after Prosus took control}}
    - {{Buyback slowed below US$4bn/year without explicit alternative use of capital}}
    - {{Bloisi exits — one-person-dependent thesis in many ways}}
    - {{Discount to NAV widening back above 25% sustained for 12+ months (implies market rejected operating thesis)}}
