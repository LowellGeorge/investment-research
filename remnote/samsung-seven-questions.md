# Samsung Electronics (005935 KS pref) — Seven Questions Flashcards

## The Seven Questions Frame

These flashcards are organised around the seven questions you ask of any investment. The question is the cognitive move you're training; the facts hang underneath. When reviewing, hold the parent question in mind — that's the superstructure that compounds across companies.

1. **What is it?** — what specifically is unique about this business that makes it irreplaceable?
2. **Can we trust them?** — how did management behave under specific past stress, not slogans
3. **Why does value compound?** — the durable engines of return, mechanism by mechanism
4. **What proves us right?** — specific KPIs you'd track to know
5. **What's it worth?** — building blocks and sensitivity, no false precision
6. **Where is the market wrong?** — variant perception plus the honest counter
7. **What could kill it / when do we sell?** — ranked risks plus specific exit triggers

**Anchor question for Samsung:** Q6 — recognising that foundry-worth-zero and HBM-permanently-behind are both consensus lazinesses *is* the trade.

## Q3 — Why does value compound?

> Frame: "What specifically is it about *this* business that makes it compound? Strip the marketing and name the mechanism."

- Engine 1 — memory leverage on AI demand <> {{world's largest DRAM and NAND producer by volume; mix shift toward HBM, server DDR5, enterprise SSDs is the same wave Hynix is riding; Q4 proved Samsung participates in the up-cycle even before HBM4 contributes; leverage steepens once HBM4 ships in volume}}
- Engine 2 — Foundry as multi-year option <> {{2nm in production; Tesla signed multi-billion-dollar deal late 2024; Qualcomm allegedly back at table for derivative nodes; if Samsung holds single-digit share at 2nm with healthy yields, foundry moves from cash drain to material profit by 2027-28; market prices at zero}}
- Engine 3 — balance sheet as a strategic weapon <> {{KRW 100tn net cash means Samsung can outspend any competitor except TSMC; sustaining a higher capex run rate than SK Hynix in absolute terms}}
- The kicker nobody is pricing <> {{Samsung's HBM4 11.7Gbps SKU — explicitly called "industry-leading" in Q4 deck; becomes a Nvidia Vera Rubin design-in or it doesn't; if it does, the entire thesis re-rates inside one quarter}}

## Q4 — What proves us right? (KPIs to track)

> Frame: "If the thesis on *this* business is right, what specific number would I see move? Vague metrics fail this test."

- KPI 1 — HBM4 Nvidia qualification status <> {{single most important external data point; Samsung needs at least 20-30% of next-gen Nvidia HBM allocation; watch Q2/Q3 2026 announcements}}
- KPI 2 — Memory segment operating margin <> {{Q4 hit ~44% (adjusting for foundry/LSI weakness implies memory OPM well into 50%); if 2026 holds this pace, cash-generation engine is real}}
- KPI 3 — Foundry revenue growth and loss narrowing <> {{management targets "double-digit revenue growth" in 2026; if 2nm ramps with reasonable yields, loss should narrow QoQ}}
- KPI 4 — Capex discipline <> {{KRW 47.5tn 2025 was disciplined; if 2026 capex blows out without HBM4 / foundry clarity, balance sheet thesis weakens}}
- KPI 5 — Buyback continuation <> {{KRW 8.2tn in 2025 was a step change; watch 2026 cadence}}
- KPI 6 — Lee family control transactions <> {{any inheritance-related share movements changes the governance calculus quickly}}

## Q6 — Where is the market wrong?

> Frame: "What's the consensus framing about *this* business that's lazy or out-of-date? What's my variant — and what's the honest counter to my own variant?"

- Variant perception #1 (summary) <> {{Samsung's foundry is being treated as worthless when it's deep optionality — TSMC at >60% share and supply-constrained through 2027, so somebody gets overflow demand; Samsung at 8-10% of leading-edge foundry by 2028 = KRW -3tn → KRW +5tn = KRW 8tn swing nobody has in their numbers}}
- Variant perception #2 (summary) <> {{the "Samsung permanently behind on HBM" view is based on one node — HBM is an iterated R&D race; Samsung has customer relationships (sells to Nvidia/Apple/Qualcomm), in-house DRAM process (no SK-Hynix-style packaging dependency on TSMC), balance sheet to throw resources at the gap; question is execution, not capability}}
- Honest counter to the variant view <> {{SK Hynix has multi-year head start on HBM customer co-engineering; if Samsung's HBM4 hits Nvidia certification but Hynix's HBM4 is also qualified at meaningful volume, Samsung gets smaller share of a bigger pie — better than nothing but less than bull case (most likely outcome)}}

## Q7 — What could kill it / when do we sell?

> Frame: "What specifically kills the thesis on *this* business, and what observable event tells me the kill is happening?"

- Risk #1 — HBM4 qualification failure at Nvidia <> {{biggest binary risk; if HBM4 doesn't get into Vera Rubin at meaningful share, memory thesis becomes "we participate but Hynix gets the cream"}}
- Risk #2 — Foundry continued losses <> {{if 2nm yields disappoint and Tesla drifts back to TSMC, foundry continues to bleed}}
- Risk #3 — Mobile share loss to Chinese OEMs <> {{Galaxy S26 is the next test; if Samsung keeps losing premium share to Apple at top and Xiaomi/Honor/Oppo at mid-tier, DX margin compresses}}
- Risk #4 — Korea / US trade dynamics <> {{tariff or export-control shift affecting Taylor, Texas foundry build or memory shipments is a real risk}}
- Risk #5 — Lee family inheritance and governance <> {{forced share sale or restructuring of cross-shareholdings could create technical pressure independent of fundamentals}}
- Risk #6 — China macro <> {{significant DRAM and NAND capacity in Xi'an and Suzhou; US export control extension could strand assets}}
- Sell triggers (five specific):
    - {{Two consecutive quarters of memory operating margin below 25% with ASPs still rising (implies share loss)}}
    - {{HBM4 Nvidia qualification announcement that explicitly excludes Samsung from Vera Rubin}}
    - {{Foundry losses widening despite revenue growth (implies yield problems on 2nm)}}
    - {{Buyback paused or materially reduced without alternative use of capital}}
    - {{Mobile market share at high end (Galaxy S26 launch) declining materially YoY}}
