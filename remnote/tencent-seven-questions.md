# Tencent (700 HK) — Seven Questions Flashcards

## The Seven Questions Frame

These flashcards are organised around the seven questions you ask of any investment. The question is the cognitive move you're training; the facts hang underneath. When reviewing, hold the parent question in mind — that's the superstructure that compounds across companies.

1. **What is it?** — what specifically is unique about this business that makes it irreplaceable?
2. **Can we trust them?** — how did management behave under specific past stress, not slogans
3. **Why does value compound?** — the durable engines of return, mechanism by mechanism
4. **What proves us right?** — specific KPIs you'd track to know
5. **What's it worth?** — building blocks and sensitivity, no false precision
6. **Where is the market wrong?** — variant perception plus the honest counter
7. **What could kill it / when do we sell?** — ranked risks plus specific exit triggers

**Anchor question for Tencent:** Q6 — recognising that AI is the tailwind, not the threat, and that the China discount is stale *is* the trade.

## Q3 — Why does value compound?

> Frame: "What specifically is it about *this* business that makes it compound? Strip the marketing and name the mechanism."

- Engine 1 — AI making the ad business structurally better <> {{Marketing services grew 19% in 2025 on essentially flat ad load; pure quality improvement from AI targeting + closed-loop attribution (Mini Programs/Mini Shops) + more Video Accounts inventory (+20% time spent); ad GM went 55% → 58%}}
- Why flat ad load matters <> {{Tencent has been the laggard on ad load for years; AI is the unlock that lets them monetise without degrading user experience}}
- Engine 2 — International Games as real engine <> {{USD 10bn+ in 2025 is bigger than Take-Two, bigger than EA standalone game business; Supercell, Riot/VALORANT, PUBG MOBILE, Wuthering Waves = multi-year evergreen franchises}}
- Tencent's content-production playbook <> {{acquire majority stake → leave studio independent → cross-promote into China + global}}
- Engine 3 — Tencent Cloud profitable at scale <> {{Q4 +22% revenue, 51% segment GM; HY 3.0 LLM and Yuanbao/WorkBuddy/QClaw contributing real external cloud workload demand}}
- The kicker nobody is pricing <> {{WeChat as the agentic AI distribution layer — when agentic AI hits China consumer scale, the interface is a Mini Program inside Weixin; Tencent owns that pipe}}

## Q4 — What proves us right? (KPIs to track)

> Frame: "If the thesis on *this* business is right, what specific number would I see move? Vague metrics fail this test."

- KPI 1 — Marketing services growth with flat ad load <> {{if revenue keeps growing 17%+ on flat load, the ceiling is much higher; if ad load creeps up, lower ceiling}}
- KPI 2 — International games trajectory <> {{can it grow 25%+ for another two years from USD 10bn base}}
- KPI 3 — Cloud revenue growth and segment GM <> {{re-accelerated to 22% Q4; if Q1/Q2 2026 keep that pace, cloud becomes a real third compounder}}
- KPI 4 — Buyback cadence <> {{HKD 80bn/year for 2024-25; if 2026 buyback drops materially, signal weakens}}
- KPI 5 — Mini Shops GMV disclosure <> {{Tencent has been coy about absolute numbers; when they start disclosing, market re-rates e-commerce optionality}}

## Q6 — Where is the market wrong?

> Frame: "What's the consensus framing about *this* business that's lazy or out-of-date? What's my variant — and what's the honest counter to my own variant?"

- Variant perception #1 (summary) <> {{AI is Tencent's single biggest tailwind, not threat — DeepSeek/Qwen/HY 3.0 competitive at inference scale, costs dramatically lower than US frontier models, 1.4bn DAU application surface is unique; marketing services already proving the thesis}}
- Variant perception #2 (summary) <> {{regulatory cycle is structurally over — Beijing now pulling on Chinese tech to be engine of consumer recovery and AI competitiveness; game licences flow, Mini Programs supported, fintech stable; the regulatory risk premium isn't warranted}}
- Honest counter to the variant view <> {{if US-China escalates (export controls extending to consumer software, ADR delisting, sanctions on specific entities), Tencent gets caught up regardless of its own behaviour — position size accordingly}}

## Q7 — What could kill it / when do we sell?

> Frame: "What specifically kills the thesis on *this* business, and what observable event tells me the kill is happening?"

- Risk #1 — Geopolitical escalation <> {{US-China tech cold war moving to consumer software (TikTok-style action against WeChat globally, restrictions on US investors holding HK-listed Chinese tech)}}
- Risk #2 — Domestic gaming policy <> {{return to 2021-style tightening on game time, monetisation, or licensing}}
- Risk #3 — AI capex outpacing returns <> {{if cloud + AI revenue doesn't keep pace with AI infrastructure depreciation, margin expansion stalls}}
- Risk #4 — Pony Ma succession <> {{not imminent but the discount widens the moment that question becomes live}}
- Risk #5 — Mini Shops failing to monetise <> {{the big variant-perception bet on Weixin-as-commerce relies on real GMV; if it stalls, one leg of the bull case is gone}}
- Risk #6 — Investment portfolio mark-downs <> {{~US$80-100bn of stakes feeds equity and book value; China internet sell-off hits reported profit via share-of-associates}}
- Sell triggers (five specific):
    - {{Marketing services growth decelerates below overall company growth for two consecutive quarters}}
    - {{Buyback paused without explicit replacement use of capital}}
    - {{Major US/EU regulatory action against WeChat in Western markets}}
    - {{Domestic regulatory rhetoric returns to 2021 tone (keywords: platform, anti-monopoly, youth gaming addiction)}}
    - {{Pony Ma announces transition or material stake sale}}
