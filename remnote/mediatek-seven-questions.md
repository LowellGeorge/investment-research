# MediaTek (2454 TT) — Seven Questions Flashcards

## The Seven Questions Frame

These flashcards are organised around the seven questions you ask of any investment. The question is the cognitive move you're training; the facts hang underneath. When reviewing, hold the parent question in mind — that's the superstructure that compounds across companies.

1. **What is it?** — what specifically is unique about this business that makes it irreplaceable?
2. **Can we trust them?** — how did management behave under specific past stress, not slogans
3. **Why does value compound?** — the durable engines of return, mechanism by mechanism
4. **What proves us right?** — specific KPIs you'd track to know
5. **What's it worth?** — building blocks and sensitivity, no false precision
6. **Where is the market wrong?** — variant perception plus the honest counter
7. **What could kill it / when do we sell?** — ranked risks plus specific exit triggers

**Anchor question for MediaTek:** Q6 — recognising that this is becoming a fabless AI ASIC house, not a mobile chipmaker, *is* the trade.

## Q6 — Where is the market wrong?

> Frame: "What's the consensus framing about *this* business that's lazy or out-of-date? What's my variant — and what's the honest counter to my own variant?"

- Variant perception #1 (summary) <> {{MediaTek is valued as a mobile chipmaker with AI optionality when it's becoming the opposite — ASIC goes from <0.5% to ~5-7% in 2026 alone, ~15-20% by 2028; at that point you stop comparing to Qualcomm and start comparing to small Broadcom or large Marvell (high-30s to mid-40s P/E)}}

- Honest counter to the variant view <> {{if Qualcomm's next Snapdragon dramatically improves on power efficiency + modem integration (historical strengths), MediaTek loses flagship momentum; if data center ASIC consolidates around Broadcom alone, 10-15% share target at risk}}

## Q7 — What could kill it / when do we sell?

> Frame: "What specifically kills the thesis on *this* business, and what observable event tells me the kill is happening?"

- Risk #1 — Hyperscaler ASIC concentration <> {{if data center is one customer (likely Google) and that customer dual-sources to Broadcom or builds in-house, the ramp stalls — biggest binary risk}}
- Risk #2 — Smartphone share reversal <> {{Qualcomm is bigger and well-capitalised; next 2nm Snapdragon beating Dimensity on benchmarks AND price would erode flagship momentum}}
- Risk #3 — Margin compression continues <> {{2025 saw 210bps GM compression; if wafer cost pressure persists and pricing doesn't offset, operating margin drifts down despite revenue growth}}
- Risk #4 — Apple Modem/SoC vertical integration <> {{not near-term but real over 5 years}}
- Risk #5 — China-specific risk <> {{biggest customers are Chinese smartphone OEMs; US export control extension targeting shipments to specific named Chinese customers would be material}}
- Risk #6 — Foundry capacity allocation <> {{MediaTek depends entirely on TSMC for leading-edge; competes with Apple/Nvidia/AMD/Qualcomm for slots as TSMC tightens}}
- Sell triggers (six specific):
    - {{Data center ASIC revenue materially misses the >US$1bn 2026 guide (e.g. <US$700m for full year)}}
    - {{Single largest ASIC customer concentration stays >70% by end-2026 (implies no second programme)}}
    - {{Two consecutive quarters of operating margin below 13%}}
    - {{Loss of a major Chinese flagship smartphone slot (Oppo, Vivo, Xiaomi) to Qualcomm in the 2027 cycle}}
    - {{Rick Tsai retires without an obvious technical successor}}
    - {{US export controls extended to MediaTek's HPC or premium mobile silicon shipments to Chinese customers}}
