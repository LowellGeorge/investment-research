# H World Group — The Seven Questions

H World Group, previously known as Huazhu, is the nineteenth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at two-point-two percent, with a market cap of around ten to twelve billion US dollars. Here is H World in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

H World just closed 2025 with full-year adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation of eight-and-a-half billion yuan, up twenty-four percent year-on-year, and adjusted net income of four-point-nine billion yuan, up thirty-three percent. Group hotel gross merchandise value reached one hundred and eight billion yuan, up sixteen percent. Rooms in operation grew sixteen percent to one-point-two-six million. The manachised and franchise business — the asset-light leg — grew revenue twenty-three percent and now contributes sixty-nine percent of group profit. The member base reached two hundred and forty-five million, with member bookings up twenty-one-and-a-half percent. And the long-troubled European Legacy-DH business — Steigenberger and the related German hotel brands — posted its first record adjusted profit after four years of restructuring pain.

Total shareholder return for 2025 was seven hundred and sixty million US dollars. The three-year two-billion-dollar return programme is now more than seventy-five percent complete. This is the China hotel compounder that finally got the asset-light model right and is now returning capital to match.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

H World is the dominant multi-brand hotel operator of mainland China, with a growing European footprint under the Legacy-DH portfolio.

The scale is genuinely national. One-point-two-six million rooms in operation at year-end. Hotel gross merchandise value of one hundred and eight billion yuan for the year. A member base of two hundred and forty-five million — about eighteen percent of China's population. The brand portfolio covers the full price spectrum: economy brands like HanTing, midscale brands like JI Hotel and Orange, upper-midscale brands like Crystal Orange and Manxin, and the premium and luxury brands under Legacy-DH.

The revenue structure has two segments. The asset-light manachised-and-franchise segment — where H World provides brand, management and distribution services to third-party hotel owners — generated eleven-point-seven billion yuan, up twenty-three percent, and crucially contributed sixty-nine percent of group profit, up five percentage points year-on-year. The operated-and-leased hotels — where H World directly operates its own inventory — is the capital-consumptive leg where the DH acquisition resides.

Full-year 2025 operating margin reached twenty-six-point-nine percent, up from twenty-one-point-eight percent in 2024 — a four-hundred-and-ninety-basis-point expansion in one year. Operating cash flow was eight-point-four billion yuan. Cash and equivalents ended the year at fifteen-point-four billion. The shareholder return programme distributed six hundred and fifty million US dollars of dividends and one hundred and ten million of share repurchases during the year.

The thing to internalise: H World is not a commodity hotel operator. It is a multi-brand, asset-light franchisor that has reached the flywheel point — where each incremental franchised hotel adds margin, not cost — and is now combining that operating leverage with material capital returns to shareholders.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the Legacy-DH turnaround is the clearest evidence.

Qi Ji founded the company in 2005 under the Hanting brand and is the controlling shareholder. Jin Hui has been Chief Executive Officer since 2020. Arthur Yu became Chief Financial Officer in March 2026, succeeding Hui Chen in a planned transition.

The past-stress case that matters is the 2019 Deutsche Hospitality acquisition and its four-year restructuring cycle. H World acquired DH — Steigenberger Hotels, InterCityHotel, Jaz in the City — in January 2020, weeks before the pandemic. DH consumed capital and posted losses for four consecutive years as H World absorbed integration costs, then lease renegotiations, then the asset-light transformation. Most Chinese consumer companies in the same situation would have written down or sold. H World held, kept investing in restructuring, and finally in 2025 delivered record adjusted profit at DH of five hundred million yuan. That is patience with a plan, not stubbornness.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are specific. Total shareholder return of seven hundred and sixty million US dollars — six hundred and fifty million of dividends and one hundred and ten million of share repurchases. An additional four hundred million US dollar cash dividend declared for the second half of 2025. The three-year two-billion-dollar return programme is over seventy-five percent complete. Operating margin expanded four hundred and ninety basis points in one year. Net cash balance remains positive at nine-point-six billion yuan. The 2026 guidance is transparent: group revenue up two to six percent, five to nine percent excluding DH; manachised and franchise up twelve to sixteen percent; two thousand two hundred to two thousand three hundred new hotel openings against six hundred to seven hundred closures. Clear, specific, accountable.

The watch item is the CFO transition. Hui Chen was a long-tenured and credible financial operator; Arthur Yu takes over at a critical period with the shareholder return programme and the final phase of DH integration. Track the first two quarters under Yu carefully.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is the manachised-and-franchise flywheel. M&F revenue grew twenty-three percent in 2025, M&F adjusted profit grew twenty-four percent, M&F now contributes sixty-nine percent of group profit. The model has genuine operating leverage: every new franchised hotel adds brand fees, loyalty-programme fees, and distribution fees on a near-fixed central cost base. The 2026 guidance of twelve to sixteen percent M&F revenue growth and twelve percent network growth — two thousand two hundred to two thousand three hundred openings against six hundred to seven hundred closures — keeps the flywheel turning with quality discipline.

The second engine is the Chinese upper-midscale and lifestyle upgrade. Upper-midscale hotels in operation and pipeline exceeded one thousand six hundred and thirty-nine at year-end, up seventeen-point-six percent. Chinese consumers are trading up within travel even when discretionary spending is otherwise cautious, and H World's brand portfolio — particularly Crystal Orange, Manxin, and the premium brands — captures the upgrade. Upper-midscale revenue per room is materially higher than economy, so category mix shifts earnings structurally.

The third engine is the member-direct distribution moat. Two hundred and forty-five million members, room nights sold to members up twenty-one-and-a-half percent. Direct bookings through the member programme and the H Rewards app reduce online-travel-agency commission costs and raise per-stay economics. The member base is a moat that multinational hotel brands — Marriott, Hilton, IHG — cannot easily replicate in China at scale.

The kicker is the lower-tier city and inbound-travel tailwind. Lower-tier Chinese cities are becoming the new growth engines for domestic tourism, and H World's brand portfolio is scaling into those markets on an asset-light basis. Inbound-to-China travel growth, driven by visa liberalisation and the broader tourism recovery, is a separate near-term tailwind for domestic inventory.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, manachised-and-franchise revenue growth — twenty-three percent in 2025, guidance twelve to sixteen percent in 2026; sustained double-digit growth keeps the flywheel intact. Second, M&F share of group profit — sixty-nine percent today, up five percentage points; continued mix shift re-rates the business. Third, full-year operating margin — twenty-six-point-nine percent today, up from twenty-one-point-eight; sustained expansion confirms operating-leverage path. Fourth, network growth — twelve percent guidance for 2026 on two thousand two hundred to two thousand three hundred openings; quality over quantity is the test. Fifth, revenue per available room — guidance flat to slight up in 2026; any meaningful RevPAR improvement is an incremental earnings lever. Sixth, shareholder return execution — seventy-five percent of two-billion-dollar programme complete; continuation through 2026 signals ongoing capital-return commitment.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 adjusted net income was four-point-nine billion yuan, roughly seven hundred million US dollars. Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation was eight-and-a-half billion yuan, roughly one-point-two billion US dollars. Free cash flow generation supported seven hundred and sixty million US dollars of total shareholder return in the year. Net cash was nine-point-six billion yuan, roughly one-point-three billion US dollars. Market cap is around ten to twelve billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, H World trades at roughly fifteen to eighteen times trailing adjusted net income. That sits below global hotel-franchisor peers — Marriott at roughly twenty-five to thirty times forward, Hilton similar, IHG in the mid-twenties. The discount reflects Chinese-ADR structural overhang rather than operating quality.

The simple framing: an asset-light hotel franchisor running at twenty-seven percent operating margin with twenty-three percent M&F revenue growth and the largest loyalty-member base in global hotels, trading at a meaningful discount to global comparable franchisors, with a fully-funded capital return programme underway. Comparable set: Marriott, Hilton, Choice, IHG.

The key sensitivity is Chinese domestic RevPAR. The 2026 guidance is flat to slightly up; any deterioration hits directly. Second sensitivity is any renewed pressure at Legacy-DH; after four years of turnaround the business is finally profitable, but European hotel demand and lease economics remain a persistent watch item.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus prices H World as a Chinese hotel cyclical, trading in lockstep with China consumer sentiment. The variant is that H World has completed the transformation to an asset-light franchisor and now has the margin, capital-return, and network-growth profile of a global hotel franchisor — which trades at a twenty-five-to-thirty-times forward multiple. The M&F business contributing sixty-nine percent of profit is the structural change; the old framing is three years out of date.

Second: the Legacy-DH turnaround is priced as zero, when it has genuinely inflected to profit in 2025 after four years of pain. Management held through the cycle rather than write down, lease renegotiations are complete, and the asset-light transformation is applied to the European portfolio as it is to China. DH posting five hundred million yuan of adjusted profit is a change that compounds in the base for years.

The honest counter: Chinese consumer recovery remains uneven and RevPAR recovery has been slower than pre-pandemic baseline. A sharper Chinese consumer downturn compresses margins and network growth simultaneously. And the Chinese-ADR structural discount is unlikely to close quickly even with continued operational execution. Position sizing should reflect that the thesis plays out over multiple years and that multiple re-rating is not the base case.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Chinese domestic RevPAR weakness. Flat RevPAR in 2026 is guidance; any persistent decline hits earnings directly. Two: Legacy-DH reversion. After four years of turnaround, DH is profitable — but European hotel demand, lease economics, and integration complexity could still reverse. Three: network quality deterioration. Opening two thousand two hundred hotels a year requires discipline; franchise-quality issues would hit the brand and the member economy. Four: competitive response. Jin Jiang and BTG Homeinns domestically, plus Marriott, Hilton, IHG internationally, are all investing in China expansion. Five: CFO transition and key-person risk. Arthur Yu is new; any integration or communication issue is a watch item. Six: Chinese-ADR policy risk. Delisting risk or data-sharing regulation would hit the stock independent of operations.

Specific sell triggers. Manachised-and-franchise revenue growth decelerating below ten percent for two consecutive quarters — asset-light flywheel stalling. Operating margin declining for two consecutive quarters without a clear one-off. Legacy-DH posting losses again for two consecutive half-year periods — turnaround reversing. Member bookings growth decelerating materially. Shareholder return programme delayed or reduced. Jin Hui stepping back without a named successor. A material adverse Chinese-ADR policy change.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. H World is the asset-light Chinese hotel franchisor that has finally got the model right, with Legacy-DH profitable after four years of pain and a two-billion-dollar shareholder return programme three-quarters complete. The next print, first-quarter 2026 on the nineteenth of May, tests whether the M&F growth cadence holds and whether RevPAR is tracking the flat-to-slightly-up guide. Watch M&F revenue growth, operating margin, and the DH adjusted profit line.
