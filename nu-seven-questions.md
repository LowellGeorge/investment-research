# Nu Holdings — Seven Questions

An investment summary of Nu Holdings, known as Nubank — the largest digital bank in Latin America, US-listed — prepared on the 2nd of July 2026, through a lens of quality and long-term ownership.

## Question One: Business Description — What Is It?

Nu, or Nubank, is the largest digital financial services platform in Latin America and one of the largest in the world, serving more than 135 million customers entirely without branches. In Brazil it has passed 115 million customers — the country's largest private financial institution — while Mexico has crossed 15 million, now the third-largest financial institution there, and Colombia is approaching 5 million. The business spans credit cards, a 24.3 billion US dollar portfolio; unsecured personal lending, about 10 billion; and secured lending, 3 billion — funded by 42.4 billion US dollars of low-cost retail deposits, alongside a growing float and fee franchise. Revenue in financial year 2025 was 15.8 billion US dollars, with net income of 2.9 billion — a business that five years ago was loss-making on 1.7 billion of revenue.

## Question Two: Management and Governance — Can We Trust Them?

Founder and chief executive David Velez has built one of the rare consumer-finance franchises that combined hyper-growth with improving unit economics: from losses in 2021 and 2022 to a 29 percent return on equity in the first quarter of 2026, with the efficiency ratio at 17.6 percent — among the best in global retail banking. Capital allocation is turning shareholder-friendly at the right moment: with growth self-funded, the board approved a 1.0 billion US dollar buyback in June 2026, explicitly framed as the output of a deliberate capital-allocation policy rather than a signal of fading opportunity.

Two governance items to keep under review. First, control: Class B shares carry 20 votes each and Velez remains the controlling shareholder — alignment is strong, but minority influence is limited. Second, the chief financial officer transition: Guilherme Lago, the architect of the profitability phase, hands over to Rob Livingston — former CFO of Visa North America, with 18 years at Capital One — in July 2026, with Lago staying on as special advisor. The hire adds deep US consumer-credit experience just as Nu tests the US market — sensible succession, but execution through the handover is a watch item.

## Question Three: Investment Thesis — Why Does Value Compound?

A huge, badly served profit pool: Brazilian banking generates over 100 billion US dollars of annual gross profit, historically captured by a high-cost incumbent oligopoly. Nu holds roughly 7 percent of that pool, with more than 115 million customers already on the platform — monetisation, not acquisition, is the remaining work.

A structural low-cost producer: branchless, cloud-native, with a 17.6 percent efficiency ratio and deposits costing 88 percent of interbank rates. The cost advantage lets Nu profitably serve customers incumbents cannot, and it widens as the platform scales.

The engagement flywheel is still turning: 83 percent monthly activity and average revenue per active customer of about 16 US dollars, rising sequentially yet still a fraction of incumbent-bank revenue per customer — the maturation of existing cohorts alone drives years of growth.

The formula travels: Mexico reached break-even in the first quarter of 2026 with 15 million customers — the customer base up about 7 times in four years, average revenue per active customer nearly doubled, efficiency ratio improved 78 percentage points — replicating the Brazil playbook. Colombia is following; the US entry is deliberately capped at less than 100 basis points of efficiency-ratio drag per year across 2026 and 2027.

Proprietary data and AI underwriting: NuFormer foundation models now price and approve every personal loan in real time on predicted net present value; AI Private Banker features serve more than 15 million monthly actives. First-party data from 135 million customers is a compounding moat in credit.

And proven compounding: revenue up about 9 times and a swing from losses to 2.9 billion US dollars of net income in four years; net income compounding at more than 80 percent a year since 2022, with return on equity now 29 percent.

## Question Four: Key Milestones and KPIs — What Proves Us Right, and What to Track?

Asset quality versus seasonality — 15-to-90-day non-performing loans rose 89 basis points to 5.0 percent in the first quarter of 2026, a typical first-quarter pattern; 90-day-plus non-performing loans eased to 6.5 percent, below the 7.0 percent peak of the third quarter of 2024. Watch whether the seasonal pattern repeats or breaks.

Risk-adjusted net interest margin recovery — 9.5 percent in the first quarter of 2026 after elevated provisions of 1.79 billion US dollars, up 33 percent quarter on quarter; management expects a return towards second-half 2025 levels, about 10.5 percent, as the year progresses. This is the single best test of the "growth, not deterioration" explanation.

Funding-led growth — the loan-to-deposit ratio moved from 49.1 percent to 58.3 percent in a quarter as the 37.2 billion US dollar credit book grew 40 percent year on year; deposits, at 42.4 billion, up 22 percent year on year, must keep pace.

Mexico past break-even, Colombia next — sustained Mexican profitability and Colombian scaling would confirm the model is a repeatable machine, not a Brazil one-off.

Discipline markers — the full-year efficiency ratio holding near the roughly 20 percent communicated range despite US and AI investment; and buyback execution.

And a consensus check, from FactSet — the Street models revenue of about 22.1 billion US dollars in estimated financial year 2026, rising to about 30.6 billion by financial year 2028, with earnings per share of 84 cents, then 1 dollar 11, then 1 dollar 44; rating consensus is overweight, 18 of 22 positive. Note that financial-year-2026 earnings-per-share revisions skew down, 10 down versus 3 up, on provisioning — the near-term debate in one number.

On the financials, in US dollars: revenue rose from 1.7 billion in financial year 2021, to 4.8 billion in 2022, to 8.0 billion in 2023, to 11.5 billion in 2024, to 15.8 billion in 2025. Gross profit went from 0.7 billion, to 1.7, to 3.5, to 5.3, to 6.6 billion. Net income swung from a loss of 0.2 billion, to a loss of 0.4 billion, to positive 1.0 billion, to 2.0 billion, to 2.9 billion. And in the first quarter of 2026, revenue topped 5.0 billion, net income was 871 million, and return on equity was 29 percent.

## Question Five: Five-Year Fair Value and Return — the Team's View

At 13 dollars 39, a market capitalisation of about 65 billion US dollars, the shares trade at about 15.9 times estimated financial-year-2026 earnings, about 12.0 times 2027, and about 9.3 times 2028 consensus earnings per share — an undemanding price for a 29 percent return-on-equity franchise still compounding revenue at about 40 percent. Two framing cautions: Nu reports in US dollars but earns largely in Brazilian real, so currency can dominate short-run outcomes — judge progress currency-neutral — and this is ultimately an unsecured consumer lender, so earnings should be assessed across a credit cycle, not at the benign point of one.

Illustrative scenarios, as placeholders for the team's house numbers. The bull case: consensus roughly holds, earnings per share of 1 dollar 44 or more by 2028, growth persisting into 2029 and 2030, and the multiple re-rates towards 20 times or more as the buyback and Mexico earnings compound — an internal rate of return of about 20 percent or more. The base case: earnings per share reach about 1 dollar 30 to 1 dollar 40 by 2028 through one normal provisioning cycle, the multiple steady in the mid-teens — a low-to-mid-teens return with currency noise around it. The bear case: a genuine Brazilian credit downturn plus Brazilian real weakness stalls earnings per share near about 80 to 90 cents and compresses the multiple towards 10 times — roughly flat to negative over five years.

## Question Six: Variant Perception — Where the Market Is Wrong

The market currently reads Nu as an emerging-market consumer lender running hot: rising 15-to-90-day non-performing loans, provisions up 33 percent in a quarter, a falling risk-adjusted net interest margin, and downward financial-year-2026 earnings-per-share revisions. The variant view is that the provisioning surge is the arithmetic of deliberate, model-driven growth — seasonality plus a 40 percent larger book tilted towards unsecured products — on a portfolio of unusually short duration that management can re-price within months, with 90-day-plus non-performing loans, the lagging truth-teller, still improving.

Meanwhile the franchise is quietly diversifying beyond credit: float and fees now balance the gross-profit mix, deposits fund the whole machine cheaply, and Mexico has flipped to profitable. If the risk-adjusted net interest margin recovers as guided, the market is paying about 12 times next year's earnings for one of the world's best retail-banking compounders — and the company is buying back stock while it waits. The bear is right that Brazil's unsecured cycle is untested at this portfolio size; the disagreement is over whether the first quarter's numbers were the first crack or ordinary seasonality. The 90-day-plus trend says the latter.

## Question Seven: Risks, Premortem, and Sell Discipline

The premortem — if this goes wrong in five years, the likely causes are these. A credit cycle: the unsecured book, grown 40 percent year on year partly into deliberately higher-risk segments, proves mispriced when Brazil's consumer cycle turns, and provisions overwhelm net interest margin. Macro and currency: sustained Brazilian real depreciation and high Brazilian rates compress US-dollar earnings and consumer capacity. Competition and regulation: incumbents and Mercado Pago replicate the digital offer; central-bank action on interchange, lending caps, or Pix economics erodes monetisation. Expansion overreach: the US bet escalates beyond the stated guardrail of less than 100 basis points, or Mexican credit — still unseasoned — disappoints after break-even. And key-man and control risk: heavy dependence on Velez, whose super-voting control leaves minorities without recourse if judgment slips; a fumbled chief-financial-officer handover would compound it.

Sell discipline: trim or exit on, first, 90-day-plus non-performing loans breaking above the 7.0 percent prior peak outside seasonal patterns with risk-adjusted net interest margin failing to recover as guided; second, a structural reversal in the efficiency-ratio trend; third, US spending breaching the stated guardrail without evidence of product-market fit; or fourth, capital-allocation or governance actions that favour the controlling shareholder over minorities. Size the position acknowledging that, at its core, this remains an unsecured consumer lender in an emerging market.
