# Trip.com — The Seven Questions

Trip.com is the thirteenth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at two-point-eight percent, with a market cap of roughly fifty billion US dollars. Here is Trip.com in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Trip.com just closed 2025 with full-year net revenue of sixty-two-point-four billion yuan, up seventeen percent. International bookings grew approximately sixty percent year-on-year. The international business now contributes roughly forty percent of total revenue, up from less than fifteen percent pre-pandemic. Inbound-to-China travel served twenty million travellers, nearly doubling year-on-year. The company ended the year with a hundred and six billion yuan of cash — about fifteen billion US dollars — and fully utilised its 2025 share-repurchase authorisation. Full-year non-GAAP net income was nearly thirty-two billion yuan, up from eighteen billion in 2024.

Consensus still prices Trip.com as a Chinese domestic online travel agency. That is increasingly the wrong frame. The right frame is that Trip.com is the pan-Asia-Pacific travel platform that happens to be headquartered in Shanghai. The entire investment case turns on which frame the market adopts, and when.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Trip.com Group is the dominant online travel agency of Greater China and the fastest-growing international travel platform in Asia.

The domestic business — historically branded as Ctrip — is the clear number-one online travel platform in mainland China. Accommodation reservation revenue for 2025 was twenty-six billion yuan, up twenty-one percent, forty-two percent of total revenue. Transportation ticketing was twenty-two-and-a-half billion yuan, up eleven percent, thirty-six percent. Packaged tours were four-point-seven billion, up eight percent. Corporate travel was two-point-eight billion, up thirteen percent.

The international business operates under the Trip.com brand across Asia-Pacific, Europe and parts of the Americas, plus separately-operated brands like Skyscanner (flight meta-search) and Ctrip Kuailu. International bookings grew approximately sixty percent year-on-year in 2025, and international revenue now represents roughly forty percent of the total. Asia-Pacific is the primary growth region — Trip.com has been the fastest-growing online travel agency in Japan, South Korea, Thailand, Singapore and Hong Kong for the past three years.

Inbound travel is the distinctive third leg. When foreign travellers come to China, Trip.com is their booking platform for hotels, transport and experiences. Twenty million inbound travellers served in 2025, nearly doubling year-on-year. Over sixty-three thousand hotels serving inbound guests for the first time through the platform. This is a uniquely positioned asset — no other platform has the China-domestic supply base plus the international customer reach.

The thing to internalise: Trip.com is not just an online travel agency. It is becoming the platform that owns the travel flows between China and the rest of Asia-Pacific, in both directions, with a structural advantage that neither Booking, Expedia, nor Agoda can replicate.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the pandemic is the cleanest evidence you can get.

James Liang co-founded Ctrip in 1999 and is executive chairman. Jane Sun has been chief executive since 2016 and was deputy CEO before that. The longtime founding core, including Min Fan and Neil Shen, retained meaningful involvement until the 2025 board refresh.

The past-stress case that matters is 2020 to 2023. COVID devastated travel globally. Trip.com's revenue fell more than fifty percent at the trough. The company posted losses for two consecutive years. Most Chinese tech peers laid off aggressively. Trip.com did not. Management kept investing in the international Trip.com brand build-out in Asia-Pacific, retained the engineering and operations talent, and used the downturn to build a stronger supply-side position with Asian hotel partners. Every strategic commitment they made in 2021 — Trip.com as the Asia-Pacific international brand, Skyscanner as the meta-search flywheel, inbound-to-China as a dedicated product line — has compounded cleanly since 2023. The 2025 print is the clearest proof point yet.

The capital discipline signals from Q4 2025 are straightforward. Full-year adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation of eighteen-point-nine billion yuan, up eleven percent. Q4 adjusted margin at twenty-two percent despite heavy international reinvestment. A hundred and six billion yuan of cash on the balance sheet, no net debt. Full utilisation of the 2025 share-repurchase quota. A hundred-million-US-dollar Tourism Innovation Fund launched during 2025 for partner and ecosystem investment. Over one billion yuan invested in the inbound travel sector in 2025 alone, and two-point-nine billion yuan in customer protection and service enhancements.

The watch item is the 2025 board refresh. Two co-founders resigned from the board and two new independent directors were appointed, alongside committee reshuffles. Governance depth is being rebuilt at the board level — worth tracking as succession implications play out over the next three to five years.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is the Chinese outbound travel normalisation. China's outbound travel market is still recovering from pandemic-era policy and visa frictions, and is below 2019 trip volume levels in some corridors. Trip.com is the dominant domestic platform for Chinese travellers going abroad. Every incremental outbound trip to Japan, Thailand, Korea or Europe is booked through the platform, at a take rate that is structurally higher than pure-China domestic trips.

The second engine is the pan-Asia-Pacific international build. The Trip.com brand is the fastest-growing international online travel agency in Japan, South Korea, Thailand, Singapore, Hong Kong and several other Asian markets. Booking and Agoda are the established incumbents, but Trip.com's brand build, supply-side relationships, and engineering discipline have compounded a clear share-gain trajectory for three years. International bookings up sixty percent in 2025 is the headline, but the quieter data point is that international margin contribution is now meaningful and improving.

The third engine is the inbound-to-China structural differentiator. Twenty million inbound travellers in 2025, nearly doubling. Sixty-three thousand hotels. No other platform in the world can offer a foreign user the combination of Chinese hotel supply, Chinese transport connectivity, Mandarin-and-English customer service, and integrated visa and payments support. Inbound is structurally higher-margin than outbound because the supply-side relationships are locked in and the competition is weaker.

The kicker is the artificial intelligence platform. Trip.com has invested heavily in an in-house large-language-model-based travel-planning stack. Agentic travel commerce — where an assistant books end-to-end itineraries — is a structural threat to aggregators that own only search, but a structural opportunity for platforms that own supply, fulfilment, and customer support. Trip.com owns all three. If agentic travel takes off, Trip.com's take-rate capture is protected in a way that Booking and Expedia's are not.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, international revenue as a percentage of total — roughly forty percent in 2025; watch for continued progression toward fifty percent by 2027. Second, international bookings growth — sixty percent in 2025; sustained forty-plus percent in 2026 would confirm the Asia-Pacific share-gain thesis. Third, inbound travel volume — twenty million in 2025 nearly doubling; management has guided to doubling inbound city participation in 2026. Fourth, Q4 adjusted margin — twenty-two percent now; margin stability or expansion during international reinvestment is the operating-leverage test. Fifth, Chinese outbound travel recovery — still below 2019 levels in some corridors; full normalisation is a near-term tailwind. Sixth, share-repurchase cadence — the 2025 authorisation was fully utilised; watch for a larger 2026 authorisation.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 non-GAAP net income was thirty-one-point-eight billion yuan, roughly four-point-four billion US dollars. Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation was eighteen-point-nine billion yuan — roughly two-point-six billion US dollars. Cash is a hundred and six billion yuan, roughly fifteen billion US dollars. Market cap at time of writing is around fifty billion US dollars.

That puts the stock at roughly eleven to twelve times trailing non-GAAP earnings and closer to eight to nine times earnings if you strip out the cash. For a business with seventeen-percent revenue growth, sixty-percent international bookings growth, a dominant domestic franchise, and a unique inbound-to-China asset, that is a clearly compressed multiple.

The simple framing: a dominant regional online travel agency growing international bookings at sixty percent, with fifteen billion dollars of net cash, trading at mid-to-high single-digit earnings multiples on an ex-cash basis. Comp: Booking Holdings trades at roughly twenty times forward earnings on mid-teens growth. If Trip.com's forward growth trajectory outpaces Booking's while maintaining margins, the mid-teens multiple is the base case and a full re-rating to Booking-comparable levels is a credible upside.

The key sensitivity is the Chinese regulatory overhang. The anti-monopoly investigation disclosed in 2025 is not yet resolved; a material adverse outcome would compress the multiple independent of operations.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus still treats Trip.com as a Chinese domestic online travel agency. The variant is that Trip.com is the pan-Asia-Pacific platform with forty percent of revenue already international and growing at sixty percent, and that the relevant comp set is increasingly Booking Holdings rather than domestic Chinese internet names. Once the international revenue share crosses fifty percent, the multiple should reflect that mix.

Second: the inbound-to-China asset is structurally unique and the market prices it as zero. Twenty million inbound travellers at economics that are higher-margin than outbound is a real profit centre that Booking, Expedia, Agoda and the US travel platforms cannot touch. The China-domestic supply base plus international customer reach combination is not replicable. This is a walled garden that the market has not learnt to value.

The honest counter: Chinese regulatory and geopolitical overhang is real, and the anti-monopoly investigation is unresolved. The multiple compression partly reflects genuine risks that do not necessarily close. A Booking-level multiple may not be realistic for a Shanghai-listed business operating in 2026's geopolitical climate. Position sizing should reflect that the re-rating thesis has a structural ceiling.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Chinese regulatory outcome. The anti-monopoly investigation is the near-term unknown; a significant fine, structural remedy, or behavioural condition could compress both earnings and multiple simultaneously. Two: international competitive escalation. Booking, Expedia and Agoda all have stronger brand equity in Western source markets; if they invest harder in Asian supply, Trip.com's international share gains slow. Three: Chinese outbound travel slowdown. A macro or policy-driven slowdown in Chinese consumer discretionary spending hits the highest-margin outbound segment first. Four: artificial intelligence disruption. If agentic travel commerce matures faster than Trip.com's internal stack, take rates compress globally. Five: governance gap. The 2025 board refresh is only partially resolved; ongoing gaps in independent oversight add a discount premium. Six: foreign exchange. Yuan weakness compresses US-dollar reported earnings for the dual-listed stock.

Specific sell triggers. International bookings growth decelerating below thirty percent for two consecutive quarters without a clear macro explanation — Asia-Pacific share gain stalling. Domestic accommodation revenue growth falling below ten percent for two consecutive quarters — core franchise weakening. Adjusted margin breaking below eighteen percent for more than one quarter without a bounded investment explanation. Inbound travel volume growth decelerating sharply — the distinctive differentiator weakening. A material adverse outcome in the anti-monopoly investigation. Jane Sun or James Liang stepping back without a named, credible successor.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. Trip.com is the pan-Asia-Pacific travel platform hiding inside a Chinese-online-travel-agency wrapper, with international scaling at sixty percent bookings growth and an inbound-to-China asset that no competitor can replicate. The next print, first-quarter 2026 on the nineteenth of May, will show whether international growth is holding and whether the anti-monopoly investigation has progressed. Watch international bookings growth and any disclosure on the regulatory review, in that order.
