# Why Agents Won't Take the Value in Our Platforms

A talking-points crib for Trip.com, Full Truck Alliance, and KE Holdings. The framework is the FSSA "Friction Test," plus the eight theories of agentic disruption.

The twenty-second answer. Agents attack interfaces and bundles. Our holdings aren't interfaces — they're two-sided networks whose value sits below the transaction, in liquidity, live inventory, trust, and settlement. An agent can sit on the front and click the button; it cannot rebuild the network underneath. So the likely outcome is adaptation, not disintermediation — and the market is applying an indiscriminate "AI discount" we're using to add.

## Reframe before you defend — don't accept the wrong analogy

The eight-theory framework was built for software-as-a-service — software sitting on top of a workflow. Run our names through it and six of the eight simply don't bite. Unbundling, the zero-dollar minimum viable product, compound engineering, and "the end of the seat" all kill per-seat software you can clone at near-zero cost. You can vibe-code a Trip.com look-alike in a weekend. You cannot vibe-code a network of hotel and airline inventory, 4.6 million verified truckers, or 545,000 property agents.

The one that's actually hitting us is the "Uncertainty Tax" — sound businesses de-rated because the market can't model year five. That shows up in the multiple, not the fundamentals. Earnings and buybacks keep growing. That's opportunity, not impairment.

The two theories that do aim at us: "superagent eats the interface," and "the invisible app" — an agent sitting above the platform. That is exactly where the Friction Test answers back.

## The Friction Test — your one weapon

The question is not "can an agent imitate the front-end?" It's: does taking the user out of the platform make the task cheaper, easier, or more reliable — or does it just relocate the friction?

Discovery is the product, not a hurdle. Nobody arrives with a fixed instruction. They compare, shift dates, react to price, reviews, and availability — the refining loop is the transaction, not friction on the way to it.

The value sits below the checkout button. Remove the user and you lose access to what makes the platform valuable: live inventory, price, availability, fulfilment, payments, credit, and trust. Friction is relocated, not removed — usually to a worse experience with more security risk.

Consumer agents aren't there yet. Eighteen months after capable agents shipped, transaction volume through them is tiny. OpenAI's Instant Checkout unwound — Walmart pulled out in March 2026 for wrong items in carts, Etsy left for lack of sales. Behaviour and trust move slower than the code.

## Trip.com — travel, an online travel agent

Discovery is the journey. A user planning a trip weighs dates, price, location, reviews, and availability in real time. That is the product; an agent that collapses it to a prompt delivers a worse trip, not a better one.

Un-agent-able plumbing. Live cross-border inventory across airlines and hotels, multi-currency payments, twenty-four-seven multilingual service, visa and complex-itinerary support. An agent can't conjure a room that isn't in inventory, or rebook you at two a.m. in Osaka.

They own the agent layer. Trip.com's own AI travel agent, Genie, uses AI to improve planning, search, and service from the inside — capturing the upside while keeping inventory, trust, and workflow ownership.

Watch-item, to be honest: top-of-funnel discovery and brand queries migrating to large language models. That's traffic sourcing to monitor — not the transaction, and they're building for it.

## Full Truck Alliance — digital freight matching, the strongest case

The asset is liquidity, not an app. 236 million orders fulfilled in 2025 across about 3.1 million monthly active shippers and about 4.6 million active truckers. Match quality is a function of network density. A perfect interface still can't summon a truck that isn't on the network.

Deterministic, trust-heavy rails. Verified capacity, credit, settlement, insurance, dispute resolution — near-zero error tolerance and embedded trust. This is the defensible, deterministic end of the map, not commoditisable workflow.

Two-sided cold-start defeats the agent. To disintermediate, an agent must rebuild both sides at once — a cold-start problem AI does nothing to solve. And Full Truck Alliance is itself the natural home to deploy matching AI.

## KE Holdings, also known as Beike — property transactions

The highest-friction, most physical transaction there is. The largest financial decision of a household's life: viewings, verification, financing, escrow, closing. Tacit judgement and physical context dominate — it fails the "digital end-to-end, bounded task" test badly.

Proprietary, governed data plus a cooperation protocol. The "housing dictionary" of standardised property records, plus the Agent Cooperation Network linking about 61,000 stores and 545,000 agents. That's a system of record, not an interface — you can't scrape or clone it.

Agents front-end the easy 5 percent. An agent can help shortlist; it can't tour the flat, verify title, arrange the mortgage, or hold the escrow. KE keeps the hard, valuable 95 percent.

## The wider bucket — one line

Same test across the consumer-internet and marketplace holdings — e-commerce ecosystems, local services: the checkout button is the thin, imitable part; the inventory, logistics, payments, credit, and trust underneath are the moat — and largely un-agent-able. Where they're de-rated on AI fear alone, that's candidate mispricing, and these teams are at the frontier deploying AI themselves.

## If they push back

"Agents will own discovery." Discovery is not the transaction, and the platforms own their own agents. Top-of-funnel is the watch-item, not an impairment.

"Weekend clones, zero-dollar minimum viable products." You can clone the interface, never the liquidity, inventory, and trust. Cold-start is unsolved by AI.

"MCP and APIs turn them into dumb pipes." Dumb-pipe risk hits systems of engagement. A network's data, liquidity, and settlement is a system of record — they expose APIs on their own terms and keep the economics.

## Close

The setup doesn't need a re-rating to work — it needs these businesses to keep doing what they're doing: growing free cash flow, buying back stock, deepening the network. Tough times never last, but tough companies do.
