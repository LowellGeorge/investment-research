# MercadoLibre — The Seven Questions

**Ticker:** MELI US | **Market cap:** ~$94bn | **GEM weight:** 4.2%
**Source base:** Quartr-grounded (Q4 2025 shareholder letter, Q4 2025 earnings call, Goldman Sachs Communicopia Sept 2025)
**Written:** April 2026

---

## Cold open

MercadoLibre has now posted **twenty-eight consecutive quarters of thirty-percent-plus revenue growth**. Not a typo — seven years, no quarter below 30%. The debate isn't whether the business works. It's whether management is right to keep spending every dollar of operating margin to grow faster, and whether the market is pricing them for the right thing.

Seven questions.

---

## 1. What is it?

MercadoLibre is the operating system of Latin American digital commerce. Three interlocking businesses, one user:

- **Mercado Libre** — the marketplace. 121 million unique buyers across the region. In Q4 2025 they moved $19.9bn of goods, up 37% year-on-year.
- **Mercado Pago** — the fintech arm. 78 million monthly active users, $83.7bn of total payment volume in Q4 alone, up 42%.
- **Mercado Crédito** — the credit engine. Loan book hit $12.5bn in Q4 2025, up 90% year-on-year.

Geographically: Brazil and Mexico are the two engines, Argentina is the third, then Chile, Colombia, Peru behind. Galperin described the strategy on the Q4 call as "commerce creates the audience, payments creates the habit, credit creates the lock-in." That's the thing to remember — not the revenue split, the flywheel.

The less obvious leg: advertising. Ad revenue grew **67% year-on-year** in Q4 and crossed $1bn run-rate in 2025. That's the highest-margin business inside MELI and nobody talks about it.

## 2. Can we trust them?

Yes, but there's a fresh change worth tracking.

**Marcos Galperin**, the founder, handed the CEO chair to **Ariel Szarfsztejn** in January 2026. This is not a surprise succession — Szarfsztejn ran commerce for years and the transition was telegraphed well in advance. Galperin stays on as Executive Chairman. Founder-led culture intact, but the hand on the throttle is new.

The signals that matter:
- Twenty-eight straight quarters of 30%+ growth under a disciplined investment regime — they've earned the benefit of the doubt on capital allocation
- Net debt to EBITDA at **0.69x** — conservative balance sheet despite $6.5bn poured into credit expansion in 2025
- They explicitly told the Street in Q4: "We will prioritize long-term growth over short-term margin optimization in 2026." This is Bezos-style communication — they're telling you what to expect and what not to whinge about

Watch Szarfsztejn's first two quarters for any style drift.

## 3. Why does value compound?

The compounder story is the **flywheel plus the ad kicker plus the credit kicker**. Unpack:

**The flywheel.** Galperin on the Q4 call: AI assistants now "advise on 20% of GMV" and handle "87% of fintech user interactions." Every transaction feeds data back into underwriting, ad targeting, and merchant tools. Shopee and TikTok Shop can build the marketplace. They can't build the thirty-year data graph.

**The ad kicker.** 67% ad growth, $1bn+ run-rate, new DSP launched in China, Disney and Google Ad Manager partnerships expanding inventory. Amazon's ad business is worth nearly $500bn as a standalone — MELI's is at a fraction of that maturity.

**The credit kicker.** Credit portfolio up 90% to $12.5bn. Credit card NPL ratio at an **all-time low of 4.4%** despite the aggressive growth. Net Interest Margin After Losses (NIMAL) of **23.3%** — meaning after provisioning, the credit book earns a gross spread that would make a Brazilian bank blush. And crucially: they're **not using customer deposits to fund it**. Deposits drive engagement; credit is funded separately. That's an unusually clean structure.

**The logistics moat.** Mexico fulfillment penetration hit **80%** — the vast majority of packages go through MELI's own network. In Brazil they dropped the free-shipping threshold from BRL 79 to BRL 19, which is a deliberate short-term margin hit that pulled in new buyers and drove a 45% increase in items sold. Classic Amazon 2005 move.

## 4. What proves us right and what to track?

Six specific things:

- **Ad revenue as % of total** — if this crosses mid-single-digits toward 10%, the stock re-rates on mix
- **Brazil market share** — competitive intensity is moderating; if MELI keeps gaining against Shopee, the bull case is intact
- **Mexico fulfillment penetration** — 80% now; benchmark for other markets
- **Credit NPL trajectory and NIMAL** — 4.4% NPL at portfolio growing 90% is the headline metric; if NPLs tick up while growth slows, thesis cracks
- **China fulfillment centre ramp** — first MELI facility in China opened Q4 2025, already shipping to top five markets; cross-border could be the next leg
- **Agentic commerce response** — are MELI's AI tools getting mentioned *by* independent AI agents when users ask them to shop? This is a new KPI and nobody has it yet

## 5. What's it worth?

Not going to fake precision here — the real 5-year IRR work needs your own model. But the frame:

- Revenue compounded ~39% in 2025. A reasonable base case: decelerate to 25-30% CAGR over next 3 years, high-teens CAGR over years 4-5 as they get closer to frontier
- Operating margin currently compressed at 10.1%, down 340bps YoY from investment cycle. Management signal: don't expect margin expansion in 2026. Base case: margins re-expand 200-400bps between 2027-2030 as ad mix and credit mature
- At ~$94bn market cap and ~$1.5bn 2025 adjusted FCF, the stock prices optically rich on trailing cash. But if FCF grows 3-4x over five years (plausible given revenue trajectory and margin path), the multiple compresses dramatically

**The key sensitivity:** ad business margin contribution. If advertising is 8% of revenue in 2030 vs ~4% today and carries 70%+ contribution margin, that alone reshapes the terminal multiple.

## 6. Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles:

**One — the margin panic is wrong.** The Street spent Q4 2025 hand-wringing about the 340bp operating margin drop. But strip out the one-time tax credit and you still had 9% margins while growing revenue 45% and credit book 90%. That's the trade-off management is making explicitly and the pattern that has delivered 28 straight quarters of 30%+ growth. The market keeps mistaking deliberate investment for a broken model.

**Two — agentic commerce is their moat, not their threat.** Market narrative: AI agents will disintermediate e-commerce platforms. But MELI owns the first-party inventory, the logistics, the payments rails, and the credit book. When an AI agent goes to transact in Latin America, it transacts *on* Mercado Pago and ships *through* Mercado Envios. The disintermediation risk sits with pure-play software (Shopify) and pure-play marketplaces without logistics (TikTok Shop). MELI is the infrastructure.

The honest counter: if Amazon's agentic stack wins globally and they finally commit to Latin America, MELI has a real fight. So far, Amazon has been in Brazil for over a decade and MELI keeps gaining share. Track it, don't assume it.

## 7. What could kill it, and when do we sell?

**Real risks:**

- **Credit book blow-up.** 90% growth is aggressive. If NPLs spike back to mid-single-digits and NIMAL compresses below 15%, the credit leg breaks and it takes margins with it. Biggest idiosyncratic risk.
- **Argentina macro.** FX-neutral growth looks strong but reported results can swing violently. Less a thesis risk, more a reported-earnings risk.
- **Competitive escalation.** Shopee has been patient and well-capitalized. TikTok Shop is new in LatAm. If both push logistics investment aggressively, MELI's moat narrows.
- **Regulatory.** Fintech regulation across Brazil, Mexico, Argentina. Pago's margin structure could be impacted by interchange caps or credit-card fee caps.
- **Agentic disintermediation.** Not the base case but needs monitoring. If AI agents start bypassing the MELI storefront and going directly to merchants via API, the take rate collapses.

**Sell triggers (specific, not vague):**
- Three consecutive quarters of credit NPL deterioration while portfolio growth slows — structural credit problem
- Market share loss in Brazil commerce for two consecutive quarters — ecosystem moat eroding
- Ad revenue growth decelerating below overall company growth rate — mix bull case breaking
- CEO Szarfsztejn walks back the "long-term over margin" positioning in favor of aggressive margin targets — culture drift away from what works
- Founder family reduces stake materially — alignment signal breaks

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. The question that matters most right now: does the ad business get the credit it deserves as a re-rating catalyst, or does the market keep staring at the margin line? Position accordingly.
