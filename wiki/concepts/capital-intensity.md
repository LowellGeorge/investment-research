# Capital Intensity

Businesses requiring large ongoing capital investment. Capital intensity is not inherently negative — when paired with high returns and barriers to entry, it creates formidable moats. But it demands exceptional capital allocation discipline.

## Extreme Capital Intensity

- **[[wiki/companies/tsmc|TSMC]]** — FY2025 CapEx $40.9B (33% of revenue); FY2026 guided at $52-56B. A single leading-edge fab costs $15-20B. This level of spending is itself a moat: neither Samsung Foundry (operating at a loss) nor Intel Foundry ($13B FY2024 loss) can match TSMC's return profile
- **[[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]]** — Combined semiconductor and display CapEx rivals TSMC's. Counter-cyclical investment strategy (spending through downturns) has been a defining feature for 40 years
- **[[wiki/companies/sk-hynix|SK Hynix]]** — Memory fabs require sustained multi-billion dollar investment cycles; HBM packaging adds further capital requirements

## High Capital Intensity (Self-Funding)

- **[[wiki/companies/dino-polska|Dino Polska]]** — 345 new stores/year, each on company-owned land. However, the model is self-funding: negative working capital, operating cash flow covers 122% of CapEx
- **[[wiki/companies/wal-mart-de-mexico-sab|Walmex]]** — Ongoing store expansion across Mexico and Central America; scale generates sufficient cash flow
- **[[wiki/companies/dbs-group|DBS Group]]** — Technology transformation required sustained investment; now delivering returns through lower cost-to-serve

## Moderate Capital Intensity

- **[[wiki/companies/quanta-computer|Quanta Computer]]** — AI server manufacturing requires ongoing equipment investment but operates more as an ODM than fab owner
- **[[wiki/companies/midea-group|Midea Group]]** — Manufacturing automation and robotics investment; offset by scale efficiencies
- **[[wiki/companies/techtronic|Techtronic]]** — Power tool manufacturing with ongoing R&D and tooling investment

## Capital-Light Models

- **[[wiki/companies/tencent|Tencent]]** — Platform business with minimal physical assets; investment portfolio rather than organic CapEx
- **[[wiki/companies/computer-age-management-services|CAMS]]** — Technology platform with limited physical infrastructure needs
- **[[wiki/companies/qualitas-controladora|Qualitas Controladora]]** — Insurance underwriting with minimal asset base required
- **[[wiki/companies/alibaba|Alibaba]]** — Marketplace model (Taobao/Tmall) is asset-light; cloud business is capital-heavy
- **[[wiki/companies/hcl-technologies|HCL Technologies]]** — Services business where talent is the primary "asset"

## Key Patterns

1. **Capital intensity + high returns = strongest moats** (TSMC at 54% ROIC is the archetype)
2. **Capital intensity + low returns = value traps** (Samsung Foundry losing money while spending billions)
3. **Self-funding capital intensity** (Dino Polska) is superior to externally funded capex programs
4. **Technology investment** as a form of capital intensity is often underappreciated in "asset-light" businesses
