## Business Overview

ICICI Bank traces its origins to the Industrial Credit and Investment Corporation of India, a development finance institution established in 1955 with World Bank backing. The institution transformed itself through India's 1990s liberalization, establishing ICICI Bank as a subsidiary in 1994 and executing a landmark reverse merger in 2002 that created India's second-largest bank by assets. Under K.V. Kamath's aggressive expansion and Chanda Kochhar's subsequent tenure, the bank grew rapidly in corporate lending but accumulated dangerous NPA exposure, with gross NPAs reaching 8.84% by fiscal 2018. The Kochhar governance scandal -- involving quid pro quo lending to Videocon -- led to her termination and criminal charges.

Sandeep Bakhshi's appointment in October 2018 marked a decisive inflection point. A career ICICI Group insider with four decades of experience across insurance and banking subsidiaries, Bakhshi implemented a three-pillar turnaround: aggressive recognition and resolution of legacy stressed assets, a strategic pivot toward retail and SME lending, and a cultural reset anchored in the mantra "Fair to Customer, Fair to Bank." The results have been extraordinary: gross NPAs fell from 8.84% to under 2%, ROA rose from 0.72% to 2.52%, and the bank built a financial ecosystem spanning banking, life insurance ([[wiki/companies/icici-lombard|ICICI Lombard]] general insurance), and asset management.

Today, ICICI Bank operates 6,983 business centres across India with a presence in 11 countries. Its diversified loan book tilts 53.5% retail, with a fast-growing business banking segment (up 22-33% YoY). The iMobile Pay app processes 558 million transactions annually, and the bank maintains a fortress balance sheet with CET1 of 16.46% and contingency provisions of INR 131 billion.

## Bull Case

- Best-in-class ROA (2.1-2.5%) among large Indian banks, with sector-leading cost-to-income ratio of 37.8%
- India's structural growth runway: credit-to-GDP at 55-57% vs. 150%+ global average implies decades of loan growth
- Business banking growing 22-33% YoY -- a high-return segment where ICICI holds competitive advantages
- 360-degree financial ecosystem (banking, insurance, AMC) deepens customer wallet share
- Fortress balance sheet: CET1 16.46%, GNPA 1.58%, NNPA 0.37%, INR 131B contingency provisions
- Digital infrastructure (iMobile Pay) positions the bank as a fintech beneficiary rather than victim

## Bear Case

- Peak profitability may be behind: ROA declined from 2.52% to 2.11% over three quarters in FY26
- RBI-directed INR 12.83B provision for agricultural PSL misclassification reveals systemic compliance failure
- Loan-to-deposit ratio rising from 82.4% to 87.4% -- loan growth outstripping deposit growth
- NIM compressing (4.41% to 4.30%) with further downside in a rate-cutting cycle
- Rapid business banking growth at 22-33% historically precedes asset quality problems at Indian banks
- No disclosed succession plan for Bakhshi, whose extended term runs through October 2028

## Competitive Position

ICICI Bank competes in a USD 2.7 trillion credit market where public sector banks hold 57-59% share but private banks are steadily gaining ground. The primary competitor is [[wiki/companies/hdfc-bank|HDFC Bank]], which operates 9,455 branches post-merger but is currently focused on integration, creating an opening ICICI has exploited. [[wiki/companies/kotak-mahindra-bank|Kotak Mahindra Bank]] and Axis Bank compete aggressively in affluent segments but lack ICICI's breadth. SBI dominates mass retail through government-backed distribution.

ICICI's moat rests on four pillars: a low-cost CASA deposit franchise (~39%), a diversified ecosystem spanning insurance and asset management, genuine technology leadership through iMobile Pay, and scale advantages across 6,983 business centres. The most significant disruptive threat comes from UPI-native platforms like PhonePe and Google Pay, which have disintermediated banks from the payments layer. However, ICICI is well-positioned to co-opt this disruption as a major UPI issuer, and the underlying credit still requires a bank balance sheet.

## Management Assessment

Bakhshi has delivered one of the most impressive turnarounds in Indian banking history. His communication style is deliberately restrained -- brief, structured opening remarks followed by data-driven CFO commentary. The "risk-calibrated profitable growth" framework has been repeated consistently and the financial trajectory validates it precisely. The executive bench is deep, with four executive directors each holding multi-decade ICICI Group tenure. However, the Q3 FY26 agricultural PSL misclassification -- a systemic practice persisting "over some years" that management characterized evasively -- reveals a compliance blind spot. Succession remains the most important unresolved governance question.

## Red Flags

- Historical near-death experience during 2008 GFC (deposit run, RBI backstop required)
- Former CEO Chanda Kochhar convicted of bribery in the Videocon loan scandal
- Agricultural PSL misclassification persisting "over some years" without internal detection
- Credit card portfolio declining 3.5% YoY -- unsecured lending engine stalling
- ROA on clear downward trajectory from cycle peak
- LDR expansion signals potential funding pressure ahead
