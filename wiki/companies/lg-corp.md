# LG Corp

> The holding company of the LG Group, offering deeply discounted (50-70% NAV discount) exposure to a portfolio spanning home electronics (LG Electronics), EV batteries (LG Energy Solution), chemicals (LG Chem), and OLED displays (LG Display), controlled by the fourth-generation Koo family.

## Business Overview

LG Corp's story begins in 1947 when Koo In-hwoi registered Lak-Hui Chemical Industrial Corp in Busan, producing facial cosmetics and later plastics. The expansion into electronics followed in 1958 with the founding of GoldStar (later LG Electronics), which produced Korea's first domestic radio, refrigerator, and television. The group was formally unified as Lucky-Goldstar in 1983 and rebranded as LG in 1995. In 2002-2003, LG became the first Korean chaebol to adopt a pure holding company model, creating LG Corp (003550) as a clean holding entity.

The group operates through four principal subsidiaries. LG Electronics (KRW 89T revenue, second consecutive record) is the world's dominant OLED TV manufacturer with ~49.7% market share and has built the webOS platform into a KRW 1T+ revenue business across 260 million installed devices. LG Energy Solution (KRW 23.7T revenue) is the world's second-largest EV battery manufacturer with 9.2% global market share, heavily dependent on US IRA subsidies. LG Chem (KRW 45.9T revenue) is a diversified chemicals and advanced materials company, currently the world's largest NCM cathode supplier. LG Display manufactures OLED panels for televisions.

The investment thesis is fundamentally about the holding company discount. LG Corp trades at 50-70% below the NAV of its listed subsidiaries. Its indirect exposure to LG Energy Solution alone (through its LG Chem stake) is worth roughly $17 billion -- exceeding the entire LG Corp market cap. The group is at its most important financial inflection point in a decade, transitioning from a capex-intensive build-out phase to what should be a cash-generation phase, with LGES guiding capex down 40% in 2026.

## Bull Case

- Deep NAV discount (50-70%) provides wide margin of safety at ~8x forward P/E, ~6x EV/EBITDA
- LG Electronics transformation: webOS platform (KRW 1T revenue), B2B pivot (37% of sales), data center chillers tripling
- LGES holds structural advantage as non-Chinese battery supplier for IRA-protected North American market
- Group transitioning from capex build-out to cash generation (LGES capex guided -40% for 2026)
- Korea Value-Up governance reforms create structural catalyst for discount narrowing
- Palliser Capital activist campaign at LG Chem pressuring for improved capital allocation

## Bear Case

- Chaebol governance: Koo family controls with 16% direct stake through pyramid structure, capital allocation historically prioritizes family over minorities
- LG Energy Solution's 0.3% net margin is effectively subsidized -- strip out IRA credits and economics are deeply negative
- LG Chem paying dividends from asset sales despite having no distributable profit
- LG Electronics' structural 3-5% operating margins under Chinese competitive pressure
- Holding company discount may persist indefinitely without a specific narrowing catalyst
- Market share falling: LGES dropped from 11.6% to 9.3% while CATL widened its lead

## Competitive Position

LG Corp's subsidiaries fight in four structurally distinct competitive battles. LG Electronics commands ~49.7% of global OLED TV market through vertically integrated OLED panel supply from LG Display, a position built over 13 consecutive years of category leadership. The webOS platform mirrors the Roku playbook with genuine network effects. However, the appliances business faces structural Chinese pressure. LG Energy Solution is the world's second-largest EV battery maker but the gap with CATL (39.2% share) is widening. LGES's genuine advantage is IRA-protected North American capacity. LG Chem faces persistent Chinese overcapacity in petrochemicals. LG Display's large-size OLED dominance faces the same Chinese capacity threat that commoditized LCD.

Key competitors include CATL and [[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]] (Samsung SDI) in batteries, [[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]] in displays, Midea and Haier in appliances, and Roku/Google/Amazon in platform TV. [[wiki/companies/sk-hynix|SK Hynix]] and other Korean industrial groups share the chaebol governance challenges.

## Management Assessment

Fourth-generation chairman Koo Kwang-mo (born 1982, Cornell/GWU MBA) inherited control in 2018 as an adopted nephew. His background is academic and strategic rather than operational. Recent leadership changes signal a generational reset: LG Electronics replaced CEO William Cho with mechanical engineer Lyu Jae-cheul in December 2025, and LG Chem rotated to insider Kim Dong-chun. Koo's ~16% stake means personal wealth (~$1.5B) is tied to group performance, but the family receives dividends from LG Corp that do not require value creation. Capital allocation has been poor historically -- LG Display has been a persistent capital sink, the LGES IPO diluted LG Chem shareholders without compensation, and LG Chem is funding dividends from asset sales. Recent improvements (buybacks, capex discipline) follow a long period of misallocation.

## Red Flags

- LG Chem paid KRW 2,000/share dividend despite negative distributable profit -- funded by asset sales
- LGES reported KRW 81B net income on KRW 23.7T revenue (0.3% margin); subsidy-dependent economics
- LG Electronics' Q4 net loss of KRW 726B despite positive operating profit flags below-the-line charges
- Chaebol governance: pyramid control structure means family control far exceeds economic interest
- Koo family inheritance litigation resolved February 2026 but potentially appealable
- US House passed legislation accelerating clean energy tax credit phaseout -- existential for LGES
