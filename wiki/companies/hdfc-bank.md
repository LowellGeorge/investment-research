# HDFC Bank

> India's largest private-sector bank by assets, navigating a multi-year post-merger integration with HDFC Limited while maintaining fortress-grade asset quality and a 9,600+ branch distribution network -- temporarily compressed to attractive valuations.

## Business Overview

HDFC Bank was incorporated on 7 August 1994 when the Reserve Bank of India opened private sector banking for the first time since nationalization in 1969. HDFC Limited, India's pioneering mortgage lender founded in 1977, received one of the new licences and recruited Aditya Puri from Citibank Malaysia to lead the institution. Under Puri's 26-year tenure, HDFC Bank became India's most valuable bank, compounding assets from zero to over INR 25 trillion.

The defining event of the current era is the July 2023 merger with parent HDFC Limited, which created India's largest private-sector bank but also inflated the balance sheet, compressed NIMs from a historical 4.0-4.2% to 3.35%, and pushed the credit-deposit ratio to approximately 110%. The multi-year normalisation -- driving the CD ratio back to the targeted 85-90% by FY27, rebuilding CASA ratios, and restoring ROE from the current 13.9% toward the historical 17-18% -- is the central investment narrative.

Net interest income constitutes approximately 71% of revenue, with fees, commissions, and trading income contributing the remaining 29%. The bank operates 9,616 branches (50% in semi-urban and rural geographies) with a 39.2% cost-to-income ratio, among the lowest in the Indian large-bank peer group. Asset quality is distinctive: GNPA of 1.24% and NNPA of 0.42% are among the cleanest in Indian banking.

## Bull Case

- India's credit-to-GDP ratio (~93%) and shallow banking penetration in semi-urban/rural India provide a multi-decade structural growth runway
- Post-merger valuation compression: trading at ~15x P/E and ~2.0x P/B versus a 10-year median P/E of ~25x, creating significant re-rating potential as ROE normalises
- Fortress balance sheet: CET1 of 17.4%, GNPA of 1.24%, and INR 40T+ in assets simultaneously -- a configuration no other Indian bank replicates
- Mortgage franchise creates a cross-sell beachhead: 14% credit card penetration, mid-30% consumer durable credit, 20-25% of deposit momentum from card customers

## Bear Case

- NIM structurally impaired by the HDFC merger: 3.35% versus ICICI's 4.30%, with CASA ratio deteriorated from 35% to 33.6% and showing no sign of pre-merger recovery
- March 2026 governance crisis: Chairman Chakraborty resigned citing "value incongruence," triggering a 10%+ stock decline and $11.5B market cap wipe; AT1 bond mis-selling scandal in Dubai
- ROE compressed from historical 17%+ to 13.9% -- recovery to pre-merger levels requires every variable (NIM, costs, CD ratio) to resolve favourably and simultaneously
- [[wiki/companies/icici-bank|ICICI Bank]] has closed the competitive gap on digital capabilities, brand perception, and growth (15% loan growth vs. HDFC's 5.4%)

## Competitive Position

HDFC Bank sits at the apex of India's private banking sector. Within the effective competitive set of HDFC Bank, [[wiki/companies/icici-bank|ICICI Bank]], Axis Bank, and [[wiki/companies/kotak-mahindra-bank|Kotak Mahindra Bank]], HDFC Bank maintains the largest branch network (9,616 branches), strongest asset quality (GNPA 1.24%), and lowest cost-to-income ratio (39.2%).

The bank's moat rests on four interlocking pillars: distribution network scale (50% of branches in semi-urban/rural), low-cost deposit franchise (CASA ratio 33-35%), relationship depth and cross-sell intensity (auto loan customers become liability customers; mortgage origination beachheads for credit cards and broking), and credit quality (75.2% of rated wholesale book is AA+). The main competitive risk is [[wiki/companies/icici-bank|ICICI Bank]], which has accelerated under its own digital transformation, posting faster loan growth and higher NIMs.

## Management Assessment

CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan inherited the institution from legendary founder-CEO Aditya Puri in October 2020. Jagdishan's style is more collegial and consensus-oriented, operating through a distributed leadership model. He has been measured and mostly consistent on guidance, acknowledging misses directly. The March 2026 governance crisis -- chairman resignation, AT1 mis-selling, FIR filed against Jagdishan by the Lilavati Trust -- creates genuine near-term uncertainty. The bank has no traditional promoter holding post-merger, and Jagdishan holds no material equity stake. Three external law firms have been engaged for an independent review.

## Red Flags

- AT1 bond mis-selling in Dubai: HDFC Bank's DIFC branch marketed Credit Suisse AT1 bonds to NRI retail clients as safe investments; DFSA barred the branch from onboarding new clients; three senior executives terminated
- Chairman Atanu Chakraborty resigned in March 2026 citing "certain happenings and practices not in congruence with my personal values" -- the most significant governance event in the bank's history
- Agricultural NPA noncompliance led to INR 5B special provision in December 2024; CFO was evasive when pressed on portfolio size
- RBI has already imposed sanctions once (2020-2021 credit card and digital product freeze); post-merger technology estate is vastly more complex
- INR 1,628B goodwill on consolidated balance sheet from HDFC merger; mortgage lending has become "irrational" per management
