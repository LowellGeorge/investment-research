# Computer Age Management Services

> India's dominant mutual fund registrar and transfer agent, operating as a toll-booth on the country's financialization with ~68% market share in a regulated duopoly, offering exceptional unit economics but facing structural yield compression.

## Business Overview

Computer Age Management Services was incorporated on 25 May 1988 in Chennai by V. Shankar, originally as a computer training and share-transfer processing outfit. The company pivoted into mutual fund record-keeping in the mid-1990s as SEBI mandated independent registrar and transfer agents for the nascent mutual fund industry. Over three decades, CAMS built the back-office infrastructure that processes investor transactions, maintains unit-holder records, and handles dividends and redemptions for 29 of India's asset management companies, covering approximately 68% of industry AUM.

CAMS operates as a regulated duopoly alongside KFintech, with no third RTA licensed in India. The business model is asset-light and toll-like: CAMS earns a fee expressed in basis points on the AUM it services, declining on a telescopic schedule as AUM grows. This creates exchange-like economics -- ROCE of 40%, EBITDA margins of 46%, and 100%+ cash conversion -- but also means revenue growth structurally lags AUM growth due to yield compression.

Beyond its core MF RTA franchise, CAMS is diversifying into payments (CAMSPay), insurance repository, KRA/KYC services, alternatives servicing, and NPS administration. Non-MF revenue is growing 24%+ annually and now contributes approximately 14.5% of total revenue, though at significantly lower margins (13% EBITDA) than the core business.

## Bull Case

- India's mutual fund AUM is growing at 18-20% annually with massive structural runway (credit-to-GDP and MF penetration remain low)
- Regulated duopoly with 68% market share, 23+ year average client tenure, and near-zero client churn creates exceptional pricing power
- Non-MF businesses (CAMSPay, alternatives, insurance repository, KRA) are compounding at 28% over five years with a clear path to 20%+ EBITDA margins
- Fortress balance sheet (INR 853 Cr cash, zero debt) with 65% dividend payout and 40% ROCE

## Bear Case

- Structural yield compression means revenue growth (5.5% in Q3 FY26) materially lags AUM growth (18.2%), and periodic repricing events create lumpy headwinds
- Non-MF businesses remain loss-making or low-margin in several segments; reaching MF-like profitability is aspirational, not guaranteed
- Demat channel's growing share of MF transactions gradually shifts servicing toward CDSL/NSDL, eroding the RTA's centrality
- At 33-35x trailing P/E, valuation leaves limited margin of safety for what is fundamentally a mid-single-digit organic revenue grower

## Competitive Position

CAMS operates in a structural duopoly with KFintech as the only other licensed RTA in India. The switching costs are genuine: migrating an AMC's entire unit-holder database, transaction processing, and compliance infrastructure is operationally complex and rarely undertaken. Average client tenure exceeds 23 years, and no major contract renewals are pending for 18 months. CAMS's 68% market share has been stable for years, though AMCs do occasionally switch -- Franklin Templeton, Navi, and Taurus have moved between RTAs.

KFintech is a credible competitor growing faster on revenue with better international diversification and a higher non-MF revenue share (30% vs. 14.5%). The competitive dynamic is cooperative oligopoly rather than destructive rivalry -- both players benefit from the regulatory framework that mandates independent RTAs.

## Management Assessment

CEO Anuj Kumar is a capable, articulate leader who has delivered consistently on guidance: 25%+ non-MF growth, 10-11% cost increases, and successful M&A integration (KRA completed in one quarter). His five-year re-appointment removes near-term succession risk. The main reservation is limited skin in the game -- Kumar holds approximately 0.09% personal shareholding with no controlling promoter. Alignment relies on institutional board quality and ESOP incentives rather than concentrated ownership conviction.

## Red Flags

- Yield compression is structural and accelerating: revenue grew 5.5% on 18.2% AUM growth in Q3 FY26
- SEBI warning letter in Q4 FY25 filings regarding operational lapses -- disclosed quietly rather than headlined
- Non-MF margin convergence to 40-50% EBITDA "after 6-7 years" is highly aspirational given payment aggregator margin compression and regulated NPS pricing
- No controlling promoter; alignment structure rests on institutional governance rather than ownership conviction
- Business has never been tested through a prolonged equity bear market as a public company
