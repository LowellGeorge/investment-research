# Samsung Electronics

## Business Overview

Samsung Electronics traces its origins to January 13, 1969, when Lee Byung-chul established Samsung Electric Industries in Suwon. The company entered semiconductors in 1974 with the acquisition of Korea Semiconductor and spent the following decades executing a relentless playbook: enter a mature business, manufacture at scale, compress the technology gap through brute-force investment, then outrun incumbents on cost and volume.

Today Samsung operates across four major segments. Device Solutions (DS) -- encompassing memory semiconductors, foundry, and system LSI -- is the profit engine, with Q4 2025 memory revenue alone reaching KRW 37.1T (+62% YoY). Mobile Experience (MX) ships approximately 60 million smartphones per quarter at an ASP of $244. Display leads the global OLED market. Visual Display dominates TVs with 28.9% global revenue share. FY2025 consolidated revenue was KRW 333.6T (+11% YoY) with record Q4 operating profit of KRW 20.1T (21.4% margin).

The current strategic challenge centers on HBM for AI. [[wiki/companies/sk-hynix|SK Hynix]] overtook Samsung as the largest DRAM supplier by revenue in 2025, holding 53% of HBM revenue versus Samsung's 22% in Q3 2025. Samsung cleared NVIDIA's HBM3E qualification in September 2025 after 18 months of delays and has entered HBM4 qualification with mass production beginning in early 2026.

## Bull Case

- Fortress balance sheet: net cash of KRW 100.6T, Aa2/AA- credit ratings, enabling counter-cyclical investment
- HBM4 qualification progressing with management claiming "no redesign required" -- HBM sales expected to triple YoY in 2026
- Tesla contract for 2nm foundry AI chips ($16.5B through 2033) represents a credible inflection point for the foundry business
- New equity compensation program aligning ~1,000 executives with shareholders (CEO receives 100% of performance bonus in equity)
- At ~8.4x forward P/E with significant "Korea discount," valuation is compelling if HBM catch-up materializes

## Bear Case

- Foundry market share collapsed from 13% to 7.2% while [[wiki/companies/tsmc|TSMC]] commands ~70%; foundry has been loss-making for years
- Operating profit can swing 6-7x across semiconductor cycles -- FY2023 OP of KRW 6.6T versus FY2025 KRW 43.4T
- Chaebol governance: Lee Jae-yong controls through cross-shareholdings despite holding only 1.63% directly; convicted of bribery in 2017
- Scattershot M&A: Rainbow Robotics, Masimo audio, FlaektGroup HVAC, ZF ADAS -- none with obvious synergy to core semiconductor business
- Talent attrition: Glassdoor 3.0/5.0 for Samsung Electronics America; engineers citing long hours and below-market pay

## Competitive Position

Samsung occupies a singular position as the only company operating at scale across memory, foundry, displays, smartphones, and consumer electronics. In DRAM, it competes in a three-player oligopoly with [[wiki/companies/sk-hynix|SK Hynix]] and Micron. The historic shift in 2025 -- SK Hynix overtaking Samsung in DRAM revenue for the first time -- reflects the cost of Samsung's late HBM qualification. In foundry, [[wiki/companies/tsmc|TSMC]] dominates with ~70% market share, and Samsung's persistent yield challenges at advanced nodes have widened the gap.

The vertical integration moat -- designing, manufacturing, and integrating memory, displays, and processors into its own devices -- provides a closed-loop advantage no competitor can replicate. However, this diversification also dilutes returns and creates management attention fragmentation. The most consequential competitive question is whether Samsung can close the HBM gap and achieve sustainable foundry profitability.

## Management Assessment

Executive Chairman Lee Jae-yong represents the third generation of family control. Co-CEO Jun Young-hyun oversees DS and memory, with TM Roh appointed as co-CEO for DX in November 2025. CFO Soon-Cheol Park is the primary investor-facing voice. The January 2026 equity compensation overhaul is a meaningful governance step. Management delivered on shareholder return commitments (50% of FCF), and Q4 2025 results validated the "Samsung is back" narrative. The primary concern remains chaebol governance opacity, particularly around foundry losses and related-party transactions.

- **Valuation:** ~8.4x forward P/E, ~12-13x EV/EBITDA

## Red Flags

- Q2 2025 "inventory value adjustments" created a conveniently low base for subsequent Q4 margin recovery -- classic earnings management pattern
- Foundry never discloses specific profitability figures despite consuming tens of trillions in cumulative capex
- Samsung Biologics accounting controversy: Securities and Futures Commission found intentional accounting rule violations
- Empire-building M&A spree in 2025 across unrelated verticals while core semiconductor competitiveness is under siege
- Six-day executive workweek imposed in 2024 as "crisis management" -- widely criticized as performative
