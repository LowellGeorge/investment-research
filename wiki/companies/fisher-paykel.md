# Fisher & Paykel

> A clinical evidence business disguised as a medical device company -- Fisher & Paykel Healthcare dominates respiratory humidification with 33 of 34 guideline-supporting studies using its systems, generating NZD 2B+ in revenue with 89% recurring consumable mix.

## Business Overview

Fisher & Paykel Healthcare's origin traces to the late 1960s, when Dr. Matt Spence noticed his ventilated patients suffering from dry, infected tracheas and turned to engineer Dave O'Hare at Fisher & Paykel Industries. Together they built a prototype humidifier from a fruit preserving jar. That accidental collaboration between clinician and engineer established the DNA that persists today: a tight loop between bedside observation and engineering iteration.

The company was demerged from Fisher & Paykel Industries and separately listed on 12 November 2001 at an estimated NZD 500M valuation. Today it is worth NZD 21.6B. FPH operates a textbook razor-and-blade model: hardware systems (Optiflow, Airvo) are placed in hospitals, generating a long tail of single-patient-use consumable revenue. Approximately 89% of revenue comes from recurring consumables. Hospital products contribute 63% of revenue (growing 18% in FY25), while Homecare OSA masks contribute 37% (growing 13%).

The company's deepest moat is clinical evidence. Of 34 published studies underpinning nasal high flow clinical practice guidelines, 33 used Fisher & Paykel Optiflow systems. The landmark RENOVATE study confirmed NHF as non-inferior to NIV for acute respiratory failure, expanding the addressable use case. This evidence base took decades to build and would take a competitor a decade to replicate.

## Bull Case

- Clinical evidence moat is among the most durable in medtech: 33/34 guideline studies, massive installed base, and protocol-level entrenchment
- New applications consumables growing 20%, with anesthesia emerging as a breakout vertical (~40% growth, now 10%+ of new applications)
- Gross margin recovery trajectory (59.3% trough to 63% in H1 FY26, targeting 65%) with operating leverage as volume recovers
- Conservative, all-organic growth model with net cash balance sheet, 11% R&D intensity, and 5,749 active patents

## Bear Case

- Valuation priced beyond perfection: ~50-57x forward P/E, approximately double the medical equipment industry average
- OSA mask growth slowing to ~9% guided, with competitive intensity from [[wiki/companies/resmed|Resmed]]'s new product launches
- Tariff exposure: US tariffs of 10% on NZ-manufactured hospital products, with annualized 130bps gross margin headwind
- Capital expenditure ramping: NZD 250M fifth building at East Tamaki, NZD 275M Karaka campus, with history of land write-downs (NZD 98M in FY24)

## Competitive Position

In hospital respiratory care, FPH holds a dominant position. Its only dedicated nasal high flow competitor of scale, Vapotherm, received a NYSE delisting notice in 2023 and was taken private through a distressed merger in 2024. The top five players in high-flow nasal cannula hold over 79% of global market share, with FPH as the clear leader. Broader competitors include Draegerwerk, Hamilton Medical, and Medtronic.

In homecare OSA masks, FPH competes as a credible but distant third behind [[wiki/companies/resmed|Resmed]] (~35% global CPAP mask share) and the weakened Philips Respironics. Philips' 2021 foam degradation recall and subsequent product withdrawals created a structural share redistribution primarily benefiting Resmed. The GLP-1 drug approval for OSA creates a new overhang, though FPH management reports no observed impact on mask usage to date.

The clinical evidence moat in hospital care is highly durable -- replicating 33+ published studies would take a challenger a decade or more. Manufacturing is split between New Zealand (55%) and Mexico (45%), with the Mexico facility largely USMCA-compliant.

## Management Assessment

CEO Lewis Gradon is a 42-year company veteran who joined as an R&D engineer, spent 15 years as SVP of Products & Technology, and became CEO in 2016. This is not a hired-gun CEO but a lifer who built the products generating NZD 2B+ in revenue. His engineering-first orientation drives consistent 11% R&D spend. Management has a strong track record of meeting or exceeding guidance: FY25 NPAT guidance of NZD 320-370M was delivered at NZD 377M. The main risk is Gradon's age (60+) and eventual succession -- the April 2024 executive restructuring (adding a COO) reads as early-stage preparation. Insider ownership under 1% is the weakest element.

## Red Flags

- Extreme valuation: ~50-57x forward P/E prices in years of flawless double-digit growth; any stumble will be punished disproportionately
- Airvo 2 voluntary recall (speaker defect) and Class I recall of infant CPAP prongs (24 detachment incidents) highlight product safety risk inherent in medical devices
- FY24 NPAT collapsed to NZD 133M due to NZD 98M Karaka land write-down, NZD 20M recall provision, and NZD 19M deferred tax charge -- pattern of lumpy one-offs
- Geographic concentration of R&D in Auckland; replicating culture across Mexico and China operations is challenging
- Management guides to a hardware pull-forward in H1 FY26, meaning H2 growth rates may disappoint
