# Dino Polska

> Poland's most disciplined organic growth story in European retail -- a proximity supermarket chain compounding revenue at 19.3% annually through a repeatable, self-funded store rollout model targeting small-town Poland.

## Business Overview

Dino Polska has spent 26 years doing exactly one thing: building approximately 400 square metre proximity supermarkets in small-town Poland, stocking them with fresh food (41% of revenue), and opening the next one. The format has not changed since the first store opened in Czeluszcina in 1999. Store count reached 3,033 at end-2025 with 345 new openings -- the most aggressive year in the company's history. Revenue has compounded at 19.3% annually since founding.

The company's competitive advantage is a bundle of reinforcing factors: approximately 95% of stores sit on company-owned land (eliminating rent exposure), vertically integrated fresh meat via the wholly owned Agro-Rydzyna processing subsidiary, negative net working capital (-17 days), and 12 distribution centres ensuring daily fresh deliveries. The store-opening machine generates ROIC of 17-22% while carrying a net cash balance sheet.

Founder Tomasz Biernacki controls 51.16% of shares and has never sold a single share since the 2017 IPO. He takes zero compensation as Chairman of the Supervisory Board. His entire economic life depends on Dino's share price, creating about as much alignment as an investor can ask for. However, he gives no press conferences, no interviews, and the company does not hold earnings calls -- a level of opacity unusual for an approximately USD 8.6B company.

## Bull Case

- Decade-long store rollout runway: management estimates ~11,000 viable locations across rural Poland versus 3,033 today
- Self-funding growth machine: operating cash flow covered 122% of capex in FY2025; negative working capital means suppliers finance expansion
- Fresh food dominance (41% of revenue) drives repeat visits and differentiates from hard discounters
- Fortress balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA -0.08x) with ROIC consistently 17-22%

## Bear Case

- EBITDA margin compression from 9.3% (FY2020) to 7.6% (FY2025), driven by rising labour costs (+22.7% in H1 2025) that management cannot offset
- Krot-Invest related-party conflict: Biernacki's privately owned construction company is the exclusive builder of all Dino stores with no competitive bidding
- No CEO since July 2020; management board turnover (another resignation in January 2026) raises questions about operating under Biernacki's shadow governance
- No earnings calls, no investor days, no live Q&A -- information asymmetry should command a governance discount

## Competitive Position

In Poland's PLN 275B food retail market, Dino occupies a distinct strategic niche. Biedronka (Jeronimo Martins) leads with 3,800+ stores and EUR 25B+ sales, but operates a hard-discount format in larger towns. Lidl has 930 stores concentrated in urban areas. Dino deliberately targets smaller towns and villages where competitive intensity is far lower and the alternative is often a fragmented family-run shop.

The owned-land model provides a structural 2-3% cost advantage over lease-dependent competitors. Fresh food dominance, anchored by Agro-Rydzyna's integrated meat supply chain (10,000 pork half-carcasses per day capacity), differentiates Dino from discounters who rely on ambient and frozen goods. The proximity format is naturally resistant to e-commerce disruption in rural Poland, where last-mile economics to dispersed communities are prohibitive.

## Management Assessment

Biernacki's alignment is unambiguous: 51% ownership worth PLN 16-17B with zero salary, zero bonuses, and zero personal share sales. Capital allocation has been exceptional -- 100% of free cash flow reinvested at 17-22% ROIC, zero dividends, zero buybacks, zero acquisitions. The Management Board (Izabela Biadala, Michal Krauze, Marcin Jedraszak, Slawomir Nizalowski) compensated modestly at average PLN 1.1M per member with zero equity incentives. The Nizalowski hire from Mars Inc. may signal the first step in institutional professionalization. The key-person risk around Biernacki (age 56, no public succession plan) is the elephant in the room.

## Red Flags

- Krot-Invest: Biernacki's private construction company builds all Dino stores exclusively, likely receiving PLN 1B+ annually with no competitive bidding or independent price verification
- No CEO for 5+ years; Management Board operates under de facto founder control with weak board independence (3 of 5 supervisory seats are insiders)
- Factoring usage surged: factored liabilities nearly doubled from PLN 1.5B to PLN 2.2B in 2025, disclosed only in Note 26 of annual report
- Q1 2025 operating cash flow collapsed to PLN 6.7M from PLN 435.7M (attributed to timing but warrants monitoring)
- Labour cost inflation (+22.7% in H1 2025) with union demands for PLN 900/month raises that could add PLN 400-600M annually
