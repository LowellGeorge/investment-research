# Alsea S.A.B.

> Latin America's largest multi-brand restaurant platform operator, running Starbucks, Domino's, Burger King, and other major franchise brands across 14 countries with over 4,820 units, but generating negative free cash flow under significant leverage.

## Business Overview

Alsea traces its origins to 1990, when the Torrado brothers opened the first Domino's Pizza in a Mexico City suburb. The company formalized as a holding company in 1997 and listed on the Mexican Stock Exchange in 1999. Two transformational moves defined its trajectory: the 2002 Starbucks alliance, which remains the dominant revenue contributor at approximately 42% of sales, and the 2013 acquisition of 362 Vips restaurants from [[wiki/companies/wal-mart-de-mexico-sab|Wal-Mart de Mexico SAB]] for MXN 8.2 billion.

The European expansion (Spain 2014, France/Benelux 2019) loaded the balance sheet. Total liabilities of MXN 71.9 billion against equity of MXN 8.8 billion create an 8:1 leverage ratio, with sub-investment-grade credit (BB+). FY2025 produced MXN 18.1 billion in EBITDA but negative MXN 1.2 billion in free cash flow, with FCF negative in every single quarter. The CFO admitted publicly: "We knew that we were failing at free cash flow generation."

Under new CEO Gurria -- a 34-year Alsea veteran who turned around Starbucks France -- and CFO Rodriguez, the company is pruning underperforming brands (TGI Fridays, P.F. Chang's, Burger King Spain) while adding Chipotle and Raising Cane's development agreements. The portfolio spans QSR (Domino's, Burger King), Coffee (Starbucks), and Casual Dining across Mexico (~54% of revenue), Europe (~30%), and South America (~16%).

## Bull Case

- Dominant franchise platform in structurally underpenetrated Latin American QSR market with 3,400+ potential new store locations identified
- New management team is credibly more disciplined: portfolio rationalization, quality-over-quantity store openings, and candid investor communication
- Inexpensive valuation at ~5x EV/EBITDA vs. franchisors like McDonald's (~22x); 11/12 analysts rate Buy with ~18% upside to consensus target

## Bear Case

- Free cash flow was negative in every quarter of FY2025; MXN 1.5B buyback and dividends are effectively debt-funded
- IFRS 16 flatters EBITDA margin by ~7 percentage points (21.4% post-IFRS 16 vs. ~14% pre-IFRS 16); lease obligations of MXN 16.9B are nearly 2x equity
- Starbucks concentration is existential: ~42% of revenue from a single franchise license expiring in 2035; MXN 3.9B SAT tax dispute represents 44% of equity

## Competitive Position

Mexico's foodservice market is large (~USD 96B) but highly fragmented, with the top five operators holding only 3.14% combined. Alsea claims the number-one position in delivery pizza and coffeehouses in Mexico. Key competitors include Arcos Dorados (McDonald's master franchisee with 2,500+ units) and AmRest Holdings (~2,300 units across Europe). Alsea's competitive advantages include master franchise exclusivity, multi-brand platform economics (centralized supply chain producing 80M+ dough balls daily), scale in real estate access, and digital infrastructure (39.6% digital sales, 8M+ loyalty users).

These advantages are moderately durable but ultimately contractual, not perpetual. Master franchise agreements are at the discretion of brand owners, and royalty escalation is a real risk. Burger King Mexico has posted negative same-store sales for 4+ consecutive quarters with no concrete turnaround plan.

## Management Assessment

Christian Gurria is the first non-family CEO in Alsea's history, a 34-year veteran who rose from Domino's store operator to deploying Starbucks across Mexico, France, and Benelux. CFO Federico Rodriguez is unusually candid for a LatAm executive, openly acknowledging FCF failures. The Torrado family collectively holds approximately 37% of shares, with Armando Torrado serving as Chairman. The new CEO reporting to his former boss who is also a major shareholder is a governance concern, though the alignment of the family's MXN 17B stake is a positive signal.

- **Operating Margin:** 9.9%
- **Net Margin:** 2.6%
- **ROE:** 25.5% (leverage-flattered); ROIC (lease-inclusive): 13.7%
- **FCF:** Negative MXN 1.2B (FY2025)
- **Balance Sheet:** Net Debt/EBITDA 2.5x; total debt including leases MXN 52.2B against MXN 8.8B equity

## Red Flags

- 88.7% net income growth in FY2025 was largely illusory: non-cash FX gains on USD-denominated debt drove Q2 up 553% and Q3 up 559%; management confirmed this "will no longer affect the P&L going forward"
- Burger King Mexico has no turnaround plan: SSS of -7.8%, -6.8%, -1.7%, -4.8% across four consecutive quarters
- Store openings missed target (169 vs. 200+) while announcing even more ambitious 3,400-store pipeline -- a credibility gap
- MXN 3.88B tax dispute with SAT related to the 2014 Vips acquisition could be existential if decided adversely
- European operations being quietly de-emphasized without explicit exit announcement
