# PT Bank Central Asia

> Indonesia's largest private bank and the most efficient large bank in Southeast Asia, built on an 81-83% CASA (current and savings account) ratio that delivers a cost of funds 200-300 basis points below peers, producing a 23%+ ROE on a fortress capital base with a 30.36% capital adequacy ratio.

## Business Overview

PT Bank Central Asia was established in 1957 as part of the Salim Group conglomerate. The 1998 Asian Financial Crisis brought a catastrophic bank run -- 12% of deposits withdrawn in days, 122 branches burned during the May riots -- leading to government nationalization. The Hartono family's Djarum Group acquired control in 2002, and under long-serving President Director Jahja Setiaatmadja, BCA was rebuilt around a single imperative: never again. That crisis-born conservatism now defines the institution.

BCA's franchise is its CASA ratio -- 81-83% of total deposits are in current and savings accounts paying near-zero interest, delivering a blended cost of funds of approximately 1.0-1.3%. This cascades through the entire P&L: a net interest margin of 5.73% (earned without taking disproportionate credit risk), a cost-to-income ratio of 30.74% (globally exceptional), and an ROE persistently above 23%. Revenue splits roughly 77/23 between net interest income and non-interest income (primarily fees and commissions from 36 billion annual digital transactions).

The loan book of Rp 979.7 trillion is oriented toward corporate (46%), consumer (24%, primarily mortgages), commercial (15%), and SME (13%). Asset quality is best-in-class: gross NPL of 1.71% versus industry averages above 2%, with NPL coverage at 208.5%. The balance sheet carries a CAR of 30.36% -- roughly double the regulatory minimum -- and essentially zero reliance on wholesale funding.

## Bull Case

- CASA franchise is the most durable competitive advantage in Indonesian banking: 81%+ deposits at near-zero cost, built over decades of transaction banking habits
- Indonesia's credit-to-GDP of ~35-40% versus 80-120% in mature economies implies decades of structural growth
- Cost-to-income ratio of 30.74% is approximately 10-13 percentage points better than state-owned peers
- At ~16x FY2025 earnings (after a 34% drawdown from peak), BCA trades well below its 10-year average P/E of ~24x
- Counter-cyclical CASA dynamics: flight-to-safety in Indonesian recessions historically channels deposits toward BCA

## Bear Case

- NIM compressed from 5.83% to 5.73% as Bank Indonesia cut policy rates; direction is not improving
- ROE has dropped from 24.56% to 23.28%; overcapitalization (30%+ CAR) means equity is growing faster than earnings
- Simultaneous turnover at the two most senior governance positions (President Director and President Commissioner in 2025)
- Hartono family tax investigation: Victor Hartono placed on a six-month travel ban in November 2025 related to Djarum tax inquiry
- Digital challengers (SeaBank, Bank Jago, Superbank) could replicate BCA's low-cost CASA product at zero marginal cost

## Competitive Position

Indonesia's banking sector is a concentrated oligopoly dominated by four large banks. BCA leads private banks, competing against state-owned Bank Mandiri (BMRI), Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BRI/BBRI), and Bank Negara Indonesia (BBNI). BCA's CIR of 30.74% compares to BBRI at ~41.6%, BBNI at ~43.8%, and BMRI at ~38-40%. The NIM advantage is structural: BCA achieves its 5.73% NIM through funding advantage rather than credit-risk premium, unlike BBRI which earns a higher NIM (~7.74%) by lending into riskier micro segments.

The digital threat comes from Indonesia's neobank ecosystem -- [[wiki/companies/sea-ltd|Sea Ltd]]'s SeaBank, Bank Jago (Gojek-linked), and Superbank (Emtek/Grab/Singtel). BCA's own digital responses (Blu, Bank Saqu) remain defensive sub-scale initiatives rather than category-defining products. However, barriers to entry in commercial banking remain formidable: minimum capital requirements, OJK licensing, and the decades needed to build credible payroll deposit relationships.

## Management Assessment

The Hartono family holds ~54.9% of shares through PT Dwimuria Investama, providing powerful economic alignment. However, the passing of Michael Bambang Hartono in March 2026 introduces succession uncertainty at the controlling shareholder level. Professional management has been competent but the simultaneous departure of President Director Setiaatmadja and President Commissioner Setijoso in 2025 removes key architects of BCA's post-crisis franchise. Tantiem payments to the Board totaled Rp 765B in 2024, exceeding the token Rp 249.9B share buyback. The dividend payout has climbed from ~21% (2015) to ~72% (FY2025).

## Red Flags

- Allowance for impairment fell from 2.53% to 2.10% while impairment charges nearly doubled; provisioning charges doubled from Rp 2.0T to Rp 4.0T in FY2025 even as reported NPL improved
- Active tax litigation spanning at least three fiscal years with cumulative disputed amounts in the hundreds of billions of Rupiah
- Related-party joint financing receivables of Rp 10.6T with an unidentified counterparty
- LAR (loans-at-risk) ratio jumped from 5.3% to 6.0% in a single quarter on one textile account
- BCA Finance Hong Kong subsidiary being wound down; possible undisclosed losses
