## Business Overview

Full Truck Alliance was born from the merger of China's two largest freight-matching apps -- Huochebang ("Truck Gang," founded 2011 in Guiyang) and Yunmanman ("Cloud Full Truck," founded 2013 in Chengdu). Before these platforms existed, China's roughly 14 million heavy- and medium-duty truckers found loads by driving to logistics parks and scanning shipping orders scribbled on blackboards. Shippers navigated multiple layers of middlemen extracting 10-15% of the freight fee.

The 2017 merger, brokered by investors including SoftBank and Tencent, eliminated the most dangerous head-to-head rivalry and created a platform with an order-of-magnitude lead in long-haul, full-truckload freight matching. Today, FTA covers 100% of China's 300+ prefecture-level cities, fulfilled 236.3 million orders in FY2025 (+19.8% YoY), and commands over half of China's online freight platform market with an estimated 19.1% share of the broader freight brokerage segment.

FTA's revenue model has three layers: freight matching (transaction commissions and membership fees), freight brokerage (FTA acts as principal, earning a gross spread), and an emerging ecosystem of value-added services including truck sales, insurance, ETC, and fuel cards. The brokerage segment has been growing fastest, though at lower margins, as FTA moves from pure platform to integrated logistics intermediary.

## Bull Case

- Dominant network effects in a RMB 6T market where 91% of truckers are individual owner-operators -- the atomised supply side makes FTA's matching platform indispensable
- Fulfilled orders growing 19.8% YoY with 236.3M orders in FY2025; monetisation per order continues to increase
- Asset-light platform model with strong operating leverage: margins expanding as the platform scales
- Expansion into brokerage and value-added services (truck sales, insurance, fuel) deepens the moat and grows ARPU

## Bear Case

- Regulatory risk: China's Cyberspace Administration suspended new user registrations in July 2021 for data security review; regulatory overhang persists for Chinese tech platforms
- Brokerage business grows revenue but at lower margins, diluting overall profitability even as top-line accelerates
- Chinese macro slowdown and freight volume weakness would directly compress platform activity
- VIE structure and US-listed Chinese company governance risks remain inherent in the investment

## Competitive Position

FTA's competitive position rests on a network effect that has proven extremely difficult to replicate. The 2017 merger eliminated the only credible domestic rival at scale. Today, the platform serves 3.14 million MAU shippers and 4.48 million MAU truckers, creating a liquidity advantage where each new user makes the platform more valuable for all existing users.

The fragmented nature of China's trucking industry -- over 700,000 registered firms operating fewer than five vehicles each -- means no traditional logistics company can aggregate supply the way FTA's platform does. Barriers to entry include the cost and complexity of building a nationwide network, years of trust-building with individual truckers, and the data advantages from processing hundreds of millions of orders.

The primary competitive risks are from Manbang's former rivals attempting comebacks, potential entry by Alibaba or JD Logistics into freight matching, and the regulatory environment that could impose new restrictions on platform businesses.

## Management Assessment

CEO Zhang Hui has led the combined entity since the 2017 merger, navigating the complex integration of two competing platforms with different cultures and investor bases. The management team has successfully grown the platform while improving monetisation. Key investors include SoftBank Vision Fund, Tencent, and Sequoia Capital. The VIE structure and Cayman Islands incorporation are standard for Chinese companies listed in the US but create governance opacity for international investors.

## Red Flags

- July 2021 cybersecurity review and new user registration suspension highlighted regulatory vulnerability; China's platform economy remains subject to unpredictable government intervention
- VIE (Variable Interest Entity) structure means NYSE shareholders hold claims on a Cayman shell, not the Chinese operating entity
- Brokerage segment growth at lower margins could compress blended profitability
- Customer concentration risk: heavy reliance on SME shippers whose freight volumes are cyclically sensitive
- Competition from vertically integrated logistics players (JD Logistics, Cainiao) who may move into freight matching
