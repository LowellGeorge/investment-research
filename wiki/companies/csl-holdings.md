# CSL Holdings

> A global leader in plasma-derived therapies operating in a rational oligopoly, with a 110-year heritage and the world's largest plasma collection network, currently navigating a messy transitional period following the value-destructive Vifor acquisition, CEO departure, and a failed demerger attempt.

## Business Overview

CSL Limited traces its origins to 1916, when the Australian government established the Commonwealth Serum Laboratories to produce vaccines during World War I. After 78 years as a government-owned entity, CSL was privatised in 1994 at A$2.30 per share. Under CEO Brian McNamee (1990-2013), CSL consolidated the global plasma fractionation industry through the acquisitions of ZLB Bioplasma (2000) and Aventis Behring (2004), establishing CSL Behring as a top-tier global player.

The company operates three segments: CSL Behring (plasma-derived therapies, 72% of FY25 revenue, $11.2B), CSL Seqirus (influenza vaccines, 14%, $2.2B), and CSL Vifor (iron/nephrology, 14%, $2.2B). The immunoglobulin (Ig) franchise is the core asset, generating $6.1B in FY25 revenue with PRIVIGEN holding 25% global IVIg share and HIZENTRA 55% of SCIg. Of 900,000 PID patients in major markets, only 120,000 are on Ig therapy, creating a multi-decade demand tailwind.

The $11.7 billion Vifor acquisition (2022) has been disappointing, with $1.05 billion in after-tax impairments in H1 FY26 and multiple products entering generic erosion. CEO Paul McKenzie was fired in February 2026 -- the day before catastrophic half-year results -- with interim CEO Gordon Naylor declaring: "As an investor, I'm not prepared to accept that we can't do better." The planned Seqirus demerger was shelved after just two months.

## Bull Case

- Core Ig franchise operates in a rational oligopoly with formidable barriers; Horizon 1/2 yield programs target 20% reduction in plasma collection needs by FY32
- FCF of $3.56B (FY25) with 113% cash conversion; balance sheet deleveraging to 1.8x net debt/EBITDA
- Garadacimab (ANDEMBRY) approval opens a USD $3.6B first-in-class opportunity in hereditary angioedema

## Bear Case

- Vifor acquisition increasingly looks value-destructive: $11.7B spent, now writing down goodwill; Venofer faces generics, Injectafer loses US exclusivity mid-2026, Velphoro TDAPA reimbursement expires FY27
- Governance in disarray: CEO fired, Seqirus demerger shelved, CFO turned over twice in six months, 40%+ shareholders voted against remuneration report
- Medium-term earnings growth downgraded from "annual double-digit" (CMD 2023) to "high single-digit NPAT growth" (CMD 2025)

## Competitive Position

CSL Behring competes in a concentrated oligopoly where the top five players (CSL, Takeda, Grifols, Octapharma, Kedrion) control approximately 80% of the global plasma fractionation market. CSL's moat rests on its nearly 330 plasma collection centres (the world's largest network), the proprietary Rika plasmapheresis system delivering 10% more plasma per donation, and the Horizon yield improvement programs that extract more Ig grams per litre of plasma.

Grifols' financial distress (~EUR 8.5B net debt) paradoxically reinforces CSL's position by constraining its closest competitor. The most significant long-term threat comes from FcRn inhibitors, a new drug class treating autoimmune conditions without requiring plasma, though Ig replacement for PID has no viable non-plasma alternative. [[wiki/companies/fisher-paykel|Fisher & Paykel]] and [[wiki/companies/resmed|Resmed]] operate in adjacent healthcare segments in the ANZ region.

## Management Assessment

The rapid leadership transition is the single most significant near-term risk. Paul McKenzie's firing, combined with the Seqirus demerger reversal and dual CFO churn, has created a governance vacuum. Interim CEO Gordon Naylor brings credibility as a 33-year veteran who built CSL Plasma and Seqirus, but the permanent CEO search will take approximately one year. McKenzie's systematic liquidation of 99.7% of equity incentive awards in FY23 was a textbook red flag the board failed to address. The LTI plan structure (70% ROIC, 30% EPS growth) is well-designed, but insider ownership is thin.

## Financial Profile

- **Gross Margin:** 51.9% (FY25); expanding to 55.6% in H1 FY26
- **EBITDA Margin:** 33.1% (FY25); ~900bps above Grifols
- **ROIC:** 11.5% (FY25, up from 10.5%); depressed by Vifor goodwill
- **FCF:** $3.56B (FY25, +58%); FCF/net income conversion 113%
- **Net Debt/EBITDA:** 1.8x, declining
- **Goodwill + Intangibles:** $15.82B (40% of total assets); further impairment risk
- **Restructuring:** $700-770M in charges; $550M cost savings targeted by FY28

## Red Flags

- $15.82B in goodwill and intangibles (40% of total assets) sitting atop a Vifor franchise entering multi-product generic erosion
- CEO systematically sold 99.7%, 43.6%, and 94% of equity incentive awards across FY23-FY25 -- a pattern the board failed to address
- China albumin revenue collapsed 27% in H1 FY26 following government policy changes
- KCENTRA competitor entered at ~30% below CSL's price, signaling structural margin pressure in specialty products
- sa-mRNA technology platform has been written off entirely after being positioned as a key growth vector
