# China Resources Beer

> The world's largest beer brand by volume (Snow), operating a dominant distribution network across China with 62 breweries, executing a textbook premiumisation strategy that has expanded gross margins 800bps since 2018 -- now pivoting to capital returns as the core beer franchise enters harvest mode.

## Business Overview

China Resources Beer traces its origins to 1993, when China Resources Enterprise partnered with the Shenyang Municipal Government to acquire Shenyang Snow Brewery, launching the Snow brand. In 1994, South African Breweries (later SABMiller) took a 49% stake, contributing world-class brewing technology. Through the late 1990s and 2000s, CR Snow executed one of China's most aggressive brewery acquisition campaigns, absorbing over 70 regional breweries. By 2010, Snow became the world's best-selling beer brand by volume.

This volume-maximization strategy peaked around 2013-2014 as Chinese beer consumption began its secular decline. The SABMiller buyout in 2016 (following AB InBev's mega-merger) and the 2018 Heineken strategic alliance (US$3.1B deal granting Heineken a ~20.67% economic interest) marked the pivot from volume to value. Under architect Hou Xiaohai, the "3+3+3" nine-year transformation program shuttered dozens of inefficient breweries, launched national premium brands (SuperX, Marrs Green, Craftsmanship), and leveraged Heineken's international portfolio. Net income surged more than fivefold between FY2018 and FY2023.

The 2023 entry into baijiu (Chinese spirits) via the RMB 12.3 billion acquisition of Guizhou Jinsha Jiaojiu Winery has been a significant value destroyer, generating a CNY 2.9 billion goodwill impairment in FY2025. Meanwhile, the entire senior leadership team turned over in mid-2025 -- chairman, president, and CFO all replaced within six months through China Resources Group's SOE rotation system.

## Bull Case

- Beer premiumisation has further runway: premium and sub-premium now exceed 50% of volume, up from ~30% a few years ago; gross margins expanded 800bps to 42.5% since 2018
- Core beer franchise entering harvest mode: capex below depreciation, FCF of CNY 7.1B, dividend payout ratio raised from 20% to 53%, net cash position of CNY 4.2B
- Heineken partnership provides premium brand portfolio and commercially-minded governance counterweight to SOE management; distribution network of 3M+ retail outlets is an unreplicable moat

## Bear Case

- Complete senior leadership turnover in six months: chairman, president, and CFO all replaced through SOE rotation; new president has audit/discipline background, not commercial
- Jinsha baijiu acquisition (RMB 12.3B) has been value-destructive: CNY 2.9B goodwill impairment, EBITDA collapsed 69% to just CNY 264M; top-of-cycle acquisition in a category outside core competence
- Overall beer consumption in China in secular decline since 2013-2014 peak; macroeconomic weakness can reverse premiumisation as consumers trade down

## Competitive Position

China's beer industry is one of the world's most consolidated consumer staples markets, with the top five players commanding approximately 92-93% of national volume. CR Beer holds the #1 position with an estimated 23-31% national share, operating 62 breweries across 25 provinces. Its distribution network reaching 3M+ retail outlets is perhaps its most durable competitive advantage -- assembled over decades of aggressive M&A and not easily replicable.

The dual brand architecture -- Snow for mass and sub-premium, Heineken/Amstel for premium and super-premium -- allows CR Beer to capture consumers across the entire price ladder. Budweiser Brewing APAC is the most formidable rival, claiming approximately 40% volume share in the premium segment through Budweiser, Corona, and Stella Artois. Tsingtao Brewery holds roughly 16% share. The oligopolistic structure limits price-destructive competition and raises barriers to entry. New entry at scale is effectively impossible given capital requirements, regulatory complexity, and entrenched distributor relationships.

## Management Assessment

The rapid triple turnover at the top is the most significant governance risk. Departing chairman Hou Xiaohai was the architect of CR Beer's premiumisation transformation, and his loss is a genuine reduction in institutional knowledge. New president Jin Hanquan comes from an audit and discipline background within China Resources Holdings -- a signal that the parent SOE is prioritising governance over commercial strategy. Executive directors hold negligible personal equity stakes, typical for Chinese SOE subsidiaries. Heineken's 40% stake in the holding company provides a commercially minded counterweight, and this structural positive distinguishes CR Beer from many mainland peers. On beer, management has consistently delivered on premiumisation targets; on baijiu, the track record is poor.

## Financial Profile

- **Beer Gross Margin:** 42.5% (FY2025, up from ~35% in FY2018); converging toward Budweiser APAC (~51.5%)
- **Beer EBITDA Margin:** 26.3% (+3.9pp YoY)
- **Consolidated Net Income:** CNY 3.17B (depressed by CNY 2.9B Jinsha impairment)
- **OCF:** CNY 7.13B; capex below depreciation (harvest mode)
- **Balance Sheet:** Net cash CNY 4.2B; debt-to-equity near zero
- **Dividends:** Payout ratio raised from 20% to 53%; DPS grew 34% in FY2025
- **ROE:** 10.5% reported (impairment-depressed); ~15-17% underlying

## Red Flags

- CNY 13.9B in goodwill (39% of equity), concentrated in the poorly performing Jinsha baijiu acquisition; further impairment risk if the baijiu downturn persists
- Complete C-suite turnover within six months raises questions about strategic continuity; interim chairman position technically remains vacant
- As a subsidiary of China Resources Holdings (Fortune Global 500 SOE), priorities may diverge from minority shareholders at critical junctures
- Chinese beer volumes in secular decline since 2013-2014 peak; premiumisation thesis depends on continued consumer upgrade in a weak macro environment
- Baijiu segment revenue contracted 30% in FY2025 to CNY 1.5B; the "beer + baijiu" dual-empowerment model remains unproven
