# Regional S.A.B.

> A high-quality, family-controlled Mexican commercial bank with a 30-year track record of credit discipline, serving underbanked SMEs in northern Mexico, now at an inflection point as NIM compression and the Hey Banco digital spin-off reshape the growth profile.

## Business Overview

Regional S.A.B. de C.V. operates as a financial holding company headquartered in San Pedro Garza Garcia, Nuevo Leon. Its primary subsidiary, Banco Regional de Monterrey (Banregio), is a commercial bank focused on serving mid-market and SME clients across Mexico's northern industrial heartland. The bank was founded on February 14, 1994, by the Rivero Santos brothers, whose grandfather had established Banco Regional del Norte in 1947 before bank nationalization in 1982 extinguished the original franchise.

The business model is deliberately narrow and relationship-driven. Over 77-80% of the loan book is directed at SMEs, with a Concierge 360 model assigning a single relationship manager to cover a client's business, family, and personal net worth. This focus has produced consistently superior asset quality: an NPL ratio of 1.3% and cost of risk of 1.0%, roughly half the Mexican banking system average. By early 2026, Regional operates as a four-subsidiary conglomerate: Banco Regional (79% of net income), Start Banregio (14%), Inmobiliaria Banregio (5%), and the newly independent Hey Banco (1-2%).

Hey Banco, launched as a fully independent digital bank on January 31, 2026, represents the most consequential strategic pivot. Incubated inside Banregio for roughly thirteen years, it targets mass-market individuals that the branch model cannot serve economically. Its NIM expanded to 10.0% in Q4 2025, though the active customer base of 476,000 fell short of the original one-million target.

## Bull Case

- Deposit franchise is genuinely durable: five-year deposit CAGR of 12% versus 6% for the system, with cost of funding at 4.32% versus peers at 5.5-6.6%
- Mexico remains structurally underbanked, and Regional holds only 3.4% of commercial loans, leaving significant share-gain runway
- ROE of 19.1% at ~7.7x P/E and ~1.35x P/B offers compelling value for a well-managed bank
- Hey Banco spin-off could unlock a 50% dividend payout ratio (up from 25%) and create an independent IPO candidate
- Conservative balance sheet: Tier 1 capitalization of 15.0%, negligible long-term debt, loans-to-deposits at 100.2%

## Bear Case

- NIM compressed from 6.6% to 6.3% across 2025, with guidance of 6.0-6.3% for 2026 as Banxico continues easing
- Real estate concentration: management acknowledged "almost half" the loan book is real estate-related when indirect linkages are included
- Guidance credibility damaged: original 2025 NI growth guidance of 10-15% was delivered at approximately 1.5%
- Operating leverage running in reverse: efficiency ratio worsened from 39.5% to 42.2% as expenses grew 17% against 7% revenue growth
- USMCA renegotiation risk is unquantified but could directly impact the northern Mexico industrial economy that constitutes Regional's core customer base

## Competitive Position

The Mexican banking system is heavily concentrated. BBVA, Banorte, and Santander hold roughly 60% of commercial loans. Regional sits in the second tier alongside Banco del Bajio and Inbursa, competing not on scale but on relationship depth and underwriting quality in regional markets. The deposit franchise is the most durable competitive advantage, with wholesale demand deposits growing 42% YoY in Q4 2025.

Digital banks represent the most-discussed but least-demonstrated competitive threat. Management stated in Q4 2025 that "competition from digital banks [is] not materially affecting deposit base or cost of funds." Revolut's Mexico entry remains limited, Plata is nascent, and Banorte's digital experiment Bineo was quietly sold.

Regional's dual-platform architecture -- traditional branch banking plus a fully digital bank -- is unusual among Mexican mid-cap banks and creates optionality that peers like [[wiki/companies/credicorp|Credicorp]] and [[wiki/companies/nu-holdings|Nu Holdings]] approach from different angles.

## Management Assessment

The Rivero family has been in Mexican banking since 1947. CEO Manuel Rivero Zambrano, the fourth generation, has led the bank since 2015 with a measured, commercially precise communication style. Aggregate insider ownership of approximately 24% aligns management with shareholders. CFO Enrique Navarro Ramirez demonstrates deep technical command. The primary governance risk is the absence of a public succession plan and the concentration of the Chairman, Honorary Chairman, and CEO roles within a single family.

## Financial Profile

- **NIM:** 6.3% (FY2025), guided 6.0-6.3% for 2026
- **ROE:** 19.1% (FY2025), guided 18-19% for 2026
- **NPL Ratio:** 1.3% (cost of risk 1.0%)
- **Efficiency Ratio:** 42.2%
- **FCF Conversion:** ~121% (MXN 8.0B FCF on MXN 6.6B net income)
- **Capitalization:** Tier 1 at 15.0%; negligible financial debt
- **Valuation:** ~7.7x P/E, ~1.35x P/B

## Red Flags

- Q4 NPL improvement from 1.6% to 1.3% depended on exactly two account resolutions, with write-offs doubling and coverage ratio declining from 167.7% to 130.2%
- Stage 2 commercial loans grew 21% YoY, signaling a pipeline of potential future deterioration
- 2026 ROE guidance range of 8-19% -- an 1,100 basis point band -- is effectively a disclaimer rather than guidance
- FX fee income fell 24% YoY in Q4 following undisclosed tightening of foreign exchange controls to comply with U.S. regulation
