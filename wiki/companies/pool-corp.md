# POOL Corp

> The world's largest wholesale distributor of swimming pool supplies, commanding ~40% of a fragmented market with the next competitor at ~5%, generating 30%+ ROIC from a non-discretionary maintenance annuity tied to 5.7 million in-ground pools in the United States.

## Business Overview

Pool Corporation traces its origins to a 1993 leveraged buyout of South Central Pool Supply in Covington, Louisiana. The company executed an aggressive roll-up strategy through the late 1990s and 2000s, acquiring regional distributors and building route density that became virtually unassailable. Under CEO Manuel Perez de la Mesa (1999-2019), POOL expanded internationally into Canada, Europe, and Australia, and diversified adjacently through acquisitions in irrigation and landscape supply (Horizon Group, 2007) and hardscape and tile (National Pool Tile, 2008).

The business model is built on the installed base of approximately 5.7 million in-ground pools in the United States, each requiring $1,200-$1,500 annually in chemicals, replacement parts, and equipment -- expenditures that are largely non-discretionary, recession-resistant, and recurring. This maintenance annuity represents roughly 60% of revenue, with renovation/remodel at ~20% and new construction at ~20%. POOL distributes through 420+ sales centers serving ~120,000 contractor, builder, and retail customers.

The COVID-19 pandemic proved an unexpected accelerant, with revenues surging from $3.2 billion in 2019 to approximately $6.2 billion by 2022. While new construction has since normalized, the pools built during the boom permanently enlarged the recurring maintenance revenue base.

## Bull Case

- Dominant distribution position: ~40% market share vs ~5% for the next competitor; 420+ sales centers creating unreplicable route density
- Non-discretionary maintenance annuity (~60% of revenue) from 5.7M pools provides a defensive, recurring revenue floor
- Exceptional capital efficiency: 30%+ ROIC consistently, capex at ~1% of revenue, FCF conversion exceeding 90%
- Structural moat from 200,000 SKUs, scale-driven vendor rebates, contractor switching costs (trade credit, POOL360 platform), and hazmat shipping barriers
- Long reinvestment runway through greenfield locations, tuck-in acquisitions, and adjacent categories (outdoor living, irrigation)

## Bear Case

- Post-COVID normalization: new pool construction volumes down 30-40% from peak; earnings have declined or stagnated since 2022
- New construction and remodeling (~40% of revenue) are cyclical and discretionary, tightly correlated with housing turnover and financing costs
- Buybacks at 2021-2022 peak valuations at premium multiples on peak earnings represent permanent capital misallocation
- Weather risk is non-trivial and unhedgeable: Q2 typically generates 30-35% of annual revenue
- Historical valuation of 25-35x earnings demands continued execution; multiple compression alone could drive 30-40% downside

## Competitive Position

POOL operates in a structurally attractive position. The competitive set includes SRS Distribution (now owned by Home Depot), which focuses on roofing and building products rather than pool supply. Hayward Holdings has periodically explored direct-to-dealer models but risks alienating the distribution channel. Leslie's serves primarily the DIY consumer at the retail level, not the professional contractor.

The Amazon and Home Depot disintermediation risk is mitigated by the service-intensive nature of the business: pool chemicals are hazardous materials with shipping restrictions, equipment requires technical sizing expertise, and professional contractors value speed, credit, and knowledgeable counter staff. No new entrant has achieved meaningful national scale in decades. Barriers to entry are substantial and rising.

## Management Assessment

Peter Arvan succeeded Perez de la Mesa in 2019 after a career at HD Supply and Hubbell. The transition has been largely seamless, with Arvan maintaining the disciplined operating philosophy while layering on technology investments (POOL360 platform). Perez de la Mesa remains on the board with a substantial personal stake worth several hundred million dollars. Compensation is tied to operating income, EPS growth, and ROIC thresholds. The company has a strong track record of meeting or exceeding guidance, with management tending toward conservatism in initial outlooks. Share count has been reduced 25-30% over the past decade while dividends have grown at double-digit rates.

## Red Flags

- Revenue nearly doubled from 2019 to 2022; the critical question is how much was structural vs. pull-forward demand
- New pool construction permits have fallen 30-40% from peak; no guarantee the floor has been reached
- Buyback spending in 2021-2022 at elevated prices represents a permanent misallocation that will not be recovered
- Potential long-term risk from major manufacturers (Pentair, Hayward) exploring direct or hybrid distribution models
- The correlation of cyclical risks (housing downturn, elevated rates, unfavorable weather, post-COVID normalization) could compress earnings 40-50% from peak
