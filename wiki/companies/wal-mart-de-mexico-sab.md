# Wal Mart de Mexico SAB

> Latin America's largest retailer and the most profitable Walmart subsidiary globally, operating 3,900+ stores across Mexico and Central America with a widening competitive moat driven by ecosystem businesses (Bait telecom, Walmart Connect advertising, financial services) -- though a profit-revenue divergence in FY2025 and a decade-long COFECE antitrust ruling introduce near-term earnings uncertainty.

## Business Overview

WALMEX traces its origins to 1958, when Cifra was founded as one of Mexico's pioneering modern retailers. Walmart entered the Mexican market through a joint venture with Cifra in 1991, eventually acquiring full control and rebranding. Today WALMEX operates approximately 3,900+ stores across Mexico and Central America (Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Costa Rica, and Nicaragua) through multiple formats: Bodega Aurrera (discount/value), Walmart Supercenter, Sam's Club, and Superama/Mi Bodega. Mexico accounts for roughly 85% of revenue, with Central America contributing the balance.

What distinguishes WALMEX from a typical emerging-market retailer is its ecosystem strategy. The company has built an interconnected platform of services that deepens customer engagement and creates layers of competitive advantage no domestic peer can replicate. Bait, its mobile virtual network operator (MVNO), reached 23.5 million users, making WALMEX one of Mexico's largest telecom providers by subscriber count. Walmart Connect, its retail media advertising network, grew 27% in FY2025. Financial services credit disbursements surged 139%. The e-commerce business continued to scale. These ecosystem businesses generate higher-margin revenue and increase customer stickiness beyond traditional grocery retail.

WALMEX outperformed ANTAD (National Association of Department and Self-Service Stores) self-service same-store sales for ten consecutive quarters through Q3 2025, with the competitive gap reaching 280 basis points. Price perception improved 260 basis points year-over-year. However, FY2025 revealed a concerning profit-revenue divergence: revenue grew 5.5% but net income declined 7.3%, driven by a higher effective tax rate, adverse SG&A trends (rising from 16.5% to 17.2% before moderating), and negative operating cash flow of MXN 4.4 billion in Q1 2025. Management deferred all 2026 guidance to a future investor day, signalling genuine near-term uncertainty.

## Bull Case

- Latin America's largest retailer with a widening moat: 10 consecutive quarters of same-store sales outperformance versus the industry, with the gap increasing
- Ecosystem businesses (Bait 23.5M users, Walmart Connect +27%, financial services +139%) create structural competitive advantages that no Mexican peer can replicate
- Mexico's structural tailwinds: nearshoring/friendshoring driving FDI, young demographics, rising formalisation of the economy, and underpenetrated modern retail
- Price perception improving 260bps YoY demonstrates growing consumer mindshare; WALMEX is winning on value
- 3% dividend yield with manageable leverage and consistent capital returns provides a floor

## Bear Case

- Profit-revenue divergence in FY2025: net income fell 7.3% on 5.5% revenue growth, with SG&A trending adversely and Q1 operating cash flow negative
- COFECE antitrust ruling structurally constrains WALMEX's supplier negotiating leverage for a decade, potentially eroding a core cost advantage
- Management deferred all 2026 guidance to a future investor day -- unusual and suggestive of internal uncertainty
- Central America remains a persistent operational drag with lower margins and less favourable competitive dynamics
- Currency risk: peso depreciation versus USD compresses dollar-denominated returns for foreign investors
- FY2025 revenue growth of 5.5% missed the 6-7% guidance range

## Competitive Position

WALMEX dominates Mexican retail with a scale advantage that is functionally unreachable. Its closest competitors -- Soriana, Chedraui, and La Comer -- operate at a fraction of WALMEX's store count, revenue base, and technology investment. The Bodega Aurrera format is the anchor of WALMEX's value proposition, capturing the price-sensitive mass market that represents the bulk of Mexican consumer spending. Sam's Club provides the membership warehouse format targeting middle-class and small business customers.

The ecosystem strategy is the key differentiator. No Mexican competitor has anything resembling the Bait telecom network, Walmart Connect advertising platform, or integrated financial services offering. These create a flywheel: customers visit more frequently, spend more per visit, and become harder to poach for competitors. The COFECE antitrust ruling, which limits WALMEX's supplier negotiation tactics for a decade, is a structural constraint worth monitoring -- it could erode the supplier leverage that has historically been a core cost advantage. Among investment peers, [[wiki/companies/alsea-s-a-b|Alsea S.A.B.]] operates in Mexican consumer discretionary, [[wiki/companies/regional-s-a-b|Regional S.A.B.]] provides exposure to Mexican financial services, and [[wiki/companies/qualitas-controladora|Qualitas Controladora]] offers another blue-chip Mexican franchise.

## Management Assessment

CEO Guilherme Loureiro leads a management team that benefits from Walmart's global best practices in retail operations, supply chain management, and technology deployment. The Walmart parentage (owning approximately 70.1% of WALMEX) provides access to the world's largest retailer's infrastructure and know-how but also means minority shareholders are subordinate to Walmart Inc.'s strategic priorities. Management's track record includes successfully building the ecosystem businesses from scratch, maintaining consistent same-store sales outperformance, and delivering operational execution across 3,900+ stores in six countries. The deferral of 2026 guidance is the primary mark against recent communication quality, and the FY2025 profit shortfall raises questions about cost management during a period of decelerating top-line growth.
