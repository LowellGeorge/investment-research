## Business Overview

H World Group, formerly known as Huazhu Group until its 2022 rebrand, was founded in 2005 by serial entrepreneur Ji Qi, who previously co-founded Ctrip and founded Home Inns. The company began as a single economy hotel brand (HanTing) in Kunshan, Jiangsu Province, and listed on NASDAQ in 2010. Ji Qi brought a technology-first mindset to hospitality, applying platform economics to an industry dominated by fragmented, unbranded independents. This founder DNA -- combining tech-sector operational discipline with bricks-and-mortar hotel management -- has defined the company for two decades.

The business has undergone three major strategic shifts: first, expanding from economy hotels into a multi-brand portfolio spanning economy (HanTing, Hi Inn), midscale (JI Hotel, Orange, Crystal Orange), upscale (Mercure, Novotel), and luxury (Steigenberger) through organic brand creation, acquisitions, and a pivotal Accor joint venture signed in 2014. Second, a decisive transition from leased-and-operated properties to a managed-and-franchised (M&F) model, with M&F now accounting for 92% of rooms and 69% of gross operating profit. Third, the EUR 700 million acquisition of Deutsche Hospitality in 2019, which gave H World a European footprint and luxury brand portfolio -- though integration has been challenging, particularly given its timing just before COVID-19.

Today, H World operates the largest hotel network in China with approximately 1.26 million rooms. Its proprietary technology platform, 300+ million member loyalty program generating 74% of room nights, and central reservation system create a powerful franchisee value proposition. The company completed a secondary listing on HKEX in 2020 and has returned over USD 760 million to shareholders in FY2025 as part of a USD 2 billion three-year capital return program.

## Bull Case

- Enormous consolidation runway: branded hotels represent only ~35% of China's room supply vs. 70%+ in the US, providing decades of growth
- Asset-light franchise model drives expanding margins: M&F revenue grew 23.1% in FY2025, operating margins reached a record 26.9%
- Powerful loyalty flywheel: 300M+ members driving 74% direct bookings, reducing OTA dependency and franchisee acquisition costs
- Fortress balance sheet: RMB 15.4B cash, RMB 9.6B net cash, near-100% cash conversion
- Management executing ahead of plan on capital returns (75%+ of USD 2B TSR program completed)
- Network effects compound as each new hotel makes the loyalty program more valuable to members

## Bear Case

- VIE structure means investors own contractual claims in a Cayman entity, not the operating business in China
- RevPAR declined 3-4% in 2025 -- growth is coming from unit additions, not pricing power
- Concentrated exposure to Chinese consumer macro: property crisis, youth unemployment, and deflationary pressures
- Deutsche Hospitality remains an underperforming drag with potential goodwill impairment risk
- Founder Ji Qi controls via dual-class shares with no disclosed succession plan
- HFCAA and PCAOB risks remain a structural overhang for the NASDAQ listing

## Competitive Position

H World's competitive position rests on an interlocking set of advantages that are difficult to replicate. The loyalty program, technology platform, and centralized procurement system create a franchisee value proposition superior to that of independent operators and smaller chains. Each new property in the network enhances the loyalty program's value, while higher occupancy from direct bookings reduces franchisee costs -- a self-reinforcing flywheel. The company holds the distinction of operating HanTing, the world's largest single hotel brand by room count.

Key domestic competitors include Jinjiang International (comparable scale but more fragmented brand portfolio), BTG Homeinns, GreenTree Hospitality, and the fast-growing Atour Lifestyle Holdings in upper-midscale. Internationally, the model draws comparison to Marriott and Hilton, both of which operate similar asset-light franchise platforms. The most credible competitive threat comes not from traditional hotel chains but from platform disruption: [[wiki/companies/trip-com.com|Trip.com]] and Meituan could deepen integration with independent hotels, potentially narrowing the gap between branded chains and independents. Barriers to entry at the individual property level are moderate, but building a 12,700+ hotel network with integrated technology and a 300 million member loyalty program requires decades and billions of cumulative investment.

## Management Assessment

The founder-CEO partnership between Executive Chairman Ji Qi and CEO Jenny Zhang has proven durable and effective. Zhang has led the company for over a decade, overseeing the transformation from economy-hotel operator to multi-brand platform, the asset-light conversion, and the Deutsche Hospitality integration. Ji Qi remains the largest individual shareholder with significant skin in the game through dual-class shares, creating strong alignment. Management has demonstrated disciplined capital allocation: the TSR program is running ahead of schedule, and the company has avoided further large-scale M&A after Deutsche Hospitality. Earnings call communication is measured, data-driven, and conservative -- guidance has been consistently under-promised and over-delivered. The primary governance concern is key-person concentration and the absence of a disclosed succession plan.

## Red Flags

- VIE structure introduces unquantifiable legal and regulatory risk
- RevPAR declining despite revenue growth -- classic treadmill risk if macro worsens
- Deutsche Hospitality goodwill may require impairment if European operations continue to underperform
- 2018 data breach exposing 130 million customer records raised cybersecurity concerns
- Related-party transactions with founder-linked entities appear in filings
- CFO transition underway (Hui Chen to Arthur Yu as of March 2026)
