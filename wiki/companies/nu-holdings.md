# Nu Holdings

> The world's largest digital bank by customer count (131 million), disrupting Latin America's concentrated banking oligopoly with a branchless, AI-driven platform delivering a 19.9% efficiency ratio and 33% ROE while spending just $0.90 per month to serve each customer.

## Business Overview

Nu Holdings was co-founded in 2013 by David Velez, a Colombian-born Stanford MBA and former Sequoia Capital partner, alongside Cristina Junqueira (ex-Itau product manager) and Edward Wible (CTO). The company launched with a single product -- a no-annual-fee credit card managed entirely through a mobile app -- as a wedge into Brazil's concentrated banking oligopoly, where five banks controlled 70-85% of all loans, deposits, and revenue while extracting enormous fees.

The critical inflection came in 2017 with NuConta (later NuAccount), which transformed Nubank from a credit card issuer into a deposit-taking institution. By FY2025, Nu serves 131 million customers across Brazil (113M), Mexico (14M), and Colombia (4M), offering credit cards, personal loans, savings accounts, investments, insurance, and mobile telecom. The company operates on a cloud-native, microservices architecture built on Clojure, with a proprietary nuFormer AI credit-risk model trained on 131 million customer relationships.

Nu's structural advantage is cost: a 19.9% efficiency ratio versus Itau's 38.8% and Bradesco's ~42%. Customer acquisition cost of ~$5 versus incumbents' $91-$115. Cost to serve of $0.90/month versus incumbents' $15-25. These economics are enabled by a branchless model with zero physical infrastructure. In December 2021, Nu listed on the NYSE at ~$9/share, achieving a $41 billion valuation. Berkshire Hathaway invested $500 million pre-IPO.

## Bull Case

- Structural cost advantage (19.9% efficiency ratio) creates an operating leverage gap incumbents cannot close without dismantling physical infrastructure
- Massive monetization runway: 30% of primary banking relationships but only 5% of industry gross profit; mature cohorts trending toward $27 ARPAC vs incumbent $43.30
- 33% ROE achieved in just three years from breakeven, with $5.2B in excess capital above regulatory requirements
- AI-driven underwriting (nuFormer) improves with scale, creating a data moat across 131M customer relationships
- Forward P/E of ~16x for a business growing revenue 40%+ implies PEG well below 1x

## Bear Case

- 90+ day NPL ratio of 6.5-6.8% is roughly 3.5x Itau's rate; customer base is more exposed to Brazilian macro than incumbents
- Gross margin compressed 500bps in a single year (45.6% to 40.6%) as credit portfolio seasons
- US market entry via OCC bank charter means competing in the world's most competitive retail banking market while Mexico and Colombia remain unprofitable
- Dual-class structure: Velez controls ~76% of voting power with ~20% economic interest; Cayman Islands incorporation offers weaker minority protections
- "Adjusted Net Income" strips out stock-based compensation; quarterly one-off items consistently flatter reported results

## Competitive Position

Brazilian banking has long been dominated by Itau Unibanco, Banco do Brasil, Bradesco, Caixa Economica Federal, and Santander Brasil, which collectively control ~65% of system assets. Nu has reached 62% of Brazil's adult population by customer count but captures only 5% of the gross profit pool -- the gap between reach and revenue extraction defines both the opportunity and the challenge.

[[wiki/companies/credicorp|Credicorp]] and other Latin American incumbents face similar digital disruption dynamics. Digital challengers include Inter & Co (43M customers), C6 Bank (JPMorgan-backed), and PicPay. MercadoLibre's Mercado Pago (72M monthly active users) represents a differentiated threat with embedded commerce data. [[wiki/companies/pt-bank-central-asia|PT Bank Central Asia]] in Indonesia offers a comparable case study of franchise banking in an emerging market.

Nu's moat rests on cost advantage, data-driven underwriting, brand trust (NPS above 90), and platform breadth creating cross-selling density and rising switching costs.

## Management Assessment

Velez's alignment is exceptional: ~20% economic stake worth $12-13 billion, voluntary forfeiture of a $423M performance award during the post-IPO downturn, and 91% of employees holding equity incentives. CFO Guilherme Lago has driven the efficiency ratio from ~90% at IPO to sub-20%. The departure of COO Youssef Lahrech in May 2025 concentrates operational authority in Velez -- a development worth monitoring. Management has delivered on every major commitment: customer growth from 48M to 131M, net losses to $2.87B net income, and SBC net burn rate below 100bps.

## Red Flags

- Regulatory changes to FGTS reduced secured loan originations by 50-60% in Q4 2025 overnight
- Mexico implementing interchange fee caps that threaten unit economics of new customer acquisition
- Purchasing third-party secured loan portfolios ($181.8M in Q1, $218.9M in Q3) rather than originating organically
- Brazilian Real depreciated from R$4.86/USD to R$6.17/USD during FY2024; a Real crisis would devastate the stock
- Foreign private issuer exemption from NYSE governance rules and Cayman Islands incorporation create governance gaps
