# Trip.com Group

> China's dominant online travel agency and the world's fourth-largest OTA by revenue, operating a multi-brand platform (Ctrip, Trip.com, Qunar, Skyscanner) with 80%+ gross margins, a fortress balance sheet of RMB 106B in cash, and a rapidly growing international business now at 40% of revenue -- trading at 8-9x earnings versus 22-32x for Western peers.

## Business Overview

Trip.com Group was founded in 1999 by James Liang, Neil Shen (later co-founder of Sequoia Capital China), Min Fan, and Qi Ji. The company built its early dominance through a hybrid "click and mortar" model combining an online booking interface with a massive call centre, securing an IPO on NASDAQ in December 2003. After a destructive price war from 2013-2015, the company executed decisive consolidation: acquiring eLong from Expedia (2015) and merging with Baidu-backed Qunar through a landmark share swap (October 2015), which gave it an estimated 70-80% share of China's online hotel and air booking market.

Internationalisation became the next chapter. The 2016 acquisition of Skyscanner for GBP 1.4 billion, the 2017 purchase of the Trip.com brand, and a 2019 share exchange giving Trip.com the largest shareholder position in India's MakeMyTrip together created a multi-brand, multi-geography platform. The company rebranded from Ctrip to Trip.com Group in 2019. Today it operates four segments: accommodation reservation (42% of FY2025 revenue, +21% YoY), transportation ticketing (36%, +11%), packaged tours (8%), and corporate travel (4%), with "Others" including advertising and financial services at 10% (+38%). International revenue crossed 40% of total in FY2025, up from under 20% pre-pandemic.

The platform model generates 80.6% gross margins, 25.3% operating margins, and 30%+ free cash flow margins. The company sits on RMB 105.9 billion ($15.1B) in cash against RMB 30.8B in debt, and has authorised a $5 billion share repurchase programme. AI tools like TripGenie (deployed in 200+ countries, driving 50% increases in session duration) and Trip.Planner are central to the company's technology strategy.

## Bull Case

- Dominant 56% GMV share of China's OTA market with powerful network effects among 600,000+ hotel partners and deeply entrenched switching costs from loyalty programmes
- International revenue growing 60% YoY and crossing 40% of total -- a structural transformation with years of runway as APAC online travel penetration lags Western markets
- 80.6% gross margins, 30%+ FCF margins, and capital-light model with minimal CapEx (~1.1% of revenue) place it among the most cash-generative internet platforms globally
- Fortress balance sheet: RMB 106B cash, net cash position exceeding 30% of market cap, providing extraordinary cushion and optionality
- At 8-9x trailing earnings versus Booking at 22x and Airbnb at 32x, the valuation embeds extreme pessimism relative to operational quality

## Bear Case

- SAMR anti-monopoly investigation (disclosed January 2026) forced shutdown of AI price adjustment tool -- the company's core hotel monetisation mechanism; potential multi-billion RMB fine based on the Alibaba precedent
- Securities class action filed alleging Trip.com concealed antitrust regulatory timelines; co-founders Min Fan and Qi Ji departed the board contemporaneously with the investigation
- VIE structure with RMB 106B cash largely trapped behind PRC capital controls; HFCAA delisting risk persists
- Operating cash flow declined 3.6% YoY to RMB 18.9B despite 17% revenue growth -- a divergence demanding investigation
- FY2025 headline net income of RMB 33.4B is misleading: RMB 20B (60%) came from investment gains, not operations

## Competitive Position

The global OTA market is an oligopoly: Booking Holdings, Expedia, Airbnb, and Trip.com account for 96% of top-tier OTA revenue. In China specifically, Trip.com holds approximately 56% GMV share, more than three times any individual rival. The competitive landscape divides geographically: Booking Holdings (~$24B revenue) dominates Europe and is expanding into APAC with $700M+ in elevated 2026 investment; Meituan competes in budget domestic hotels using its food-delivery cross-sell; and ByteDance's Douyin launched into hotels with 40% discounts and billion-yuan subsidies in 2025, triggering a summer price war.

Trip.com's moat rests on network effects (600,000+ high-star hotel partnerships creating the broadest premium inventory), switching costs from loyalty programmes, proprietary data from two decades of booking history powering AI tools, and structural cost advantages from AI customer service handling 65%+ of inquiries. The primary disruptive threat comes from content-to-commerce platforms like Douyin, though their actual OTA market share remains small. [[wiki/companies/huazhu-group|Huazhu Group]] is a major hotel chain partner in China, while [[wiki/companies/sea-ltd|Sea Ltd]] competes in Southeast Asian digital services.

## Management Assessment

Executive Chairman James Liang (co-founder, Stanford PhD in economics, 5.3% ownership worth ~$1.7B) provides strategic vision and intellectual depth, including published research on demographics and a $500M Genovation Foundation. CEO Jane Sun (with the company since 2005, CEO since 2016) brings a Morgan Stanley and KPMG background and has delivered consistent execution through the post-COVID recovery. CFO Cindy Wang (12+ years in the role, with Trip.com since 2001) provides institutional memory and financial discipline. Management has delivered on qualitative guidance consistently: 60% international growth and 100%+ inbound growth sustained quarterly throughout 2025. The key concern is transparency: the securities class action alleges concealment of antitrust regulatory risk, and the unexplained departure of two co-founders from the board raises governance questions. No visible succession plan exists below the Liang-Sun level.

## Financial Profile

- **Gross Margin:** 80.6% (FY2025) -- asset-light platform model
- **Operating Margin:** 25.3% (FY2025), contracting from 26.7% due to international reinvestment
- **Free Cash Flow:** ~RMB 18.9B operating cash flow; >29% FCF margin
- **ROE:** ~21% (GAAP, distorted by investment gains); operating ROIC ~14%
- **Balance Sheet:** Net cash ~RMB 75B; cash of RMB 105.9B vs. debt of RMB 30.8B
- **Buybacks:** $5B programme authorised; $200M in dividends paid in 2025
- **Valuation:** ~8-9x trailing P/E; dramatic discount to Booking (22x), Airbnb (32x)

## Red Flags

- SAMR anti-monopoly investigation is the defining near-term risk; AI pricing tool forced shutdown directly weakens pricing power in the 42%-of-revenue accommodation segment
- Securities class action (class period April 2024 to January 2026) alleges material concealment of regulatory risk
- Two co-founders (Min Fan and Qi Ji) resigned from the board in February 2026 contemporaneously with the investigation
- 60% of FY2025 reported net income came from investment gains, not operations; operating cash flow actually declined YoY
- VIE structure with significant cash trapped behind PRC capital controls
- Depreciation and amortization reportedly surged roughly fourfold from FY2024 levels with insufficient explanation
- Goodwill has never been impaired despite COVID-era travel collapse lasting three years
