## Business Overview

Credicorp Ltd. is Peru's largest financial services holding company, incorporated in Bermuda and listed on the NYSE since 1995. Its flagship subsidiary, Banco de Credito del Peru (BCP), traces its origins to 1889 when a group of Italian merchants founded Banco Italiano in Lima. BCP commands approximately 36% of Peruvian banking system assets and 46% of industry profits, making it the country's undisputed financial champion.

The group operates through purpose-built subsidiaries: BCP for universal banking, Mibanco for microfinance (Latin America's second-largest), Pacifico for insurance, Prima AFP for pension fund management, and Atlantic Security Holding for wealth management. The Romero family, founders of BCP's modern incarnation, maintains approximately 12% direct ownership plus a further 15.5% through Atlantic Security Holding Corporation.

Yape, BCP's mobile payments platform with 15.9 million monthly active users in a country of 34 million, has evolved from a customer acquisition tool into a genuine profit centre. Revenue per MAU has tripled since early 2023, with lending now constituting 23% of platform revenue. The Investor Day 2025 materials reveal Yape processes six times more transactions than its nearest rival, positioning it as national payments infrastructure rather than a fintech experiment.

## Bull Case

- Low-cost deposit franchise (61% of funding) produces a 6.3% NIM that competitors cannot replicate without decades of retail banking infrastructure
- Yape's transition from acquisition tool to profit centre is accelerating faster than the market appreciates, with lending now 23% of platform revenue
- Peru's structural financial inclusion tailwind: 50%+ of the adult population remains underbanked, providing a multi-decade growth runway
- ROE consistently above 18%, with disciplined capital allocation and growing dividends

## Bear Case

- Peru's political instability creates macro overhang -- the country has had multiple presidents in recent years and social unrest remains a risk
- Concentration risk: BCP still represents 76.1% of total assets; Peruvian macro shock would hit the franchise disproportionately
- Microfinance asset quality (Mibanco) is inherently volatile and can deteriorate rapidly in economic downturns
- Chile expansion via Tenpo neobank and US cross-border banking are unproven growth vectors with execution risk

## Competitive Position

Credicorp's competitive position in Peru is structurally entrenched and widening. BCP's 36% asset share and 46% profit share reflect a low-cost deposit franchise that took decades to build. The 61% deposit-funded balance sheet generates NIMs that are structurally higher than competitors who rely on wholesale funding.

Yape has established network effects that are extremely difficult to replicate -- with 15.9 million MAUs in a 34-million-person country, it has become Peru's default digital payments infrastructure. The competitive moat compounds across the group: BCP cross-sells insurance (Pacifico), pensions (Prima AFP), and microfinance (Mibanco) through shared customer data and distribution.

Domestic competitors include Scotiabank Peru, BBVA Peru, and Interbank, but none approach BCP's scale or deposit franchise depth. The more interesting competitive dynamic is whether Yape can expand regionally, where it would face established players in each market.

## Management Assessment

The Romero family's continued 27%+ economic interest (direct plus ASHC holdings) provides strong alignment with minority shareholders. Management has delivered consistently on its Investor Day targets and communicates transparently through detailed quarterly earnings calls and strategic presentations. The succession from founding-family leadership to professional management has been executed smoothly over decades, a rarity in Latin American conglomerates.

## Red Flags

- Peru's chronic political instability creates recurring macro shocks that can compress multiples regardless of company quality
- Mibanco microfinance asset quality is inherently volatile and can spike in downturns
- Geographic concentration in a single-country franchise (Peru represents the vast majority of earnings)
- Yape's monetisation trajectory, while promising, is early-stage; revenue per MAU improvements must be sustained
