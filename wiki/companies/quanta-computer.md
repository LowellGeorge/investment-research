# Quanta Computer

> Taiwan's second-largest AI server manufacturer and a top-three NVIDIA rack-scale partner, undergoing a fundamental identity transformation from the world's largest notebook ODM to an AI infrastructure manufacturer, with revenue surging 50.5% to TWD 2.12 trillion in FY2025 but facing structural margin compression at a 7% gross margin.

## Business Overview

Barry Lam co-founded Quanta Computer in Taipei in 1988 with less than USD 900,000, betting that branded PC companies would increasingly outsource notebook design and manufacturing. By 2001, Quanta had become the world's largest notebook manufacturer. The company's most consequential strategic shift began in 2011 when it joined Facebook's Open Compute Project, designing servers for hyperscale data centers, and formalized this with the 2012 launch of Quanta Cloud Technology (QCT).

The AI infrastructure buildout has transformed Quanta's trajectory. As a primary ODM for NVIDIA's GPU-based server platforms, Quanta became one of two lead manufacturers (alongside Foxconn) of NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 AI server racks. Revenue jumped 30% in FY2024 and accelerated to TWD 2.12 trillion in FY2025 (+50.5%). AI servers now comprise more than 60% of server revenue, and servers constitute 65-70% of total sales. Direct customers include Microsoft, Google, Amazon AWS, and Meta.

The company is aggressively expanding US manufacturing to serve hyperscaler demand and navigate geopolitical pressures, with its Nashville subsidiary receiving $330 million in capital injections during 2024 and additional factory space leased in California. Quanta also has production capacity in Mexico and Thailand.

## Bull Case

- Top-three position in a Taiwanese AI server oligopoly riding the most powerful demand cycle in hardware history
- Revenue growth of 50.5% with FY2025 net income of TWD 75 billion; ROE of 27-35% through extreme capital efficiency
- Balance sheet capacity to finance massive GPU inventory cycles is a genuine barrier to entry -- "only a few players can work with the CSP for this type of product"
- Undemanding valuation at ~15x trailing P/E with 50%+ revenue growth; forward P/E of ~12-13x vs peer average of ~21x
- 25 consecutive years of uninterrupted dividend payments with ~80% payout ratio and ~5.5% yield

## Bear Case

- Gross margin compressed from 7.9% to 7.0% and still declining; management warned of "some degree of margin dilution moving forward"
- FY2024 produced negative TWD 28.4B operating cash flow and negative TWD 41.5B free cash flow despite TWD 60B net income
- Foxconn commands ~52% of GB200/GB300 rack shipments vs Quanta's ~19%; the gap is widening, not narrowing
- US$2B in convertible bonds create dilution risk; short-term borrowings peaked at TWD 234B
- Founder Barry Lam is 77 with no publicly disclosed succession plan; co-founder C.C. Leung is approximately the same age

## Competitive Position

The AI server ODM market is a Taiwanese oligopoly. Four companies hold virtually all NVIDIA rack-scale production: Hon Hai/Foxconn (~52% share), Wistron (~21%), Quanta (~19%), and Wiwynn. Taiwan accounts for over 90% of global AI server builds. Foxconn's scale advantage is growing: it was first to deliver both GB200 and GB300 racks at scale and has the largest manufacturing footprint.

The rise of custom ASICs threatens to restructure the value chain. Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium), Meta (MTIA), and [[wiki/companies/microsoft|Microsoft]] (Maia) are all investing in proprietary silicon. NVIDIA's share of data center compute has reportedly slipped from ~90% to ~75%. Quanta builds ASIC-based AI servers alongside NVIDIA-based ones.

[[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]], [[wiki/companies/sk-hynix|SK Hynix]], and [[wiki/companies/tsmc|TSMC]] are key upstream suppliers in the AI server value chain. [[wiki/companies/mediatek|Mediatek]] competes in adjacent semiconductor design.

## Management Assessment

Barry Lam's 25-26% ownership stake (worth ~USD 8-9 billion) provides genuine alignment, making him Taiwan's richest person. However, Lam is 77 and does not participate in quarterly earnings calls -- CFO Elton Yang runs investor communication. The company does not publish individual executive pay packages, relying on cash bonuses and profit-sharing rather than equity-based incentives. Management has a strong track record of underpromising and overdelivering: they guided for triple-digit AI server revenue growth in 2025 and delivered it. The 25-year uninterrupted dividend record at ~80% payout demonstrates capital return discipline, though paying dividends from borrowings while operating cash flow is negative raises questions.

## Red Flags

- Consolidated financial statements carry a qualified audit opinion: 9.52% of total assets (~TWD 89B) are in subsidiaries not reviewed by independent auditors
- Working capital consumed TWD 245B in FY2024 (AR +107B, inventory +138B); Quanta is effectively financing the AI buildout for hyperscalers
- Customer concentration: revenue dominated by a handful of hyperscalers (Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon) each with enormous bargaining power
- Converting margins: the faster AI server revenue grows, the lower consolidated margins go (structural trap)
- Paying 80% of earnings as dividends while operating cash flow is negative and borrowing to fund working capital
