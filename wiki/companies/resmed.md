# Resmed

> The dominant global provider of CPAP devices and masks for obstructive sleep apnea, operating as a capital-light, high-margin compounder with a growing digital health platform built on 22 billion nights of proprietary respiratory data.

## Business Overview

ResMed was founded in 1989 when Dr. Peter Farrell organized a management buyout of CPAP technology rights from Baxter, building on Professor Colin Sullivan's pioneering 1980 demonstration of continuous positive airway pressure as a treatment for obstructive sleep apnea. The company has since grown into the global leader in sleep-disordered breathing devices, masks, and the digital health infrastructure that connects them.

The revenue model combines device hardware (51-52% of FY2025 revenue), recurring consumable masks and accessories (35-37%), and Residential Care Software (12%), principally through Brightree and MatrixCare SaaS platforms. The AirView cloud platform manages over 32 million patients, supported by 26 million connected devices and 22 billion nights of respiratory data -- creating a proprietary data flywheel that competitors cannot replicate. ResMed holds approximately 55-60% global market share in sleep apnea devices, operating in an effective near-monopoly since Philips Respironics' 2021 recall removed the category co-leader from the US market.

The most recent strategic extension was the 2022 acquisition of MEDIFOX DAN (~$1B), Germany's leading out-of-hospital software provider, and the 2025 acquisition of VirtuOx ($140M), extending upstream into home-based diagnostics. Capital intensity is remarkably low at 1.7% of revenue.

## Bull Case

- Structural under-diagnosis: 1.4 billion estimated global OSA sufferers with only 15-20% US penetration and under 5% in APAC
- Gross margins expanding rapidly -- from 56.7% (FY2024) to 61.8% (Q2 FY2026) -- driven by manufacturing efficiency and mask mix
- FCF of $1.66B in FY2025 at a 119% conversion ratio; net cash balance sheet of $541M
- AI-enabled Smart Comfort device (FDA cleared December 2025) extends product differentiation at the moment Philips attempts re-entry
- At ~22x trailing earnings and ~18x FCF, valuation sits meaningfully below five-year averages

## Bear Case

- Philips windfall is fading: management has never cleanly disaggregated Philips-driven share capture from organic growth
- GLP-1 obesity drugs approved for OSA (tirzepatide, December 2024) represent a long-term structural uncertainty despite near-term data showing higher CPAP initiation among GLP-1 users
- Residential Care Software growing only mid-single-digits, deferred to FY2027 recovery -- $3B of goodwill sits on the balance sheet
- Combined Chairman/CEO dynastic governance: father founded the company, son runs it, zero open-market insider purchases over five years against 45 CEO share sales
- Recurring restructuring charges dressed as one-offs undermine non-GAAP credibility

## Competitive Position

The OSA device market is a concentrated duopoly that has functioned as a near-monopoly since Philips' recall. [[wiki/companies/fisher-paykel|Fisher & Paykel]] occupies a distant third position, primarily in masks and humidification. Inspire Medical Systems targets the CPAP-intolerant subset with implantable neurostimulation. Chinese manufacturers (BMC, DeVilbiss) are gaining ground in APAC but lack clinical credibility and data infrastructure for Western markets.

ResMed's moat is multi-layered: clinical evidence and regulatory approvals built over three decades, the AirView digital platform creating genuine switching costs, and vertical integration across the entire OSA care pathway from screening to device therapy to provider workflow software. CPAP devices are excluded from CMS competitive bidding, protecting margins from race-to-the-bottom pricing.

## Management Assessment

Mick Farrell has been CEO since 2013 and Chairman since 2023, bringing twelve-plus years of operational credibility. CFO Brett Sandercock has served since 2006. The guidance track record is notably clean, with management delivering on specific operational commitments. The primary governance concern is the combined Chair/CEO role held by a second-generation family member, with only 0.3% insider ownership and no open-market buying during the 2023 GLP-1 selloff when the stock fell roughly 40%.

## Red Flags

- The SERVE-HF trial (2015) showed Adaptive Servo-Ventilation increased cardiovascular mortality -- a precedent for clinical evidence risk
- DOJ/OIG kickback settlement of $37.5M and Corporate Integrity Agreement for providing inducements to DME suppliers
- Class I FDA recall (2023-2024) covering 20M+ masks with magnets due to cardiac implant interference risk (six hospitalizations reported)
- Goodwill of $3.05B (37% of total assets) with MEDIFOX DAN underperforming acquisition underwriting model
