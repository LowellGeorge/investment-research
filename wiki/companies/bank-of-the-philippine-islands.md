# Bank of the Philippine Islands

> Southeast Asia's oldest bank (founded 1851), operating as the most profitable among the Philippine Big Three with a dominant low-cost deposit franchise, accelerating consumer lending transformation, and best-in-class operating efficiency -- backed by the Ayala conglomerate.

## Business Overview

Bank of the Philippine Islands traces its origins to August 1, 1851, when a royal decree established El Banco Espanol Filipino de Isabel II in Manila -- making it the oldest bank in Southeast Asia. BPI printed the Philippines' first domestic currency in 1852 and effectively served as the country's original central bank. In 1969, Ayala Corporation displaced the Catholic Church as BPI's largest shareholder, transforming it into the financial flagship of one of the Philippines' most powerful conglomerates.

Through decades of disciplined acquisitions (People's Bank 1974, Commercial Bank 1981, CityTrust 1996, Far East Bank 1999, Prudential Bank 2005, Robinsons Bank 2024), BPI grew into the third-largest Philippine bank by assets at PHP 3.65 trillion, behind BDO Unibank and Metrobank. Under CEO Limcaoco, BPI has doubled its client base from 8 million in 2021 to over 18 million by end-2025, driven by digital onboarding and the BPI mobile app.

The defining strategic shift has been the deliberate move into higher-yielding consumer and SME lending. Non-institutional loans have grown from 20% to 30.8% of the book -- a year ahead of the 2026 target -- while FY2025 net income reached PHP 66.8 billion, up from PHP 28 billion in 2021. The CASA ratio of 60.7% with 77% from retail customers underpins BPI's low-cost funding advantage.

## Bull Case

- Best-in-class operating efficiency: cost-to-income ratio of 47.2% is the lowest among Philippine Big Three (Metrobank ~50.7%, BDO ~50-53%)
- Dominant and widening wealth management position: PHP 1.91 trillion AUM, 21% trust industry share, 31% investment funds share
- Aggressive market share gains across lending: +245bps credit cards, +520bps auto loans, +465bps mortgage loans since 2021

## Bear Case

- Provision surge of 168.9% to PHP 17.75B in FY2025 reflects the riskier consumer book; credit card NPLs at 4.55%, personal loans at 5.04%, microfinance at 13%
- NPL coverage ratio declined from 136% to 94.9% in less than two years -- below 100% means BPI is not fully reserved against existing problem loans
- 100% Philippines concentration means earnings are tightly coupled to domestic GDP, interest rates, and credit demand

## Competitive Position

The Philippine banking system is a concentrated oligopoly where BDO, Metrobank, and BPI collectively control roughly half of universal and commercial bank assets. BPI's competitive position rests on its low-cost funding franchise (CASA ratio 60.7%), deep digital ecosystem (seven client-facing platforms), and the Ayala conglomerate's relationships and reputation. Digital customers generate twice as much revenue as non-digital customers at the same per-capita cost.

In wealth management, BPI holds a dominant position with PHP 1.91 trillion AUM and market-leading shares across trusts, investment funds, and employee benefit plans. Fintech challengers GCash (81M active users) and Maya have built enormous payment user bases but lack BPI's balance sheet capacity for large-scale lending, wealth management expertise, and regulatory standing. BPI has responded by embracing open banking (131 API partners) and launching its own VYBE e-wallet. Regulatory barriers remain formidable, with BSP capping digital banking licenses at ten.

## Management Assessment

CEO TG Limcaoco is a Stanford/Wharton-educated Ayala Group career executive who returned to BPI after serving as Ayala Corporation's CFO. Under his leadership, BPI has delivered consistently ahead of stated targets: the 30% non-institutional loan target was hit a year early, dividends have increased every year, and net income has compounded at roughly 24% annually over four years. The management team fields an unusually wide panel on earnings calls, suggesting genuine decentralization of knowledge. Ayala Corporation's approximately 30.6% stake provides alignment through the controlling shareholder relationship, though CEO personal ownership is modest at ~0.05%.

## Financial Profile

- **NIM:** 4.60% (FY2025, up 30bps YoY; industry average ~4.2%)
- **Cost-to-Income:** 47.2% (best among Big Three)
- **ROE:** 14.5%; ROA: 2.0%
- **Net Income:** PHP 66.8B (FY2025)
- **CET1:** 13.9% (380bps above regulatory minimum)
- **Dividends:** PHP 4.36/share (up 142% from pre-variable-payout era); ~40% payout ratio
- **Loan Growth:** 14.7% vs. 9.7% industry average

## Red Flags

- Rapid consumer lending growth (SME +79.7%, credit cards +31.9%, personal loans +30%) with deteriorating credit quality; microfinance NPLs at 13%
- PwC flagged impairment losses on loans as the sole Key Audit Matter, citing "complexity and significant judgments"
- CASA ratio declined 249bps YoY to 60.7%; loan-to-deposit ratio tightened 500bps to 92.4%
- Net insider selling activity has been flagged recently
- Geographic concentration in a single emerging market with political and currency risk
