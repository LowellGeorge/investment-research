# Prosus

> A $104 billion Amsterdam-listed internet conglomerate spanning food delivery (iFood), classifieds (OLX), payments (PayU), and e-commerce (eMAG) across 90+ countries, anchored by a ~25% stake in [[wiki/companies/tencent|Tencent]] that drives most of the market value while the operated portfolio -- now generating $1B+ annual FCF -- trades at an implied near-zero valuation.

## Business Overview

Prosus N.V. is the product of one of the most improbable corporate transformations in history: a 111-year journey from a nationalist Afrikaans-language newspaper publisher (Die Nasionale Pers, founded 1915) to a global internet conglomerate. The pivotal event was Naspers's 2001 acquisition of a 46.5% stake in Tencent for $32 million, which became arguably the greatest venture investment in corporate history, worth $130+ billion at Prosus's 2019 listing.

Prosus listed on Euronext Amsterdam in September 2019 with Naspers retaining ~73% ownership. The company operates four core segments: eMAG (~40% of revenue, online marketplace in Central and Eastern Europe), iFood (~22%, Brazil's dominant food delivery with ~80-86% order share), PayU (~22%, payments and fintech primarily in India), and OLX (~13%, classifieds across Poland, Romania, and other markets). Under new CEO Fabricio Bloisi -- the Brazilian founder who built iFood -- the company completed the EUR 4.1B acquisition of Just Eat Takeaway.com and the $1.7B acquisition of Despegar (Latin American travel platform).

The defining challenge is the persistent NAV discount: the market values Prosus at ~23-27% below the sum of its listed holdings alone, meaning the entire operated portfolio trades at near-zero implied value. The aggressive buyback program ($50B+ returned to shareholders) is mathematically accretive but depletes the Tencent stake that provides the valuation anchor.

## Bull Case

- Structural NAV discount: entire $7B+ revenue e-commerce portfolio -- now generating $1B+ FCF -- is valued at essentially zero
- Operated businesses reaching profitability inflection: FY2025 FCF of $1.02B, H1 FY2026 already at $1.3B; aEBITDA growing 70% YoY
- iFood holds ~80-86% order share in Brazil with 32% aEBITDA margin; OLX achieves 49% aEBITDA margin
- Fortress balance sheet: $7.11B cash, $11.91B short-term investments, investment-grade ratings (BBB/Baa2)
- Buyback at 30%+ NAV discount is the most capital-efficient use of cash available

## Bear Case

- Net income dominated by Tencent equity-method earnings, masking thin operating margins ($173M operating income on $6.17B revenue in FY2025)
- Goodwill collapsed from $3.37B (FY2022) to $1.16B (FY2025) through impairments; Byju's written to zero ($493M loss)
- New CEO Bloisi's $100M "moonshot" award (contingent on doubling market cap by 2028) could incentivize aggressive M&A
- Just Eat Takeaway has negative order growth and requires turnaround; delisting from Euronext removes public scrutiny
- Naspers retains control via super-voting shares; governance flows through Johannesburg, not Amsterdam
- No permanent CFO 16+ months after predecessor's retirement

## Competitive Position

Prosus's competitive advantages are most durable in iFood (overwhelming network density, 300,000 restaurant partners, ecosystem extensions into fintech and travel) and OLX (two-sided network effects, 14M daily active users, 62M active listings). PayU faces intense competitive pressure from Razorpay (~55% of India's online payment gateway market). eMAG competes against Amazon and Alibaba-backed Trendyol, which acquired 500,000 Romanian customers within eight months.

The simultaneous arrival of Meituan in Brazilian food delivery ($1B committed), Trendyol in Central European e-tail, and AI-native disruptors in classifieds means Prosus faces the most competitive headwinds in years. [[wiki/companies/alibaba|Alibaba]] and [[wiki/companies/sea-ltd|Sea Ltd]] compete in overlapping emerging-market e-commerce segments.

## Management Assessment

Bloisi brings genuine operational credibility from building iFood from a 20-person startup to Latin America's dominant food delivery platform. His base salary of $750,000 is deliberately modest for a $104B company CEO, with 75%+ of on-target remuneration at-risk. The $100M moonshot award -- payable in shares contingent on doubling market cap by 2028 -- creates alignment but also M&A incentive risk. The departure of President/CIO Ervin Tu (ex-SoftBank, co-led investments in Uber and ByteDance) concentrates strategic authority in Bloisi. Board is chaired by Koos Bekker, the architect of the original Tencent investment, who remains the controlling influence through Naspers.

## Red Flags

- aEBITDA definition excludes all share-based compensation; external auditor flagged SBC schemes as a key audit matter
- $2.5B in impairment losses recognized in FY2023 alone; goodwill is the wreckage of a capital allocation strategy that destroyed value
- Brazil operations carry $142M in labour litigation claims and $529M in tax exposures
- Hyperinflation accounting for Turkey and Argentina subsidiaries introduces measurement uncertainty
- The buyback program is a financial treadmill: it depletes the Tencent stake that provides the valuation anchor
