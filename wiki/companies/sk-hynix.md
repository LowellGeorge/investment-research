# SK Hynix

> The world's second-largest memory semiconductor manufacturer and the undisputed leader in High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) for AI accelerators, currently generating historically unprecedented margins at the epicenter of the AI infrastructure buildout.

## Business Overview

SK Hynix traces its origins to 1983 as Hyundai Electronics, surviving near-insolvency during the DRAM price collapse of 2001, a creditor-led restructuring, and ultimately acquisition by SK Group in 2012. Through four names and three ownership structures, the company has evolved from a cyclical commodity DRAM manufacturer into the most strategically positioned company in memory semiconductors, driven by its first-mover advantage in High Bandwidth Memory for AI training and inference.

The revenue model is concentrated: DRAM contributes roughly 80% of revenue and NAND the remaining 20%. Within DRAM, HBM commands ASPs approximately 4-6x higher per bit than commodity DDR5. Approximately 60% of revenue comes from US customers, reflecting NVIDIA and hyperscaler dominance. FY2025 revenue reached KRW 97.1 trillion (up 47% YoY) with operating profit of KRW 47.2 trillion (up 101%), producing a 49% operating margin -- historically unprecedented for any memory company.

The Solidigm acquisition (~$9B, acquiring Intel's NAND operations) and the Yong-in mega-fab complex (expected to exceed KRW 120 trillion across full build-out into the 2040s) define the current capital allocation posture.

## Bull Case

- HBM technology leadership: 1-2 quarter lead over [[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]] and Micron, widening through advanced packaging know-how
- DRAM industry is a three-player oligopoly with near-insurmountable barriers -- among the most favorable industry structures in technology
- Net cash of KRW 12.7 trillion (34.9T cash vs 22.2T debt) after generating operating losses just two years ago
- KRW 14.3 trillion shareholder return package including permanent retirement of 50 million treasury shares signals management confidence
- OpenAI LOI reframes SK Hynix from supplier to strategic AI infrastructure partner

## Bear Case

- A 49% operating margin for a memory company is a sign of the cycle peak -- SK Hynix posted operating losses in FY2023, just two years ago
- Dangerous customer concentration: ~60% of revenue from US customers with NVIDIA likely the single largest
- Massive capex ramp (mid-30% of revenue, ~KRW 34T+) is largely irreversible; if AI demand slows, industry faces simultaneous overcapacity
- Chaebol governance: SK Group cross-subsidy risk (SK On has burned enormous cash), minimal executive equity ownership
- Geopolitical exposure from DRAM fab in Wuxi and former Intel NAND fab in Dalian, China, operating under US export control waivers

## Competitive Position

The DRAM market is a textbook oligopoly: [[wiki/companies/samsung-electronics|Samsung Electronics]] (~40% share), SK Hynix (~30%), and Micron (~25%) control approximately 95% of global supply. Within HBM, SK Hynix holds clear leadership as the first to mass-produce HBM3E. Samsung's HBM3E struggled with yield and heat dissipation issues; Micron remains a smaller participant. China's CXMT is the most credible DRAM entrant but remains multiple generations behind and constrained by US export controls.

HBM qualification with GPU platforms involves months of validation, creating meaningful switching costs that benefit the incumbent supplier. The Yong-in mega-fab and M15X expansion position SK Hynix for the HBM4/HBM5 transition, though Samsung possesses the engineering depth to close the gap. The "order-first, produce-later" framework management articulated in Q3 2025, if durable, would represent a structural improvement in memory economics.

## Management Assessment

CEO Kwak Noh-Jung's return in March 2024 signals both competence and key-person risk -- the company recalled a retired CEO because interim leadership was inadequate. Kwak's first-term bets on HBM were spectacularly validated, and the team under-promised and over-delivered across all four FY2025 quarters. Capital allocation has been rational: the Solidigm acquisition proved prescient, M15X opened ahead of schedule, and the shareholder return package with permanent share retirement demonstrates discipline. Korean executive compensation structures emphasize salary and bonuses over equity ownership, making alignment more reputational than financial.

## Red Flags

- Every prior memory super-cycle ended with suppliers interpreting peak margins as the new normal and committing to massive capex
- DRAM price-fixing guilty plea in 2005 ($185M fine) -- the oligopoly enabling today's margins has previously attracted antitrust scrutiny
- Solidigm integration may mask ongoing NAND weakness with HBM profits
- SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won's embezzlement conviction (pardoned 2015) adds parent-company governance concern
- Management's narrative became progressively more bullish without corresponding increase in risk disclosure through FY2025
