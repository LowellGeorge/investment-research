## Strategic Positioning

**DBS Group** is Singapore's largest bank and the global benchmark for incumbent digital transformation. Piyush Gupta's 15-year tenure transformed "Damn Bloody Slow" into a technology platform that processes 29,000 transactions per second at 99.999% uptime. Regional footprint spans Singapore, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, and China.

**PT Bank Central Asia (BCA)** is Indonesia's most profitable bank, built on a transactional banking franchise that generates the lowest funding cost in the country. BCA's CASA ratio (~80%+) is among the highest globally. The Djarum Group (Hartono family) controls 54.9% — alignment between the controlling family's long-term wealth and minority shareholders has been strong.

**Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI)** is the Philippines' oldest bank (founded 1851), with a strong deposit franchise and conservative credit culture. The Ayala family's involvement provides governance stability and strategic coherence. Smaller scale but positioned for the Philippines' young-population growth story.

## Growth vs. Maturity

**DBS** is a mature franchise in a developed market — growth comes from regional expansion and digital innovation rather than domestic underpenetration. The dividend yield reflects this maturity. Technology investment creates cost advantages (cost-to-income below 40%).

**BCA** combines the best of both worlds — high returns from a mature transactional franchise in the world's fourth-largest country, with long-term structural growth from Indonesia's low credit penetration. The Hartono family's patient capital allocation approach has consistently created shareholder value.

**BPI** is the purest underpenetration play — the Philippines has the youngest population and lowest banking penetration among the three markets. The challenge is scaling efficiently in an archipelago geography with complex logistics.

## Key Question

Can the Southeast Asian banking premium (these banks trade at 2-4x book) be sustained? The answer hinges on whether Southeast Asia's demographic and digital growth translate into sustained high-teens ROEs — or whether competition (from fintech, from each other, from Chinese banks) compresses returns.
