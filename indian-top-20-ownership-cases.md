# Indian top 20 ownership cases

This mirrors the earlier internal-style format: a short ownership case for each name that you can stretch into a two-minute answer by moving from business quality, to why it belongs in the book, to the live debate.

## Ticker assumptions

The Bloomberg-style mnemonics BOS IN, CLGT IN, BAF IN, and TMCV IN were treated as Bosch Limited, Colgate-Palmolive (India) Limited, Bajaj Finance, and the Tata Motors commercial-vehicle listing respectively, based on market references for those tickers.

If your internal map for TMCV or BAF points to a different listed line, that section is the only one that may need a swap; the rest map cleanly to the intended Indian equities.

## HDFC Bank

- Pitch: HDFC Bank is still the cleanest core India financials compounding story in the list: a scaled private bank with a powerful deposit franchise, broad retail and wholesale reach, and the balance-sheet depth to keep taking share across cycles.
- Evidence: The hard evidence is in the economics of the franchise: FY2025 revenue grew 19.1% to INR 4.2 trillion, operating income rose to INR 861.5 billion, and operating margin expanded to 20.5% from 20.0%, which is what you want to see if the deposit base and distribution engine are still doing the heavy lifting after the merger. Net margin did slip to 16.1% from 17.7%, so the moat still looks intact rather than clearly widening yet, but the core franchise is still compounding at serious scale.
- Debate: The live question is whether deposit costs and post-merger integration keep returns below the old HDFC Bank standard for longer than bulls expect.

## Kotak Mahindra

- Pitch: Kotak Mahindra Bank is the high-quality, conservative private-bank holding where the case rests on franchise quality first and growth re-acceleration second.
- Evidence: The competitive advantage is that Kotak still monetizes conservatism unusually well: FY2025 revenue grew 13.4%, net income grew 21.5%, operating margin widened to 27.7% from 25.9%, and net margin widened to 21.2% from 19.7%. When a bank is growing and expanding margins at the same time, that usually means the moat is widening through underwriting discipline and franchise quality rather than through balance-sheet stretch.
- Debate: The bear case is that the premium quality is well understood already, so the stock only really works if loan growth and operating leverage visibly improve.

## ICICI Bank

- Pitch: ICICI Bank is the more obvious execution-led private-bank compounder: strong retail mix, cleaner underwriting, and proven ability to convert scale into earnings growth.
- Evidence: The case rests on sustained execution quality: FY2025 revenue grew 24.8% to INR 2.9 trillion and net income grew 15.3% to INR 510.3 billion, which says ICICI is still taking profitable share at scale. Operating margin and net margin both eased year on year to 24.9% and 17.4% respectively, so the moat still looks strong but more stable than widening at this point.
- Debate: The main pushback is simply that it is no longer misunderstood, so future upside depends more on sustaining superior execution than on rerating alone.

## Bharti Airtel

- Pitch: Bharti Airtel is a structural telecom and digital-infrastructure story rather than a pure cyclical telecom trade, with India mobile pricing power, enterprise assets, and Africa optionality all inside one platform.
- Evidence: Airtel’s edge is network scale and pricing power, and the numbers still support that: FY2025 revenue grew 15.3% to INR 1.7 trillion, operating income reached INR 414.8 billion, and operating cash flow was INR 813.2 billion, which shows how much cash the platform can throw off once traffic is on the network. Operating margin eased to 24.0% from 25.8%, but net margin jumped to 19.4% from 5.0%, so the moat looks at least stable and arguably widening if tariff discipline holds.
- Debate: The argument against it is that a lot of tariff optimism is already in the stock, so future returns need continued ARPU improvement and disciplined competition.

## ICICI Lombard

- Pitch: ICICI Lombard is one of the best non-life insurance assets in India because it combines underwriting quality, distribution breadth, and long runway from still-low insurance penetration.
- Evidence: The moat here is underwriting plus distribution, and FY2025 backed that up: revenue grew 14.4%, net income grew 30.7%, operating margin widened to 13.3% from 11.7%, and net margin widened to 10.1% from 8.8%. That is what a widening non-life insurance franchise should look like, because growth is being converted into better economics rather than bought with looser pricing.
- Debate: The risk is that insurance can always look optically expensive, and any slippage in claims or pricing discipline can compress the premium rating fast.

## Bosch

- Pitch: Bosch is the India auto-content and localization story through a high-quality engineering supplier, which means it benefits when vehicle technology content rises even if headline auto volumes are only moderate.
- Evidence: Bosch’s advantage is high-value content and engineering intensity rather than raw auto volume, and you can see that in a 26.2% operating margin in FY2025 versus 24.8% in FY2024 on 8.2% revenue growth. Net income still fell 19.1% and net margin dropped to 11.3% from 15.1%, so the moat looks real but not clearly widening because the franchise is still exposed to mix and cycle noise below the operating line.
- Debate: The hesitation is valuation, because the stock already prices in quality, while near-term earnings still move around with the auto cycle and customer mix.

## KEI Industries

- Pitch: KEI is a clean way to own India electrification, construction, and premiumization in wires and cables, with organized share gains as the central thesis.
- Evidence: The evidence for KEI is organized share gain rather than headline glamour: FY2025 revenue grew 19.5%, net income grew 19.9%, and the company ended with net cash of roughly INR 8.3 billion. Operating margin slipped only modestly to 9.7% from 9.9% while net margin was essentially flat at 7.2%, which says the moat is probably widening slowly through scale and brand, but not yet through a big step-change in margins.
- Debate: The challenge is that the stock is already priced like a high-confidence compounder, so any slowdown in capex demand or margin pressure matters disproportionately.

## HCL Technologies

- Pitch: HCLTech is the cash-generative Indian IT services name with enough exposure to infrastructure, engineering, and products to look different from a plain vanilla outsourcing business.
- Evidence: HCLTech’s edge is that it still converts services scale into cash: FY2025 revenue grew 6.9%, net income grew 10.4%, operating margin held at 18.3%, net margin improved to 14.7% from 14.3%, and free-cash-flow proxy remained very strong at roughly INR 224.2 billion. That reads like a stable moat rather than a rapidly widening one, but stability is exactly what you want from a mature IT compounder.
- Debate: The question is whether that mix premium is enough to offset a still-muted global IT demand backdrop and slower discretionary spending.

## State Bank

- Pitch: SBI is the scale public-sector bank that gives you direct exposure to India’s credit cycle, but with much better profitability and balance-sheet credibility than the old PSU-bank stereotype.
- Evidence: The best evidence that SBI is no longer just a lumbering PSU bank is that FY2025 revenue grew 11.5%, net income grew 15.6%, operating margin widened to 16.4% from 15.6%, and net margin improved to 11.9% from 11.5%. Those are not distressed-state-bank economics; they suggest the scale and deposit franchise are translating into a stronger competitive position, so the moat may actually be widening from a low historical base.
- Debate: The reason some investors still hesitate is that the state ownership overhang and lower-quality historical memory can cap the multiple even when earnings are strong.

## Maruti Suzuki

- Pitch: Maruti is still the cleanest way to own Indian passenger-vehicle volume depth, first-time buyer exposure, and distribution strength, especially if the domestic auto cycle remains healthy.
- Evidence: Maruti’s competitive advantage is still unmatched domestic scale and distribution, but the financials say the moat is holding more than widening: FY2025 revenue grew 7.8%, net income grew 7.5%, operating margin was 23.0% versus 23.2% last year, and net margin was effectively flat at 9.5%. That is still a very healthy earnings profile for a mass auto OEM, just not one showing obvious incremental margin power yet.
- Debate: The debate is whether the company can keep defending share and pricing as the SUV mix shifts and competition intensifies higher up the value curve.

## Oberoi Realty

- Pitch: Oberoi Realty is the scarce Mumbai premium real-estate developer where the thesis is less about generic housing beta and more about asset quality, execution, and embedded annuity value.
- Evidence: The reason investors pay up for Oberoi is visible in the margins: FY2025 revenue grew 17.0%, operating margin widened to 57.5% from 53.2%, and net margin stayed extraordinary at 42.5% even after a very strong prior year. Those are premium-asset, premium-execution numbers, so the moat looks stable to slightly widening as long as the company keeps proving it can monetize scarce Mumbai inventory this efficiently.
- Debate: The risk is that property stocks always carry timing risk, so if luxury demand or launch momentum pauses, the market can turn quickly on sentiment.

## Colgate India

- Pitch: Colgate India is the classic category-leader compounder, with oral-care leadership, deep distribution, and scope to keep premiumizing rather than relying only on category growth.
- Evidence: Colgate India’s moat is brand plus distribution, and the numbers still validate that formula: FY2025 revenue grew 6.3%, net income grew 8.5%, operating margin stayed near 30%, and net margin improved to 24.0% from 23.5% while the balance sheet remained net-cash positive. That is not explosive growth, but it is exactly the kind of resilient profitability that says the moat is at least holding and perhaps inching wider through premiumization.
- Debate: The pushback is simple: this is a high-quality defensive business, but it is not an obviously cheap one, so growth has to stay resilient to justify the premium.

## Niva Bupa

- Pitch: Niva Bupa is the pure-play health-insurance penetration story in India, so the attraction is the long runway and category structure more than current-period earnings optics.
- Evidence: The evidence here is less about current moat and more about proof that the model can scale: in the latest annual filing available, revenue was INR 56.9 billion, operating income was INR 4.2 billion, net income was INR 2.1 billion, and operating cash flow was INR 16.3 billion. Those are decent underlying economics for a business still building share, but at this stage the moat looks emergent rather than established, so the key question is whether scale will keep outrunning claims and acquisition costs.
- Debate: The stock only works if the market keeps believing in the long-duration health-insurance growth story, because on near-term earnings it already looks very expensive.

## Bajaj Auto

- Pitch: Bajaj Auto is a disciplined two-wheeler and exports compounder, with capital allocation credibility and optionality from electric two-wheelers layered on top.
- Evidence: Bajaj Auto’s edge is efficient manufacturing plus exports, and the operating numbers were still good in FY2025: revenue grew 12.3% and operating margin improved to 26.9% from 25.8%. Net income still fell 5.0% and net margin dropped to 14.1% from 16.7%, so the moat looks solid at the operating level but not clearly widening once you get below the line.
- Debate: The argument against it is that a lot of quality is already in the price, while exports, competition, and EV transition can all make the earnings path lumpier than it first appears.

## CAMS

- Pitch: CAMS is effectively a toll-road on Indian financialization, because it sits in the plumbing of mutual-fund and adjacent asset-servicing flows without taking direct balance-sheet risk.
- Evidence: CAMS has one of the clearest structural moats in the list, and the numbers show it: FY2025 revenue grew 25.2%, net income grew 33.0%, operating margin widened to 65.2% from 63.2%, and net margin widened to 33.1% from 31.1%. When a market-infrastructure business grows that quickly while sustaining those margins and net cash, the moat is not just intact, it is probably widening.
- Debate: The main concern is that this is an obviously good business and therefore rarely cheap, so the stock depends on maintaining its quasi-infrastructure status in investor minds.

## Rallis India

- Pitch: Rallis is more of a recovery and execution name than a pristine compounder, with the case built around crop-protection normalization, seeds, and operational improvement in agri-inputs.
- Evidence: The evidence is weaker here than for the compounders: FY2025 revenue grew only 1.2%, net income fell 15.5%, operating margin fell to 6.2% from 8.0%, and net margin fell to 4.7% from 5.6%. That tells you the franchise is under pressure rather than obviously strengthening, so this is more a recovery-underwriting story than a moat-widening one.
- Debate: That also explains the risk, because if execution and demand do not improve meaningfully, the stock can look expensive for what is still a relatively modest earnings base.

## Mahindra & Mahindra

- Pitch: M&M is one of the best ways to play Indian domestic cyclicals through two unusually strong franchises at once, namely SUVs and tractors, backed by better capital allocation than the group used to get credit for.
- Evidence: The core evidence for M&M is that the group now earns much better economics from its SUV and farm franchises than old M&M skeptics still assume: FY2025 revenue was INR 1.6 trillion, operating income was INR 245.8 billion, operating margin was 15.8%, and net income was INR 129.3 billion. That does not prove a rapidly widening moat on its own, but it does support the idea that capital allocation and franchise quality are materially better than they used to be.
- Debate: The risk is that you are still buying a cyclical manufacturer, so the stock needs sustained strength in farm and utility-vehicle demand to keep compounding.

## Escorts Kubota

- Pitch: Escorts Kubota is a focused farm-mechanization and construction-equipment play where the core attraction is improving product quality, distribution leverage, and strategic support from Kubota.
- Evidence: Escorts Kubota’s edge is quality and channel depth in farm equipment, and FY2025 showed solid if not spectacular confirmation: revenue grew 4.5%, net income grew 17.5%, and net margin improved to 12.3% from 11.0%. Operating margin slipped slightly to 18.4% from 18.7%, so the moat looks stable to modestly improving rather than clearly widening yet.
- Debate: The challenge is that the story is still tied to agri and construction sentiment, so it can look very cyclical when demand expectations soften.

## Tata Motors CV

- Pitch: TMCV is the direct India commercial-vehicles and freight-cycle holding, so the case is really about infrastructure, replacement demand, construction activity, and Tata’s ability to capture those flows with a broad truck-and-bus portfolio.
- Evidence: The best evidence for the TMCV case is segment-level operating momentum rather than a static group P&L line: market references tied TMCV-IN to Tata Motors, commercial-vehicle sales were reported up 25% year on year in Q4 FY26 to 132,465 units, and the business had delivered 10 consecutive quarters of double-digit EBITDA margins by Q3 FY26. Those are exactly the markers you want if distribution, service network, and product breadth are reinforcing the moat, though it is still a cyclical moat rather than a defensive one.
- Debate: This remains a cyclical industrial vehicle name, so if replacement demand, infrastructure spending, or pricing discipline soften, the market will punish it quickly.

## Bajaj Finance

- Pitch: Bajaj Finance is still one of the clearest plays on Indian consumer and SME credit compounding, with product breadth, underwriting data, and distribution scale that smaller NBFCs struggle to match.
- Evidence: Bajaj Finance still has one of the strongest scale moats in Indian lending, and FY2025 kept proving that on growth: revenue grew 26.5% to INR 671.3 billion and net income grew 15.1% to INR 166.4 billion. Operating margin did compress to 32.9% from 36.4% and net margin to 24.8% from 27.2%, so the moat looks intact but not widening at the moment because funding and credit normalization are absorbing some of the scale benefit.
- Debate: The reason this is always debated is valuation, because when a lender already trades on a premium multiple, even small disappointments in growth, funding, or credit costs matter.

## Closing take

The portfolio’s Indian book still looks deliberately tilted toward high-quality financials, formalization beneficiaries, and a handful of cyclical names where the argument is “better business than the cycle implies” rather than “deep value”.

The fastest way to sound credible on these names is to keep repeating the same four-part structure: what the business really is, why it deserves a place in the portfolio, what hard evidence supports the case, and what the market is still arguing about.
