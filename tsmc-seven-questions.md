# TSMC — The Seven Questions

**Ticker:** TSM US (ADR) / 2330 TT | **Q1 2026 revenue:** US$35.9bn | **GEM weight:** 9.6% (#1 in portfolio)
**Source base:** Quartr-grounded (Q1 2026 earnings release & call, 16 April 2026; Q4 2025 call, 15 January 2026; Q4 2023 call, 18 January 2024 for capital allocation framework)
**Written:** April 2026

---

## Cold open

TSMC just printed a quarter no foundry has ever printed. **Revenue +40.6% YoY in USD. Gross margin 66.2%. Operating margin 58.1%. ROE 40.5%.** And on the call, C.C. Wei told analysts that supply will remain tight through 2027, that they're now adding three new N3 fabs they hadn't planned to build, and that "the application is simple — it's still HPC AI."

This is what an actual monopoly on the world's most strategic resource looks like once the demand curve goes vertical.

Seven questions.

---

## 1. What is it?

TSMC is the **only company on earth** that can manufacture leading-edge logic chips at volume. Not the best — the only one. Morris Chang invented the pure-play foundry model in 1987; without it, fabless semiconductors don't exist as an industry. Nvidia, AMD, Apple silicon, Broadcom, Qualcomm, MediaTek — none of them are the companies they are without the foundry layer underneath.

The mix:

- **Advanced nodes (7nm and below) = 74% of wafer revenue** in Q1 2026
- **3nm = 25%, 5nm = 36%, 7nm = 13%**
- **HPC = 61% of revenue** (up from 51% YoY, +20% QoQ)
- **North America = 76%** — essentially Nvidia, AMD, Apple, Broadcom, plus the hyperscalers' custom silicon
- **305 process technologies, 12,682 products, 534 customers** in 2025

The thing to internalise: TSMC is no longer a cyclical foundry. It is the AI infrastructure layer with a smartphone business attached. Customer concentration (Apple ~22%, Nvidia ~12% and rising) used to be the worry — it has now inverted, because the customers depend on TSMC, not the other way around.

## 2. Can we trust them?

Yes — and the evidence is in the past-stress behaviour, not the slogans.

**The lineage.** Morris Chang founded TSMC in 1987, retired in 2005, **came back as CEO during the 2009 GFC**, retired again, and finally stepped down as Chairman in 2018. Mark Liu and C.C. Wei ran the dual-leadership era from 2018-2024. Wei now holds **both Chairman and CEO roles** — the first combined role since Chang. Notably, this is *not* a family-controlled chaebol; the largest single shareholder is the Taiwan **National Development Fund at ~6.4%**, never used to steer commercial decisions.

**The 2009 tell that defines the franchise.** When peers slashed capex in the financial crisis, Chang came back as CEO and *raised* TSMC's capex to prepare for the recovery. That counter-cyclical bet captured Qualcomm and Broadcom from competitors that exited and is the moment TSMC's lead over UMC and SMIC moved from significant to permanent. The pattern has repeated: aggressive capex into the 2020-22 supply shock (controversial at the time, vindicated within 18 months), and now the unprecedented N3 expansion in 2026.

**Capital allocation — verbatim from CFO Wendell Huang, Q4 2023:** *"fund the company's growth organically, generate good profitability, preserve financial flexibility, and distribute a sustainable and steadily increasing cash dividend to shareholders."* That has translated into capex/sales never above 50%, a dividend that's grown almost every year since the 1994 IPO, and **no meaningful buybacks** — every dollar reinvested. When pressed by analysts for more shareholder returns (Q1 2024, Q3 2024), management defended the reinvestment-first posture explicitly.

**The watch item.** Wei is 71. Internal bench is deep (SVPs Y.P. Chyn, Cliff Hou, Y.J. Mii) but no obvious heir. Likely 2026-28 announcement could briefly widen the discount even though the franchise is untouched.

## 3. Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a fourth nobody fully prices.

**Process leadership compounding in dollars.** The 18-24 month lead vs Intel/Samsung translates into a multi-year revenue tail because customers design around TSMC's roadmap, not the other way around. ASP gaps between N5/N3/N2 are widening, not narrowing — AI customers will pay anything for performance-per-watt at the leading edge.

**Capacity is the moat right now.** Wei said the quiet part out loud on Q1 2026: *"Historically, we do not add additional capacity to a node once it has reached its target capacity. However... we are stepping up our CapEx investment to increase our N3 capacity."* This is unprecedented. Three new N3 fabs (Tainan 1H 2027, Arizona Fab 2 2H 2027, Japan Fab 2 2028), N5→N3 conversions, and cross-node optimisation. The depreciation cliff on original N3 equipment hits in 2027, so capacity expansion AND margin tailwind arrive together.

**Advanced packaging.** CoWoS is the bottleneck for every Nvidia GPU shipped. TSMC is the only player at scale, building CoPoS (panel-level) and very-large-reticle CoWoS for next-gen accelerators. Small in dollar terms, massive in strategic terms — it locks the customer at the system level, not just the wafer level.

**The depreciation flywheel nobody models.** Headline GM 66.2%. Steady-state margin on the fully depreciated N5/N7 book is probably north of 75%. CFO confirmed Q1 2026: *"after the full depreciation, the gross margins are generally very high."* This is why $54bn/year capex doesn't crush returns — old fabs print money for years after depreciation runs off.

## 4. What proves us right and what to track?

Five specific KPIs:

- **Gross margin through the N2 ramp** — stay above ~62% = moat intact. Below 58% = yellow flag
- **N3 ASP behaviour** — watch whether they get away with raising prices in the next two quarters now they're expanding capacity (almost certainly will)
- **CoWoS capacity vs demand** — independent reports peg 2026 capacity at 80-90k wafers/month; if Nvidia/AMD/Broadcom satisfied, demand has peaked. Still waiting = years of cycle left
- **Arizona Fab 2 ramp execution** — 2H 2027 volume is the test of whether US fabs are real second source or political artefact
- **HPC % of revenue** — 61% Q1 2026, up from 51% YoY. If it crosses 65%, the AI-infrastructure framing is structurally established

## 5. What's it worth?

The frame, not a fake-precise DCF:

- 2026 run-rate revenue ~$155-160bn against ~$1.0-1.1tn market cap
- **~22-24x forward P/E** for a business growing 30%+ at 50%+ net margins with $100bn of net cash and 40.5% ROE
- Natural 5-year case: revenue compounds ~20% (decel from 30%+), margins flat (N2 dilution offset by N3 maturation + depreciation), EPS ~20%, multiple flat → **~20% IRR + dividend**

**Key sensitivity:** capex efficiency. If the next $200bn of capex generates the same revenue per dollar as the last $200bn, the stock is materially undervalued. If demand normalises and capex becomes a stranded asset, the stock is fine but uninspiring. The straddle is asymmetric to the upside.

## 6. Where is the market wrong?

**One — overseas dilution is being treated as cost when it's actually insurance.** Yes, Arizona/Japan/Germany dilute group GM 2-4%. But that's the premium TSMC is paying to protect *the multiple* against Taiwan disruption risk. Without Arizona, TSMC trades at 12x for geopolitics. With Arizona, it trades at 22x. The "dilution" is the cheapest insurance premium ever paid.

**Two — agentic AI is a step-change, not a continuation.** Wei on Q1 2026: *"The shift from generative AI and the query mode to agentic AI and command and action mode is leading to another step-up in the amount of tokens being consumed."* Buy-side is modelling AI demand additively when it's actually multiplicative — every query becomes 10-100 sub-queries as agents plan/execute/verify/retry. If you believe agents are real, you have to revise 2027-2030 wafer demand higher.

**The honest counter:** if Intel 18A is genuinely competitive AND Samsung's 2nm yield improves, TSMC's >90% leading-edge share compresses. Still largest player; no longer only one. That re-rates the stock down even with growth intact.

## 7. What could kill it, and when do we sell?

**Real risks, ranked:**

- **Taiwan strait** — unhedgeable, binary; you insure with position size, not sell triggers
- **Customer concentration in HPC** — ~60% of revenue from a handful of US chip designers; Tesla just dual-sourced to Samsung; Nvidia could too
- **Capex digestion** — $54bn 2026, more in 2027; if AI compute plateaus before fabs ramp, 2008-style overcapacity
- **Yield risk on N2/A14** — gate-all-around ramp is not zero-risk
- **US export controls extending to specific customers** — China revenue only 7% but the next leg is named-customer restrictions

**Sell triggers (specific, not vague):**

- Two consecutive quarters of GM below 56% with no clear FX or one-off
- Loss of a major customer's leading-edge node to Intel 18A or Samsung
- Capex/sales above 45% for two years with revenue growth decelerating below 15%
- Wei retires without an obvious successor of similar calibre
- Cross-strait tensions escalate to active military mobilisation (sizing question, not fundamentals)

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. The thing to remember from the Q1 2026 print: TSMC just told you, in CFO English, that it is *adding fabs to a node it never adds fabs to*, that supply will be tight through 2027, and that demand visibility is the strongest C.C. Wei has ever called out. The biggest risk is the one you can't model. The biggest opportunity is the one the buy-side is still under-modelling: agents.
