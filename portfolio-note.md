# Portfolio Note: Concentrated Emerging-Market Quality Growth

This is not a generic emerging-markets portfolio. It is a concentrated collection of high-quality emerging-market and emerging-market-linked franchises, with the top 10 at roughly 57 percent and the top 20 at roughly 80 percent. The main buckets are: a very large semiconductor and AI compute supply-chain sleeve; a substantial digital platforms, marketplaces and software sleeve; a meaningful financials sleeve built around deposit franchises and trusted financial rails; and a smaller consumer and domestic-demand tail.

## Semiconductor Supply Chain: Compute Bottlenecks And Power Efficiency

This is the portfolio's most visible structural-growth exposure, roughly 31 percent across TSMC, SK Hynix, MediaTek, Samsung and Silergy. I would call it "semiconductor supply chain" or "AI compute supply chain" rather than "semis and equipment," because these are mostly foundry, memory, fabless design and power-management businesses rather than equipment makers. The common thread is ownership of the enabling layers of compute: the companies without which AI, mobile compute and electrification cannot scale.

TSMC, at 9.2 percent of the portfolio: the world's leading outsourced foundry, manufacturing advanced chips for Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm and others. Its advantage is process leadership, customer neutrality, scale, yield know-how and an ecosystem that is extraordinarily difficult to replicate.

SK Hynix, at 8.2 percent: a leading memory producer and one of the clearest beneficiaries of high-bandwidth memory demand for AI accelerators. Its distinctive flavour is high-bandwidth memory leadership, deep customer relevance to AI chipmakers and powerful operating leverage in a tight memory cycle.

MediaTek, at 7.0 percent: a fabless chip designer across smartphones, connectivity, TV, automotive and edge devices. Its advantage is strong cost and performance design capability, especially in Android and emerging-market device ecosystems where value, power efficiency and integration matter.

Samsung Electronics, at 5.1 percent: a global memory, logic, foundry and consumer-device giant. Its appeal is scale and optionality: even if execution has lagged in high-bandwidth memory, the balance sheet, research base and manufacturing breadth give it meaningful catch-up potential.

Silergy, at 1.9 percent: a power-management and analog semiconductor company. Its edge is application-specific know-how in power efficiency, a more important bottleneck as servers, devices, industrial systems and electric applications become increasingly power constrained.

## Digital Platforms, Marketplaces And Software

"Consumer internet" is fine shorthand, but it is not quite precise enough. This bucket includes social platforms, e-commerce, online travel, freight marketplaces, enterprise software and gaming. "Digital platforms and software" is cleaner. The common thread is network effects, data advantages, embedded payments, high-frequency usage or mission-critical software. These businesses often look very different by sector, but the shared economic feature is scale plus high incremental margins once competitive intensity normalizes.

Tencent, at 7.5 percent: China's social, gaming, payments and digital-services platform. Its core advantage is WeChat: a super-app distribution layer with messaging, payments, mini-programs, content, advertising and gaming attached.

MercadoLibre, at 4.0 percent: Latin America's leading e-commerce, payments, logistics and credit ecosystem. Its distinctive flavour is the flywheel between marketplace scale, Mercado Pago, fulfillment, merchant tools and consumer finance.

Prosus, at 3.7 percent: a holding company whose most important asset remains Tencent, alongside global classifieds, food delivery, fintech and edtech exposure. Its appeal is Tencent look-through, discount-narrowing potential and capital-allocation optionality.

TOTVS, at 3.6 percent: Brazil's leading enterprise resource planning and enterprise software provider. Its advantage is mission-critical software, high switching costs, recurring revenue and deep local specialization for small and medium-sized enterprises and vertical industries.

Sea, at 3.4 percent: Southeast Asia's e-commerce, gaming and fintech platform. Its edge is regional scale, Shopee's marketplace density, Garena's cash generation and improving discipline in digital financial services.

Full Truck Alliance, at 3.1 percent: a Chinese digital freight marketplace matching shippers and truckers. Its advantage is network density, route liquidity, data and lower search and transaction costs in a fragmented logistics market.

PDD, at 2.2 percent: a Chinese value e-commerce platform with Temu as international optionality. Its advantage is extreme cost discipline, merchant density, algorithmic merchandising and a sharp consumer value proposition.

Trip.com, at 2.2 percent: China's leading online travel agency. Its edge is brand, supplier relationships, user data and leverage to domestic, outbound and inbound travel recovery.

NetEase, at 1.8 percent: a Chinese gaming and content company. Its advantage is long-lived game intellectual property, in-house development capability and disciplined monetization.

KE Holdings, at 1.7 percent: China's housing transaction and brokerage platform. Its edge is agent-network scale, transaction data and standardized service quality in a historically fragmented property market.

FPT, at 0.6 percent: Vietnam's leading IT services and software company. Its distinctive flavour is Vietnam-as-a-technology-hub: low-cost engineering talent, outsourcing demand and domestic digitalization.

## Financials: Deposit-Led Compounding And Trusted Financial Rails

The common thread across the financials is not leverage for its own sake, but franchise quality. The banks are mostly dominant, high-quality deposit franchises: low-cost funding, customer primacy, conservative underwriting and long runways for formal credit growth. That deposit engine is what allows them to compound through cycles. Around that, the portfolio also owns insurers and infrastructure businesses that benefit from the same structural tailwinds: formalization, rising savings, underpenetrated insurance and digitized financial distribution.

Credicorp, at 4.5 percent: the Peruvian national champion. Its appeal is the combination of BCP's dominant deposit franchise, strong brand trust and multiple growth engines across banking, insurance, wealth and microfinance.

Nu Holdings, at 4.1 percent: the digital-native version of the deposit franchise story. It has built a massive low-cost customer base in Brazil and is now layering deposits, credit, payments and cross-sell across Latin America.

Capitec, at 2.3 percent: the South African retail disruptor that became the incumbent scale winner. Its edge is simplicity: a low-cost, highly efficient, mass-market banking model with extraordinary customer reach.

ICICI Lombard, at 2.2 percent: not a deposit franchise, but a high-quality underwriting and distribution franchise. It gives exposure to India's underpenetrated general insurance market through brand, data and claims capability.

Regional, at 2.1 percent: a local, relationship-led Mexican bank. The attraction is disciplined lending to small, medium and corporate borrowers, and potential benefit from nearshoring-driven investment in northern Mexico.

ICICI Bank, at 1.9 percent: the cleaned-up Indian private bank. The story is improved risk culture, strong deposit growth, granular lending and the ability to keep taking share from weaker public-sector banks.

CAMS, at 1.8 percent: financial infrastructure rather than balance-sheet financials. It is an oligopolistic picks-and-shovels play on Indian household savings moving into mutual funds and formal financial products.

HDFC Bank, at 1.7 percent: the benchmark Indian private-sector banking franchise. Its advantage is scale, low-cost deposits, underwriting discipline and one of the longest runways for formal retail and small-business credit growth.

Qualitas, at 1.4 percent: a specialist Mexican auto insurer. Its edge is focus: underwriting discipline, claims infrastructure and market knowledge in a category where execution is the moat.

Kotak Mahindra Bank, at 1.1 percent: the conservative compounder. It brings strong credit culture, affluent customers, capital strength and optionality across banking, broking, wealth and asset management.

Bank Central Asia, at 0.6 percent: probably the purest deposit franchise in the portfolio. Bank Central Asia is Indonesia's transaction-banking utility: very high-quality low-cost deposit funding, deep corporate and small-business relationships and a culture of conservatism.

The line to tie it together: these are not simply financial cyclicals; they are trusted financial franchises sitting on the liability side, distribution layer or infrastructure rails of underpenetrated financial systems.

## Consumer, Healthcare And Domestic Demand

This sleeve is smaller, but it gives the portfolio exposure to premiumization, formal retail, healthcare consumption, travel, hospitality and branded domestic demand. These are not all the same kind of consumer company; the commonality is that they convert rising income, formalization and better distribution into durable category leadership.

ANTA Sports, at 1.9 percent: China's leading domestic sportswear group. Its advantage is brand-building, local consumer insight, distribution and premiumization through Anta, Fila China and Amer Sports exposure.

H World, at 1.5 percent: a Chinese hotel operator. Its edge is franchise scale, a multi-brand ladder and operating leverage to domestic travel recovery.

Dino Polska, at 1.3 percent: a fast-growing Polish grocery retailer. Its advantage is dense local store rollout, efficient logistics, private-label economics and strong execution in smaller towns.

Shenzhen Mindray, at 0.9 percent: China's leading medical-equipment company. Its advantage is research and development, local distribution, cost competitiveness and import-substitution tailwinds in hospitals.

United Breweries, at 0.8 percent: India's leading beer company. Its advantage is Kingfisher brand strength, distribution and exposure to premiumization in a low-penetration beer category.

Raia Drogasil, at 0.6 percent: Brazil's leading pharmacy chain. Its advantage is store density, trusted health-retail brand, procurement scale and structural growth in formal pharmacy penetration.

Walmex, at about 0.9 percent: Walmart de Mexico, the dominant modern retailer in Mexico and Central America. Its edge is purchasing scale, logistics, price leadership and omnichannel reach.

Alsea, at about 0.4 percent: a restaurant operator running brands such as Starbucks, Domino's and Burger King across Mexico, Latin America and Europe. Its advantage is franchise operating scale, real-estate know-how and exposure to eating-out growth.

Tsingtao Brewery, at about 0.2 percent: a leading Chinese beer brand. Its edge is national brand heritage, broad distribution and premiumization within a mature but still brand-sensitive category.

Colgate-Palmolive India, at about 0.1 percent: India's oral-care leader. Its advantage is brand trust, distribution, habit formation and very high category share.

## Valuation And Derating Overlay

The portfolio has two return engines that are behaving very differently. The AI hardware sleeve has been rewarded because earnings upgrades are visible and immediate: high-bandwidth memory, advanced foundry and compute-related semiconductors have a clear demand narrative. But a lot of the non-AI portfolio has endured valuation compression even where the businesses themselves remain high quality.

That is the interesting tension. The market has been willing to pay up for visible AI earnings, while many emerging-market banks, platforms, consumer names and China-linked franchises have had to fight macro concern, China skepticism, higher discount rates or simple multiple compression. The result is that the portfolio is not only an AI portfolio. It has current earnings momentum from semiconductors, stable compounding from financial franchises, and rerating optionality in non-AI platforms and domestic-demand businesses where expectations have already been reset.

A useful closing line would be this: the portfolio owns both the bottlenecks of the new compute cycle and the old-fashioned compounding engines of emerging markets: deposits, distribution, software, brands and marketplaces. The near-term market has rewarded the former and derated much of the latter, but the underlying business quality across the non-AI sleeve remains far better than recent multiples imply.
