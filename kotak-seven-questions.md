# Kotak Mahindra Bank — The Seven Questions

Kotak Mahindra Bank is the twenty-eighth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at one-point-six percent, with a market cap of around forty-five billion US dollars. Here is Kotak Mahindra Bank in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Kotak Mahindra Bank just printed its third-quarter fiscal 2026 results. Consolidated profit after tax was forty-nine billion rupees, up five percent year-on-year and ten percent sequentially. Net advances grew sixteen percent year-on-year; average deposits grew fifteen percent. Customer assets under management reached seven-point-nine trillion rupees, up fifteen percent. Net interest margin held at four-point-five-four percent — among the highest of any major Indian bank. Book value per share grew fifteen percent year-on-year to one hundred and seventy-six rupees. Capital adequacy stood at twenty-two-point-six percent at the standalone bank level and twenty-three-point-three percent at the group. The bank completed the sale of a seventy-percent stake in its general insurance subsidiary to Zurich Insurance, generating a significant one-time gain. And gross non-performing assets held at one-point-three percent with provisioning coverage at seventy-six percent.

This is the founder-built Indian private bank in its first full cycle under new leadership, navigating succession while the group subsidiaries keep compounding.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Kotak Mahindra Bank is the fourth-largest private sector bank in India by market capitalisation, and the core of a financial services group that extends across banking, capital markets, asset management, life insurance, and consumer finance. The group has been built by Uday Kotak over forty years and represents the cleanest example of founder-led Indian financial services.

The scale is substantial. Net advances crossed a few trillion rupees after the sixteen-percent growth in the quarter. Average deposits of comparable size grew fifteen percent. Current-and-savings-account ratio at forty-one-point-three percent provides a low-cost funding base. The balance sheet is deeply over-capitalised — capital adequacy at twenty-two-point-six percent standalone is almost three times the regulatory minimum.

The subsidiary structure is what makes Kotak distinctive. Banking and lending entities contributed seventy-seven percent of consolidated profit after tax in the quarter. The remaining twenty-three percent came from diversified subsidiaries. The asset management business grew profit forty-seven percent year-on-year to four-and-a-half billion rupees with assets under management up nineteen percent. The life insurance business grew gross written premium fifteen percent. The general insurance business — formerly wholly-owned — has just been sold seventy percent to Zurich, reclassified as an associate, and generated a significant one-time gain. Investment banking, capital markets, and alternate assets added further diversified earnings.

The thing to internalise: Kotak is not a retail bank with a few subsidiary stakes. It is a diversified financial-services group where banking is the largest contributor but capital markets, asset management, and insurance are material and compounding. Each subsidiary is top-five in its category in India.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2023-to-2024 transition through two material stress events is the evidence.

Uday Kotak founded the firm in 1985 as Kotak Mahindra Finance and built it into a bank in 2003. He stepped down as Managing Director and Chief Executive in September 2023. Ashok Vaswani — a former Barclays senior executive with deep retail and digital banking experience — took over as Chief Executive in early 2024. Anup Saha was appointed to oversee Consumer Bank, data analytics, and marketing.

The past-stress case that matters is twofold. First, in April 2024 the Reserve Bank of India imposed an embargo on Kotak Bank accepting new customers through digital channels and issuing new credit cards, citing serious technology and governance deficiencies. This was a major regulatory intervention. Most observers viewed it as a multi-year overhang. Kotak responded by significantly upgrading its technology stack, strengthening its risk and compliance infrastructure, and engaging constructively with the regulator. The embargo was gradually eased through 2024 and 2025, with full restoration of normal business activity. Second, the founder transition from Uday Kotak to Ashok Vaswani required cultural rebuilding under a new, non-founder leader — a genuinely difficult transition that many Indian private banks have managed poorly in the past. By late 2025, under Vaswani, the bank was growing advances at sixteen percent, expanding net interest margin through cost-of-funds improvement, and generating diversified subsidiary earnings growth. That is the track record that matters.

The capital discipline signals from the latest print are consistent with quality. Gross non-performing assets at one-point-three percent. Net non-performing assets at zero-point-three-one percent. Provisioning coverage at seventy-six percent. Capital adequacy at twenty-two-point-six percent standalone — deeply over-capitalised. Book value growth at fifteen percent year-on-year. Cost-to-income ratio at forty-eight-point-three percent, elevated modestly by a one-time labour-code provision of nine-point-six billion rupees at the bank and twelve-point-eight billion at the group; excluding that one-off the cost-to-income ratio was forty-seven-point-four. The sale of the general insurance stake to Zurich is a capital-allocation signal — Kotak retains a thirty-percent associate stake while accessing Zurich's global expertise in general insurance. The share sub-division from five-rupee face value to one-rupee face value completed in January 2026, improving retail liquidity.

The watch item is succession depth. Vaswani has been in role for less than two years. Execution continuity and the depth of leadership below him are the multi-year quality tests.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is Indian retail and SME banking growth. Net advances grew sixteen percent year-on-year. The SME book grew seventeen percent to over one-point-one trillion rupees. Tractor finance — a specific growth segment — grew sixteen percent. Indian credit penetration remains well below regional peers; Kotak is taking share with a combination of digital capability, relationship-management coverage, and disciplined underwriting.

The second engine is net interest margin expansion. Net interest margin at four-point-five-four percent is genuinely high by Indian private-bank standards, up from lower levels earlier in the year. Cost of funds declined from five-point-zero-one percent in the first quarter to four-point-five-four percent in the third quarter — a very meaningful improvement. Further improvement is expected in the fourth quarter as the Reserve Bank of India cash reserve ratio cut takes full effect. Stable net interest margin is expected from the first quarter of the next fiscal year.

The third engine is the subsidiary portfolio. Asset management profit up forty-seven percent; life insurance gross written premium up fifteen percent; capital markets and alternate asset businesses scaling. Each subsidiary is a meaningful earnings contributor with its own category tailwind. As the subsidiaries compound, the group's consolidated earnings grow faster than the core bank alone would suggest.

The kicker is the Zurich general insurance partnership. By selling seventy percent to Zurich while retaining a thirty-percent associate stake, Kotak accesses global insurance expertise while crystallising capital. That capital can be redeployed into higher-return segments of the group — retail lending, digital infrastructure, or further M&A in asset management or securities. This kind of portfolio pruning is exactly what a disciplined financial-services holding company should do over time.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, net advances growth — sixteen percent; sustained double-digit growth confirms the franchise is gaining. Second, net interest margin — four-point-five-four percent; stability above four-point-three through the cycle confirms the spread engine. Third, gross non-performing assets — one-point-three percent; maintenance below one-point-seven confirms underwriting discipline. Fourth, cost-to-income ratio — forty-seven-point-four percent excluding the labour-code one-off; progression toward forty-five percent or lower confirms operating leverage. Fifth, subsidiary earnings contribution — twenty-three percent of consolidated profit after tax; continued growth confirms diversification. Sixth, capital adequacy — twenty-two-point-six percent; stability or continued over-capitalisation confirms balance-sheet discipline.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Third-quarter fiscal 2026 consolidated profit after tax was forty-nine billion rupees on a quarterly basis; annualised run-rate is in the low two hundred billion. Book value per share is one hundred and seventy-six rupees. Group net worth is seventeen-and-a-half trillion rupees. Market cap is around forty to forty-five billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, Kotak trades at roughly two-and-a-half to three times book value and around eighteen to twenty times consolidated earnings. For a top-four Indian private bank with twenty-two-percent capital adequacy, one-point-three-percent gross non-performing assets, and diversified subsidiary compounding, that is a reasonable multiple but below the HDFC Bank premium. The gap reflects the recent regulatory overhang and the founder transition.

The simple framing: a top-tier Indian private bank and financial services group at a discount to HDFC Bank on comparable operating metrics, with subsidiaries that are top-five in their categories and with the post-Uday Kotak transition progressing. Comparable set: HDFC Bank at three-to-four times book; ICICI Bank at similar multiples; Axis Bank at lower multiples.

The key sensitivity is the Indian macro and Reserve Bank of India rate cycle. Further rate cuts would compress net interest margin. Second sensitivity is regulatory evolution — any renewed regulatory intervention on the digital or credit card business would compress sentiment.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus still prices in a Kotak discount to HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank based on the 2024 RBI embargo overhang. The variant is that the embargo has been resolved, the technology and compliance upgrade is complete, and the bank is now growing advances at sixteen percent with a four-and-a-half-percent net interest margin and improving subsidiary earnings. If consolidated earnings growth continues at low-teens rates with stable asset quality, the multiple discount relative to HDFC Bank should narrow over time.

Second: the subsidiary portfolio is priced as a modest consolidated premium rather than as distinct earnings compounders. Kotak AMC alone, as one of the top three Indian asset managers, would command a material standalone multiple. Kotak Life, Kotak Securities, and the alternate assets business similarly. The Zurich general insurance transaction demonstrates that these subsidiaries can be crystallised at attractive valuations — which is a pattern that could repeat.

The honest counter: the Uday Kotak succession is a real risk. Founder-led Indian financial services have a mixed track record through founder transitions. Vaswani has been in role less than two years; the real test is a full cycle under his leadership. Position sizing should reflect that the leadership transition has multi-year uncertainty.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Reserve Bank of India regulatory action. Any new regulatory intervention on digital onboarding, credit cards, or capital adequacy would compress earnings and sentiment. Two: Indian credit cycle. Unemployment rising, rural cash flow stress, or MSME distress could elevate non-performing asset formation. Three: net interest margin compression. Deeper Reserve Bank of India rate cuts compress the spread engine. Four: Vaswani execution. Any sign of cultural drift, strategic mis-steps, or material operational issues under the new CEO. Five: Subsidiary integration or performance. Asset management, life insurance, or securities business hiccups could compress consolidated earnings. Six: Indian equity market concentration. Any de-rating wave in Indian banks would hit Kotak alongside peers.

Specific sell triggers. Gross non-performing assets rising above two percent for two consecutive quarters. Net interest margin falling below four percent for two consecutive quarters. Net advances growth decelerating below ten percent. Cost-to-income ratio expanding above fifty-five percent sustainably. Subsidiary earnings contribution declining materially. Ashok Vaswani stepping back without a named, credible successor.
