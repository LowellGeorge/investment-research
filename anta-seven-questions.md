# ANTA Sports — The Seven Questions

ANTA Sports is the sixteenth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at two-point-two percent, with a market cap of around thirty billion US dollars. Here is ANTA in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

ANTA just closed 2025 with full-year revenue of eighty-point-two billion yuan, up thirteen-point-three percent — the twelfth consecutive year of growth. Adjusted profit grew thirteen-point-nine percent to thirteen-point-six billion yuan. Operating margin expanded to twenty-three-point-eight percent. Free cash flow reached sixteen-point-one billion yuan, up twenty-one-and-a-half percent. Net cash on the balance sheet is thirty-one-point-seven billion yuan. Domestic Chinese sportswear market share expanded another percentage point to twenty-one percent. The dividend payout ratio held at fifty percent of adjusted profit. And the other-brands cluster — which is DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, and the Amer Sports-derived portfolio — grew nearly sixty percent to nineteen billion yuan.

This is Nike meets LVMH, built for the Chinese consumer, with an Amer Sports global asset attached. The question is whether the market is pricing the compounding right.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

ANTA Sports is a multi-brand Chinese sportswear group that has become the number-one local player by market share and is one of the top three globally by revenue.

The portfolio has three structural layers. The first is the core ANTA brand, the mass-market and performance-oriented Chinese sportswear label with revenue of thirty-four-point-eight billion yuan for the year, up three-point-seven percent, and operating margin above twenty percent. This is the scale foundation.

The second is FILA, the premium and athleisure brand ANTA acquired in 2009 and has since turned into one of the most profitable sportswear franchises in Asia. FILA revenue for 2025 was twenty-eight-point-five billion yuan, up six-point-nine percent, with operating margin above twenty-five percent and particular strength in tennis and golf categories.

The third is the other-brands cluster — DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, and the Amer Sports-derived portfolio following the 2019 consortium acquisition. DESCENTE grew thirty-four-and-a-half percent. KOLON SPORT traffic grew seventy percent with hiking-shoe sales up one hundred and forty percent. The overseas business grew seventy percent to eight hundred and fifty million yuan, with ANTA's first North American flagship opening in 2025. Online revenue now represents thirty-six percent of total, up fifteen-and-a-half percent year-on-year.

The thing to internalise: ANTA is not a Chinese apparel company. It is a disciplined multi-brand operator applying a proven playbook — Chinese-first commercial operations on globally-acquired brand portfolios — with the scale and capital to keep buying and integrating strategic brands. The Amer Sports acquisition in 2019 is the most prominent example of this playbook working.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2021-to-2023 FILA inventory cycle is the clearest evidence.

Ding Shizhong is co-founder and Chief Executive Officer. The Ding family controls the company. The broader founding team has remained operationally active across the two-decade history.

The past-stress case that matters is 2021 to 2023. Chinese domestic lockdowns disrupted offline retail through 2022. FILA, the higher-margin brand, accumulated inventory as discretionary spending collapsed. Competitors — Li Ning in particular — similarly suffered but also took aggressive promotional action that hurt brand equity. ANTA's response was disciplined: they tightened buying, worked inventory down through channel quality management rather than through aggressive discounting, protected FILA's premium brand positioning, and used the period to deepen integration of the 2019 Amer Sports acquisition. By 2024 FILA had recovered margin, the other-brands cluster was firing, and ANTA's market share in China was expanding. That is the capital-allocation and brand-stewardship discipline that compounds.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are consistent. Free cash flow of sixteen-point-one billion yuan, up twenty-one-and-a-half percent. Net cash of thirty-one-point-seven billion on the balance sheet. Dividend payout held at fifty percent of adjusted profit — management is returning half of every yuan of profit to shareholders. Capital expenditure was two-point-seven billion yuan and acquisition spending was two-point-two billion. R&D investment was two-point-two billion yuan, including AI-driven design capabilities that generated nine billion yuan of product orders and improved sales conversion by twenty percent. ESG progress is measurable: MSCI AA rating, sixty-five-percent reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions, inclusion in the Hang Seng and Dow Jones Sustainability Indices.

The watch item is inventory. At one hundred and thirty-seven days, inventory life rose fourteen days year-on-year — a visible concession by management. The inventory-to-sales ratio of five times is still described as healthy, but track the first-half 2026 print carefully for normalisation.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is the core ANTA brand's Chinese market-share compounding. ANTA's domestic market share expanded one percentage point to twenty-one percent in 2025, despite a consumer-confidence index still at low levels around eighty-nine-point-six. Each point of market-share gain against Nike, Adidas and Li Ning is a large absolute revenue contribution given the Chinese sportswear market's size. The brand's positioning straddles mass-market cost-effectiveness and premium performance — the exact positioning Chinese consumers are increasingly rewarding.

The second engine is the multi-brand portfolio playbook. FILA's operating margin above twenty-five percent tells you what happens when ANTA takes an acquired brand and applies its commercial discipline — brand equity remains, unit economics improve. The other-brands cluster growing nearly sixty percent at still-emerging scale tells you the playbook is still early across DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, and the Amer Sports-derived labels. Each brand adds an independent compounding leg.

The third engine is the Chinese outdoor-sports structural tailwind. KOLON SPORT traffic up seventy percent and hiking shoes up one hundred and forty percent is not a macro demand story; it is a category shift. Chinese consumers are moving aggressively into outdoor, hiking, skiing, tennis and golf — categories where ANTA's premium and specialty brands are already positioned. DESCENTE revenue growth of thirty-four-and-a-half percent with store efficiency above two-point-seven million yuan per store confirms the structural shift.

The kicker is the overseas and AI levers. Overseas revenue grew seventy percent from a small base, with the first North American flagship opened. If even a modest US and European international footprint emerges, the addressable market expands dramatically. Separately, AI-driven design is already commercial — nine billion yuan of generated product orders and twenty-percent conversion improvement — giving ANTA an efficiency advantage on design-to-shelf cycles.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, Chinese market share — twenty-one percent today, continued expansion toward twenty-three-to-twenty-five percent is the core-brand compounder signal. Second, FILA operating margin — above twenty-five percent today, maintenance through the cycle confirms the premium brand discipline. Third, other-brands revenue growth — plus fifty-nine percent in 2025, sustained thirty-plus-percent growth confirms the multi-brand playbook at emerging scale. Fourth, overseas revenue growth — plus seventy percent from a small base, tracking absolute revenue toward two billion yuan is the international kicker signal. Fifth, inventory days — one hundred and thirty-seven today, up fourteen; normalisation below one-hundred-and-thirty is the discipline test. Sixth, dividend payout ratio — fifty percent today, maintenance or increase signals capital-return commitment.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 adjusted profit was thirteen-point-six billion yuan. Basic earnings per share were four-point-eight-nine yuan. Free cash flow was sixteen-point-one billion. Net cash was thirty-one-point-seven billion. Market cap is roughly thirty billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, ANTA trades at roughly fifteen to eighteen times trailing earnings and closer to twelve to fourteen times ex-cash. For a multi-brand sportswear compounder with fifty-percent payout, free cash flow growing twenty-plus percent, and the Amer Sports-derived portfolio still emerging, that is a compressed multiple.

The simple framing: a multi-brand sportswear compounder running at twenty-four-percent operating margin with a global comp set trading at double the multiple. Nike at roughly twenty-five times forward, Lululemon at thirty-plus times, Adidas at similar ranges. ANTA's Chinese-listed, Hong Kong-traded status creates a persistent discount, but the operational profile is directly comparable to the global leaders.

The key sensitivity is Chinese consumer spending. A sharper consumer-confidence deterioration compresses core ANTA and FILA margins simultaneously. A second sensitivity is the inventory normalisation — if the fourteen-day expansion does not reverse by mid-2026, it signals channel health is weakening.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: the market prices ANTA primarily as a Chinese domestic consumer play, when it is actually a proven multi-brand operator with one of the clearest capital-allocation track records in Asian consumer. The Amer Sports acquisition in 2019 — widely criticised at the time — has been a multi-bagger for the consortium, and ANTA retained equity that it now consolidates into the other-brands cluster growing sixty percent. The multi-brand playbook is validated. Comparable global sportswear names trade at materially higher multiples for worse capital-allocation records.

Second: the outdoor-sports structural shift in China is underweighted by consensus. KOLON SPORT traffic up seventy percent and hiking-shoe sales up one hundred and forty percent is not a one-quarter fluke. Chinese consumer behaviour is shifting toward experiential, specialty, and premium categories at exactly the time ANTA has assembled the brand portfolio to capture it. This is a category-mix tailwind on top of the market-share compounder.

The honest counter: Chinese consumer cycles are real and the inventory build is a real signal that discretionary demand is not as strong as the top-line suggests. Another year of consumer weakness could compress margins. And Li Ning's competitive response, combined with Nike's inventory-backed promotional pressure, are live threats to the ANTA brand's share-gain trajectory.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Chinese consumer spending deterioration. A deeper and longer consumer recession compresses FILA and core ANTA margins simultaneously. Two: inventory cycle. One hundred and thirty-seven days is manageable; a further fifteen-to-twenty-day expansion would signal serious channel distress. Three: competitive escalation. Li Ning's brand turnaround, Nike and Adidas's inventory-backed promotional response, or a domestic insurgent taking share in premium categories. Four: international expansion execution. The North American flagship and European ambitions are capital-consumptive and execution-risky. Five: Amer Sports consolidation accounting complexity. The consortium structure and accounting treatment of Amer Sports-derived revenue and profit could create earnings-reporting volatility. Six: founder-family governance. The Ding family controls the company; succession and alignment with external shareholders is a structural watch item.

Specific sell triggers. Core ANTA brand operating margin falling below eighteen percent for two consecutive reporting periods. FILA operating margin breaking below twenty percent for two consecutive reporting periods. Other-brands revenue growth decelerating below twenty percent for two consecutive reporting periods. Inventory days exceeding one hundred and sixty for two consecutive reporting periods. Chinese domestic market share flat or declining for two consecutive reporting periods. Dividend payout ratio cut below forty-five percent without a clear capital-allocation rationale.
