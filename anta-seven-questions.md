# ANTA Sports — Seven Questions

An investment summary of ANTA Sports — China's largest sportswear group, Hong Kong listed, whose brands include ANTA, FILA, and Amer Sports — prepared on the 2nd of July 2026, through a lens of quality and long-term ownership.

## Question One: Business Description — What Is It?

ANTA Sports is China's largest sportswear company and a global top-three player by revenue. Financial year 2025 revenue reached 80.2 billion renminbi, up 13.3 percent, a record, with an estimated share of about 21.8 percent of the Chinese sportswear market. Founded in 1991 in Jinjiang, Fujian, and listed in Hong Kong in 2007, it runs a deliberately segmented "single-focus, multi-brand, globalisation" strategy: the ANTA brand serves mass-market sports performance; FILA — with the China, Hong Kong, and Macao rights — owns premium sports fashion; and DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, JACK WOLFSKIN, and MAIA ACTIVE cover high-end outdoor and specialist niches. The group pairs brand ownership with direct retail and e-commerce, at 35.8 percent of revenue, under its "Brand plus Retail" model. Beyond the consolidated group, ANTA is the largest shareholder of New-York-Stock-Exchange-listed Amer Sports — Arc'teryx, Salomon, Wilson — with roughly 42 to 43 percent, and in January 2026 agreed to buy 29.06 percent of PUMA from Groupe Artémis for about 1.5 billion euros in cash.

## Question Two: Management and Governance — Can We Trust Them?

This is a founder-led, family-controlled business: chairman Ding Shizhong has run ANTA since inception, his brother Ding Shijia is co-founder, and day-to-day leadership moved to co-CEOs Lai Shixian and Wu Yonghua in the 2023 management restructure. The family retains majority control. The capital-allocation record is unusually good for the sector: FILA, acquired as a loss-making licence in 2009, is now a 28.5 billion renminbi revenue business earning an operating margin of about 26 percent; DESCENTE passed 10 billion renminbi of retail sales in 2025; and the 2019 Amer consortium deal delivered Arc'teryx and Salomon exposure ahead of the outdoor boom. Financial discipline is visible in the numbers — net cash of 31.7 billion renminbi, free cash flow of 16.1 billion renminbi, up 21.5 percent, and a stable payout of about 50 percent — and the sustainability track record, with the MSCI ESG rating upgraded to double-A, supports the quality case.

The caveats are the familiar ones for a family-controlled acquirer: minority shareholders ride on the family's judgement, and the group's appetite for large minority stakes in Western brands — Amer then, PUMA now — concentrates key-man and empire-building risk. The PUMA stake is a 1.5 billion euro bet on a troubled turnaround without control. Track record earns trust; capital allocation outside China is the item to keep under continuous review.

## Question Three: Investment Thesis — Why Does Value Compound?

A proven multi-brand compounding machine: ANTA's core skill is acquiring brands and scaling them through Chinese retail — FILA, DESCENTE, and KOLON SPORT are successive proofs. The "all other brands" segment grew 59.2 percent in financial year 2025 to 17.0 billion renminbi at an operating margin of about 28 percent, and is now the group's growth engine.

A structural tailwind: Chinese sports apparel consumption grew about 6 percent in 2025 against about 3.7 percent total retail sales growth, driven by sports participation, the outdoor boom, and premiumisation — with per-capita sportswear spend still well below developed markets.

A share gainer in a consolidating market: market share reached a record of about 21.8 percent in 2025, up about a point, taken largely from global incumbents; scale advantages in retail, marketing, and research and development, at 2.2 billion renminbi, reinforce the lead.

High-quality economics: 62 percent gross margin, 23.8 percent operating margin, up 0.4 percentage points, 16.1 billion renminbi free cash flow, and a large net cash position fund both the payout of about 50 percent and strategic optionality without leverage.

And globalisation optionality: the Amer stake compounds outside the China consumer cycle; ANTA-brand overseas revenue grew 70 percent, albeit from a small base of 850 million renminbi; and the PUMA stake — if execution follows the FILA playbook of patience plus operating discipline — adds a further leg the market currently values at nil or negative.

On segment economics, the portfolio in one view for financial year 2025: the ANTA brand generated 34.8 billion renminbi of revenue, up 3.7 percent, with 7.2 billion renminbi of operating profit at a 20.7 percent margin. FILA generated 28.5 billion renminbi, up 6.9 percent, with 7.4 billion renminbi of operating profit at a 26.1 percent margin. And all other brands generated 17.0 billion renminbi, up 59.2 percent, with 4.7 billion renminbi of operating profit at a 27.9 percent margin. Those figures come from the ANTA financial-year-2025 results announcement of the 25th of March 2026; other brands include DESCENTE, KOLON SPORT, and JACK WOLFSKIN, consolidated in mid-2025, and segment profit is before headquarters and unallocated items.

The mix is shifting toward the faster-growing, higher-margin outdoor and premium brands, while the core ANTA brand — still the largest segment — has slowed to low-single-digit growth. The thesis rests on the portfolio effect: no single brand needs to be heroic for the group to compound at a low-double-digit rate.

## Question Four: Key Milestones and KPIs — What Proves Us Right, and What to Track?

Core-brand reacceleration — ANTA-brand retail sales swung from a low-single-digit decline in the fourth quarter of 2025 to high-single-digit growth in the first quarter of 2026; management attributed part of the lift to Lunar New Year timing, so the first-half 2026 results, due the 27th of August 2026, are the honest test.

FILA momentum — low-teens first-quarter retail growth against a fashion-cycle-exposed base; watch it holding above segment-average growth with margin intact.

Other-brands trajectory — 40 to 45 percent first-quarter retail growth; DESCENTE store efficiency, at more than 2.7 million renminbi per store, and JACK WOLFSKIN integration are the sub-plots.

Inventory discipline — inventory days rose 14 to 137 in financial year 2025; a 5-times inventory-to-sales ratio is still healthy, but this is the first metric to break in a China consumer downturn.

Capital allocation — the PUMA close, expected end-2026, any move on the remaining PUMA and Amer stakes, and payout stability at about 50 percent.

And a consensus check, from FactSet — the Street models earnings per share of 5.96 renminbi in estimated financial year 2026, rising to 7.14 renminbi by financial year 2028, about 9 to 10 percent a year after financial-year-2026's normalisation; rating consensus is a buy — 32 buys and 7 overweights of 41 analysts, no sells.

On the reported financials, in renminbi: revenue rose from 62.4 billion in financial year 2023, to 70.8 billion in 2024, to 80.2 billion in 2025. Operating profit went from 15.4 billion, to 16.6 billion, to 19.1 billion, with operating margin running 24.6 percent, then 23.4 percent, then 23.8 percent. Net profit was 11.3 billion, then 17.0 billion, then 15.7 billion, and basic earnings per share 3.69 renminbi, then 5.55, then 4.89. Those come from ANTA annual reports via Quartr; financial-year-2024 net profit is flattered by one-off Amer listing gains, while financial year 2025 absorbs a 2.89 billion renminbi FX loss. On the company's comparable basis, financial-year-2025 attributable profit rose 13.9 percent to 13.59 billion renminbi.

## Question Five: Five-Year Fair Value and Return — the Team's View

The starting point does a lot of work: at 71.05 Hong Kong dollars, on the 30th of June 2026, the shares trade at roughly 11 times estimated financial-year-2026 consensus earnings, with roughly a sixth of the market cap in net cash, and the payout of about 50 percent implies a dividend yield approaching 4 percent — a multiple far below the stock's own history for a business still growing earnings high-single to low-double digits. This is not an obviously cyclical business, but China consumer sentiment does drive the cycle around the trend, so anchor on trend earnings rather than any single year.

Illustrative five-year scenarios, as placeholders for the team's house numbers. The bull case: core brand reaccelerates, outdoor brands and Amer compound, and the PUMA turnaround takes hold — low-teens earnings-per-share growth and a re-rating toward 17 to 18 times gives an internal rate of return of about 20 percent or more. The base case: consensus-like growth of about 9 to 10 percent in earnings per share and a partial re-rating to about 14 to 15 times gives a mid-teens internal rate of return including the dividend. The bear case: China consumer stagnates, FILA fades, and PUMA absorbs cash and attention — mid-single-digit earnings-per-share growth with the multiple stuck near 11 times still returns roughly the dividend plus growth, a mid-to-high-single-digit internal rate of return. The skew is attractive because the entry multiple already prices much of the bear case.

## Question Six: Variant Perception — Where the Market Is Wrong

The market prices ANTA as a China consumer cyclical with acquisition risk — the multiple compressed through 2025 and 2026 even as the company printed record revenue, record share, and rising cash returns, and the January PUMA announcement reinforced the "empire-builder" narrative. The variant view: ANTA is a multi-brand operating platform with a repeatable, demonstrated brand-scaling capability — the relevant comparison is a well-run portfolio compounder, not a single-brand apparel cyclical. The portfolio is migrating toward premium outdoor segments that carry higher margins and longer runways, the balance sheet means the globalisation bets are being made from strength, and at about 11 times earnings the market is paying for the China mass-market business and giving the optionality away. Where the market may be right: the core ANTA brand grew only 3.7 percent in financial year 2025, and its fourth-quarter decline shows it is not immune to share loss at the mass end — the variant view requires the portfolio, not the founding brand, to carry growth from here.

## Question Seven: Risks, Premortem, and Sell Discipline

The premortem — if this goes wrong in five years, the likely causes are these. China consumer malaise: consumer confidence remains depressed, with the index at about 89.6; a prolonged downturn caps growth across every brand at once. Core-brand erosion: the ANTA brand loses relevance to Li Ning, Xtep, returning global brands, or new entrants at the mass end — the fourth-quarter 2025 decline was the warning shot. Fashion risk at FILA: a third of group revenue rests on a trend-exposed premium fashion brand that has slowed before. Multi-brand overreach: complexity and inventory build, at 137 days, up 14, as the brand count rises, with integration costs at JACK WOLFSKIN and beyond. Capital-allocation drift: 1.5 billion euros into a PUMA minority stake with no control; a follow-on takeover of a structurally challenged Western brand would be the escalation to fear. And governance and geopolitics: majority family control limits minority influence, and US-China tensions could touch Amer, PUMA approvals, or Chinese consumer sentiment toward the portfolio's Western brands.

Sell discipline: trim or exit on, first, ANTA-brand retail sales declining for two or more consecutive quarters outside a macro shock; second, inventory persistently above about 150 days, or discounting that pulls group gross margin decisively below about 58 percent; third, capital allocation escalating into control transactions for troubled Western brands at demanding prices; or fourth, any break in the payout of about 50 percent without a clearly better use of cash. Position sizing should respect that the growth engine is now the acquired-brand portfolio rather than the founding brand.
