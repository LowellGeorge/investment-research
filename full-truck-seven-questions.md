# Full Truck Alliance — The Seven Questions

Full Truck Alliance is the seventeenth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at two-and-a-half percent, with a market cap of roughly fourteen to fifteen billion US dollars. Here is Full Truck Alliance in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Full Truck Alliance just closed 2025 with full-year fulfilled orders of two hundred and thirty-six million, up twenty percent. Full-year net income of four-and-a-half billion yuan, up forty-three percent. Fourth-quarter net income grew seventy-three percent year-on-year. Non-GAAP adjusted operating margin expanded from twenty-seven percent in 2024 to thirty-six percent in 2025 — nine hundred basis points of margin expansion in one year. Free cash flow reached four-and-a-half billion yuan, up from two-point-nine. The company announced a medium-to-long-term shareholder return plan of four hundred million US dollars for 2026. And, critically, the platform now covers one hundred percent of prefecture-level cities in China.

The market still largely treats this as a post-cybersecurity-review, post-IPO-bust Chinese internet name. The 2025 numbers say that framing is three years out of date. The right frame is a profitable logistics-platform compounder with capital returns starting.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Full Truck Alliance is the dominant digital freight-matching platform in China. Think of it as the Uber of long-haul trucking — it connects shippers who need to move cargo with truckers who have spare capacity, intermediates the price, and takes a cut.

The scale is genuinely national. Full-year 2025 fulfilled orders were two hundred and thirty-six million. Monthly active shippers averaged three-point-one-four million, up nineteen percent. The platform now covers one hundred percent of prefecture-level cities in China, spanning over three hundred cities. Fourth-quarter fulfilment rate — the percentage of posted orders that get matched and completed — reached forty-three percent, a new record and up more than five percentage points year-on-year.

The revenue structure has three layers. The transaction-service layer is commission on matched freight; this grew thirty-eight percent in 2025 to just over five billion yuan. The freight-matching-services layer is broader matching and membership revenue, about ten-and-a-half billion yuan for the year. The value-added layer includes credit solutions to truckers and shippers, insurance, advertising, and the consolidation of Giga.AI Technology — nearly two billion yuan, with the credit book now at a controlled ninety-day delinquency ratio of two-point-nine percent.

The thing to internalise: Full Truck Alliance is not a freight company. It owns no trucks, no warehouses, no terminals. It is a software platform on the trucking industry that has reached national saturation and is now monetising through take-rate expansion, value-added services, and capital returns. That shape is structurally different from any Chinese internet peer.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2021-to-2023 cybersecurity review is the cleanest evidence.

Peter Zhang — Zhang Hui — is co-founder and Chief Executive. The company was formed in 2017 through the merger of Yunmanman and Huochebang, the two dominant Chinese digital freight platforms. It listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2021.

The past-stress case that matters is 2021 to 2023. Full Truck Alliance IPO'd in June 2021 at twenty dollars per share, raised one-and-a-half billion dollars. Weeks later the Chinese Cyberspace Administration launched a cybersecurity review, following the same playbook it had used against DiDi. New user registration on the platform was suspended. The stock fell more than seventy percent from the IPO price. Management did not lay off, did not cut technology investment, and did not panic. They complied with the review, resumed normal operations within twelve months, used the trough to deepen the platform's share and begin the fulfilment-rate improvement cycle, and retained the founding technical leadership. By 2024 the platform was gaining share and expanding monetisation. By 2025 the company is posting forty-three-percent net-income growth and announcing four-hundred-million-dollar capital returns. The patience through stress is real.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are specific. Non-GAAP operating margin stepped from twenty-seven percent to thirty-six percent in one year. Free cash flow grew from two-point-nine to four-and-a-half billion yuan. Cash and investments at year-end reached thirty-one-and-a-half billion yuan, about four-point-four billion US dollars. Management completed the regulated transition of all credit solutions to interest rates at or below twenty-six percent, in full compliance with Chinese guidance, without a material earnings hit. They distributed approximately two hundred million US dollars of dividends in 2025 and bought back fifty-two-and-a-half million dollars of stock since the start of the year. For 2026 they have announced a four-hundred-million-dollar shareholder return plan, including an eighty-seven-and-a-half-million-dollar first-quarter dividend.

The watch item is governance transparency. As with other Chinese platform names, board independence, related-party disclosure, and regulatory-review cadence are the structural discount. Worth tracking any further disclosure enhancements.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is take-rate expansion on a network that has reached geographic saturation. One hundred percent prefecture-level city coverage means the user-acquisition phase is largely over; the next ten years are about deepening monetisation per order. Commission penetration in the fourth quarter reached eighty-eight-point-six percent, up six percentage points year-on-year — meaning most matched orders now pay a commission. Average monetisation per order was twenty-six-point-three yuan in the fourth quarter. Every one-percent improvement in fulfilment rate, commission penetration, or per-order economics drops almost entirely to operating margin because the technology infrastructure is fixed.

The second engine is value-added services compounding on the installed base. Credit solutions to truckers — who use the platform daily and whose earnings flow through the platform — is a uniquely low-risk fintech opportunity, with a ninety-day delinquency ratio of two-point-nine percent even after the regulatory rate cap. Insurance, fuel, parts, and payment services are all on-platform adjacencies that multinationals cannot replicate. Giga.AI consolidation adds an artificial-intelligence layer — AI assistants for shippers, AI-routing for dispatch — that improves matching efficiency and reinforces platform stickiness.

The third engine is operating leverage. Non-GAAP adjusted operating margin at thirty-six percent in 2025 is already high, but the trajectory is what matters: the nine hundred basis points of expansion in one year tells you the underlying unit economics are still improving rapidly. If order growth stays in the teens, margin expansion this year suggests there is still another thousand basis points of potential margin over three years.

The kicker is the overseas expansion, deliberately asset-light. Management has telegraphed that they are targeting markets with structural similarities to China's trucking market a decade ago — fragmented, analogue, capital-light to enter. If even one of those international bets works, it is incremental growth at no meaningful capital cost. If none do, the capital return programme absorbs it.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, fulfilment rate — forty-three percent in the fourth quarter, a record; continued expansion toward fifty percent confirms the matching flywheel is still compounding. Second, commission penetration — eighty-eight-point-six percent; saturation at above ninety is the take-rate ceiling signal. Third, non-GAAP adjusted operating margin — thirty-six percent today; continued expansion toward the low forties confirms the operating-leverage path. Fourth, monthly active shippers growth — three-point-one million, up nineteen percent; this is the demand-side network-effects signal. Fifth, credit solutions ninety-day delinquency — two-point-nine percent; sustained below four percent confirms credit discipline through the regulated rate-cap environment. Sixth, capital return cadence — four-hundred-million US dollars announced for 2026; watch for a scaling up in 2027 and beyond.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 non-GAAP adjusted net income was just under five billion yuan — about seven hundred million US dollars. Full-year GAAP net income was four-and-a-half billion yuan. Free cash flow was four-and-a-half billion yuan. Cash and investments are thirty-one-and-a-half billion yuan, roughly four-and-a-half billion US dollars — nearly a third of the market cap.

On headline multiples the stock trades at roughly twenty times trailing earnings and closer to fifteen times on an ex-cash basis. For a business with forty-plus-percent net-income growth, nine-hundred-basis-points of margin expansion in one year, and free-cash-flow yield above five percent pre-dividend, that is a reasonable-to-compressed multiple.

The simple framing: a platform monopoly over Chinese digital freight, operating at thirty-six percent margins with teen-percent order growth and just-starting capital returns, trading at mid-teens ex-cash earnings multiple. Comps: Uber's mobility and delivery segments trade at much higher multiples on lower margin and slower growth. Descartes Systems Group, the Canadian logistics software compounder, trades at roughly forty times forward. Full Truck Alliance's multiple reflects the Chinese-internet structural discount, not the business quality.

The key sensitivity is Chinese freight-demand volume. Freight volume is pro-cyclical to Chinese industrial production and consumer goods output; a deeper slowdown compresses both take-rate progression and operating leverage.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus still prices Full Truck Alliance as a post-IPO-bust, cybersecurity-review casualty. The variant is that the cybersecurity review ended over two years ago, the platform has since gained share, expanded operating margin nine hundred basis points in twelve months, and is now announcing multi-hundred-million-dollar capital returns. A forty-three-percent net-income-growth business returning capital aggressively is not a recovery story; it is a profitable compounder. The comparable set is global logistics-software platforms like Descartes Systems Group and JB Hunt's asset-light businesses, not damaged Chinese ADR names.

Second: the value-added services layer — credit, insurance, AI-assistants, Giga.AI — is priced as zero despite being the highest-margin revenue. Chinese regulatory capping of credit rates to twenty-six percent has been absorbed without hitting delinquency or profit meaningfully. Monetising a captive-truck-driver base with on-platform financial services is an unusually clean fintech position. The market is not paying for it.

The honest counter: Chinese-internet structural discount is persistent and may not close even with perfect operational performance. Regulatory risk on freight pricing, platform intermediation, or cross-border data flow could resurface. Position sizing should reflect that the re-rating thesis has a structural ceiling in a complex geopolitical climate.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: Chinese freight volume slowdown. Fulfilled orders growth could compress sharply if industrial production slows, and operating leverage reverses into operating deleverage. Two: regulatory return. Another cybersecurity review, a cap on platform commission rates, or a new credit-solutions restriction would compress both earnings and multiple. Three: competitive intrusion. Logistics-platform competition from JD Logistics, Cainiao, and newer entrants could cap take-rate expansion. Four: credit-solutions cycle. The delinquency ratio is low today at two-point-nine percent, but a sharper Chinese small-business downturn would stress the trucker-borrower base. Five: capital-allocation misstep. Overseas expansion is asset-light in plan; a larger M&A surprise would change the story. Six: Chinese-ADR delisting risk. Remains a tail risk independent of operational performance.

Specific sell triggers. Non-GAAP adjusted operating margin declining for two consecutive quarters without a one-off — margin-expansion story breaking. Fulfilment rate flat or declining for two consecutive quarters — matching-flywheel stalling. Credit-solutions ninety-day delinquency rising above four percent for two consecutive quarters — credit cycle turning. Commission penetration declining — take-rate ceiling hit early. A material adverse regulatory event. Founder or executive-team departure without named successor. Capital-return programme scaled back in 2027 versus 2026 without a clear alternative use.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. Full Truck Alliance is the Chinese digital-freight compounder that the market is still pricing as a cybersecurity-review casualty, despite nine-hundred-basis-points of margin expansion in twelve months and the just-starting capital return programme. The next print, first-quarter 2026 on the twentieth of May, tests whether the Q1 guide — roughly flat to four percent reported revenue growth, but fourteen to nineteen percent ex-freight-brokerage — translates to continued margin expansion. Watch fulfilment rate and operating margin, in that order.
