# Full Truck Alliance — Seven Questions

An investment summary of Full Truck Alliance — the China digital freight-matching platform — prepared on the 2nd of July 2026, through a lens of quality and long-term ownership.

## Question One: Business Description — What Is It?

Full Truck Alliance, known as FTA or Manbang, operates China's dominant digital freight-matching marketplace — often called the "Uber for trucks" — created by the 2017 merger of Yunmanman and Huochebang. The platform matches 3.11 million monthly-active shippers with truckers across intercity full-truckload and, increasingly, less-than-truckload freight; 55 million orders were fulfilled in the first quarter of 2026 alone, up 14.3 percent year on year. Revenue comes from freight-matching services — transaction commissions, freight brokerage, and listing fees — plus value-added services such as credit, insurance, electronic tolling, energy, and intelligent-driving services.

The economics are those of an asset-light marketplace: financial-year 2025 gross margin was about 63 percent and operating margin 33 percent, against operating losses as recently as 2022. The balance sheet holds 32.3 billion renminbi, or 4.7 billion US dollars, of cash and investments — more than half the roughly 8.9 billion US dollar market capitalisation — with quarterly free cash flow of about 1.5 billion renminbi.

## Question Two: Management and Governance — Can We Trust Them?

Founder Hui Zhang, who built Yunmanman and architected the merger, remains chairman and chief executive. The operating record supports trust: management converted a subsidy-fuelled land-grab into a profitable monetisation engine, and — notably for a quality owner — is deliberately winding down the low-quality, gross-basis freight-brokerage revenue line even though it flatters headline growth, migrating it to an aggregator model. Capital returns have been formalised: a quarterly dividend plus buybacks targeting about 400 million US dollars of total shareholder return for 2026, roughly 4.5 percent of the market capitalisation.

The caveats are the standard China-ADR set: a Cayman holding structure with consolidated affiliates, concentrated founder control, and SoftBank Vision Fund ownership of just over 20 percent that presents a supply overhang. The Cyberspace Administration of China opened a cybersecurity review days after the June 2021 New York Stock Exchange listing; it was resolved cooperatively, with new-user registration restored in June 2022 — but it is a permanent reminder of the state's reach over data-rich platforms. A Hong Kong secondary listing is under consideration as a hedge against US delisting risk. The main item to keep under review is allocation of the 4.7 billion US dollar cash pile.

## Question Three: Investment Thesis — Why Does Value Compound?

A network-effect franchise: the largest two-sided freight marketplace in the world's largest road-freight market. Order density keeps improving matching quality — trucker fulfilment rates exceeded 44 percent in the first quarter of 2026, up 5 points year on year — which deepens the moat as it grows.

A long monetisation runway: commission penetration has reached more than 94 percent of fulfilled orders, but average monetisation is only about 26.9 renminbi per order — a small fraction of the freight value of a typical intercity shipment. Take-rate deepening can compound revenue for years, largely independent of Chinese macro growth.

A revenue-mix upgrade in progress: transaction-service revenue grew 33 percent year on year in the first quarter of 2026 while low-margin brokerage shrinks; revenue excluding brokerage grew 17 percent. Mix shift is why net margin reached about 36 percent in financial-year 2025 and why profit quality keeps improving beneath a subdued headline.

A fortress balance sheet, with rising returns: 32.3 billion renminbi of net cash, strongly cash-generative operations, and a roughly 400 million US dollar annual return commitment provide downside protection and optional firepower.

And optionality, lightly priced: less-than-truckload expansion nationwide, the QMove international product in four markets, autonomous-delivery pilots with improving unit economics, and a cold-chain spin-off eyeing a 2026-to-2027 listing.

On the brokerage wind-down — and why headline growth understates the franchise. Headline first-quarter 2026 revenue rose just 5.5 percent to 2.85 billion renminbi, and consensus models similarly modest growth for financial-year 2026. The optics are misleading: freight brokerage — recognised gross, historically dependent on VAT-rebate arrangements — fell to 827 million renminbi from 966 million as FTA moves it to an aggregator model, while the high-margin core grew 17 percent and transaction services 33 percent. As the wind-down annualises, reported growth should converge with underlying growth — consensus already models re-acceleration into the teens for financial years 2027 and 2028.

## Question Four: Key Milestones and KPIs — What Proves Us Right, and What to Track?

Volume: fulfilled-order growth — 55.0 million in the first quarter of 2026, up 14.3 percent — and shipper monthly-active-user growth — 3.11 million, up 12.7 percent — the franchise health metrics.

Monetisation: commission penetration, more than 94 percent, and monetisation per order, about 26.9 renminbi — the runway thesis proves out here, quarter by quarter.

Mix and margin: revenue growth excluding brokerage, up 17 percent, versus headline; operating margin trajectory, 33 percent in financial-year 2025; and second-quarter 2026 guidance of 3.07 to 3.17 billion renminbi, up 7.1 to 11.7 percent.

Capital: delivery of the roughly 400 million US dollar 2026 shareholder-return target; and progress on the Hong Kong listing and cold-chain spin-off.

And a consensus check, from FactSet — rating consensus is a buy: 12 buy, 1 overweight, 2 hold, of 15; the Street models earnings per share compounding at roughly 20 percent a year through financial-year 2028, with revenue growth re-accelerating to low-to-mid teens as the brokerage wind-down annualises.

On the financials, in renminbi: revenue rose from 6.73 billion in financial-year 2022, to 8.44 billion in 2023, to 11.24 billion in 2024, to 12.49 billion in 2025, and was 2.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Net income went from 0.41 billion, to 2.23 billion, to about 3.12 billion, to 4.46 billion, with 0.99 billion in the first quarter. Net margin ran 6 percent, 26 percent, about 28 percent, then 36 percent, and 35 percent in the latest quarter. Net income includes substantial interest income on the cash balance; first-quarter 2026 net income fell year on year on higher reinvestment — operating expenses of 1.86 billion renminbi versus 1.54 billion — and lower interest income, not franchise deterioration.

## Question Five: Five-Year Fair Value and Return — the Team's View

The starting valuation does much of the work. At about 8.6 US dollars per American depositary share the company trades at roughly 14 times trailing earnings; strip out 4.7 billion US dollars of net cash — about 4.5 US dollars per share — and the operating business is priced near 6 to 7 times, for a more-than-90-percent-penetrated network business growing underlying revenue about 17 percent with rising margins.

Illustrative scenarios, as placeholders for the team's house numbers. The bull case: take-rate deepening plus a China freight recovery compound earnings in the high teens; modest re-rating gives a low-to-mid-20s-percent internal rate of return. The base case: earnings compound low-to-mid teens on monetisation alone, no re-rating; dividends and buybacks lift the internal rate of return to the mid-teens. The bear case: regulators cap commissions and freight volumes stagnate; earnings flatten, but the cash pile and about 4-to-5-percent shareholder yield hold the outcome near flat rather than deeply negative. The distribution is asymmetric because half the market value is cash.

## Question Six: Variant Perception — Where the Market Is Wrong

The market prices FTA as a China-macro and regulatory proxy — a single-digit ex-cash multiple usually reserved for structurally challenged businesses. The variant view is that this is one of the few genuine network-effect franchises in physical logistics anywhere, with a monetisation runway that compounds independently of GDP: penetration is done, and the take rate is still a small fraction of freight value. Two optical distortions feed the mispricing: the deliberate brokerage wind-down suppresses headline growth just as the underlying mix improves, and the first-quarter 2026 profit decline reflected reinvestment and lower interest income rather than any weakening of the core. The market is right that regulatory risk is real and permanent; it likely overprices that risk against a management team that has already navigated one review, pays a growing dividend, and runs the platform with visible restraint on trucker economics.

## Question Seven: Risks, Premortem, and Sell Discipline

The premortem — if this goes wrong in five years, the likely causes are these. Commission regulation: trucker livelihoods are politically sensitive; Beijing's campaign against platform "involution" could cap or roll back take rates — the single largest risk to the thesis. Freight macro stagnation: a prolonged Chinese industrial and property slump caps order volumes, leaving monetisation as the only growth engine — and more exposed politically. Earnings quality: a meaningful share of net income is interest income on the cash pile; falling renminbi rates or poor allocation of that cash would erode both earnings and the valuation floor. Competition: Didi Freight, JD Logistics, and regional aggregators; disruptive autonomous-trucking models could eventually disintermediate the marketplace, though FTA is investing here itself. And structure and geopolitics: ADR delisting risk, the consolidated-affiliate structure, and a more-than-20-percent SoftBank stake as an overhang.

Sell discipline: trim or exit on, first, regulatory action that caps or cuts commission rates; second, two-plus quarters of stalling fulfilled-order growth alongside rising monetisation — evidence of squeezing rather than growing the network; third, a large, low-return acquisition or failure to deliver the stated shareholder-return programme; or fourth, governance drift around the founder or the onshore structure. Size the position respecting single-country regulatory risk.
