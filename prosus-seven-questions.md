# Prosus — The Seven Questions

**Ticker:** PRX NA (Amsterdam) / PRX SJ (Johannesburg) | **H1 FY26 e-commerce aEBITDA:** US$530m | **GEM weight:** 4.4% (#6 in portfolio)
**Source base:** Quartr-grounded (Investor Update "FORWARD", 12 March 2026; H1 FY26 results call, 24 November 2025; Capital Markets Day, 25 June 2025)
**Written:** April 2026

---

## Cold open

In May 2024, Prosus appointed **Fabricio Bloisi** — the Brazilian founder of iFood — as CEO. The remit: stop being a discount-to-Tencent trade. Eighteen months later:

- **E-commerce aEBITDA grew 70% YoY in H1 FY26 to US$530m** — entire e-commerce portfolio now profitable. FY26 guide: at least **US$1.1bn aEBITDA** (ex-JET and La Centrale)
- **Acquired Just Eat Takeaway** (delisted 17 Nov 2025) and **La Centrale** (closed same date) — the biggest European bets in Prosus's history
- Built **Large Commerce Models (LCM)** — proprietary AI trained on 500m daily customer interactions, **10x cheaper than commercial LLMs**, already driving +15% conversion at iFood
- **20,000 active AI agents** internally; Toqan agent platform used by 25,000 associates with 1,000-FTE-equivalent impact
- Continued the buyback at **US$6–7bn/year** (cumulative >US$40bn, generating >US$60bn in NAV uplift)

This isn't a stub trade anymore. It's an emerging-markets consumer-internet operating company with the Tencent stake as the world's biggest balance-sheet asset behind it.

Seven questions.

---

## 1. What is it?

Prosus is the global emerging-markets consumer-internet arm of **Naspers** (South Africa). One transformational asset and a portfolio of operating businesses:

- **Tencent (~24% stake, ~US$169bn at recent prices)** — by far the most valuable line. Provides dividend stream and strategic balance-sheet ballast
- **iFood** (Brazil, 100%) — dominant LatAm food delivery + fintech + ads platform. ~100m customers, ~US$25bn annualised GMV, ~US$500m aEBITDA expected FY26. iFood Pago is a real fintech now
- **Just Eat Takeaway** (Europe, 100%, delisted Nov 2025) — biggest single ecommerce investment ever; reset project under direct Prosus control
- **OLX** (classifieds, multi-region) + **La Centrale** (French car classifieds) — combined to be a serious classifieds franchise outside Auto Trader
- **PayU** (India + emerging markets payments and credit)
- **Despegar/Decolar** (LatAm OTA, partial stake) — integrating with iFood ecosystem
- **Indian growth bets**: Swiggy (~24% listed), Meesho, Rapido, Ixigo (15%), Urban Company, Vastu, Mintifi
- **eMAG** (Romanian/SEE e-commerce), **iyzico** (Turkish payments)

The thing to internalise: Prosus is now best understood as **Tencent + ~US$50–100bn of operating businesses across LatAm, India and Europe + an AI infrastructure layer (LCM + Toqan) being built to make all of them better**. The market still partially prices it as the old Tencent-discount trade — that's the inefficiency.

## 2. Can we trust them?

This is the question that has changed most dramatically in the past 18 months.

**Fabricio Bloisi** became CEO in July 2024. Founder of iFood, the most credible operating executive Prosus has ever had. His reputation: ferocious execution and Brazilian-style compounder mindset — small bets, fast learning, ruthless capital reallocation.

**The Bloisi tell.** When he took over in July 2024, e-commerce was loss-making at the group level. He set a target of "multiple billion dollars of profit in the next few years" on the first call. Analysts were sceptical. By H1 FY26, e-commerce aEBITDA was +70% YoY at US$530m with every business profitable — on track for US$1.1bn FY26. Bloisi quote from H1 FY26 call: *"One year ago, I told you I expect us to be -- have more profit than the dividends, and I expect us to get to multiple billion dollars of profit... Many shareholders didn't see it coming. It is coming."* That delivery-on-stated-target is the most important single thing to know about trusting him.

**Capital allocation framework explicitly stated:**
- Sell non-Tencent stakes with less strategic potential than Tencent
- Use proceeds to fund the buyback rather than selling Tencent
- Result: **per-share Tencent exposure goes UP** as you reduce share count without selling the stake
- FY26 asset disposals target: at least US$2bn
- Buyback cadence US$6–7bn/year continues
- Cumulative >US$40bn of buybacks have generated >US$60bn in NAV uplift

**The watch item.** Naspers/Prosus control structure (high-vote shares, dual listing) is governance complexity historically warranting some discount. Minor on operating decisions, real on headline multiple.

## 3. Why does value compound?

Three engines with meaningfully different time horizons.

**The buyback flywheel.** The cleanest mechanical compounder in the portfolio. Prosus trades at a sustained discount to NAV. CFO sells underperforming portfolio assets at fair value, uses proceeds to buy back Prosus shares at a discount, which mechanically increases per-share NAV and per-share Tencent exposure. As long as the discount persists, buyback is value-accretive. Cumulative US$40bn of buybacks generated US$60bn in NAV uplift — a 5–8% per annum tailwind to per-share NAV regardless of what anything else does.

**E-commerce franchise inflecting from break-even to multi-billion EBITDA.** Two years ago e-commerce was a cost centre. H1 FY26 ran at US$530m aEBITDA with FY26 guided US$1.1bn+. Bloisi's stated ambition: *"a couple of billion dollars of profit in the next few years."* If even half materialises, the operating business alone is worth US$30–50bn (15–25x EBITDA on consumer-internet multiples) — a meaningful chunk of the whole market cap.

**Large Commerce Models (LCM) as a structural cost-and-conversion advantage.** Bloisi's argument: the most valuable AI won't be trained on text — it'll be trained on real-world commerce interactions. Prosus has 1bn customers, 5m partners, 500m daily interactions. LCM at iFood: **+15% homepage conversion, -51% ad cost, +75% notification conversion, 10x cheaper than commercial LLMs**. Rolling LCM across Just Eat, OLX, eMAG. If this is real (and the iFood data suggests it is), Prosus has a *proprietary AI moat* that pure software companies cannot replicate without owning the consumer interaction surface.

**The kicker nobody is pricing:** Toqan + 20,000 internal agents + the Bloisi-led "agentic OS" thesis. Prosus has already cut 1,000-FTE-equivalent of internal cost via agents, while building products designed to make agentic commerce the default consumer interface in their geographies. If agentic commerce is real, Prosus is one of a very small number of companies with the underlying infrastructure (payments, logistics, inventory, identity) to monetise it at scale outside the US and China.

## 4. What proves us right and what to track?

Six specific KPIs:

- **E-commerce aEBITDA trajectory** — US$530m H1 FY26; FY26 guide >US$1.1bn ex-JET/La Centrale; FY27 at US$1.5bn+ confirms operating business has scaled
- **iFood ecosystem cross-sell** — 5% of Despegar revenue is now from iFood after 5 months; Bloisi's short-term target is 10–15%; if the flywheel works in LatAm, replicate in India and Europe
- **JET turnaround signs** — Bloisi has had control ~6 months as of H1 call; watch Q1/H2 FY26 commentary on whether revenue growth and margin trajectory turn
- **LCM rollout breadth and conversion lift** — iFood is the proof case; OLX, eMAG, JET, Despegar are the fan-out tests
- **Buyback continuation at US$6–7bn/year** — pace matters; if it slows, either Tencent has appreciated so much they don't want to sell, or operating cash flow is needed elsewhere
- **Asset disposal cadence** — US$2bn+ guided FY26; watch which assets get sold — tells you what Prosus believes has less strategic value than Tencent

## 5. What's it worth?

Most interesting valuation in the GEM portfolio because the components are individually visible.

- Market cap at €84.50/share × ~2.5bn shares ≈ **€211bn (~US$220bn)**
- **Tencent stake (~24%)**: at Tencent's ~US$600bn market cap, **~US$144bn** before any control discount
- **iFood**: US$15–25bn standalone given US$500m aEBITDA and growth
- **JET**: paid ~US$5bn at discount to peak
- **La Centrale, OLX, eMAG, PayU, Despegar, Indian stakes aggregate**: US$15–25bn
- **Sum of parts** ≈ **US$185–210bn before any discount**

Discount to NAV currently ~5–15% — historically narrow vs the 30–40% range Prosus traded at for years. Two readings:

1. *Bear:* discount narrowed because operating businesses now contribute, but easy money in discount-narrowing trade has been made
2. *Bull:* operating businesses still being valued at break-even-ish multiples; if they hit US$2bn+ aEBITDA Bloisi has signalled, operating business alone re-rates by US$20–30bn

**Key sensitivity:** ratio between Tencent appreciation and Prosus discount narrowing. Owning Prosus is implicitly a bet that the operating business closes more of the gap than Tencent itself opens.

## 6. Where is the market wrong?

**One — the "Prosus is just a Tencent proxy" framing is two years out of date.** Most institutional investors still think of Prosus as a way to own Tencent at a discount. That was the right framing in 2022. Today, Prosus is a serious operating company with US$1bn+ of *its own* EBITDA and a coherent AI-first strategy. Tencent discount-narrowing is the floor; upside is operating business compounding independently. Most sell-side hasn't caught up.

**Two — Large Commerce Models are a real moat nobody outside the company is modelling.** The March 12 investor update is genuinely radical: consumer-AI training on commerce interactions (intent, preferences, decisions) produces better recommendation/conversion outcomes than LLMs trained on text. Early iFood data supports this (-51% ad cost, +75% notification CTR is enormous). If LCM scales across the portfolio, it's a structural cost advantage not in any sell-side model and that competitors literally cannot replicate without owning a comparable interaction surface.

**The honest counter:** Bloisi's ambition could overshoot operational reality. Just Eat Takeaway is a difficult turnaround in a structurally challenged European market. Indian competition is intense. "Build a US$100bn company outside Tencent" is aspirational — could happen over 5–10 years or not.

## 7. What could kill it, and when do we sell?

**Real risks, ranked:**

- **Tencent value collapse.** ~70%+ of group NAV is the Tencent stake. Anything that tanks Tencent tanks Prosus regardless of how iFood is doing
- **JET turnaround failure.** Big-ticket bet on European food delivery. If Just Eat continues losing share to Uber Eats/Wolt/Deliveroo, writedown could be material
- **Indian competitive intensity.** Swiggy/Zomato war, PayU vs PhonePe/Razorpay, Meesho vs Flipkart/Amazon. If any bet fails materially, NAV impacted
- **Naspers/Prosus governance.** Periodic shareholder unhappiness with the structure; any restructuring could create technical share-price pressure
- **AI capex blowout.** LCM and Toqan investments real and rising; if productivity/conversion gains don't sustain, spend becomes drag
- **EM currencies.** Reported earnings hit by BRL, INR, TRY, ZAR fluctuations; operating economics often unaffected but reported numbers ugly

**Sell triggers (specific, not vague):**

- Two consecutive halves of e-commerce aEBITDA missing the trajectory implied by management guidance
- JET running at meaningfully negative aEBITDA 18 months after Prosus took control
- Buyback slowed below US$4bn/year without explicit alternative use of capital
- Bloisi exits — one-person-dependent thesis in many ways
- Discount to NAV widening back above 25% sustained for 12+ months — implies market has rejected the operating thesis
- Material deterioration in Tencent fundamentals

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. Prosus has gone from a cleverly-structured discount trade to an actual operating company with clear AI-first ambition and a CEO who has delivered on every promise made in the past 18 months. The valuation still partially reflects the old framing — that's the inefficiency. H2 FY26 results in late June tell you whether the JET integration is on track and whether LCM is scaling beyond iFood. If both show up positive, the discount keeps narrowing and you make money on top of whatever Tencent does. If neither, you still own Tencent at a discount with a profitable operating business attached.
