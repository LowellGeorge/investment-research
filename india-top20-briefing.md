# India Top 20 briefing notes

A Quartr-grounded run-through of twenty Indian listed names, built to be listened to like a podcast. Each name has a pitch, evidence, balance sheet, portfolio fit, and live debate from the most recent earnings calls.

## 1. HDFC Bank

**Pitch.** HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector bank and the country's single most valuable financial franchise, with a market capitalisation of roughly 134 billion US dollars. Following the July 2023 merger with parent HDFC Limited, it now bundles the country's leading mortgage book on top of its existing deposit, credit card, auto loan, and retail asset engine, and reaches customers through more than 9,600 branches, about half of them in semi-urban and rural India. Inside an India Top 20 portfolio, it is the default core financial — the most liquid, best-capitalised, least-exciting way to own Indian household credit and savings growth at once.

**Evidence.** Full-year fiscal 2024–25 revenues came in at roughly 2.19 trillion Indian rupees, up from about 1.85 trillion the year before, with operating income of around 862 billion rupees and net income of about 687 billion rupees, up from 628 billion the prior year. The recent quarterly numbers are just as strong. In the December 2025 quarter, profit after tax was 187 billion rupees, net revenue grew almost 9 percent year over year to 459 billion rupees, average advances were up 19 percent and average deposits up 12 percent. Return on assets held at 1.92 percent and return on equity at 13.9 percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The total balance sheet crossed 40.9 trillion rupees at the end of December 2025, up from 37.6 trillion a year earlier. Capital is very comfortable — capital adequacy sits at 19.9 percent with core tier one at 17.4 percent — and management has said it has enough capital to support three to four more years of growth before any need to raise. Asset quality is stable, with gross non-performing assets at 1.24 percent and net at 0.42 percent, and the bank has also built a nine-thousand-crore floating provision cushion during the first half of fiscal 2026.

**Portfolio fit.** Within this India Top 20, HDFC Bank sits alongside ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank and State Bank of India as the private-sector anchor of the financials sleeve. ICICI is the higher-return growth name, Kotak the premium compounder, and SBI the public-sector cyclical torque — HDFC Bank is the largest, most diversified, and the one you hold for stability of earnings across cycles rather than for torque.

**Debate.** The live debate is whether the post-merger HDFC Bank can get back to its old return profile. Net interest margin is currently stuck around 3.27 to 3.35 percent, well below the pre-merger level, and return on assets has been operating in a narrow 1.85 to 1.95 percent band for eight quarters. Management is telling analysts that deposit repricing over the next four to five quarters and a glide path down on the credit-to-deposit ratio toward 85 to 90 percent by fiscal 2027 will push return on assets back toward the upper end of the 1.8 to 2.2 percent range — but analysts on the last two calls kept pushing on this, along with a one-off five-billion-rupee agri provision and the uncertain recurring impact of India's new labour code. The bull case is that the bank grinds back to its old margins. The bear case is that scale and the merged mortgage book permanently cap profitability a notch below where it used to be.

## 2. Kotak Mahindra Bank

**Pitch.** Kotak Mahindra Bank is one of India's premium private-sector banks, but the label undersells it. Alongside a full-service commercial bank, the group runs an unusually deep non-banking stack: Kotak Securities in capital markets, Kotak AMC in mutual funds, Kotak Life, a general insurance associate following the Zurich deal, plus NBFC, wealth and private banking arms. Banking and lending deliver just over three-quarters of consolidated profit, with capital markets, asset management and insurance filling out the rest. In an India Top 20 portfolio, Kotak earns its seat as the high-quality, high-capital private financial compounder with a genuinely diversified earnings engine.

**Evidence.** Third-quarter FY26 consolidated profit after tax was 4,924 crore rupees, up 5 percent year-over-year and 10 percent sequentially, following 4,468 crore rupees in the prior quarter. Net advances grew 16 percent and average deposits grew 15 percent year-over-year. Net interest margin held at 4.54 percent, supported by cost of funds falling from 5.01 percent in the first quarter to 4.54 percent. CASA came in at 41.3 percent. Asset management profit rose 47 percent year-over-year to 435 crore rupees, and customer assets under management reached 787,950 crore rupees, up 15 percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The standalone balance sheet has crossed 7 trillion rupees. Group net worth is around 1.75 trillion rupees, standalone roughly 1.3 trillion. Standalone capital adequacy sits at 22.6 percent with CET1 at 21.5 percent, group CET1 at 22.4 percent, among the highest in Indian banking. Gross non-performing assets are 1.3 percent and net non-performing assets just 0.31 percent, with provision coverage of 76 percent. The standout item is the sale of a 70 percent stake in Kotak Mahindra General Insurance to Zurich for 4,096 crore rupees, booking a pre-tax gain of roughly 3,800 crore rupees and reclassifying the entity as an associate.

**Portfolio fit.** Kotak is the quality anchor in the Indian banks sleeve. HDFC Bank is the post-merger giant rebuilding margins, ICICI Bank the consistent operator with the cleanest execution, SBI the macro play on India's deposit franchise. Kotak is smaller, slower on loan growth, but structurally over-capitalised and the only one of the four with a material non-bank earnings mix. It is the premium compounder you hold for book-value growth and optionality, not headline loan expansion.

**Debate.** The live debate is margin durability and the growth rebuild. Analysts are pressing management on when net interest margin actually expands rather than just holds, on the pace of rebuilding the unsecured and microfinance books after last year's caution, on stress in retail commercial vehicles and rural cash flows, and on one-off drags from new Labour Code provisions and the pending expected credit loss framework. The legacy questions around the RBI technology ban and premium valuation versus peers still sit in the background.

## 3. ICICI Bank

**Pitch.** ICICI Bank is India's second-largest private-sector bank, with a retail-led loan book, a dense urban branch network of over 7,200 branches, and a market capitalisation of around 9.5 trillion rupees, roughly 102 billion dollars. Alongside the core bank sit wholly-owned or majority-owned subsidiaries in life insurance, general insurance, asset management, securities, and housing finance, making it one of the cleanest ways to own the formalisation of Indian household finance. For an India Top 20 portfolio it is the core private-bank allocation: scale, best-in-class returns, and a digital stack that has become the benchmark for the sector.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2024-25, ending March 2025, delivered net revenue of roughly 1.08 trillion rupees, up about 11 percent year-on-year, with standalone net profit of 472 billion rupees versus 461 billion the year before. The most recent quarter, Q3 of fiscal 2026, showed core operating profit up 6 percent to 175 billion rupees and net interest income up 7.7 percent to 219 billion rupees. Domestic loans grew 11.5 percent, deposits 9.2 percent, net interest margin held firm at 4.3 percent, and return on assets came in at 2.11 percent with standalone return on equity of 14.3 percent, or 15.5 percent adjusted.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Total assets sit above 21 trillion rupees. Capital is fortress-grade, with a CET1 ratio of 16.46 percent and total capital adequacy of 17.34 percent at end-December 2025. Asset quality is pristine for a bank of this size: net non-performing assets of just 0.37 percent, provision coverage of 75.4 percent, and a contingency buffer of 131 billion rupees, roughly 0.9 percent of advances. The one blemish in the latest quarter was a 12.8 billion rupee additional standard-asset provision mandated by the Reserve Bank of India for agricultural priority-sector loans not fully conforming to guidelines.

**Portfolio fit.** Inside the India Top 20, ICICI is the best-in-class return-on-assets private-bank pitch. HDFC Bank is larger but still digesting its HDFC Limited merger and runs lower returns on assets. Kotak offers premium asset quality but slower growth. State Bank of India delivers public-sector torque and cheaper valuation but weaker discipline. ICICI combines mid-teens return on equity, retail-led growth, and a subsidiary stack that compounds alongside the bank.

**Debate.** The live debates from the last two earnings calls are margin trajectory under a falling-rate cycle, caution around unsecured retail and credit cards where the book shrank 3.5 percent year-on-year, whether return on assets can stay above 2 percent as deposit repricing lags loan repricing, the rising cost-to-income ratio now at 40.6 percent, and the valuation premium the stock commands over peers.

## 4. Bharti Airtel

**Pitch.** Bharti Airtel is India's number two wireless operator by subscribers but the clear number one by ARPU, premium mix and postpaid share, with a roughly 645 million customer base spanning India and a fifteen-country Airtel Africa franchise. Around that wireless core sit a fast-growing home broadband business (FTTH plus fixed wireless access), Airtel Business for enterprise connectivity, Nxtra data centres, and the Airtel Payments Bank. In an India Top 20 it is the cleanest listed proxy for rising Indian data consumption and the ARPU uplift cycle — a defensive consumption compounder sitting next to the cyclicals.

**Evidence.** In the year ended March 2024, consolidated revenue was 1.50 trillion rupees, operating income 202 billion rupees and net income 74.7 billion rupees, on reported EBITDA of 790 billion rupees. The business has since accelerated. In the December 2025 quarter, revenue hit 53,982 crore, up 15 percent year on year, with EBITDA up 7 percent to 31,144 crore at a 57.7 percent margin, and net income before exceptionals up 26 percent to 6,920 crore. India mobile ARPU reached 259 rupees, India mobile revenue grew 9 percent, and the homes business grew 33 percent. Africa added another 25 percent constant-currency revenue growth. The group run rate is now comfortably above 200,000 crore.

**Balance sheet and cash.** FY24 cash from operations was 532 billion rupees against capex of 262 billion, and the trajectory has tightened further. Q3 FY26 alone generated 19,357 crore of operating free cash flow against capex of 11,787 crore, funding the 5G build and FTTH push. Net debt excluding leases fell to 1,124,912 million rupees, taking net debt to EBITDAaL to just 1.02 times — a stark contrast with the post-5G stress of three years ago. Airtel Africa is buying back stock, the board has approved a final call of 401 rupees on partly-paid shares due March 2026, and Airtel has flagged an intention to lift its stake in Indus Towers by another five percent.

**Portfolio fit.** Bharti is the only telco in the India Top 20 and plays the role of defensive consumption proxy with pricing power — a useful counterweight to the consumer cyclicality of Bosch, Maruti and M&M. The long thesis rests on duopoly discipline with Jio, ongoing ARPU uplift as feature-phone users migrate to smartphones, a widening homes and FWA opportunity into a 100 million connected-homes TAM, and visible deleveraging as 5G capex rolls over.

**Debate.** The two most recent calls centred on the timing and magnitude of the next tariff hike, 5G monetisation that remains largely invisible in ARPU, competition from Jio in both mobile and FWA, Africa currency risk, capex intensity from aggressive home-pass rollout and data centres including the new Google partnership in Visakhapatnam, AGR liability reassessment risk, and whether the stock's re-rating has already priced in the ARPU cycle.

## 5. ICICI Lombard General Insurance

**Pitch.** ICICI Lombard is India's largest private-sector general insurer, offering motor, health, fire, marine, engineering and liability cover to retail customers and corporates. Listed in 2017 and majority-owned by ICICI Bank, the company carries a roughly 960 billion rupee market cap, just under 10 billion US dollars. It earns its India Top 20 slot as the purest liquid way to own Indian non-life penetration, which still sits at only around one percent of GDP, roughly a quarter of the global average. The diversified book and trusted parent brand make it the quality vehicle in the category.

**Evidence.** For the first nine months of the fiscal year ending March 2026, gross direct premium rose 3.6 percent year on year to roughly 214 billion rupees, with third-quarter GDPI growth accelerating to 13.3 percent. Profit after tax for the nine months was around 22.3 billion rupees, up 11.3 percent, though the third quarter slipped 9 percent to 6.6 billion on a one-off wage code charge. The combined ratio was 104.2 percent, 103.9 excluding that wage impact. Segment mix was strong: retail health GDPI up 86 percent in the quarter, overall health up 41, fire up 19, motor up 9. Book value per share stepped up to 325 rupees.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The investment portfolio reached around 583 billion rupees at end-December, with 87.5 percent in sovereign or AAA-rated debt and zero defaults since inception. Investment leverage is 3.6 times, unrealised gains sit near 16.6 billion rupees, and nine-month investment income was 37.6 billion. The solvency ratio of 2.69 times is comfortably above the 1.5 regulatory floor. Management declared a 6.50 rupee interim dividend, signalling continued retail-friendly capital returns alongside the steady compounding of float.

**Portfolio fit.** Inside India Top 20, ICICI Lombard is the diversified non-life anchor alongside the financials-adjacent names: Niva Bupa as the pure health play and CAMS as the transfer-agent utility. Where Niva is a high-growth specialist and CAMS a capital-light fee machine, ICICI GI is the highest-quality way to own general insurance penetration, combining the strongest brand, widest distribution, and a long track record of disciplined underwriting across cycles.

**Debate.** The live debate from the last two calls centres on the combined ratio trajectory — stuck above 104 despite motor loss ratio at 66 percent and retail indemnity health improving to 63 — plus motor pricing competition, health claim inflation, IRDAI one-over-n accounting noise, and whether the targeted 18-to-20 percent ROE supports the premium valuation.

## 6. Bosch India

**Pitch.** Bosch India is the listed Indian arm of Robert Bosch, the world's largest auto components group, and it is the single highest-quality supplier franchise in the Indian vehicle value chain. The business spans Power Solutions, which covers fuel injection, diesel and petrol systems, and off-highway, plus Mobility Aftermarket, a fast-growing two-wheeler business, and a Consumer Goods arm housing power tools. It earns its place in the India Top 20 as the pick-and-shovel play on Indian vehicle production — a parent-backed technology moat, deep localisation, and an enviable balance sheet, while it navigates the transition from diesel injection legacy to hydrogen ICE, EV components and ADAS.

**Evidence.** For the year to March 2025, revenue reached roughly 181 billion rupees, up about 8 percent year on year, with operating income of around 27.3 billion rupees, lifting the operating margin into the 15 percent zone from under 14 the year before. Reported net income of around 20 billion rupees was lower year on year, but only because the prior year was flattered by exceptional items. The run-rate has accelerated since. In the most recent quarter, the December 2025 print, revenue grew 9.4 percent to about 48.9 billion rupees and nine-month profit after tax jumped over 50 percent, helped by the sale of the Building Technologies business. The standout was the two-wheeler segment, which grew 58 percent on OBD-II implementation, while Power Solutions added nearly 20 percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Bosch India is essentially debt-free. At March 2025, cash and short-term investments sat at around 12 billion rupees on top of roughly 63 billion rupees of long-term investments, against borrowings of under 2 billion which are almost entirely lease liabilities. Equity stood near 138 billion rupees. Management has just amended the dividend distribution policy to a 55 to 85 percent payout band, and paid a 512 rupee final dividend. Capex is demand-linked and modest.

**Portfolio fit.** Bosch sits in the industrials and auto components sleeve alongside KEI Industries, Maruti, Mahindra and Mahindra, Escorts, Bajaj Auto and Tata Motors. It is the quality supplier, not the OEM — the cleanest balance sheet in the bucket and the highest-grade technology franchise.

**Debate.** The two live debates on the last two calls are transition risk from the diesel injection legacy, where margins are constrained by product mix and localisation cycles, and the tension of having no pure-play EV vehicle exposure while carrying a premium valuation versus auto ancillary peers. Exports guidance remains cautious on tariffs, EV segment margins are tough, and there is no multi-year financial guidance.

## 7. KEI Industries

**Pitch.** KEI Industries is one of India's top-three wires and cables makers, selling house wires and flexible cables to retail and trade through a roughly two-thousand-dealer network, and high-tension, low-tension and extra-high-voltage power cables to utilities, infrastructure contractors and industrial customers. It also runs a small stainless steel wire business and an EPC arm that executes turnkey transmission projects. In an India Top 20 it earns its slot as the cleanest listed read on India's power capex supercycle, grid upgrades, renewables integration and the data centre buildout, with optionality from a fast-growing export book into Australia, the Middle East, Europe and selectively the US.

**Evidence.** Full-year FY25 revenue was around one hundred billion rupees, up roughly twenty percent on the year before, with operating income near nine point four billion and net income close to seven billion, for an EBITDA margin a touch under eleven percent. Momentum into FY26 has if anything strengthened. On the latest Q3 call in January, management reported net sales of two thousand nine hundred and fifty four crore, up nineteen and a half percent, with EBITDA up thirty-nine percent and margin expanding to twelve percent. Exports jumped ninety-five percent to five hundred and forty-four crore, the order book stood at nearly four thousand crore, and capacity utilisation in cables was already seventy-six percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The group sits on roughly fifteen to sixteen billion rupees of cash and liquid investments, essentially zero long-term debt and only a sliver of working-capital borrowings, supported by the twenty billion rupee QIP raised in 2024. That warchest is being spent. FY25 capex was close to seven billion rupees versus two and a half the year before, working capital has ballooned, and reported free cash flow was meaningfully negative as the Sanand greenfield is built out. Management has guided to another twenty billion of capex over the next three to four years.

**Portfolio fit.** KEI is the higher-beta industrial in the sleeve, the pure-play power infrastructure read. Versus Bosch's defensive quality-supplier profile, the thesis here is volume growth compounding at a twenty percent kind of pace, with margin leverage kicking in once Sanand utilisation climbs and exports scale.

**Debate.** The live debates on recent calls are Sanand execution after a four-to-nine-month phase delay, whether eleven percent EBITDA margins hold through the capex ramp, copper pass-through, US tariff risk on exports, and whether the valuation multiple survives intensifying wires competition from Polycab and Havells.

## 8. HCL Technologies

**Pitch.** India's third-largest IT services exporter, behind TCS and Infosys, with a market cap around forty-two billion US dollars. The business runs on three legs: IT and Business Services, which is the bulk; Engineering and R&D Services, where HCL has built arguably the strongest franchise among Indian peers; and HCLSoftware, a genuine products and intellectual property arm now running at over a billion dollars of annual recurring revenue. End-markets span financial services, technology, life sciences, manufacturing, retail and telecom. It belongs in an India Top 20 because it is the cleanest way to own the global IT services story with a differentiated ERS and software angle rather than just labour arbitrage.

**Evidence.** In the most recent quarter, the third quarter of fiscal twenty-six, HCL posted USD revenue of three point seven nine billion dollars, up four point eight percent year-on-year in constant currency, with rupee revenue up over thirteen percent. EBIT margin came in at eighteen point six percent excluding a one-off labour code provision, and net income was five hundred and thirty-seven million dollars. The standout number was bookings: three billion dollars of net new deal wins, up forty-three percent year-on-year, the highest annual contract value haul in four years, including a four hundred and seventy-three million dollar AI-led contract with a global apparel retailer. Digital revenue grew nearly eighteen percent and now represents forty-three percent of services. Engineering and R&D Services grew almost eleven percent, comfortably ahead of the core.

**Balance sheet and cash.** HCL is a cash machine. Gross cash sits at around three point eight billion dollars, net cash at three point five five billion. Free cash flow to net income conversion ran at one hundred and twenty percent on a trailing basis, and return on invested capital is nearly forty percent. The dividend policy is the real giveaway: ninety-two consecutive quarters of payouts, with roughly eighty-nine percent of net income returned to shareholders over the last twelve months.

**Portfolio fit.** This is the only IT services name in the India Top 20 and the USD-earning leg of the book. Where HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank are rupee-domestic compounders, HCL bills predominantly in dollars and pounds, acting as a natural hedge against rupee weakness while paying a substantial dividend yield along the way.

**Debate.** The live debate is whether four to five percent constant currency growth is the ceiling this cycle or a trough. Bulls point to the record bookings, AI deal momentum and ERS strength; bears worry about GenAI-driven deflation in renewals, muted manufacturing and auto demand in Europe, and margin pressure from new labour codes and H-1B fee changes. Management insists AI will be net accretive. FY26 guidance: four to four point five percent company growth, seventeen to eighteen percent EBIT margin.

## 9. State Bank of India

**Pitch.** State Bank of India is the country's largest bank and the clearest public-sector proxy on Indian credit growth. Majority-owned by the Government of India, it carries roughly a fifth of all deposits and advances in the system and runs a physical network no private competitor can match, with reach deep into SME, retail and agri India. Around that core sit listed and unlisted subsidiaries across life and general insurance, asset management, credit cards and capital markets. The thesis is simple: you want a PSU balance-sheet option alongside the private-bank core, bought at a structural discount to book, with embedded subsidiary value.

**Evidence.** FY24/25, ending March 2025, delivered standalone total revenues of around 5.24 lakh crore rupees and net profit of about 70,900 crore, up from roughly 61,100 crore the year before, for earnings per share near 79 rupees. The Q3 FY26 call struck a record tone: quarterly net profit of roughly 21,000 crore, up about 24 percent year-on-year, net interest income up 9 percent to 45,190 crore, operating profit up nearly 40 percent, advances and deposits each growing about 15 percent, and total business crossing 103 lakh crore. Whole-bank NIM printed at 2.99 percent, domestic NIM at 3.12 percent, GNPA at 1.57 percent, net NPA at 0.39 percent and credit cost at just 29 basis points for the quarter.

**Balance sheet and cash.** On management's numbers the balance sheet has crossed 100 lakh crore of business, with CASA near 39 percent and a dominant deposit franchise. Capital adequacy stood at 14.04 percent in December, with CET-1 at 10.99 percent, or 12.57 percent including profits, lifted further by a 25,000 crore QIP, India's largest ever. Provision coverage is 75.5 percent, above 92 percent including technical write-offs. Asset quality is the cleanest in two decades, a world away from the PSU stereotype and in striking distance of the private peers.

**Portfolio fit.** SBI is the public-sector torque in India Top 20, sitting alongside HDFC Bank, ICICI and Kotak. It trades at a persistent discount to private banks on price-to-book. The thesis is post-cleanup balance sheet, slow-burn digital transformation through YONO and Project Saral, and eventual value crystallisation from SBI AMC and SBI General listings.

**Debate.** The live debate is whether RoA, now running at 1.16 percent for nine months, can sustainably clear 1.1 to 1.2 percent through cycles given management's more conservative 1 percent through-cycle guidance. Investors are watching NIM as rate cuts work through, slippages in unsecured retail and MSME, the eventual wage revision, ECL transition, cost-to-income glidepath towards below 50 percent, and the government divestment overhang.

## 10. Maruti Suzuki India

**Pitch.** India's largest passenger vehicle OEM and a roughly 40 percent share household name, majority-owned by Suzuki Motor Corporation of Japan. Maruti runs the deepest dealer footprint of any Indian carmaker, split across the mainstream ARENA channel, the premium NEXA channel for SUVs and upper trims, a True Value used-car business, and a captive Smart Finance arm that has now written over 2.5 million car loans. End markets are the Indian domestic passenger vehicle cycle plus a rapidly scaling export book into Africa, Latin America and Japan. In an India Top 20, Maruti is the default way to own the Indian car volume cycle and the rising mass-market consumer.

**Evidence.** FY24/25 revenue reached roughly 1,567 billion rupees, up about 8 percent year on year, with operating income of 192 billion, EBITDA of 223 billion and net income of 140 billion rupees, against 1,409 billion of revenue and 132 billion of net income a year earlier. The most recent quarter, Q3 FY25/26 to December 2025, was a step-change: volumes jumped almost 18 percent to 668,000 units and net sales rose 29 percent to 475 billion rupees as the GST cut on small cars unleashed a wave of first-time buyers. Operating EBITDA grew 10 percent but EBITDA margin slipped 210 basis points to 11.7 percent on commodity, rare-earth and a one-off labour-code provision. New launches Victoris and the Jimny 5-door lifted SUV mix.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Maruti remains the fortress balance sheet of Indian autos. Short-term borrowings are effectively zero, with 56 billion rupees of cash and short investments and another 689 billion rupees of long-term investments sitting as a treasury portfolio alongside 236 billion of net fixed assets. FY25 generated 140 billion of operating cash flow and 57 billion of free cash flow after 83 billion of capex, and H1 FY26 paid out 42 billion in dividends. The capex run-rate is now running at around 100 billion rupees a year, funding the new Kharkhoda plant in Haryana and the Suzuki Motor Gujarat amalgamation that completed in December 2025.

**Portfolio fit.** Core Indian consumer cyclical and the volume anchor of the sleeve alongside Mahindra, Tata Motors, Bajaj Auto and Escorts. Maruti is the clear leader in units but has ceded share in higher-priced SUVs over the last cycle, so the thesis rests on a share recovery via Victoris, Grand Vitara and the e-Vitara, a genuinely differentiated CNG and hybrid positioning, exports ramping towards 400,000 units this year, and a capex cycle that should start peaking as Kharkhoda comes on stream.

**Debate.** The last two calls are dominated by four arguments. First, EV timing and scale, with the e-Vitara now exported to 28 countries and a target of 100,000 charging points by 2030, but volumes still small. Second, whether the GST-driven small-car surge is sustainable demand or borrowed from later quarters, with management pegging underlying growth nearer 7 percent. Third, margin trajectory given rare earths, platinum group metals, labour codes and ELV compliance costs. Fourth, whether Maruti can really claw SUV share back from Tata and Mahindra while the heavy capex cycle is still dragging on RoCE.

## 11. Oberoi Realty

**Pitch.** Oberoi Realty is the premium Mumbai-focused real estate developer, with four integrated verticals: luxury residential across Goregaon, Worli, Borivali, Mulund and Thane; commercial office leasing under the Commerz brand; the retail mall franchise anchored by Oberoi Mall and Sky City Mall in Goregaon; and hospitality through the Westin, with the Ritz-Carlton opening later in the current financial year. It is the only pure-play listed exposure to Mumbai premium and uber-luxury real estate, where land scarcity, brand, and execution track record translate into real pricing power. It earns its India Top 20 seat as the highest-quality vehicle for the Indian residential upcycle.

**Evidence.** FY25, ended March 2025, delivered roughly five thousand four hundred crore of consolidated revenue, up from about four thousand eight hundred crore the year before, with EBITDA near thirty-two hundred crore and net profit around twenty-two hundred crore, giving an operating margin in the mid-fifties. By the third quarter just reported, 9-month FY26 revenue was about forty-two hundred crore with net margin near forty percent. The most recent call highlighted near-full occupancy in investment properties, with Commerz II at 100 percent, Commerz III at 90 to 91 percent on 91 percent EBITDA margin, Oberoi Mall at 99 percent, and Sky City Mall ramping fast with footfalls already running at double Oberoi Mall levels. Residential bookings in Q3 ran at roughly eight hundred and thirty crore of booking value across one hundred and thirty units.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Oberoi runs the cleanest balance sheet in the listed peer set. At FY25 year end cash and short-term investments were around three thousand crore, debt around thirty-four hundred crore and equity over fifteen thousand crore. The latest quarter shows gross debt-to-equity of zero point one seven and net debt-to-equity of minus zero point zero two — effectively net cash. Nine-month FY26 operating cash flow was about eleven hundred crore, absorbing around three hundred crore of FSI and acquisition payments. The board has already declared three interim dividends this year at two rupees per share.

**Portfolio fit.** This is the only real estate line in the India Top 20 and sits on the defensive side of the sector versus DLF, Lodha and Godrej Properties. The thesis rests on three compounders: Mumbai premium residential absorption, a Commerz and mall annuity stream compounding at near-peak occupancy, and a measured geographic step-out into Gurugram through a new NCR office and land deals.

**Debate.** Live debate on the Q2 and Q3 FY26 calls centred on launch slippage — projects in Goregaon, Borivali, Thane, Pedder Road and Gurugram pushed from Q3 into Q4 and FY27 — balance-sheet deployment into expensive Mumbai land, the pace of Sky City Mall stabilisation, the margin optics of residential revenue recognition on the early phases of Jardin, and whether the premium rating versus peers can stretch further.

## 12. Colgate-Palmolive India

**Pitch.** Colgate-Palmolive India is the dominant oral care franchise in the country, a majority-owned subsidiary of Colgate-Palmolive in the US, holding roughly half the Indian toothpaste market under the Colgate master brand and layering on Palmolive soaps, shower gels and handwash. Distribution reaches deep into rural India through a multi-decade kirana network, and brand equity is among the strongest in Indian FMCG. It earns a place in the India Top 20 as the purest defensive staples proxy in the list, the highest-return-on-capital name in the Indian FMCG bucket, and a clean way to own Indian premiumisation in a daily-use category where penetration is already universal but value per user keeps rising.

**Evidence.** FY25, ended March 2025, delivered revenue of about six thousand one hundred and eighty crore rupees, operating profit around nineteen hundred and thirty crore for a roughly 31 percent operating margin, EBITDA near twenty ninety crore and net income of about fourteen hundred and forty crore, with EPS of 52 rupees 83 paisa. The year just completed, however, has been bumpier: Q2 FY26 saw revenue down 6.3 percent year-on-year to fifteen hundred and seven crore and net profit fall from three hundred and ninety-five crore to three hundred and twenty-eight crore as GST rate changes disrupted the trade. Q3 FY26 showed the turn: net sales up 1.4 percent to fourteen hundred and seventy-three crore, net profit up 2.3 percent excluding a one-off labour-code charge, gross margin expanding 50 basis points sequentially to 69.7 percent, premium oral care growing strong double digits, and both urban and rural channels recovering post-GST.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The balance sheet is essentially net cash, with cash and equivalents rising to about thirteen hundred and six crore at end-September 2025 from six hundred and sixty-eight crore six months earlier. FY25 operating cash flow was roughly thirteen hundred and ninety-four crore against trivial capex near seventy-one crore, and dividends paid during FY25 totalled around sixteen hundred and twenty-six crore — a payout ratio above 100 percent of reported earnings, with an additional 24-rupee-per-share first interim dividend already declared for FY26. Market cap sits around fifty-two thousand seven hundred and fifty crore, roughly 5.7 billion dollars.

**Portfolio fit.** Colgate is the sole pure FMCG name in the India Top 20 and the defensive anchor alongside Bharti Airtel's telecom cash flows.
