# United Breweries — The Seven Questions

United Breweries is the thirty-first-largest position in the GEM portfolio at zero-point-nine percent, with a market cap of around seven billion US dollars. Here is United Breweries in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

United Breweries — the maker of Kingfisher beer in India — just printed its third-quarter fiscal 2026 results. Net sales grew four percent year-on-year despite a one-point-three-percent volume decline driven by colder weather. Gross margin reached forty-five-point-three percent — the highest in three years and up over two hundred basis points year-on-year. Earnings before interest and tax grew eighty-six percent in the quarter. Profit after tax surged one hundred and eleven percent. Premium volumes grew twenty-three percent year-to-date, outpacing the overall portfolio. National market share reached forty-eight to forty-nine percent, up ninety basis points for the year.

This is the Heineken-controlled Indian beer franchise, four years into the Heineken control transition, finally putting up margin numbers that prove the transition is working.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

United Breweries is the largest beer company in India by market share, with a forty-eight-to-forty-nine percent national share, controlled by Heineken since 2021. The flagship brand is Kingfisher, with extensions including Kingfisher Strong, Kingfisher Ultra, and the recently launched Kingfisher Strong Smooth. The brand portfolio also includes Heineken India editions, premium imports, and select non-alcoholic beverages.

Premium product growth is the strategic priority. Premium volume grew twenty-three percent year-to-date in fiscal 2026 and grew faster than the overall portfolio in every quarter. Market-share gains have been concentrated in premium segments, with market share of one hundred and eighty to two hundred basis points gained in the year.

The execution infrastructure is genuine. Over thirty-five thousand stores are now equipped with visi-coolers — the refrigerated cabinet displays that drive impulse beer purchases. New bottle infusion was thirty-seven percent year-to-date, with continuous improvement in returnable bottle yield. Brewery network optimisation, portfolio simplification, and business reorganisation are all in active progress.

The thing to internalise: United Breweries is not just a beer company. It is the structural beneficiary of Indian premiumisation under Heineken global discipline, in a market where beer category penetration remains a small fraction of global norms.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2021-onwards Heineken transition is the evidence.

Heineken acquired control of United Breweries in 2021, taking over from the Vijay Mallya era — a saga that included corporate-governance issues, regulatory investigations, and a complex ownership transition over several years. Heineken has since deployed global brewing discipline, productivity programmes, brand investment, and category-leadership strategy to a business that historically operated with weaker capital allocation.

The past-stress case that matters is the multi-year governance and operational repair. The Mallya-era United Breweries had elevated working capital, weaker cost discipline, regulatory issues including a Competition Commission of India penalty of seventy-five-billion rupees that remains under legal review, and operational complexity. Heineken stewarded a clinical turnaround. By third-quarter fiscal 2026, gross margin reached the highest in three years at forty-five-point-three percent. Earnings-before-interest-and-tax growth at eighty-six percent in the quarter, with profit after tax up one hundred and eleven percent, demonstrate that the operating leverage is finally arriving.

The capital discipline signals from the latest print: productivity and cost-savings programme targets three to six percent gross savings on the fiscal 2025 revenue base, to be realised between 2026 and 2028. Substantial share of cost efficiencies is planned for strategic reinvestment in brand and innovation. Monetisation of non-core land assets completed. Brewery network optimisation in progress. Sponsorship of the Women's Premier League added to brand visibility.

The watch item is the Competition Commission of India penalty proceedings and Bihar plant litigation — both legacy items under legal review.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a kicker. First: Indian beer category growth, with long-term industry volume growth expected at six to seven percent annually as disposable income rises and demographics favour. Second: premiumisation — premium volume growing twenty-three percent year-to-date is the highest-margin segment scaling fastest. Third: cost productivity, with three to six percent gross savings targeted on the fiscal 2025 base over three years, with reinvestment supporting sustained growth. The kicker is favourable state policy changes in Maharashtra, West Bengal, and Jharkhand providing tactical near-term tailwinds.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six metrics. Gross margin trajectory — forty-five-point-three percent, sustained above forty-three confirms the cost discipline. Premium volume growth — twenty-three percent year-to-date, sustained twenty-plus confirms the mix shift. Earnings-before-interest-and-tax growth — eighty-six percent in the quarter; sustained above twenty percent annually confirms operating leverage. National market share — forty-eight to forty-nine percent, continued gain confirms competitive position. New bottle infusion percentage — operational efficiency lever. Productivity programme execution — savings realised against the three-to-six-percent target.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

Q3 fiscal 2026 net profit was eighty-one crore rupees on a quarterly basis. Year-to-date earnings per share was eleven-point-seven-eight rupees. Market cap is around seven billion US dollars. Trading multiples sit at premium levels reflecting Heineken control and Indian premiumisation thesis — generally fifty-plus times earnings. Comparable set: Carlsberg India, Anheuser-Busch InBev India, Indian premium consumer staples like Hindustan Unilever and Nestle India at similar premium multiples for similar Indian-consumer compounder profiles.

The key sensitivity is Indian state-level beer regulation. Excise tax structures, distribution rules, and pricing-regulation evolution materially affect margins. Second sensitivity is aluminium and barley input costs.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

First variant: consensus still discounts United Breweries for the Mallya-era legacy and Competition Commission of India overhang. The Heineken-led turnaround is genuinely working — gross margin at three-year highs, earnings-before-interest-and-tax up eighty-six percent, premium share gain — but the multiple premium has been slow to reflect this. Second: Indian beer category growth is structurally underweighted by global investor models, who tend to import US or European beer-category-decline assumptions. Indian per-capita beer consumption is a small fraction of global averages with multi-decade convergence runway.

The honest counter: Indian state-level regulatory environment is genuinely complex. The CCI penalty and Bihar plant litigation are real, even if currently expected to be neutral. Premium multiples leave little room for disappointment.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

Risks: state-level regulatory shock (excise tax hikes or distribution changes); CCI penalty crystallising materially; Indian consumer recession or affordability stress; aluminium and barley cost spikes; competitive intensification from AB InBev or Carlsberg; productivity programme delivery missing targets.

Sell triggers: gross margin falling below forty percent for two consecutive quarters; premium volume growth decelerating below ten percent for two consecutive quarters; market share losing for two consecutive quarters; CCI penalty crystallising at the full seventy-five-billion-rupee level; Heineken stepping back from active management; productivity programme delivering below half the targeted savings.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions. United Breweries is the Heineken-controlled Indian beer compounder finally delivering operating leverage — gross margin three-year high, premium volumes twenty-three percent — with multi-decade Indian beer category growth ahead. The next print is the fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 result on the sixth of May. Watch gross margin, premium volume growth, and productivity-programme execution.
