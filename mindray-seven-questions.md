# Mindray — The Seven Questions

Shenzhen Mindray Bio-Medical is the twenty-seventh-largest position in the GEM portfolio at one-point-one percent, with a market cap of around thirty-five billion US dollars. Here is Mindray in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Mindray just closed 2025 with revenue of thirty-three-point-three billion yuan, down nine percent year-on-year, and net profit of eight-point-one billion yuan, down thirty percent. Those are ugly-looking numbers. Look closer. International revenue grew seven percent and now represents fifty-three percent of total. Domestic Chinese revenue fell twenty-three percent under the combined weight of centralised hospital procurement, diagnosis-related-group payment reforms, and an ongoing anti-corruption campaign on Chinese hospital equipment purchasing. The emerging businesses — cardiovascular and high-value consumables — grew thirty-nine percent. High-end ultrasound grew over seventy percent. Gross margin held above sixty percent despite the revenue pressure. Return on equity was still twenty-two percent. And the company paid out sixty-five percent of earnings in cash dividends — five-point-three billion yuan distributed.

This is what a world-class Chinese medical device company looks like when the domestic market is in cyclical distress and the international business is picking up the slack.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Mindray is the largest Chinese medical device manufacturer — ranked twenty-third globally by revenue in 2024 and the only Chinese firm in the global top thirty. Three core business segments plus an emerging fourth.

The first segment is In Vitro Diagnostics — blood chemistry analysers, immunoassay systems, haematology, urinalysis, and the reagents that run on those machines. Revenue in 2025 was twelve-point-two billion yuan, down nine percent, now representing more than thirty-six percent of total revenue. The international IVD business is expected to maintain rapid growth.

The second segment is Life Information and Support — patient monitors, anaesthesia machines, ventilators, defibrillators, operating-room integration. Revenue was nine-point-eight billion yuan, down twenty percent. Seventy-four percent of this segment's revenue is international — Mindray is a genuine global competitor to GE Healthcare, Philips, and Mindray's German peer Dräger in specific product lines.

The third segment is Medical Imaging — ultrasound, digital X-ray, diagnostic imaging systems. Revenue was five-point-seven billion yuan, down eighteen percent. Sixty-five percent international. Within imaging, high-end ultrasound products grew over seventy percent — a category-mix signal of premium-product acceptance in global markets.

The emerging segment includes cardiovascular and high-value consumables — the APT Medical acquisition added scale here. Emerging businesses revenue was five-point-four billion yuan, up thirty-nine percent, now sixteen percent of total.

The thing to internalise: Mindray is not a commodity Chinese medical device company. It is a top-global-thirty medical device compounder, currently absorbing the worst domestic-healthcare-procurement cycle in Chinese medical equipment history while accelerating international expansion, maintaining gross margin above sixty percent, and paying out two-thirds of earnings in cash.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2024-to-2025 Chinese healthcare-procurement stress test is the clearest evidence.

Li Xiting is founder, chairman and the strategic anchor of the company, with substantial ownership and long operational tenure. Management depth extends through a seasoned team of business-unit leaders.

The past-stress case that matters is 2024 to 2025. Three simultaneous pressures hit Chinese domestic medical equipment procurement: centralised hospital procurement programmes pushing prices down, diagnosis-related-group and diagnosis-intervention-payment reforms restricting hospital spending on high-cost equipment, and an ongoing anti-corruption campaign on hospital procurement practices that delayed large equipment purchases. Domestic revenue fell twenty-three percent in 2025 on the combined weight of these pressures. Most Chinese medical device peers saw similar or worse domestic pressure.

Mindray's response was disciplined. They did not cut research and development — R&D spend was maintained at eleven-point-eight percent of revenue, three-point-nine billion yuan. They did not abandon growth in international markets — instead, they accelerated international expansion, with international revenue growing seven percent and crossing fifty-three percent of the mix for the first time. They completed the DiaSys acquisition at a seventy-five percent stake to strengthen the international IVD supply chain. They acquired control of APT Medical to expand into cardiovascular high-value consumables. They paid out sixty-five percent of earnings as dividends — three interim dividends through 2025 totalling five-point-three billion yuan — signalling confidence in cash generation despite the earnings decline.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are strong despite the headline revenue pressure. Gross margin held above sixty percent. Return on equity at twenty-two percent is exceptional for an industrial company. Cash and equivalents at seventeen-and-a-half billion yuan on an asset-liability ratio of twenty-seven percent — very clean balance sheet. Net margin at twenty-four percent. Operating cash flow of ten-point-one billion yuan. The clean audit opinion from Ernst and Young confirms no accounting concerns.

The watch item is Li Xiting's succession. He has been the strategic architect for decades; depth of bench below him and a clearly communicated succession path are multi-year quality signals.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines, plus a kicker.

The first engine is international expansion. International revenue at fifty-three percent of the total and growing means Mindray is transitioning from a Chinese medical device company to a global medical device company. The international business grew seven percent in 2025 despite broader challenges, and management explicitly guides to rapid growth in 2026 led by developing markets and Europe. High-end ultrasound growth of seventy percent in global markets is evidence that product competitiveness is real. The DiaSys acquisition is a specific step to deepen international supply-chain capability.

The second engine is emerging businesses. Cardiovascular high-value consumables and newer product categories grew thirty-nine percent and now represent sixteen percent of total revenue. The APT Medical acquisition scaled this segment meaningfully. High-value consumables are higher-margin, recurring-revenue, razor-and-blade-style businesses — a structurally better economic profile than the equipment base.

The third engine is domestic recovery potential. Domestic revenue down twenty-three percent in 2025 is not the new steady state — it reflects specific one-time pressures. Management guidance is for domestic positive growth in 2026 and stable fast growth from 2027 onwards as the anti-corruption cycle normalises, the centralised procurement programmes complete their price-reset cycles, and DRG/DIP reforms settle into steady operating rhythm. The cyclical trough is the opportunity.

The kicker is the artificial intelligence and digital healthcare investment. Mindray is investing heavily in AI-driven medical devices and connected health platforms. As hospital digitisation accelerates globally, connected-device vendors with integrated software stacks capture disproportionate share. This is a multi-year strategic lever that should expand gross margin on both the domestic and international mix.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six specific metrics.

First, international revenue growth and mix — growing seven percent in 2025 and at fifty-three percent mix; continued mix progression confirms the transition to global company. Second, domestic revenue trajectory — down twenty-three percent; guidance for positive growth in 2026; timing of domestic recovery is the key near-term signal. Third, gross margin — sixty percent today, down from sixty-three; recovery toward sixty-three-plus is the mix-and-pricing test. Fourth, emerging businesses growth — plus thirty-nine percent; continued thirty-plus-percent growth confirms the diversification leg. Fifth, return on equity — twenty-two percent even in the cycle trough; sustained above twenty through the cycle confirms structural quality. Sixth, research-and-development intensity — eleven-point-eight percent of revenue; continued investment confirms compounder discipline.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

The honest framing. Full-year 2025 net profit was eight-point-one billion yuan, roughly one-point-two billion US dollars. Return on equity was twenty-two percent. Gross margin was sixty percent. Market cap is around thirty-five billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, Mindray trades at roughly thirty times trailing earnings and closer to twenty-five times on forward earnings that assume domestic recovery. For a medical device company with twenty-two percent return on equity, sixty-percent gross margins, sixty-five-percent dividend payout, and international expansion, that is reasonable but not cheap. The key question is whether the domestic trough is persistent or transient.

The simple framing: a global top-thirty medical device manufacturer, currently absorbing a specific Chinese cyclical shock while growing internationally, trading at mid-twenty-times forward earnings. Comparable set: Becton Dickinson, Edwards Lifesciences, Masimo, Dräger in Germany — global medical device peers trade at similar or higher multiples for comparable returns on equity.

The key sensitivity is the pace of Chinese domestic recovery. If anti-corruption and procurement reforms take longer to normalise than the 2026 guidance implies, the multiple gets pressured and earnings stay weak. Second sensitivity is international macro — developing-markets health-system budgets are tied to local macro conditions.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

Two variant perception angles.

First: consensus treats the 2025 domestic revenue collapse as a signal of permanent impairment to Mindray's Chinese franchise. The variant is that it is cyclical — driven by specific anti-corruption timing, centralised procurement cycle-reset, and DRG/DIP implementation — and that by 2027 domestic revenue is back in growth mode. The medical equipment Chinese hospitals need does not disappear; procurement timing shifts but eventually normalises.

Second: the international business is priced as a gradual transition rather than as a step-change. With international at fifty-three percent of revenue and accelerating, Mindray is structurally a different company than three years ago — the comparable set is shifting from Chinese medical device peers to global medical device peers. Once international exceeds sixty percent of the mix and international growth is durably double-digit, the multiple re-rates toward global comparables.

The honest counter: Chinese medical device pricing pressure may be structural rather than cyclical. If DRG/DIP reforms become more restrictive, if centralised procurement becomes more aggressive on pricing, or if the anti-corruption campaign has lingering equipment-upgrade-delay effects, the domestic earnings base could remain depressed for longer than consensus and the international growth may not fully offset.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

The real risks, ranked.

One: domestic Chinese healthcare policy further tightening. Additional centralised procurement rounds or DRG/DIP restrictions that further depress Chinese medical equipment pricing. Two: international expansion execution. Developing-markets and European expansion requires local regulatory, distribution, and service capability that takes years to build. Three: research-and-development productivity. Maintaining eleven-percent-of-revenue R&D spend is fine if productivity holds; any decline in new-product success would compress the compounder. Four: currency. Yuan weakness affects international reported margins. Five: Li Xiting succession. Founder is the strategic anchor; any leadership transition is a multi-year risk. Six: acquisition integration. DiaSys and APT Medical are ongoing integration priorities; any material issue would delay the diversification thesis.

Specific sell triggers. Domestic revenue continuing to decline in 2026 — cycle thesis broken. Gross margin falling below fifty-five percent for two consecutive periods — pricing structural. International revenue growth decelerating below five percent — transition stalling. Return on equity falling below fifteen percent for two consecutive years — quality eroding. R&D spend materially reduced in absolute terms. Li Xiting stepping back without a named credible successor.
