# Tencent — The Seven Questions

**Ticker:** 700 HK | **FY 2025 revenue:** RMB 751.8bn (~US$104bn) | **GEM weight:** 6.7% (#3 in portfolio)
**Source base:** Quartr-grounded (Q4/FY 2025 results announcement, 18 March 2026; Q3 2025 call, 13 November 2025)
**Written:** April 2026

---

## Cold open

Tencent did something on 18 March that almost nobody outside Asia noticed: it **reported FY 2025 revenue growth of 14%, gross margin expansion to 56% (up 300bps), and non-IFRS profit growth of 17%** — at a US$104bn revenue base. International games crossed **USD 10 billion**. Marketing services grew **19%** with ad load *still well below peers*, because AI-powered targeting is doing the lifting. The dividend was raised 18% to HKD 5.30.

This is supposed to be a mature, regulated, China-tech-discounted business in structural decline. It is, instead, growing faster than it has in three years and getting more profitable at every layer.

Seven questions.

---

## 1. What is it?

Tencent is the operating system of Chinese digital life. Three legs, one ecosystem:

- **Value-Added Services (VAS) — 49% of revenue.** Domestic Games +18% in 2025 on Delta Force, Honour of Kings and VALORANT. International Games +33% to RMB 77.4bn — Supercell, PUBG MOBILE, Wuthering Waves
- **Marketing Services — 19% of revenue.** Weixin/WeChat ads, Video Accounts, Weixin Search. Q4 +17% YoY, FY +19%, gross margin at **60%**. AI ad targeting is the structural driver
- **FinTech and Business Services — 31% of revenue.** Tenpay, wealth management, consumer credit, Tencent Cloud. Cloud growth re-accelerated to **22% in Q4** as enterprise AI workloads drove demand. Whole segment hit 51% gross margin (up from 47%)

**Combined Weixin + WeChat MAU is 1.42 billion.** It is the most important consumer surface in China. Nothing else comes close.

The thing to internalise: Tencent is not three businesses bolted together. It is **one user, one ID, one wallet, one social graph** — monetised through three different lenses. Every other Chinese tech company has to compete with this graph; Tencent owns it.

## 2. Can we trust them?

Yes — and this is one of the genuinely under-appreciated facts about the franchise.

**Pony Ma (Ma Huateng)** is still chairman and CEO, 27 years in. **Martin Lau** runs day-to-day as President. The leadership team is the most stable in Chinese tech and has the longest track record of capital discipline of any tech company at this scale.

**The 2021-22 regulatory tell.** When Beijing launched the platform crackdown, Tencent cut costs hard, stopped chasing vanity metrics, and divested non-core assets (JD.com, Meituan stakes) *at scale*. They didn't lobby, didn't whinge, didn't fight regulators publicly. They complied, de-risked, and waited. The result: three years later, Tencent is the Chinese tech company regulators now *cite approvingly* while competitors still wear scars. That cold-blooded adaptability is the franchise's deepest operating asset.

**Capital allocation signals from FY 2025:**

- **Final dividend raised 18% to HKD 5.30/share** — aggressive **HK$80bn/year buybacks** for last two years continue
- Operating margin expansion driven by *mix shift toward higher-margin revenue streams*, not cost-cutting — the right kind of operating leverage
- Capex stepped up materially for AI infrastructure but absorbed inside the cash flow envelope without breaking the buyback / dividend cadence
- Investment portfolio is a real number: share of profits from associates was **RMB 23.7bn** in 2025 (Tencent owns large stakes in Meituan, Pinduoduo, Sea, Spotify — these feed the balance sheet)
- Communication style is conservative: they consistently underpromise and overdeliver on profit vs revenue growth

**The watch item.** Succession at Pony Ma. He is 54 — younger than Wei or Galperin — so this is a 5-10 year question, not a 1-2 year question.

## 3. Why does value compound?

Three engines, each currently inflecting in the right direction.

**AI is making the ad business structurally better.** Marketing services grew 19% in 2025 on essentially flat ad load. That delta is pure quality improvement: better targeting (AI), better closed-loop attribution (Mini Programs and Mini Shops let users transact without leaving Weixin), more inventory in Video Accounts (user time spent +20% YoY). Ad gross margin went from 55% to 58%. Tencent has been the laggard on ad load for years; AI is the unlock that lets them monetise without degrading the user experience.

**International games is finally a real engine.** International games crossed **USD 10bn** in 2025 — bigger than Take-Two, bigger than EA's standalone game business. Supercell, Riot/VALORANT, PUBG MOBILE, Wuthering Waves are all live evergreen franchises with multi-year revenue tails. Tencent's content-production playbook (acquire majority stake → leave studio independent → cross-promote into China + global) has been quietly perfected over a decade.

**Tencent Cloud is finally profitable at scale.** Q4 cloud revenue grew 22%, segment hit 51% gross margin. The unsung leverage: Tencent's HY 3.0 LLM and AI products (Yuanbao, WorkBuddy, QClaw) are now contributing real cloud workload demand from external customers. They aren't just spending on AI — they're earning back.

**The kicker nobody is pricing:** WeChat as the agentic AI distribution layer. When agentic AI hits China consumer scale, the interface is *not* a standalone app — it's a Mini Program inside Weixin. Tencent owns that pipe.

## 4. What proves us right and what to track?

Five specific KPIs:

- **Marketing services revenue growth and ad load** — AI-driven growth without ad load expansion is the structural thesis. If ad load creeps up, the ceiling is lower. If revenue keeps growing 17%+ on flat load, the ceiling is much higher
- **International games revenue trajectory** — crossed USD 10bn; if it can grow 25%+ for another two years, this becomes a cash compounder of its own
- **Cloud revenue growth and segment GM** — re-accelerated to 22% Q4; if Q1/Q2 2026 keep that pace, cloud is a real third compounder
- **Buyback cadence** — HKD 80bn/year for 2024 and 2025; if 2026 buyback drops materially, the signal weakens
- **Mini Shops GMV disclosure** — Tencent has been coy about absolute numbers; when they start disclosing, that's when the market re-rates e-commerce optionality

## 5. What's it worth?

The valuation is unreasonable for what you get.

- Market cap at HKD 523/share × ~9.0bn shares ≈ **HKD 4.7tn (~US$600bn)**
- FY 2025 non-IFRS profit attributable: **RMB 259.6bn (~US$36bn)**
- Trailing P/E on non-IFRS ~**16.5x**, growing 17%
- Net cash + investment portfolio (Meituan, Pinduoduo, Sea stakes etc.) conservatively worth **US$80–100bn**
- Strip that out and the operating business trades at roughly **13–14x earnings**, growing 17%, with three structurally improving engines

Compare to US large-cap tech: Meta on ~24x, Google on ~22x, Microsoft on ~32x. Tencent has similarly entrenched ecosystem, similar gross margins, faster gaming growth, superior capital return. The discount is entirely *political*.

**Key sensitivity:** the China multiple. If the China discount compresses 25% over the next 3 years (because earnings keep delivering), you get most of your return from re-rating on top of mid-teens earnings growth. If the discount widens, you still get mid-teens compounding. The asymmetry sits entirely with the multiple.

## 6. Where is the market wrong?

**One — AI is Tencent's single biggest tailwind, not threat.** Western narrative: chip controls block China AI, Tencent falls behind. Reality: DeepSeek, Qwen, and HY 3.0 are competitive enough at inference scale, costs are dramatically lower than US frontier models, and the *application surface* (1.4bn DAU social graph) is unique. Marketing services is already proving the thesis — AI is *making the existing ad business work better*. The same pattern is going to play out in cloud (happening), gaming (content production), and consumer assistants (Yuanbao).

**Two — the regulatory cycle is structurally over.** The 2021-22 crackdown still defines how investors think about Tencent. But the actual evidence is that Beijing is now *pulling* on Chinese tech to be the engine of consumer recovery and AI competitiveness. Game licence approvals flow. Mini Programs are supported. Fintech regime stable. Tencent has been compliant so long that the regulatory *risk premium* in the multiple isn't warranted.

**The honest counter:** if US-China escalates further (export controls extending to consumer software, ADR delisting, sanctions on specific entities), Tencent gets caught up regardless of its own behaviour. Position size accordingly.

## 7. What could kill it, and when do we sell?

**Real risks, ranked:**

- **Geopolitical escalation.** US-China tech cold war moving to consumer software (TikTok-style action against WeChat globally, restrictions on US investors holding HK-listed Chinese tech) — Tencent multiple compresses regardless of fundamentals
- **Domestic gaming policy.** Return to 2021-style tightening on game time, monetisation, or licensing would hit the highest-margin segment
- **AI capex outpacing returns.** Stepped up AI infrastructure spend significantly; if cloud + AI revenue doesn't keep pace with depreciation, margin expansion stalls
- **Pony Ma succession.** Not imminent but the firm has been his since 1998; discount widens the moment that question becomes live
- **Mini Shops failing to monetise.** Big variant-perception bet on Weixin-as-commerce relies on real GMV
- **Investment portfolio mark-downs.** ~US$80-100bn of stakes feeds equity and book value; China internet sell-off hits reported profit via share-of-associates

**Sell triggers (specific, not vague):**

- Marketing services growth decelerates below overall company growth for two consecutive quarters — AI thesis breaking
- Buyback paused without explicit replacement use of capital
- Major US/EU regulatory action against WeChat in Western markets
- Domestic regulatory rhetoric returns to 2021 tone (keywords: "platform", "anti-monopoly", "youth gaming addiction")
- Pony Ma announces transition or material stake sale

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions, twelve minutes. The Tencent setup right now is unusual: a business growing 17% in profit, expanding margins, re-accelerating cloud, doing an HK$80bn/year buyback, *and* trading at a 13-14x multiple stripped of net cash and investments. The China discount has done you a favour. The question that matters most: do you believe AI is making this business *structurally better* — or is it a temporary boost that will normalise in 12 months? The Q1 2026 print on 13 May is the next checkpoint.
