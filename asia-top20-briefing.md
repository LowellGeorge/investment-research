# Asia Top 20 briefing notes 🌏

A Quartr-grounded run-through of the Asia Top 20 portfolio, built to listen to on repeat. Each name gets five short paragraphs: the pitch, the financial evidence, the balance sheet, the portfolio fit, and the live debate from the most recent earnings call.

## 1. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (2330 TT)

**Pitch.** TSMC is the dedicated foundry that manufactures leading-edge logic chips for almost every fabless designer that matters — Apple, Nvidia, AMD, Qualcomm, Broadcom, MediaTek. It does not design its own chips and does not compete with its customers, which is why the whole fabless ecosystem routes through it. The business today is dominated by high-performance computing and smartphone silicon at 3, 5, and 7 nanometres, with N2 entering high-volume manufacturing in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the A16 node ramping in 2026. In the Asia Top 20, TSMC is the single anchor exposure to the AI build-out — it is the one name that compounds regardless of which hyperscaler wins.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 revenue grew 35.9% in US dollar terms, with fourth-quarter revenue of NT$1,046 billion, or USD 33.7 billion, up 25.5% year on year. Full-year gross margin was 59.9%, up 3.8 percentage points, and operating margin was 50.8%, up 5.1 points — so the entire revenue step-up dropped through with significant positive mix. Fourth-quarter gross margin reached 62.3% and operating margin 54.0%. Advanced nodes at 7 nanometres and below were 77% of fourth-quarter wafer revenue, and AI accelerators contributed high-teens percent of total 2025 revenue. Diluted EPS for the fourth quarter was NT$19.50, and full-year EPS was up 46.4% with return on equity at 35.4%.

**Balance sheet and cash.** TSMC ended 2025 with NT$3,068 billion of cash and marketable securities, or roughly USD 98 billion, and a net cash position of NT$2,036 billion after rising NT$279 billion in the quarter. Free cash flow for the year was NT$1,003 billion, up 15.2%, even as capital expenditure stepped up to USD 40.9 billion from USD 29.8 billion the prior year. Management has guided 2026 capex to USD 52 to 56 billion, with 70 to 80% going to advanced process technologies — so cash generation is being reinvested aggressively rather than returned. Dividends per share rose to NT$18 for 2025, and the board has guided at least NT$23 for 2026.

**Portfolio fit.** TSMC plays the role of the non-negotiable core compounder in the Asia Top 20 — the single name that captures the AI capex cycle without picking a winner among the designers. Its nearest peer in the portfolio, SK Hynix, offers the same AI exposure through HBM memory but with far more cyclicality and lower through-cycle margins; MediaTek and Realtek sit downstream on the design side. TSMC is where you put the structural, low-beta bet on compute demand. The risk a portfolio manager has to accept is concentration — at a 1.63 trillion US dollar market cap, the name is already a top-two weight in almost every Asia and emerging-market benchmark.

**Debate.** The live debate on the most recent earnings call was almost entirely about margin dilution. Management repeated its guidance that overseas fab ramp-up — principally Arizona, with Japan and Germany following — will dilute gross margin by 2 to 3 percentage points initially, widening to 3 to 4 points over time, and that the N2 ramp will cost a further 2 to 3 points in 2026. Analysts pressed repeatedly on whether that compression is already priced in and whether pricing power is sufficient to offset it; management pointed to a supply-demand gap expected to persist through 2027 and a long-term revenue CAGR approaching 25% for 2024 to 2029 as the offset. The second strand of debate was geopolitical — tariffs, foreign exchange, and the pace of the Arizona expansion — but on this the tone was notably more confident than six months ago, with a second Arizona land purchase already completed.

## 2. Tencent Holdings (700 HK)

**Pitch.** Tencent is the operating system of Chinese digital life. Weixin and WeChat together reached one billion four hundred and fourteen million monthly active users, and the company monetises that attention through the world's largest games franchise, a fast-growing marketing services business built on Video Accounts and Mini Programs, fintech rails covering payments and consumer loans, and a cloud unit that has now reached profitability at scale. It is the natural internet anchor for Asia Top 20: a rare platform that owns distribution, content, and payments inside one closed ecosystem, with international gaming revenue crossing ten billion United States dollars in 2025.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 revenue rose fourteen percent to seven hundred and fifty-one point eight billion Chinese yuan, with fourth-quarter revenue up thirteen percent to one hundred and ninety-four point four billion yuan. Gross profit climbed twenty-one percent to four hundred and twenty-two point six billion yuan as group gross margin expanded roughly three points to fifty-six percent. Non-IFRS net profit grew seventeen percent to two hundred and fifty-nine point six billion yuan, and non-IFRS operating margin reached thirty-six to thirty-seven percent. International games grew thirty-two percent in the fourth quarter, marketing services grew seventeen to nineteen percent, and fintech and business services grew eight percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Tencent ended 2025 with a net cash position of one hundred and seven point one billion yuan, up forty percent year on year. Free cash flow for the year reached one hundred and eighty-two point six billion yuan, and fourth-quarter free cash flow jumped more than sixfold to thirty-four billion yuan. The company repurchased around one hundred and fifty-three million shares for roughly eighty billion Hong Kong dollars during 2025 and proposed a dividend of five point three Hong Kong dollars per share, up eighteen percent. Full-year capital expenditure was seventy-nine point two billion yuan.

**Portfolio fit.** Tencent is the internet and platforms anchor of Asia Top 20, sitting alongside NetEase on games but broadening the thesis far beyond titles into advertising, payments, and cloud. Where Taiwan Semiconductor owns the silicon layer that artificial intelligence runs on, Tencent owns the Chinese consumer surface where it gets used. Against the Asian bank holdings, which are rate and credit trades, Tencent gives the book a high-margin software compounder with a global game publisher attached, and a valuation still discounted for Beijing regulatory risk.

**Debate.** The live question is the pivot in capital allocation. Management has said artificial intelligence investment in Hunyuan and Yuanbao will more than double in 2026, funded out of core earnings, and that revenue growth may outpace profit growth as a result. Share buybacks are being dialled down to pay for it. Bears worry about temporary margin compression, graphics processing unit and talent constraints, and a gaming industry stuck in oversupply. Bulls point to cloud reaching profitability at scale, an improving advertising environment, and the Apple App Store China commission cut as a gross margin tailwind.

## 3. Samsung Electronics preferred (005935 KS)

**Pitch.** Samsung Electronics is Korea's flagship technology conglomerate and the world's largest memory maker, spanning DRAM and NAND, a leading-edge foundry, Galaxy smartphones, OLED and large-format displays, and a global consumer-electronics arm covering televisions and appliances. End markets stretch from hyperscale AI servers and data centres to handsets, PCs, cars and home appliances. The portfolio holds the preferred share class, ticker zero zero five nine three five KS, which carries identical economic rights to the common stock but historically trades at a meaningful discount, giving a higher dividend yield on the same underlying cash flows without sacrificing the business exposure.

**Evidence.** Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue hit a record ninety-three point eight trillion Korean won, up twenty-four percent year on year, with operating profit of twenty point one trillion won, more than tripling year on year, and an operating margin of twenty-one point four percent. Device Solutions drove the jump, with memory sales of thirty-seven point one trillion won, up sixty-two percent year on year, DRAM average selling prices up around forty percent sequentially and NAND up mid-twenties. Full-year sales reached three hundred and thirty-three point six trillion won, with operating profit of forty-three point six trillion won and net income of forty-five point two trillion won.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Year-end cash and equivalents stood at one hundred and twenty-five point eight five trillion Korean won, with a net cash position of one hundred point six one trillion won, keeping net debt to equity firmly negative. Free cash flow in 2025 was thirty-six point five trillion won, with fifty percent earmarked for shareholder returns. Capital expenditure totalled fifty-two point seven trillion won, reflecting heavy memory and foundry intensity. Treasury stock purchases of eight point one nine trillion won were completed, with six point six trillion won of shares to be cancelled, and a year-end dividend of five hundred and sixty-seven won was declared on the preferred shares.

**Portfolio fit.** Samsung preferred is the diversified memory-and-handset anchor in Asia Top 20, sitting alongside SK Hynix, which is the pure-play high-bandwidth memory torque name. Hynix offers the cleaner, higher-beta expression of the AI memory cycle; Samsung adds optionality through a leading-edge foundry, the world's number-one smartphone franchise at nineteen point seven percent unit share, number-one global television share, and OLED displays, plus strategic bets in robotics, automotive and audio. The preferred line specifically captures all of that at a structural discount to the common, improving the running yield on an identical claim.

**Debate.** Management flags persistent global trade barriers, rising component costs, and memory supply shortages as AI-driven demand outpaces capacity. The live bear case centres on high-bandwidth memory: HBM4 has only just entered full-scale mass production, leaving Samsung chasing SK Hynix's incumbent position with Nvidia, while foundry still leans on ramping first-generation two-nanometre and four-nanometre HBM base-die. Mobile delivered just one point nine trillion won of operating profit on shipments of sixty million units, with rising memory input costs threatening handset margins into 2026, and network orders face industry headwinds.

## 4. AIA Group (1299 HK)

**Pitch.** AIA is the pure pan-Asian life insurer, operating across eighteen markets with the crown jewels being Hong Kong (including the mainland Chinese visitor, or MCV, business), mainland China, Thailand, Singapore and Malaysia, plus a fast-growing stake in Tata AIA in India. Distribution is agency-led, through the Premier Agency model that it has refined for decades. Long-dated protection and savings policies compound the in-force book across multi-decade horizons, turning today's new business into tomorrow's operating profit. It earns its slot in Asia Top 20 as the insurance anchor: the cleanest structural bet on rising Asian wealth, low penetration and the regional protection gap.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 delivered record results. Value of new business, or VONB, grew fifteen percent to five point five billion dollars, with margin up three point six points to fifty-eight point five percent. Operating profit after tax per share rose twelve percent to seven point one billion dollars, and operating return on equity reached fifteen point five percent. Hong Kong VONB jumped twenty-eight percent to two point three billion, with agency contributing seventy percent. Mainland China VONB rose two percent to one point two billion, with new-region expansion up forty-five percent. ASEAN VONB was two billion, led by Thailand at plus thirteen percent and Singapore at plus fourteen. Tata AIA VONB rose thirty-three percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** The shareholder capital ratio ended 2025 at two hundred and twenty-one percent after returning four point seven billion dollars to holders. Free surplus stood at just under eleven billion dollars, with underlying free surplus generation up eleven percent per share to six point eight billion. A fresh one point seven billion dollar buyback was launched alongside results, and the final dividend was raised ten percent. Since March 2022 AIA has repurchased one point six billion shares, cutting the count by thirteen percent, and returned twenty-two point nine billion dollars cumulatively. The stated policy is a seventy-five percent payout of net free surplus generation. S and P upgraded the financial strength rating to double-A.

**Portfolio fit.** Within Asia Top 20, AIA is the pure long-duration compounder and the non-credit financial. Earnings are driven by the build of policies in force, VONB conversion into contractual service margin, and disciplined capital return, not by net interest margin or loan growth. That contrasts cleanly with the bank sleeve, where ICICI, HDFC, Kotak, BCA and OCBC are exposed to the credit cycle, rate curves and deposit competition. AIA's sensitivities to interest rates and equity markets are small: a fifty basis point rate shift moves embedded value by only about one percent. It is the insurance anchor that diversifies, rather than doubling, the book's credit exposure.

**Debate.** The live bear case centres on the pace of mainland Chinese visitor recovery in Hong Kong, where the twenty-eight percent VONB jump partly reflects temporary regulatory tailwinds management itself flagged. Mainland China agency productivity is a second question: headline VONB grew only two percent, and the ten percent underlying figure relies on new-region expansion holding a forty percent compound path. Discount rate and economic assumption changes dragged reported China growth. Net profit fell nine percent on non-operating items, a reminder of investment variance noise. Finally, bulls want confirmation the seventy-five percent payout and buyback cadence survive new capital standards and the global minimum tax regime.

## 5. ICICI Bank (ICICIBC IN)

**Pitch.** ICICI Bank is India's number-two private-sector bank by assets, revitalised under Sandeep Bakhshi into a best-in-class universal franchise spanning retail, business banking, SME, and corporate lending, backed by a rapidly expanding branch network that now exceeds seven thousand outlets and a modern, in-house tech stack. It sits in Asia Top 20 as the purest high-quality compounder on the Indian growth story: rising financialisation of household savings, formalising SME credit, and a corporate capex cycle that is finally turning. Among Indian private banks it combines scale, execution, and asset-quality discipline better than any peer right now.

**Evidence.** In the December quarter of fiscal 2026, net interest income rose seven point seven percent year on year to about INR 219 billion, with net interest margin expanding slightly to four point three zero percent. Core operating profit grew six percent to roughly INR 175 billion, while profit after tax came in at INR 113 billion, modestly lower year on year due to one-off standard asset provisions. Domestic loans grew eleven point five percent and deposits nine point two percent; business banking surged twenty-two point eight percent. Return on assets held at two point one one percent and standalone return on equity at fourteen point three percent, with adjusted return on equity around fifteen point five percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Asset quality remains the standout. Net non-performing assets improved to just zero point three seven percent, with provision coverage on non-performing assets at seventy-five point four percent and gross non-performing assets at one point five eight percent at the September stage. Annualised credit cost in the first half ran at forty basis points, with contingency buffers of roughly INR 131 billion, about zero point nine percent of advances, sitting on top. Common equity tier one was sixteen point four six percent and total capital adequacy seventeen point three four percent, with average liquidity coverage at one hundred and twenty-six percent. Capital is abundant; the trajectory is benign, not deteriorating.

**Portfolio fit.** ICICI is the private-sector banking growth compounder slot in Asia Top 20, alongside HDFC Bank, Kotak Mahindra, and Bank Central Asia. HDFC is bigger but digesting the housing-finance merger and running slower on margins and return on equity. Kotak is smaller, more conservative, and structurally lower-growth. Bank Central Asia offers Indonesian deposit-franchise quality but a smaller addressable market. ICICI uniquely combines scale, above-system loan growth, sector-leading margins above four percent, and a mid-teens return on equity, which is the best combination of growth and profitability in the Indian private-bank complex today.

**Debate.** The live bear case is margin pressure as deposit repricing continues to catch up with loan yields, with management guiding that net interest margin will stay range-bound as repo and marginal-cost lending-rate cuts flow through. Unsecured retail is softer: credit cards shrank three point five percent year on year and six point seven percent sequentially, and operating costs climbed over thirteen percent on new labour-code provisioning. A Reserve Bank of India directive forced extra standard asset provisions against a roughly INR 200 to 250 billion priority-sector agricultural book, a reminder that credit cost can normalise abruptly. Deposit growth also continues to trail loan growth.

## 6. HDFC Bank (HDFCB IN)

**Pitch.** HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector lender — a universal franchise with a historically pristine retail credit book and a near-legendary compounding track record. The July 2023 merger with parent HDFC Limited pulled India's biggest mortgage book onto the bank balance sheet, making HDFC simultaneously the country's top home-loan originator and its leading deposit franchise. With roughly nine thousand six hundred branches, half in semi-urban and rural India, it is the scaled private-bank bet on Indian household formation, credit penetration and formalisation — a core anchor for any Asia Top 20 portfolio.

**Evidence.** Latest quarter, the three months to December 2025: net interest income of three hundred and twenty-six point two billion rupees, up six point four percent year on year; non-interest income one hundred and thirty-three billion, up sixteen percent; net revenue four hundred and fifty-nine billion. Reported net interest margin three point three five percent. Profit after tax one hundred and eighty-seven billion rupees, with earnings per share of twelve point one. Return on assets one point nine two percent and return on equity thirteen point nine percent. Average deposits grew twelve percent year on year and average advances nineteen percent, outpacing the system.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Asset quality held stable: gross non-performing assets one point two four percent, net NPAs zero point four two percent, and ex-agri gross NPAs a cleaner zero point nine seven percent. Net credit cost landed at thirty-seven basis points after recoveries, with net slippages ex-agri of twenty-four to twenty-six basis points per quarter — still enviable by any global yardstick, though agri remains the watch item after a five-billion-rupee one-off provision. Capital is fortress-like: total capital adequacy nineteen point nine percent, common equity tier one seventeen point four percent. Balance sheet of forty point nine trillion rupees.

**Portfolio fit.** HDFC anchors the Indian private-bank sleeve alongside ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra and Bank Central Asia. ICICI is currently compounding faster with higher margins; Kotak is smaller, more conservative and slower-growing. HDFC is the steady-hand scale leg — enormous, diversified, CASA-funded at thirty-three point six percent, still digesting the merger but with optionality as the loan-deposit ratio normalises. This is the "compound at fifteen percent for a decade and sleep at night" position, providing defensive ballast while ICICI captures the cyclical upside.

**Debate.** The live bear case is execution on the post-merger glide path: credit-deposit ratio is being walked down toward ninety percent in fiscal 2026 and eighty-five to ninety percent by fiscal 2027, which mechanically caps advances growth below ICICI until deposits catch up. Net interest margin at three point three five percent is still below pre-merger levels; management expects deposit repricing to aid recovery over four to five quarters. Additional overhangs: agri regulatory recalibration, an eight-billion-rupee labour-code hit, and expected credit loss transition. Loan growth guided at system plus a couple of points in fiscal 2027.

## 7. MediaTek (2454 TT)

**Pitch.** MediaTek is Taiwan's number-two fabless chip designer, dominant in mid-range smartphone application processors through its Dimensity family and a clear leader in WiFi, Bluetooth and TV system-on-chips. It manufactures exclusively through TSMC, just completed its first two-nanometre tape-out, and is pivoting aggressively into data-centre ASICs, automotive ADAS and edge AI. Recent wins include the GB10 edge AI chip co-designed with Nvidia that powers the DGX Spark, plus a Denso ADAS partnership. In the Asia Top 20 it is the fabless design leg, capturing handset unit volume alongside growing custom-silicon optionality.

**Evidence.** Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue was one hundred and fifty point two billion New Taiwan dollars, up eight point eight percent year on year, with full-year revenue a record five hundred and ninety-six billion, up twelve point three percent. Q4 gross margin was forty-six point one percent, operating margin fourteen point five percent, net income twenty-three point one billion. Mobile was fifty-nine percent of Q4 revenue, up eighteen percent sequentially on Dimensity 9500 traction. Smart Edge grew thirteen percent year on year, Power IC was five percent of mix. Connectivity revenue exceeded three billion United States dollars, with WiFi 7 tripling.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Cash and current financial assets at year-end were two hundred and forty-seven point seven billion New Taiwan dollars, with current ratio one point three one and only modest short-term loans, leaving a clear net cash position. Operating cash flow for 2025 was one hundred and sixty-three billion, up from one hundred and fifty-six billion. Cash dividends paid during the year totalled eighty-six billion. Research and development spend was one hundred and forty-eight billion, or twenty-four point nine percent of sales, consistent with the prior year, reflecting heavy investment in two-nanometre, ASIC and AI-edge roadmaps.

**Portfolio fit.** MediaTek sits at the fabless design layer of the Asia Top 20 semiconductor stack, complementing TSMC as foundry, Samsung preferred as integrated device manufacturer, SK Hynix in memory, Realtek in niche connectivity and Quanta as server ODM. It captures mid-market and increasingly flagship handset unit volume through Dimensity, layers in WiFi 7 and 5G connectivity dominance, and adds optionality via data-centre ASIC and automotive. It is the vehicle for edge-AI inference and custom silicon exposure that the pure foundry and memory names cannot provide.

**Debate.** The live bear case is whether Dimensity can durably hold premium share against Qualcomm at the high end, where management admits pricing pressure is fierce. Full-year gross margin fell from forty-nine point six percent to forty-seven point five percent, and 2026 is guided to around forty-six percent, so margins are still drifting lower. Q1 2026 revenue is guided down two to eight percent year on year as smartphone demand weakens on rising memory and bill-of-materials costs. The data-centre ASIC ramp, targeted above one billion United States dollars in 2026, is real but unproven at scale.

## 8. Midea Group (000333 CH)

**Pitch.** Midea is China's largest home appliance group — heating, ventilation and air conditioning, white goods and small appliances — paired with a fast-growing business-to-business arm spanning industrial automation through KUKA robotics, smart building technology, and new energy and industrial tech. Management is aggressively pushing own-brand exports through fifty country markets and twenty-nine global R and D centres, turning a domestic champion into a genuine global consumer-industrial hybrid. It belongs in Asia Top 20 as the only explicit China-domiciled consumer compounder, with a credible second engine in robotics, elevators and climate systems.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 revenue hit four hundred and fifty-eight point five billion renminbi, up double digits, with net profit attributable to shareholders of forty-three point nine five billion renminbi, up fourteen percent. Smart home delivered two hundred and ninety-nine point nine billion renminbi, up eleven percent; business-to-business revenue reached one hundred and twenty-two point eight billion renminbi, up seventeen point five percent, split across building technology at thirty-five point eight billion, robotics and automation at thirty-one billion, and industrial tech at twenty-seven point two billion. Overseas sales were one hundred and ninety-five point nine five billion renminbi, up nearly sixteen percent. Smart home gross margin was twenty-nine point nine percent; business-to-business gross margin twenty point eight percent.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Year-end cash and equivalents stood at sixty-eight point five billion renminbi, with total assets of six hundred and eight point eight billion and shareholders' equity of two hundred and twenty-three point two billion. Operating cash flow was fifty-three point four billion renminbi. The board proposed a final dividend of thirty-eight renminbi per ten shares, lifting the full-year payout to forty-three renminbi per ten shares. Two buyback programmes retired over one hundred and thirty-five million shares. S and P upgraded the credit rating to A plus in September. EBITDA to total debt reached sixty-eight percent and interest cover twenty-five times, confirming a very robust balance sheet alongside an aggressive cross-border M and A programme.

**Portfolio fit.** Midea is the China consumer-industrial compounder slot in Asia Top 20. It contrasts sharply with Tencent and NetEase, which capture the digital consumer, and with the Chinese banks proxies, which express financial and macro exposure. Midea instead gives direct exposure to Chinese household spending on air conditioners, kitchen and laundry appliances, plus a rising global export story through own-brand platforms across Europe, the Middle East and Southeast Asia. It is the only explicit China-domiciled consumer name in the book, and uniquely adds factory automation, elevators and building climate through KUKA, Toshiba Elevator China and Arbonia.

**Debate.** The live bear case starts with a domestic appliance market that contracted four point three percent in 2025, with further shrinkage expected, tying Midea to soft China property and stimulus-dependent replacement demand. Overseas own-brand traction is real but margin-dilutive versus legacy original equipment manufacturing contracts, and tariff and geopolitical risk is rising. KUKA and TLSC remain flagged as key audit matters for goodwill impairment, raising questions about robotics profitability. Recent deals — Arbonia, Toshiba Elevator China, Carestream Health international, Teka — test M and A discipline and lifted goodwill materially. Finally, a forty-three renminbi per ten share dividend plus buybacks must be reconciled with capex and deal pipeline.

## 9. NetEase (9999 HK)

**Pitch.** NetEase is China's number-two games publisher after Tencent, anchored by hardcore PC MMO franchises like Fantasy Westward Journey alongside a widening mobile and global portfolio spanning Identity V, Naraka Bladepoint, Marvel Rivals, Once Human, and the breakout Where Winds Meet. The group also operates Youdao education and NetEase Cloud Music, and is the exclusive China partner for Blizzard titles. Founder-led by William Ding, the business combines cash-gushing legacy franchises with an aggressive overseas push. It earns its Asia Top 20 slot as the cleanest, highest-margin pure-play on China interactive entertainment.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 net revenues hit one hundred and twelve point six billion renminbi, up seven percent, with games and related value-added services of ninety-two point one billion, up ten percent. Fourth-quarter games revenue was twenty-two billion renminbi, up three point four percent. Gross margin reached sixty-four point three percent for the year, with the games segment at roughly sixty-nine point three percent in Q3. Non-GAAP net income of thirty-seven point three billion renminbi was up eleven percent. Where Winds Meet surpassed eighty million players globally, and Blizzard titles in China hit record annual revenue.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Year-end net cash stood at one hundred and sixty-three point five billion renminbi, or roughly twenty-three point four billion United States dollars, up from one hundred and thirty-one point five billion a year earlier. Operating cash flow for 2025 was fifty point seven billion renminbi. The board declared a Q4 dividend of twenty-three point two cents per share, or one dollar sixteen per ADS. The buyback program was extended to January 2029 and upsized to five billion United States dollars over thirty-six months; two billion had already been spent on twenty-two point one million ADSs by year-end.

**Portfolio fit.** NetEase is the pure-play China games exposure in Asia Top 20, complementing Tencent's more diversified games, ads, and fintech mix with a tighter, higher-margin games book and a cleaner regulatory surface. The PC legacy of Fantasy Westward Journey still delivers record annual revenues, anchoring cash generation while mobile and global launches drive growth optionality. With Sony also in the book, the three names form a neat triangulation on global interactive entertainment, spanning Western console hardware, Chinese super-app distribution, and China's premier games studio.

**Debate.** Live bear case hinges on game approval pace in China, pipeline risk versus reliance on Fantasy Westward Journey and Blizzard re-runs, and execution on global launches like Sea of Remnants and Diablo 4 China. Management flagged that AI lowers entry barriers but raises the bar for top-tier success, intensifying competition. Operating expenses rose to thirty-six percent of revenue in Q3 on higher marketing. NetEase Cloud Music revenue slipped year over year and innovative businesses shrank double digits, keeping non-games profitability a question. Capital return pace versus the twenty-three billion dollar cash pile remains the key debate.

## 10. H World Group (HTHT US)

**Pitch.** H World, formerly Huazhu, is China's largest hotel operator by room count, running an asset-light manachise and franchise model across economy, midscale, and upper-midscale brands — HanTing, JI Hotel, the newly launched JI Icons, Ni Hao, Crystal Orange, Joya and stablemates — plus the acquired Deutsche Hospitality Steigenberger portfolio serving Europe. Membership sits at about three hundred and one million with direct channels now over seventy percent of room nights. It earns a place in Asia Top 20 as the cleanest listed proxy for China's domestic travel and services consumer recovery, compounding via franchised unit economics.

**Evidence.** At end-2025 the group ran twelve thousand seven hundred and two hotels with around one point two six million rooms, opening over two thousand year to date through Q3 and targeting more than two thousand three hundred full-year openings. Full-year group revenue rose five point nine percent to twenty-five point three billion renminbi; adjusted EBITDA climbed twenty-four percent to eight point five billion at a thirty-three point five percent margin; adjusted net income grew thirty-three percent to four point nine billion. Q3 Legacy-Huazhu average daily rate was three hundred and four renminbi, occupancy eighty-four point one percent, blended RevPAR two hundred and fifty-six. Legacy-DH RevPAR was eighty-seven euros, up six point four percent, at seventy-four point four percent occupancy.

**Balance sheet and cash.** Year-end cash and equivalents stood at fifteen point four billion renminbi with net cash of nine point six billion, operating cash flow of eight point four billion, and an unutilised credit facility of six point three billion. DH-era leverage has unwound as the European arm swung to a record half-billion-renminbi adjusted EBITDA. Capital return is live: 2025 shareholder distributions reached seven hundred and sixty million dollars — six hundred and fifty million in dividends plus one hundred and ten million in buybacks — and more than seventy-five percent of a three-year, two-billion-dollar return plan is complete.

**Portfolio fit.** Inside Asia Top 20, H World is the cyclical, higher-beta China consumer recovery torque — distinct from Midea's durable-goods cycle and more direct than Tencent or NetEase's digital rails. It plays domestic business and leisure demand head-on, with scale-led unit-economic compounding: manachise and franchise now contribute about sixty-nine percent of group profit and seventy percent of gross operating profit, at sixty-eight percent gross margin. Network additions in lower-tier cities and the upper-midscale push, with over one thousand six hundred hotels in operation and pipeline, up twenty-five percent, extend runway without absorbing balance-sheet capital.

**Debate.** Bears point to RevPAR guided only flat to slightly up for 2026 against tough comps, with business demand still soft and leisure carrying the load. Group revenue guidance of just two to six percent growth, five to nine excluding DH, underwhelms versus twelve percent network growth, implying RevPAR drag. DH profitability is at record levels and may be hard to lap, while aggressive openings of two thousand two hundred to two thousand three hundred gross with six hundred to seven hundred closures test absorption in lower-tier cities. Macro sensitivity to China's consumer, oversupply of low-quality product, and a CFO transition in March add execution overhang.

## 11. OCBC (OCBC SP)

**Pitch.** OCBC is Singapore's second-largest bank and the Republic's oldest local lender, running a universal franchise across ASEAN and Greater China with wholesale, consumer and private banking anchored in Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and Hong Kong. What sets it apart from DBS and UOB is an approximately eighty-eight percent stake in Great Eastern Holdings, the dominant Singapore and Malaysia life insurer, which plugs a differentiated non-bank earnings stream into the group. The thesis for Asia Top 20: a Singapore-dollar hard-currency compounder bundling deposit-funded lending, a top-three regional wealth platform and a captive life insurer, at a through-cycle fortress capital ratio.

**Evidence.** Full-year 2025 pre-tax profit hit a record nine point one two billion Singapore dollars, up two percent, while group net profit came in at SGD seven point four two billion. Net interest income fell six percent to SGD nine point one five billion as the full-year margin settled at one point nine one percent and the December exit margin at one point eight four percent. Non-interest income jumped sixteen percent to SGD five point four six billion, with fees up twenty-two percent and insurance income up seventeen percent. Wealth management income rose fourteen percent to SGD five point six billion, or thirty-eight percent of group income, on banking AUM of SGD three hundred and forty-three billion.
