# GEM Top 20 Briefing Notes

These are internal-style speaking notes designed to help explain why each name can belong in a fundamentally oriented GEM portfolio, using the current business mix, financial profile, and valuation backdrop in the saved finance dataset.

## 1. TSMC

TSMC is the cleanest way to own global compute intensity because the business is centered on wafer fabrication for high performance computing, smartphones, IoT, automotive, and other semiconductor end markets, with high performance computing already 58% of revenue and smartphones 29%.

The core ownership case is that scale and process leadership are still translating into exceptional economics, with revenue up 31.6% year over year, operating margin at 50.8%, and net margin at 45% on the latest annual numbers.

This is not just a growth story but a balance-sheet-backed compounding story, with TWD 2.8 trillion of cash and TWD 2.3 trillion of operating cash flow, which supports continued heavy reinvestment even after TWD 1.3 trillion of capex.

In a portfolio context, the attraction is that TSMC gives exposure to AI, advanced packaging, and semiconductor content growth without forcing a single-product bet on any one fabless customer or end-device cycle.

The risk to watch is always whether the stock is already discounting too much, because it trades on 35.5 times earnings and the average analyst target in the dataset is 392 versus a 370.6 share price, so the debate is less about quality and more about how much upside remains after a very strong run.

## 2. SK Hynix

SK hynix is a direct way to own the memory content cycle because the company is fundamentally a DRAM and NAND producer serving server, networking, mobile, PC, consumer, and automotive markets.

The reason to own it now is operating leverage, with latest revenue of KRW 97.1 trillion growing 46.8% year over year and net income reaching KRW 42.9 trillion, which shows how violently memory earnings inflect when pricing and mix improve.

Cash generation is also supporting the story, with KRW 53.4 trillion of operating cash flow against KRW 27.5 trillion of capex, meaning the company is funding the cycle and still strengthening its position rather than simply chasing growth with weak returns.

For a GEM portfolio, this name works as a higher-beta semiconductor complement to TSMC because it gives more direct exposure to memory scarcity, pricing normalization, and the performance demand coming out of data center and AI workloads.

The key question to monitor is how much of today's profitability is cyclical rather than structural, because this is still a memory business and the stock has already rerated sharply toward its 1,099,000 year high from a 171,800 year low.

## 3. Tencent

Tencent is attractive because it combines multiple profit pools inside one platform, spanning value-added services, online advertising, and fintech and business services, rather than depending on any single game or ad product.

The quality of the franchise shows up in the numbers, with revenue of CNY 732.4 billion, operating profit of CNY 238.4 billion, net income of CNY 219 billion, and operating cash flow of CNY 303.1 billion.

That combination matters because it means Tencent is still compounding from a very large base, with 10.9% revenue growth coming on top of already dominant scale and a 17.6 times P/E that is not demanding for this level of cash generation.

From a portfolio construction standpoint, Tencent offers a broad China internet exposure with better diversification than a single-subsector name, since gaming, advertising, and fintech can offset one another through the cycle.

The main thing to watch is whether regulation, competition, or capital allocation starts to dilute returns, especially because gross debt of CNY 406.6 billion is meaningful even if the cash flow engine remains very strong.

## 4. Prosus

Prosus fits as a diversified internet and e-commerce platform exposure, with activities across classifieds, payments and fintech, food delivery, travel, education technology, health, ventures, and other internet categories.

The investment case is that the market is still paying a modest multiple for a broad portfolio of digital assets, with the stock on 8.4 times earnings despite EUR 12.4 billion of net income and EUR 1.9 billion of operating cash flow in the latest annual data.

This can make sense in GEM as a lower-multiple way to own global consumer internet optionality, because you are not paying a pure-play premium for any one vertical such as food delivery or payments.

The balance sheet is not pristine, with EUR 16.4 billion of debt against EUR 7.1 billion of cash, so the thesis relies on disciplined portfolio management and on the underlying internet assets continuing to mature into cash generators.

In conversation, the simplest framing is that Prosus offers a diversified basket of internet assets at a valuation that already embeds skepticism, which creates room for value realization if execution continues improving.

## 5. Samsung Electronics

Samsung works as a broad hardware and semiconductor compounder because it spans consumer electronics, mobile and IT, and device solutions, giving the portfolio exposure to both chips and end devices.

The latest annual figures show that the earnings base has re-expanded, with revenue of KRW 333.6 trillion, operating profit of KRW 43.6 trillion, net income of KRW 44.3 trillion, and operating cash flow of KRW 85.3 trillion.

The reason to own it is that you are getting cyclical recovery and franchise quality together, because 10.9% revenue growth is being generated with a very strong balance sheet that still holds KRW 57.9 trillion of cash against KRW 25.2 trillion of debt.

Relative to narrower semiconductor names, Samsung gives optionality across memory, handsets, and consumer electronics, which can make it a steadier way to express a constructive view on global technology demand.

The debate is whether returns can stay elevated once the cycle normalizes, but at 21.1 times earnings the valuation still looks reasonable for a company of this scale, diversification, and cash generation profile.

## 6. MediaTek

MediaTek is a fabless semiconductor way to own device connectivity and compute content, with products spanning mobile communication, tablets, automotive chipsets, Bluetooth, GPS, Wi-Fi, broadband, and multimedia ICs.

The attraction is a mix of growth and profitability, with revenue of TWD 596 billion growing 12.3% year over year and net income of TWD 105.3 billion on operating profit of TWD 103.5 billion.

This is also a financially resilient business, carrying TWD 235.3 billion of cash, just TWD 21.9 billion of debt, and TWD 158.3 billion of operating cash flow, which gives it room to invest through product cycles.

In the portfolio, MediaTek can be pitched as a differentiated semiconductor exposure because it monetizes rising connectivity and silicon content without owning manufacturing risk directly.

The key watchpoint is whether mix remains favorable enough to justify a 24.6 times P/E, but if new product ramps hold, the combination of scale, net cash, and healthy cash conversion is compelling.

## 7. Credicorp

Credicorp is a high-quality Andean financial holding company with exposure across universal banking, insurance, and health services, which gives more breadth than a single-line lender.

The ownership case is that the franchise is already producing material earnings, with PEN 7.1 billion of net income, a 33.8% net margin, and PEN 8.6 billion of operating cash flow in the latest annual data.

This matters because you are buying profitability and balance-sheet liquidity rather than a speculative turnaround, with PEN 49 billion of cash and only a 14 times P/E multiple.

For GEM, the appeal is exposure to financial deepening in Peru through a diversified local champion that can capture loan growth, fee income, and insurance penetration together.

The point to watch is that the sell-side setup is not euphoric, with only a buy consensus and 50% bullish ratings in the dataset, so the stock likely needs steady execution rather than multiple expansion alone to work.

## 8. MercadoLibre

MercadoLibre is one of the clearest platform compounding stories in emerging markets because it operates both the commerce marketplace and the Mercado Pago fintech ecosystem across Latin America.

The operating evidence is strong, with revenue of USD 28.9 billion growing 39.1% year over year, operating profit of USD 3.2 billion, net income of USD 2 billion, and operating cash flow of USD 12.1 billion.

The dual-engine thesis is visible in the KPI mix, with 121 million unique active buyers, 78 million fintech monthly active users, USD 16.3 billion of commerce revenue, USD 12.6 billion of fintech revenue, and USD 277.8 billion of payment volume.

This is why the stock can justify a premium multiple, because analysts in the dataset still model revenue rising to USD 38.5 billion and net income to USD 2.6 billion next year while maintaining a strong buy consensus and a USD 2,531 average target.

The investment conversation should focus on sustained share gains and ecosystem monetization, while acknowledging that at 45 times earnings the market already expects continued execution and cannot tolerate a sharp slowdown.

## 9. TOTVS

TOTVS is a practical way to own enterprise digitization in Brazil because it sells business management software, productivity and collaboration platforms, and related implementation and consulting services.

The case rests on steady, recurring, locally embedded software economics, with revenue of BRL 5.8 billion growing 10.5% year over year, operating profit of BRL 1.1 billion, and net income of BRL 891.7 million.

Cash generation is solid, with BRL 1.2 billion of operating cash flow and a manageable balance sheet of BRL 1.8 billion cash against BRL 2.1 billion debt, which supports the idea of a durable compounding software asset rather than a cash-burning tech story.

In portfolio terms, TOTVS adds a different kind of growth exposure because it is tied to SME and enterprise software adoption rather than consumer internet or semiconductors.

The stock is not obviously cheap at 23.5 times earnings, so the ongoing question is whether the platform can keep deepening wallet share and lifting margins enough to sustain that quality premium.

## 10. Nu Holdings

Nu is a digital financial services platform built across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia, offering cards, accounts, payments, personal loans, investments, insurance, and business banking products.

The central ownership case is that scale is now turning into real earnings, with USD 2.9 billion of net income, a 24.8% net margin, and USD 3.5 billion of operating cash flow in the latest annual numbers.

The best proof of franchise strength is customer engagement, because the company already serves 131 million customers and an 83.4% activity rate, which suggests the base is both large and monetizable.

This can be framed as financial inclusion meeting operating leverage, where the digital model keeps unit costs low while product breadth allows revenue per customer to expand over time.

The pushback is valuation discipline, since the shares trade on 25.8 times earnings, but the dataset still shows a strong buy consensus and an 18.52 average target versus a 14.96 share price, implying the market still sees runway.

## 11. PDD

PDD is a multinational commerce platform built around value-oriented online retail, with exposure to broad merchandise categories and a meaningful transaction-services profit stream.

The key attraction is that the company is still growing while already throwing off huge profits, with revenue of CNY 431.8 billion, revenue growth of 9.7%, operating margin of 21.9%, net margin of 23%, and net income of CNY 99.4 billion.

Cash generation reinforces the case, with CNY 106.9 billion of operating cash flow and CNY 108.9 billion of cash, which gives the company flexibility to invest in users, merchants, and logistics without balance-sheet stress.

The reason this can still work is valuation, because a 10.2 times P/E is not demanding relative to consensus estimates for CNY 495.2 billion of forward revenue and CNY 112.9 billion of forward net income.

The thing to watch is whether slower top-line growth means the market starts treating PDD as mature rather than disruptive, but if monetization and efficiency hold, the risk-reward still looks favorable.

## 12. Regional

Regional is a Mexican banking group serving entrepreneurs and small businesses through loans, payments, insurance, and other financial products, so it is exposed to productive domestic credit growth rather than only consumer lending.

The numbers show a business that is scaling quickly, with MXN 34.3 billion of revenue, 141.2% reported revenue growth, MXN 9 billion of operating profit, and MXN 6.6 billion of net income.

Valuation is an important part of the thesis because the stock trades on just 7.5 times earnings, which suggests the market is still discounting either macro or asset-quality risk despite the level of profitability.

For a GEM portfolio, Regional offers exposure to Mexican financial deepening in a way that is more leveraged to SME activity and formalization than a pure consumer bank would be.

The main issue to monitor is quality of growth, because operating cash flow was negative MXN 16 billion in the latest year, so the balance between expansion and funding intensity needs close attention.

## 13. Trip.com

Trip.com is a scaled travel platform spanning accommodation reservations, transportation ticketing, packaged tours, in-destination services, and corporate travel management in China and internationally.

The ownership case is that travel recovery has turned into real profitability, with revenue of CNY 62.4 billion growing 17.1%, operating margin of 25.3%, net margin of 52.9%, and net income of CNY 33 billion.

Segment data supports the breadth of the platform, with CNY 26.1 billion from accommodation reservations and CNY 22.5 billion from transportation ticketing, which shows the earnings base is not dependent on a narrow niche.

The stock also looks inexpensive relative to its quality and cash generation, trading on 7.4 times earnings with CNY 46.5 billion of cash and consensus estimates pointing to CNY 70.9 billion of forward revenue.

The conversation point is that this is a high-return travel franchise with a net-cash balance sheet and a strong buy consensus, but the risk is always that outbound and domestic travel normalize slower than the market hopes.

## 14. ICICI Lombard

ICICI Lombard is an Indian general insurer with exposure across motor, health, travel, home, crop, fire, marine, and commercial insurance lines, making it a broad play on insurance penetration rather than a narrow mono-line insurer.

The core reason to hold it is that the business is already scaling profitably, with INR 249 billion of revenue, 14.4% revenue growth, operating profit of INR 33.2 billion, and net income of INR 25.1 billion.

This is a quality compounding setup rather than a deep-value setup, because consensus in the dataset expects revenue to rise to INR 257.2 billion and net income to INR 28.8 billion while the stock trades on 32.6 times earnings.

In a GEM portfolio, the appeal is access to Indian under-penetrated insurance demand through a scaled, branded platform that can benefit from both premium growth and underwriting discipline.

The key watchpoint is whether growth remains profitable enough to defend the multiple, because the market is already paying up for resilience and does not need much disappointment to de-rate the shares.

## 15. Sea

Sea is attractive because it combines three major digital businesses in one company: Shopee in e-commerce, Monee in digital financial services, and Garena in digital entertainment.

The financial picture has improved sharply, with revenue of USD 22.9 billion growing 36.4%, operating margin of 8.7%, net margin of 7%, net income of USD 1.6 billion, and operating cash flow of USD 5 billion.

The platform depth matters, because Shopee GMV reached USD 127.4 billion with a 13% take rate, while Shopee revenue was USD 16.6 billion, Monee services revenue was USD 3.8 billion, and Garena revenue was USD 2.4 billion.

The reason to own it is that Sea has moved beyond a pure growth-at-all-costs story and now has evidence that its ecosystem can grow and make money at the same time, with consensus still modeling USD 29.1 billion of forward revenue.

The debate is valuation versus durability, because a 34 times P/E assumes continued execution, but the strong buy consensus and 169 average target suggest the market still sees substantial upside if profitability keeps improving.

## 16. ANTA

ANTA is a scaled Chinese sportswear platform that designs, develops, manufactures, and markets sporting footwear, apparel, and accessories, giving exposure to branded domestic consumption rather than generic retail.

The ownership case is grounded in a healthy earnings base, with revenue of CNY 78 billion, revenue growth of 10.2%, operating profit of CNY 15.7 billion, and net income of CNY 13.2 billion.

Cash generation is also meaningful, with CNY 20.4 billion of operating cash flow, which supports the idea that this is a real consumer franchise and not just a revenue-growth story.

In portfolio terms, ANTA adds a China domestic demand angle with brand equity and category leadership rather than another internet or hardware exposure.

The balance-sheet point to monitor is leverage, with CNY 33.4 billion of debt versus CNY 12.8 billion of cash, but at 15.3 times earnings the valuation still leaves room if brand momentum and operating discipline continue.

## 17. CAMS

CAMS is effectively financial infrastructure for Indian savings, acting as a mutual fund transfer agency and also serving private equity funds, banks, and non-bank financial institutions.

The reason to own it is the attractive combination of scale and profitability, with revenue of INR 14.2 billion growing 25.2%, operating profit of INR 9.3 billion, and net income of INR 4.7 billion.

It also converts earnings well, producing INR 4.8 billion of operating cash flow with low debt of INR 885 million, which makes the franchise look more like a toll-road asset on the growth of Indian financial assets.

The investment pitch is that as mutual fund penetration, SIP flows, and outsourced financial administration grow in India, CAMS can capture volume growth without taking direct credit or underwriting risk.

The pushback is valuation, because 37.7 times earnings is full, so the stock likely only works if investors stay convinced that the market structure and operating leverage are durable enough to support a premium multiple.

## 18. Full Truck Alliance

Full Truck Alliance is a digital freight platform connecting shippers with truckers across multiple distance ranges, cargo weights, and shipment types in China, so it is essentially a logistics marketplace and network business.

The financial quality is better than the market seems to credit, with revenue of CNY 12.5 billion growing 11.1%, operating margin of 33.2%, net margin of 35.7%, and net income of CNY 4.5 billion.

The balance sheet also helps the case, with CNY 6.1 billion of cash and CNY 3 billion of operating cash flow, which gives the company flexibility to deepen network density and service quality.

The interesting angle is that the stock trades on just 13.6 times earnings even though the business looks asset-light and profitable, which suggests skepticism is already reflected in the price.

In discussion, it is worth noting that consensus in the dataset is outright negative, with a sell rating and zero percent bullish, so this is the kind of position that only belongs in the portfolio if the team believes the market is missing the durability of the freight platform model.

## 19. H World

H World is a large hotel operator in China built across leased and owned and franchised formats, with brands ranging from economy to upscale lodging.

The appeal is that the travel recovery and brand system are translating into solid numbers, with revenue of CNY 25.3 billion, 5.9% revenue growth, operating margin of 26.9%, and net income of CNY 5.1 billion.

Cash generation is strong, with CNY 8.4 billion of operating cash flow and CNY 10.5 billion of cash, which supports expansion and brand investment without balance-sheet strain.

The stock can be framed as a domestic China services recovery play with a proven operating model, and consensus still projects CNY 26.7 billion of forward revenue and CNY 5.4 billion of forward net income.

At 22.5 times earnings this is not a bargain, but the strong buy consensus and 61 average target imply the market still believes branded lodging consolidation and asset-light expansion have room to run.

## 20. Capitec

Capitec is a South African financial group spanning retail banking, business banking, and insurance, so the franchise is broader than a single-product lender.

The main reason to own it is the operating performance, with revenue of ZAR 44.1 billion growing 61.4%, operating profit of ZAR 26 billion, net income of ZAR 13.7 billion, and operating cash flow of ZAR 24.9 billion.

Balance-sheet strength is part of the case too, with ZAR 29.3 billion of cash and zero debt in the latest annual dataset, which gives the business room to keep expanding products and customers from a position of strength.

In a GEM portfolio, Capitec offers exposure to financial inclusion and retail banking modernization in South Africa through a profitable incumbent challenger rather than a speculative fintech.

The stock is clearly a quality name and not a value name, trading on 32.7 times earnings, so the ongoing question is whether the franchise can compound fast enough to keep justifying that premium valuation.

## Fast Recall

The semiconductor core is TSMC, SK hynix, Samsung, and MediaTek, with TSMC as quality and scale, SK hynix as memory torque, Samsung as diversified hardware and memory, and MediaTek as fabless connectivity and compute.

The internet and platform compounders are Tencent, Prosus, MercadoLibre, Nu, PDD, Trip.com, Sea, and Full Truck Alliance, with the main distinction being whether you are underwriting monetization scale, ecosystem breadth, or a valuation disconnect.

The financials are Credicorp, Regional, ICICI Lombard, CAMS, and Capitec, where the common thread is formalization and financial deepening but each name expresses it through a different product architecture and country backdrop.

The consumer and services domestics are TOTVS, ANTA, and H World, where the case is less about macro beta and more about owning local champions with repeat usage, pricing power, and scalable operating models.
