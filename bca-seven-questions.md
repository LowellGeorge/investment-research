# Bank Central Asia — The Seven Questions

Bank Central Asia, known as BCA, is the thirty-second-largest position in the GEM portfolio at zero-point-eight percent, with a market cap of around fifty billion US dollars. Here is BCA in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

BCA just printed its first-quarter 2026 results. Net profit grew nearly four percent year-on-year to fourteen-point-seven trillion rupiah. Total assets reached one thousand six hundred and forty-one trillion rupiah. The current-and-savings-account ratio reached eighty-five percent — among the highest of any major bank globally. Cost-to-income ratio fell to twenty-seven-point-three percent — best-in-class. Capital adequacy stood at twenty-seven percent of risk-weighted assets. Digital transactions accounted for ninety-nine-point-eight percent of total transaction volume. The company's market capitalisation reached seven hundred and ninety-eight trillion rupiah — the largest of any Indonesian bank. And artificial-intelligence-driven operational improvements led to a forty-percent reduction in ATM downtime and a fifty-six-percent drop in fraud complaints year-on-year.

This is the highest-quality bank franchise in Indonesia, controlled by the Hartono family, running at world-class deposit-funding economics.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

BCA is Indonesia's largest private bank by market capitalisation, deposits, and profitability — and one of the highest-quality bank franchises globally by structural economics. The bank operates over one thousand two hundred and seventy domestic branches and one overseas office, with deep institutional, corporate, retail, and small-and-medium enterprise relationships built over six decades.

The structural advantage is the deposit franchise. The current-and-savings-account ratio sits at eighty-five percent — meaning eighty-five percent of all deposits are in low-cost or no-cost transaction accounts. Most global banks operate with current-and-savings-account ratios of thirty to fifty percent. This is the foundational economic moat. Total deposits support a loan-to-deposit ratio of just seventy-four percent — meaning BCA does not need to grow loans aggressively to deploy its funding.

The loan portfolio is diversified. Corporate loans grew nine percent year-on-year. Small-and-medium-enterprise loans grew five percent. Mortgage new bookings grew ten percent. Total loans receivable reached nine hundred and forty trillion rupiah. Asset quality is strong — gross non-performing-loan ratio of one-point-eight-five percent with non-performing-loan coverage of one hundred and seventy-five percent.

The thing to internalise: BCA is not a typical Indonesian bank. It is a deposit-franchise compounder where the cost-of-funds advantage compounds every year, supported by the broadest digital transaction infrastructure in Indonesia and disciplined Hartono-family governance.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and forty years of consistent execution under Hartono family stewardship is the evidence.

BCA was acquired by the Djarum-controlled Hartono family — Robert and Michael Hartono — through Farallon Capital in 2002 following the Asian financial crisis. The family has held the controlling stake for over two decades, providing strategic continuity and capital-allocation discipline that few Indonesian banking peers can match.

The capital discipline signals are persistent. Capital adequacy at twenty-seven percent — well above regulatory minimums. A final dividend of thirty-four-and-a-half trillion rupiah is being paid in April 2026, on top of an interim dividend of nearly seven trillion in December 2025. Share buybacks were conducted in 2025 and early 2026, with treasury shares totalling nearly four hundred million. Sustainable financing portfolio grew ten percent year-on-year. Multiple ESG and governance awards including top rankings in ASEAN and global indices.

Operational discipline is equally striking. AI-driven operational improvements led to a forty-percent reduction in ATM downtime and a fifty-six-percent drop in fraud complaints year-on-year. Mobile banking usage and transaction value rose sharply. The cost-to-income ratio of twenty-seven percent is among the lowest of any major bank globally.

The watch item is asset-quality at the margin. The non-performing-loan ratio rose slightly in the quarter; the loans-at-risk ratio sits at five-point-one percent. Indonesian rupiah weakness and declining FX reserves create macroeconomic risk that the bank monitors carefully through credit-risk and stress-testing systems.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a kicker.

The first engine is the deposit-franchise compounder. Eighty-five percent current-and-savings-account ratio means cost-of-funds advantage compounds every year. As Indonesian financialisation continues and BCA's deposit market share grows or holds, the spread engine on the loan book grows in absolute terms while peer banks pay higher costs of funding.

The second engine is Indonesian credit penetration. Indonesia is significantly under-penetrated in mortgage lending, retail unsecured credit, and SME financing relative to other Southeast Asian peers. BCA, with the broadest distribution network and the strongest brand among private Indonesian banks, captures disproportionate share as penetration deepens. Mortgage new bookings up ten percent and corporate loans up nine percent are the early evidence.

The third engine is digital and AI-driven operational efficiency. Cost-to-income at twenty-seven percent already among the global best, driven by ninety-nine-point-eight percent digital transaction penetration plus AI-driven productivity in operations and fraud management. Continued progress here adds basis points to the structural return on equity.

The kicker is the sustainable finance portfolio growing ten percent year-on-year. As ESG-aligned capital flows into Indonesia and global sustainable-finance frameworks expand, BCA captures the early-mover advantage on green and MSME-aligned lending, with multiple international ESG awards confirming the positioning.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six metrics. Current-and-savings-account ratio — eighty-five percent today, maintenance above eighty confirms the funding moat. Cost-to-income ratio — twenty-seven percent today, sustained below thirty confirms operational discipline. Risk-adjusted net interest margin — four-point-eight percent today, watch for stability. Gross non-performing-loan ratio — one-point-eight-five percent, maintenance below two-and-a-half confirms asset quality. Loan growth — five-point-six percent overall today, watch for retail and SME acceleration. Capital adequacy — twenty-seven percent, deeply over-capitalised.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

First-quarter 2026 net profit was fourteen-point-seven trillion rupiah on a quarterly basis. Annualised run-rate suggests full-year net profit of around sixty trillion rupiah, roughly three-and-a-half billion US dollars. Market cap is around fifty billion US dollars, implying a trailing price-to-earnings of fourteen to sixteen times. For a bank with eighty-five percent current-and-savings-account ratio, twenty-seven-percent cost-to-income, and structurally under-penetrated Indonesian credit market, that is a quality multiple but not extreme.

The simple framing: best-in-class Asian bank franchise at premium-but-not-extreme multiples. Comparable set: Singapore banks DBS, OCBC, UOB at lower multiples for lower returns; Indian private banks like HDFC and ICICI at higher multiples for similar returns; Indonesian peers Bank Mandiri and Bank Rakyat Indonesia at lower multiples for weaker franchise economics.

The key sensitivity is Indonesian rupiah and macroeconomic stability. Rupiah depreciation directly compresses US-dollar reported earnings. Indonesian rate cycle compresses or expands net interest margin.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

First variant: consensus prices BCA as a generic Indonesian bank with country macro risk dominating. The eighty-five-percent current-and-savings-account ratio plus twenty-seven percent cost-to-income are world-class metrics that distinguish BCA from any Indonesian peer and put it in the same conversation as the best global private banks. The right comp set is global premium banks rather than Indonesian peers. Second: AI-driven operational efficiency — ATM downtime down forty percent, fraud complaints down fifty-six percent — is a structural multi-year compounding lever that consensus models do not yet capture.

The honest counter: Indonesian rupiah and political risk are persistent and structural. The Hartono family ownership creates excellent strategic continuity but also concentration risk. Position sizing should reflect that even a world-class franchise in Indonesia carries country discounts that may not close.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

Risks: Indonesian rupiah depreciation; loan growth deceleration with rising non-performing loans; political and policy instability; competitive intrusion from international or fintech players; cyber and fraud risk; succession beyond the current Hartono leadership cycle.

Sell triggers: current-and-savings-account ratio falling below seventy-five percent for two consecutive quarters; cost-to-income ratio rising above thirty-three percent sustainably; non-performing-loan ratio above three percent for two consecutive quarters; capital adequacy falling below twenty percent without explicit redeployment plan; net interest margin breaking below four percent; major Hartono family ownership change.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions. BCA is the world-class Indonesian deposit-franchise compounder running at eighty-five-percent current-and-savings-account ratio, twenty-seven-percent cost-to-income, and under-penetrated Indonesian credit market still ahead. The next print is the second-quarter 2026 result on the twenty-eighth of July. Watch deposit growth, loan growth in retail and SME, and asset quality.
