# Walmex — The Seven Questions

Walmart de Mexico, known as Walmex, is the thirty-fourth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at zero-point-nine percent, with a market cap of around fifty billion US dollars. Here is Walmex in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Walmex just closed 2025 with consolidated revenue of one trillion mexican pesos — up five-and-a-half percent for the year and three percent in the fourth quarter. Mexico same-store sales grew three-point-three percent, with ticket size up nearly four percent. E-commerce gross merchandise value in Mexico grew thirteen percent in the fourth quarter and seventeen percent for the year, with penetration reaching nine-point-one percent of total. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation expanded fifty basis points to ten-and-a-half percent margin in the fourth quarter. The company opened one hundred and fifteen new stores in the fourth quarter alone. Beneficios — the loyalty programme — reached nearly fifty million active users, identifying over seventy percent of omnichannel sales. And the company returned nearly thirty-eight billion pesos to shareholders during the year through dividends and buybacks.

This is the dominant Mexican retailer compounding through a fragmenting Mexican consumer environment.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Walmex is the largest retail company in Mexico by revenue, store count, and market position. Walmex operates through four primary banners. Bodega Aurrerá is the discount banner serving lower-income households — the largest format by store count and the fastest-growing in 2025. Walmart Supercenter is the full-format hypermarket. Sam's Club is the membership-warehouse format. The smaller-format Walmart Express targets dense urban catchments.

The geographic footprint includes Mexico — the dominant business — and Central America, with operations in Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, and Nicaragua. The Mexico business contributed disproportionately to growth, with fourth-quarter revenue up nearly five percent and earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation up ten percent. Central America grew at lower rates due to deflation, weaker household consumption, and competitive intensity in Costa Rica.

Total store count reached four thousand two hundred and sixty-five units at year-end 2025, with one hundred and fifteen new stores opened in the fourth quarter alone — one hundred and two in Mexico and thirteen in Central America. Walmex also operates one of the largest e-commerce and omnichannel platforms in Mexico, with e-commerce gross merchandise value penetration at nine-point-one percent of total and growing rapidly.

The thing to internalise: Walmex is not a typical retailer. It is the dominant multi-format Mexican retail platform with structural pricing power, an omnichannel and loyalty flywheel, and disciplined capital allocation supported by Walmart Inc.'s global parent.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the disciplined capital return through fiscal 2025 is the evidence.

The 2025 print is consistent with multi-decade Walmex operating discipline. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation margin expanded fifty basis points to ten-and-a-half percent in the fourth quarter, despite competitive intensity. Gross margin expanded ten basis points for the year. Price perception improved by two hundred and sixty basis points year-on-year — a deliberate strategic investment that pays back through customer retention. Days on hand improved by nearly two days versus the previous year. Product availability improved by one hundred and thirty basis points sequentially.

Capital return is genuine. Full-year 2025 dividend payments and share repurchases totalled nearly thirty-eight billion pesos. Net cash from operating activities was eighty-two billion pesos. Capital expenditure was thirty-six billion. Cash at year-end was nearly twenty-nine billion. The board is disciplined in returning capital while still investing for growth.

The watch item is the announced leadership transition at regional and international levels — new CEO appointments and retirements were communicated through 2025 and into early 2026. Continuity in execution is the test.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a kicker. First: Mexican consumer growth, with household formation and disposable income trends supporting multi-decade retail demand. Second: Bodega Aurrerá leadership in the lower-income segment, where competitive intensity is genuine but Walmex's scale and price discipline maintain advantage. Third: omnichannel and e-commerce flywheel — e-commerce growth at thirteen percent quarter-on-quarter, store-based fulfillment expanding, and the Beneficios loyalty programme identifying over seventy percent of omnichannel sales. The kicker is retail media and advertising — supplier advertising budgets are pressured short-term but retail media outpaced market growth and is positioned for recovery as macro conditions improve.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six metrics. Same-store sales growth — three-point-three percent in Mexico, sustained three-plus confirms the consumer franchise. E-commerce gross merchandise value growth — thirteen percent quarter-on-quarter; sustained double-digit confirms the omnichannel flywheel. Earnings margin — ten-and-a-half percent in the fourth quarter; sustained expansion confirms operating leverage. Beneficios active users — nearly fifty million; continued growth confirms the loyalty engine. Store openings — one hundred and fifteen in the fourth quarter; continued rollout confirms physical expansion. Capital return — thirty-eight billion pesos in 2025; continuation confirms shareholder commitment.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

Full-year 2025 net income was nearly fifty billion pesos. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation was one hundred and three billion. Cash from operations was eighty-two billion. Market cap is around fifty billion US dollars. On trading multiples, Walmex trades at roughly twenty-three to twenty-five times trailing earnings — premium for Mexican retail but consistent with its franchise quality.

The simple framing: dominant Mexican retailer at premium multiples, with omnichannel and loyalty levers underpriced. Comparable set: Walmart Inc. at similar-to-higher multiples; international retailers like Costco and Target at variable multiples. Mexican peers like Soriana and Chedraui trade at lower multiples for weaker franchise economics.

The key sensitivity is Mexican consumer health and peso volatility. A sharper Mexican slowdown directly hits same-store sales. Peso weakness compresses US-dollar-reported returns.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

First variant: e-commerce and Beneficios loyalty are still priced as marginal contributions; e-commerce growth at thirteen percent quarter-on-quarter and Beneficios identifying seventy percent of omnichannel sales are structural compounders that consensus models do not yet fully capture. Second: retail media and supplier advertising recovery is a near-term lever as Mexican macro conditions normalise.

The honest counter: Mexican retail is intensely competitive. Bodega Aurrerá is dominant but Soriana, Chedraui, and OXXO are real competitors. The recent net income decline of nearly seven percent for the full year reflects higher tax rates that may persist.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

Risks: Mexican consumer slowdown; competitive escalation from Soriana, Chedraui, and OXXO; e-commerce execution in the platform transition; Central America macro weakness; peso volatility; tax rate changes.

Sell triggers: same-store sales growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters; e-commerce growth decelerating below ten percent; earnings margin compressing more than fifty basis points for two consecutive quarters; Beneficios loyalty active user growth stalling; major management transitions disrupting execution.

## Sign-off

One company, seven questions. Walmex is the dominant Mexican retailer compounding through omnichannel, loyalty, and store rollout, returning capital while still investing. The next print is the first-quarter 2026 result on the twenty-ninth of April. Watch same-store sales, e-commerce, and earnings margin trajectory.
