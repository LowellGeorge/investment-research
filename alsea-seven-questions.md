# Alsea — The Seven Questions

Alsea is the thirty-fifth-largest position in the GEM portfolio at zero-point-six percent, with a market cap of around two-and-a-half billion US dollars. Here is Alsea in seven questions.

---

## Cold open

Alsea just closed 2025 with full-year revenue of around eighty-three billion pesos and full-year net income up nearly eighty-nine percent to two-and-a-half billion pesos. Same-store sales grew four-point-three percent for the year and three-point-three percent in the fourth quarter. Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation grew nearly three percent in the fourth quarter, with margin expanding forty basis points to seventeen percent. Mexico revenue grew almost eight percent in the fourth quarter and full year. Europe revenue grew fourteen percent for the year. The company opened one hundred and sixty-nine new stores in 2025, divested non-core operations in Chile, Spain, and parts of South America, and added Chipotle and Raising Cane's to the brand portfolio. Earnings per share rose nearly two hundred percent. And the recently completed debt refinancing is expected to save twenty-five million dollars annually.

This is a deliberately reshaped Mexican-anchored multi-brand restaurant operator simplifying the portfolio and unlocking margin.

Seven questions.

---

## Question One: What is it?

Alsea is the largest publicly-traded multi-brand restaurant operator in Latin America and Iberia, with a portfolio spanning quick-service, casual dining, and coffee. The brand stable includes Starbucks Mexico and franchised territories in Europe and South America, Domino's Pizza, Burger King in select markets, Chili's, P.F. Chang's, and recently added Chipotle and Raising Cane's. Total store count reached four thousand eight hundred and twenty units globally, with seventy-seven percent corporately operated and twenty-three percent franchised.

The geographic mix is Mexico-anchored. Mexico revenue grew seven-and-a-half percent for the year and eight percent in the fourth quarter — the highest-margin and most consistent contributor. Europe — primarily Starbucks and Domino's franchise operations in Spain, France, and select markets — grew fourteen percent for the year, though France remains below pre-boycott sales levels. South America operations were rationalised through 2025 as the company exited Chile and certain other markets to focus on quality returns.

Same-store sales by segment: Starbucks up two-point-nine percent overall in the fourth quarter, with Mexico up two-point-six percent and South America up nearly nine percent. Domino's Pizza up five-point-two percent in the fourth quarter, with strong performance in Mexico, Spain, and Colombia. Burger King down nearly four percent excluding Argentina. Full-service restaurants up three percent overall.

The thing to internalise: Alsea is not just a Latin American Starbucks operator. It is a deliberately-curated multi-brand restaurant platform with disciplined portfolio management, refinancing efficiency, and brand-mix optimisation now coming through.

## Question Two: Can we trust them?

Yes — and the 2024-to-2025 portfolio rationalisation is the evidence.

Alsea executed a clinical portfolio reshape during 2024 and 2025. The company divested TGI Fridays, Chili's, and P.F. Chang's operations in Chile and Spain — markets where unit economics did not justify continued investment. The company exited certain South American operations. At the same time, Alsea added Chipotle and Raising Cane's — premium brands with proven United States-market unit economics that the company can scale into Mexican and Latin American markets where similar concepts have not yet penetrated. This is the active capital-allocation discipline that transforms multi-brand operators over time.

The capital discipline signals from the 2025 print are clear. Net income up eighty-nine percent for the year. Earnings per share up nearly two hundred percent. Same-store sales of four-point-three percent ahead of inflation in most markets. Capital expenditure of five-point-one billion pesos with seventy-five percent for store development and twenty-five percent for strategic projects. Allocation: sixty-three percent to Europe, twenty-seven percent to South America, ten percent to Mexico — a deliberate reinvestment in the European business where margin expansion potential is highest. Recently completed debt refinancing improved cost and maturity profile and is expected to save twenty-five million dollars annually. Sustainable financing rounds in Europe and Mexico tied to ESG KPIs.

The watch item is execution of the Chipotle and Raising Cane's introductions. Both are premium brands in concept but require careful launch market discipline.

## Question Three: Why does value compound?

Three engines plus a kicker. First: Mexico growth, where Starbucks, Domino's, and full-service brands all grew positively in the fourth quarter. Second: portfolio simplification and margin expansion through divestments of underperforming brands and additions of high-quality concepts. Third: refinancing-driven free cash flow expansion — annualised twenty-five-million-dollar savings translate to meaningful free-cash-flow improvement. The kicker is the World Cup 2026 — expected to boost traffic for Domino's, Starbucks, and Chili's during the tournament period in Mexico and other regional markets.

## Question Four: What proves us right, and what to track?

Six metrics. Same-store sales growth — four-point-three percent for the year; sustained low-to-mid-single-digit confirms category resilience. Mexico revenue growth — seven-and-a-half percent; sustained mid-single-digit confirms the Mexico anchor. Earnings margin — seventeen percent in the fourth quarter; sustained expansion confirms portfolio simplification works. New brand performance — Chipotle and Raising Cane's roll-out execution. Net debt to earnings — two-and-a-half times; trajectory toward two times confirms balance-sheet discipline. Free cash flow generation — refinancing savings should improve.

## Question Five: What's it worth?

Full-year 2025 net income was two-and-a-half billion pesos, roughly one hundred and thirty million US dollars. Earnings per share was two-point-seven-eight pesos. The share price ended the year at fifty-three-point-eight-seven pesos, up twenty-four percent. Net debt to earnings was two-and-a-half times. Market cap is around two-and-a-half billion US dollars.

On trading multiples, Alsea trades at roughly fifteen to eighteen times trailing earnings on the recovery numbers and around nine to ten times earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation. For a Mexican-anchored multi-brand operator with the portfolio reshape complete and World Cup 2026 ahead, that is reasonable but reflects the smaller-cap and South American exposure discounts.

The simple framing: a portfolio-reshaped multi-brand restaurant operator at small-cap multiples, with margin expansion and free-cash-flow improvement ahead. Comparable set: Restaurant Brands International, Yum Brands, Domino's Pizza Inc. at higher multiples for similar growth.

The key sensitivity is Mexican and European consumer health. France remains below pre-boycott sales — recovery is slow.

## Question Six: Where is the market wrong?

First variant: consensus prices Alsea on the messy 2023-to-2024 portfolio period, when the 2025 reshape has clinically simplified the portfolio and added premium brands. The eighty-nine-percent net income growth in 2025 is signal, not noise. Second: the Chipotle and Raising Cane's additions are priced as marketing announcements but are real growth optionality — Mexican premium fast-casual is significantly under-supplied versus US benchmarks.

The honest counter: France and Argentina remain weak; South America rationalisation is incomplete; the smaller market cap creates illiquidity discounts. Net debt to earnings at two-and-a-half is reasonable but not low.

## Question Seven: What could kill it, and when do we sell?

Risks: Mexican consumer slowdown; European consumer weakness in France and Spain; FX volatility; integration of Chipotle and Raising Cane's; debt refinancing pressure if rate environment shifts; security events affecting operations.

Sell triggers: same-store sales growth turning negative for two consecutive quarters; Mexico revenue growth decelerating below five percent; earnings margin compressing more than one hundred basis points; Chipotle or Raising Cane's launches producing material losses; net debt to earnings rising above three times.
